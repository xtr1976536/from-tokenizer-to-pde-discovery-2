Threshold scattering below the ground-state energy and gradient is established for the three-dimensional focusing energy-critical nonlinear Schrödinger equation. A single solution-dependent Gaussian interaction scale, mass-free angular coercivity, and a frequency-gap source estimate control the projected dynamics. These estimates yield quartic spacetime integrability and a zero-mass contradiction, without radial symmetry or an a priori $L^{2}$ bound. Key words and phrases: focusing energy-critical nonlinear Schrödinger equation, threshold scattering, interaction Morawetz estimate, concentration compactness, rigidity, nonradial data 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification Primary 35Q55; Secondary 35B40, 35P25 1. Introduction The competition between dispersion and concentration is a central problem
in nonlinear Schrödinger dynamics. Linear Schrödinger evolution spreads
a wave, whereas a focusing interaction can sustain a stationary profile or
produce concentration. At the energy-critical power, rescaling can change
the spatial and temporal concentration scales without changing the energy
norm. Energy conservation therefore does not by itself distinguish
scattering from a persistent concentration scenario. The sharp question is
whether the ground state marks the boundary between these behaviors. In three dimensions this question is particularly delicate for nonradial
data in the homogeneous energy space. Such data need not have finite mass,
and a concentrated profile may move without any prescribed control of its
center. A proof must simultaneously recover positivity for the focusing
interaction, accommodate this motion, and control the low-frequency part
without assuming the mass that the argument is intended to construct.
The present paper gives a Gaussian interaction proof in which these issues
are separated into quantitative spatial estimates, a density-selected scale,
and an explicit estimate for the projected nonlinear source. The equation and its threshold. We consider (1.1) $(i\partial_{t}+\Delta)u=-|u|^{4}u,\qquad u(0)=u_{0}\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\qquad F(z):=|z|^{4}z.$ Here the homogeneous space is represented by $L^{6}$ functions with weak
gradient in $L^{2}$ , and its norm is $\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}:=\|\nabla f\|_{2}$ .
The critical scaling is $u(t,x)\mapsto a^{1/2}u(a^{2}t,ax)$ , $a>0$ .
For $f\in\dot{H}^{1}$ , set $A(f):=\|\nabla f\|_{2}^{2},\qquad V(f):=\|f\|_{6}^{6},\qquad E(f):=\tfrac{1}{2}A(f)-\tfrac{1}{6}V(f).$ The stationary ground state is $W(x):=(1+|x|^{2}/3)^{-1/2}$ . With $G:=3^{3/2}\pi^{2}/4$ , the sharp
Sobolev inequality and its normalization are (1.2) $\forall f\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\qquad V(f)\leq G^{-2}A(f)^{3},\qquad A(W)=V(W)=G,\qquad E(W)=G/3.$ The sharp constant and optimizer are given in [ 1 ] and [ 24 ] .
Indeed the sixth power of Talenti’s constant is $16/(27\pi^{4})=G^{-2}$ .
The elliptic identity [ 15 ] gives $0<A(W)=V(W)$ ; equality in the Sobolev estimate gives $A(W)=G^{-2}A(W)^{3}$ . Positivity then gives $A(W)=G$ and $E(W)=G/3$ . A strong solution on an interval $I\ni 0$ means a function $u\in C(I;\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))$ such that $u\in L^{10}_{t,x}(J\times\mathbb{R}^{3})$ for every compact interval $J\Subset I$ and such that Duhamel’s formula holds on $I$ . The continuation, stability
and scattering criteria used below are those of [ 3 , 26 ] and [ 15 ] . For initial data $u_{0}\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})$ , let $u:I_{\max}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}$ denote the unique
maximal-lifespan strong solution of ( 1.1 ). With the solution class fixed, the threshold-scattering statement is as follows. Theorem 1.1 . Let $u_{0}\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})$ satisfy $E(u_{0})<E(W),\qquad\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{2}<\|\nabla W\|_{2}.$ Then $I_{\max}=\mathbb{R},\qquad\|u\|_{L^{10}_{t,x}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}<\infty.$ Moreover, there exist $u_{+},u_{-}\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})$ such that $\lim_{t\to+\infty}\|u(t)-e^{it\Delta}u_{+}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}=0,\qquad\lim_{t\to-\infty}\|u(t)-e^{it\Delta}u_{-}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}=0.$ Thus the theorem gives global existence, finite critical spacetime size,
and scattering in both time directions in the homogeneous energy class;
no $L^{2}$ assumption is imposed on $u_{0}$ . We next place this statement in
the existing scattering theory. Development of the scattering theory. For the three-dimensional defocusing energy-critical equation, Colliander,
Keel, Staffilani, Takaoka and Tao [ 6 ] established scattering for
general energy-space data through frequency-localized interaction Morawetz
estimates and induction on energy. The difference-variable interaction
can follow concentration without choosing a fixed spatial center.
For the focusing equation, Kenig and Merle [ 15 ] proved the
below-ground-state result for radial data in dimensions three through five,
using concentration compactness and a rigidity argument. Killip and Visan [ 17 ] removed radial symmetry in dimensions at least five through
additional decay and negative regularity. Dodson [ 8 ] treated
dimension four using long-time Strichartz estimates to overcome the
logarithmic obstruction. These results identify both the role of the
threshold and the sensitivity of the low-frequency argument to dimension. Recent work on the three-dimensional nonradial problem includes
Chung–Han’s conditional rigidity under a center-speed hypothesis [ 5 ] , and their exclusion of the finite-mass bounded-scale channel [ 4 ] . The latter leaves a residual quasi-soliton channel and does not
by itself prove scattering for all homogeneous-energy data.
Su and Zhao [ 23 ] also study this threshold-scattering problem in a recent
preprint. Related interaction and moving-scale methods appear in [ 20 , 10 , 9 , 2 ] . The homogeneous three-dimensional obstacles. The threshold inequalities first provide an unweighted coercive gap.
Energy conservation, ( 1.2 ) and continuity prevent $A(u(t))$ from
reaching $G$ , since at such a time the energy would be at least $G/3$ .
The quantitative energy trap [ 15 ] consequently gives $\sup_{t}A(u(t))\leq B^{2}\leq(1-\gamma)G,\qquad A(u(t))-V(u(t))\geq(2\gamma-\gamma^{2})A(u(t))$ for fixed $\gamma>0$ . This global inequality does not directly control
a focusing interaction with a spatially varying anisotropic Hessian.
The negative potential term must be compared with the appropriate
directional derivatives at each relative separation. There is also an infrared obstruction. The energy controls $\nabla u$ in $L^{2}$ , but the sum of low-frequency $L^{2}$ contributions
can diverge. In particular, even the ground state has infinite mass in
dimension three. A Galilean normalization of momentum would introduce
a multiple of $u$ into the gradient and is therefore unavailable without
additional control. A fixed-center virial cutoff encounters the separate
difficulty that almost periodicity does not furnish a velocity or a
sublinear-drift bound for the center. We instead apply a translation-invariant
interaction to the finite-mass highpass $h=P_{>\nu}u$ .
Its mass depends on $\nu$ , so recovering positivity by constants involving
that mass would not close the argument. Finally, projection is not compatible with the quintic nonlinearity.
The defect $P_{>\nu}F(u)-F(P_{>\nu}u)$ contributes both momentum and mass
sources. A bound that is merely finite, or that presupposes the desired
spacetime norm, cannot be absorbed. The source estimate must use the
same quartic quantity and the same terminal intervals as the positive
interaction term, with a coefficient tending to zero as $\nu\downarrow 0$ . The spatial estimates and the Gaussian choice. The first part of our construction converts the scalar threshold gap into
a usable two-point tensor inequality. Convex phase reduction expresses the
complex-to-modulus difference as an explicit nonnegative square.
The angular estimate then treats every nonnegative radial weight for which $rw(r)$ is bounded and nondecreasing. Its proof combines a one-direction
dilation, a two-dimensional inversion, a change of the longitudinal

The resulting constant is explicit and independent of the $L^{2}$ size.
A complementary convolution estimate is obtained through
a regularized Fisher-type contraction. The two lemmas have different

two lemmas, while the density form has a nonnegative Fourier multiplier. Its physical-space kernel changes sign, so the
proof keeps the complete quadratic form throughout the core–tail

neighborhood of the normalized compact orbit. All cutoff and error choices

control. The density-dependent selector resolves this by choosing the same

radius. More importantly, the Gaussian profile determining the root is

small bound on the absolute speed of the radius is not needed. A spatial frequency-gap estimate and closure. The projected output is handled by holding the complete

Exact Fourier-support separation removes every low-weight far interaction.

For each fixed lowpass frequency, dual endpoint Strichartz and no-waste

Low-Frequency Control and Time Selection 3.1. A mass-free forcing envelope The refined Sobolev inequality [ 19 ] applies to $v\in\dot{H}^{1}$ with finite displayed Besov seminorm and has the form (3.1) $\|v\|_{4}^{4}\leq C\left(\sup_{L>0}L^{-1}\|P_{L}v\|_{\infty}\right)^{2}\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}.$ The bound over all $L>0$ includes the dyadic Besov bound in this refinement.
