Running Title: Boussinesq-MHD-Landau-Lifshitz 1 Introduction Let $\Omega$ be a bounded, simply connected domain in $\mathbb{R}^{3}$ with smooth boundary $\partial\Omega$ , and $\nu$ be the unit outward normal vector to $\partial\Omega$ . First, we consider the regularity criterion of the Boussinesq system with zero heat conductivity: $\displaystyle\mathrm{div}\,u=0,$ (1.1) $\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla\pi-\Delta u=\theta e_{3},$ (1.2) $\displaystyle\partial_{t}\theta+u\cdot\nabla\theta=0\ \ \mathrm{in}\ \ \Omega\times(0,\infty),$ (1.3) $\displaystyle u\cdot\nu=0,\mathrm{curl}\,u\times\nu=0\ \ \mathrm{on}\ \ \partial\Omega\times(0,\infty),$ (1.4) $\displaystyle(u,\theta)(\cdot,0)=(u_{0},\theta_{0})\ \ \mathrm{in}\ \ \Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3},$ (1.5) where $u,\pi$ , and $\theta$ denote unknown velocity vector field, pressure scalar, and temperature scalar of the fluid, respectively. $\omega:=\mathrm{curl}\,u$ is the vorticity and $e_{3}:=(0,0,1)^{t}$ . When $\theta=0$ , ( 1.1 ) and ( 1.2 ) are the well-known Navier-Stokes system. Giga [ 1 ] , Kim [ 2 ] , Kang and Kim [ 3 ] have proved some Serrin type regularity criteria. The first aim of this paper is to prove a new regularity criterion for the problem ( 1.1 )-( 1.5 ), we will prove Theorem 1.1 . Let $u_{0}\in H^{3},\theta_{0}\in W^{1,p}$ with $3<p\leq 6$ and $\mathrm{div}\,u_{0}=0$ in $\Omega$ and $u_{0}\cdot\nu=0,\mathrm{curl}\,u_{0}\times\nu=0$ on $\partial\Omega$ . Let $(u,\theta)$ be a strong solution of the problem ( 1.1 )-( 1.5 ). If $u$ satisfies $\nabla u\in L^{1}(0,T;BMO(\Omega))$ (1.6) with $0<T<\infty$ , then the solution $(u,\theta)$ can be extended beyond $T>0$ . Here $BMO$ denotes the space of bounded mean oscillation. Secondly, we consider the blow-up criterion of the 3D MHD system $\displaystyle\mathrm{div}\,u=\mathrm{div}\,b=0,$ (1.7) $\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla\left(\pi+\frac{1}{2}|b|^{2}\right)-\Delta u=b\cdot\nabla b,$ (1.8) $\displaystyle\partial_{t}b+u\cdot\nabla b-b\cdot\nabla u=\Delta b\ \ in\ \ \Omega\times(0,\infty),$ (1.9) $\displaystyle u\cdot\nu=0,\mathrm{curl}\,u\times\nu=0,b\cdot\nu=0,\mathrm{curl}\,b\times\nu=0\ \ on\ \ \partial\Omega\times(0,\infty),$ (1.10) $\displaystyle(u,b)(\cdot,0)=(u_{0},b_{0})\ \ in\ \ \Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}.$ (1.11) Here $b$ is the magnetic field of the fluid. It is well-known that the problem ( 1.7 )-( 1.11 ) has a unique local strong solution [ 4 ] . But whether this local solution can exist globally is an outstanding problem. Kang and Kim [ 3 ] prove some Serrin type regularity criteria. The second aim of this paper is to prove a new regularity criterion for the problem ( 1.7 )-( 1.11 ), we will prove Theorem 1.2 . Let $u_{0},b_{0}\in H^{3}$ with $\mathrm{div}\,u_{0}=\mathrm{div}\,b_{0}=0$ in $\Omega$ and $u_{0}\cdot\nu=b_{0}\cdot\nu=0,\mathrm{curl}\,u_{0}\times\nu=\mathrm{curl}\,b_{0}\times\nu=0$ on $\partial\Omega$ . Let $(u,b)$ be a strong solution to the problem ( 1.7 )-( 1.11 ). If ( 1.6 ) holds true, then the solution $(u,b)$ can be extended beyond $T>0$ . Remark 1.1 . When $\Omega:=\mathbb{R}^{3}$ , our result gives the following
well-known regularity criterion $\omega:=\mathrm{curl}\,u\in L^{1}(0,T;\dot{B}_{\infty,\infty}^{0}),$ but the method of proof we used is different
from that in [ 14 , 15 ] . Here $\dot{B}_{\infty,\infty}^{0}$ denotes
the homogeneous Besov space [ 13 ] . Next, we consider the following 3D density-dependent MHD equations: $\displaystyle\mathrm{div}\,u=\mathrm{div}\,b=0,$ (1.12) $\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\mathrm{div}\,(\rho u)=0,$ (1.13) $\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\mathrm{div}\,(\rho u\otimes u)+\nabla\left(\pi+\frac{1}{2}|b|^{2}\right)-\Delta u=b\cdot\nabla b,$ (1.14) $\displaystyle\partial_{t}b+u\cdot\nabla b-b\cdot\nabla u=\Delta b\ \ in\ \ \Omega\times(0,\infty),$ (1.15) $\displaystyle u=0,b\cdot\nu=0,\mathrm{curl}\,b\times\nu=0\ \ on\ \ \partial\Omega\times(0,\infty),$ (1.16) $\displaystyle(\rho,\rho u,b)(\cdot,0)=(\rho_{0},\rho_{0}u_{0},b_{0})\ \ in\ \ \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}.$ (1.17) For this problem, in [ 5 ] , Wu proved that if the initial data $\rho_{0},u_{0}$ , and $b_{0}$ satisfy $0\leq\rho_{0}\in H^{2},u_{0}\in H_{0}^{1}\cap H^{2},b_{0}\in H^{2},-\Delta u_{0}+\nabla\left(\pi_{0}+\frac{1}{2}|b_{0}|^{2}\right)=b_{0}\cdot\nabla b_{0}+\sqrt{\rho_{0}}g$ (1.18) for some $(\pi_{0},g)\in H^{1}\times L^{2}$ , then there exists a positive time $T_{*}$ and a unique strong solution $(\rho,u,b)$ to the problem ( 1.12 )-( 1.17 ) such that $\begin{array}[]{l}\rho\in C([0,T_{*}];H^{2}),u\in C([0,T_{*}];H_{0}^{1}\cap H^{2})\cap L^{2}(0,T_{*};H^{2}),\\
u_{t}\in L^{2}(0,T_{*};H_{0}^{1}),\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\in L^{\infty}(0,T_{*};L^{2}),\\
b\in L^{\infty}(0,T_{*};H^{2})\cap L^{2}(0,T_{*};H^{3}),b_{t}\in L^{\infty}(0,T_{*};L^{2})\cap L^{2}(0,T_{*};H^{1}).\end{array}$ (1.19) And when $b=0$ , Kim [ 2 ] proved the following regularity
criterion: $u\in L^{\frac{2s}{s-3}}(0,T;L_{w}^{s}(\Omega))\ \ \mathrm{with}\ \ 3<s\leq\infty.$ (1.20) Here $L_{w}^{s}$ denotes the weak- $L^{s}$ space and $L_{w}^{\infty}=L^{\infty}$ . The aim of this paper is to refine ( 1.20 ), we will prove Theorem 1.3 . Let $\rho_{0},u_{0}$ , and $b_{0}$ satisfy ( 1.18 ). Let $(\rho,u,b)$ be a strong solution of the problem ( 1.12 )-( 1.17 ) in the class ( 1.19 ). If $u$ satisfies one of the following two conditions: $\displaystyle(i)\quad\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\|u(t)\|_{L_{w}^{s}}^{\frac{2s}{s-3}}}{1+\log(e+\|u(t)\|_{L_{w}^{s}})}dt<\infty\ \ with\ \ 3<s\leq\infty,$ (1.21) $\displaystyle(ii)\quad u\in L^{2}(0,T;BMO(\Omega))$ (1.22) with $0<T<\infty$ , then the solution $(\rho,u,b)$ can be extended beyond $T>0$ . Finally, we consider the 3D Landau-Lifshitz system: $\displaystyle\partial_{t}d-\Delta d=d|\nabla d|^{2}+d\times\Delta d,|d|=1\ \ in\ \ \Omega\times(0,\infty),$ (1.23) $\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}d=0\ \ on\ \ \partial\Omega\times(0,\infty),$ (1.24) $\displaystyle d(\cdot,0)=d_{0},|d_{0}|=1\ \ in\ \ \Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}.$ (1.25) Carbou and Fabrie [ 6 ] showed the existence and uniqueness of local smooth solutions. When $\Omega:=\mathbb{R}^{n}\ (n=2,3,4)$ , Fan and Ozawa [ 7 ] proved some regularity criteria. The aim of this paper is to prove a logarithmic blow-up criterion for the problem ( 1.23 )-( 1.25 ) when $\Omega$ is a bounded domain. We will prove Theorem 1.4 . Let $d_{0}\in H^{3}(\Omega)$ with $|d_{0}|=1$ in $\Omega$ and $\partial_{\nu}d_{0}=0$ on $\partial\Omega$ . Let $d$ be a local smooth solution to the problem ( 1.23 )-( 1.25 ). If $d$ satisfies $\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\|\nabla d\|_{L^{q}}^{\frac{2q}{q-3}}}{1+\log(e+\|\nabla d\|_{L^{q}})}dt<\infty\ \ with\ \ 3<q\leq\infty,$ (1.26) and $0<T<\infty$ , then the solution can be extended beyond $T>0$ . First, using the formula $a\times(b\times c)=(a\cdot c)b-(a\cdot b)c$ , and the fact that $|d|=1$ implies $d\Delta d=-|\nabla d|^{2}$ , we have the following equivalent equation $\frac{1}{2}d_{t}-\frac{1}{2}d\times d_{t}=\Delta d+d|\nabla d|^{2}.$ (6.1) Testing ( 6.1 ) by $d_{t}$ and using $(a\times b)\cdot b=0$ and $d\cdot d_{t}=0$ , we get $\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla d|^{2}dx+\int_{\Omega}|d_{t}|^{2}dx\leq 0.$ (6.2) Testing ( 1.23 ) by $-\Delta d_{t}$ and using $|d|=1$ , we find that $\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta d|^{2}dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla d_{t}|^{2}dx=-\int_{\Omega}(d|\nabla d|^{2}+d\times\Delta d)\cdot\Delta d_{t}dx$ (6.3) $\displaystyle=$ $\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla(d|\nabla d|^{2}+d\times\Delta d)\cdot\nabla d_{t}dx$ $\displaystyle\leq$ $\displaystyle C(\|\nabla d\|_{L^{q}}\|\nabla d\|_{L^{\frac{4q}{q-2}}}^{2}+\|\nabla d\|_{L^{q}}\|\Delta d\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-2}}}+\|\nabla\Delta d\|_{L^{2}})\|\nabla d_{t}\|_{L^{2}}$ $\displaystyle\leq$ $\displaystyle C(\|\nabla d\|_{L^{q}}\|\Delta d\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-2}}}+\|\nabla\Delta d\|_{L^{2}})\|\nabla d_{t}\|_{L^{2}}$ $\displaystyle\leq$ $\displaystyle C(\|\nabla d\|_{L^{q}}\|\Delta d\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\frac{3}{q}}\|d\|_{H^{3}}^{\frac{3}{q}}+\|d\|_{H^{3}})\|\nabla d_{t}\|_{L^{2}}$ $\displaystyle\leq$ $\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla d_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\delta\|d\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+C\|\nabla d\|_{L^{q}}^{\frac{2q}{q-3}}\|\Delta d\|_{L^{2}}^{2}$ for any $0<\delta<1$ . Here we have used the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities: $\displaystyle\|\nabla d\|_{L^{\frac{4q}{q-2}}}^{2}\leq C\|d\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Delta d\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-2}}},$ (6.4) $\displaystyle\|\Delta d\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-2}}}\leq C\|\Delta d\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\frac{3}{q}}\|d\|_{H^{3}}^{\frac{3}{q}}.$ (6.5) Applying $\partial_{i}$ to ( 1.23 ), we get $\partial_{i}d_{t}-\Delta\partial_{i}d=\partial_{i}(d|\nabla d|^{2})+\partial_{i}d\times\Delta d+d\times\Delta\partial_{i}d.$ Testing the above equation by $\Delta\partial_{i}d$ , summing over $i$ , and using ( 6.4 ) and ( 6.5 ) and $|d|=1$ , we obtain $\displaystyle\|d\|_{H^{3}}$ $\displaystyle\leq$ $\displaystyle C(\|d\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla\Delta d\|_{L^{2}})$ $\displaystyle\leq$ $\displaystyle C+C\|\nabla d_{t}\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\nabla(d|\nabla d|^{2})\|_{L^{2}}+\sum\limits_{i}C\|\partial_{i}d\times\Delta d\|_{L^{2}}$ $\displaystyle\leq$ $\displaystyle C+C\|\nabla d_{t}\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\nabla d\|_{L^{q}}\|\nabla d\|_{L^{\frac{4q}{q-2}}}^{2}+C\|\nabla d\|_{L^{q}}\|\Delta d\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-2}}}$ $\displaystyle\leq$ $\displaystyle C+C\|\nabla d_{t}\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\nabla d\|_{L^{q}}\|\Delta d\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-2}}}$ $\displaystyle\leq$ $\displaystyle C+C\|\nabla d_{t}\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\nabla d\|_{L^{q}}\|\Delta d\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\frac{3}{q}}\|d\|_{H^{3}}^{\frac{3}{q}},$ which yields $\|d\|_{H^{3}}\leq C+C\|\nabla d_{t}\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\nabla d\|_{L^{q}}^{\frac{q}{q-3}}\|\Delta d\|_{L^{2}}.$ (6.6) Plugging ( 6.6 ) into ( 6.3 ) and taking $\delta$ small enough, we have $\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta d|^{2}dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla d_{t}|^{2}dx$ $\displaystyle\leq$ $\displaystyle C+C\|\nabla d\|_{L^{q}}^{\frac{2q}{q-3}}\|\Delta d\|_{L^{2}}^{2}$ $\displaystyle\leq$ $\displaystyle C+C\frac{\|\nabla d\|_{L^{q}}^{\frac{2q}{q-3}}}{1+\log(e+\|\nabla d\|_{L^{q}})}\|\Delta d\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\log(e+\|\nabla d\|_{L^{q}})$ $\displaystyle\leq$ $\displaystyle C+C\frac{\|\nabla d\|_{L^{q}}^{\frac{2q}{q-3}}}{1+\log(e+\|\nabla d\|_{L^{q}})}\|\Delta d\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\log(e+y),$ which implies $\int_{\Omega}|\Delta d|^{2}dx+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla d_{t}|^{2}dxd\tau\leq C(e+y)^{C_{0}\epsilon}.$ (6.7) Provided that $\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\frac{\|\nabla d\|_{L^{q}}^{\frac{2q}{q-3}}}{1+\log(e+\|\nabla d\|_{L^{q}})}d\tau\leq\epsilon<<1,$ with $y(t):=\sup\limits_{[t_{0},t]}\|d\|_{H^{3}}$ for any $0<t_{0}\leq t\leq T$ and $C_{0}$ is an absolute constant. It follows from ( 1.23 ), ( 6.6 ) and ( 6.7 ) that $\int_{\Omega}|d_{t}|^{2}dx+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\|d\|_{H^{3}}^{2}d\tau\leq C(e+y)^{C_{0}\epsilon}.$ (6.8) Applying $\partial_{t}$ to ( 1.23 ), testing by $-\Delta d_{t}$ , and using $|d|=1$ , ( 6.7 ) and ( 6.8 ), we have $\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla d_{t}|^{2}dx+\int_{\Omega}|\Delta d_{t}|^{2}dx=-\int_{\Omega}[\partial_{t}(d|\nabla d|^{2})+d_{t}\times\Delta d]\Delta d_{t}dx$ $\displaystyle\leq$ $\displaystyle C(\|\nabla d\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\|d_{t}\|_{L^{6}}+\|\nabla d\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla d_{t}\|_{L^{3}}+\|d_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Delta d\|_{L^{2}})\|\Delta d_{t}\|_{L^{2}}$ $\displaystyle\leq$ $\displaystyle C(\|\nabla d\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\|d_{t}\|_{L^{6}}+\|\Delta d\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla d_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\Delta d_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|\Delta d\|_{L^{2}}\|d_{t}\|_{L^{2}})\|\Delta d_{t}\|_{L^{2}}$ $\displaystyle\leq$ $\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta d_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|d\|_{H^{2}}^{4}\|d_{t}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+C\|d\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\|d_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},$ which implies $\int_{\Omega}|\nabla d_{t}|^{2}dx+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\|\Delta d_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau\leq C(e+y)^{C_{0}\epsilon}.$ (6.9) It follows from ( 6.6 ), ( 6.7 ), ( 6.8 ) and ( 6.9 ) that $\|d\|_{H^{3}}\leq C+C\|\nabla d_{t}\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\nabla d\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\|\Delta d\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(e+y)^{C_{0}\epsilon},$ which leads to $\|d\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{3})}\leq C.$ This completes the proof of Theorem 1.4 . $\square$ 7 Acknowledgments J.
