China Abstract The low Mach number limit for one-dimensional non-isentropic compressible Navier-Stokes system without viscosity is investigated, where the density and temperature have different asymptotic states at far fields. It is proved that the solution of the system converges to a nonlinear diffusion wave globally in time as Mach number goes to zero. It is remarked that the velocity of diffusion wave is proportional with the variation of temperature. Furthermore, it is shown that the solution of compressible Navier-Stokes system also has the same phenomenon when Mach number is suitably small. Keywords: Compressible Navier-Stokes equations, low Mach limit, nonlinear diffusion wave 1. Introduction The one-dimensional compressible Navier-Stokes system in Lagrangian coordinates reads $\displaystyle v_{t}-u_{x}=0,$ $\displaystyle u_{t}+P_{x}=(\mu\frac{u_{x}}{v})_{x},$ (1.1) $\displaystyle(e+\frac{u^{2}}{2})_{t}+(Pu)_{x}=(\kappa\frac{\theta_{x}}{v}+\mu\frac{uu_{x}}{v})_{x},$ where the unknown functions $v$ , $u$ and $\theta$ represent the specific volume, velocity and temperature, respectively, while $\mu>0$ and $\kappa>0$ denote the viscosity and heat conductivity coefficients respectively. Here, we consider the perfect gas, so that the pressure function $P$ and the internal function $e$ are given by $P=R\frac{\theta}{v},\quad\textrm{and}\quad e=c_{v}\theta+const.,$ where the parameters $R>0$ and $c_{v}>0$ are the gas constant and heat capacity at the constant volume respectively. For simplicity, we assume $\mu$ and $\kappa$ are constants, and normalize $R=1$ and $c_{v}=1$ . The low Mach limit is an important and interesting problem in fluid dynamics. The first result is due to Klainerman and Majda [ 16 , 17 ] , in which they proved the incompressible limit of the isentropic Euler equations to the incompressible Euler equations for local smooth solutions. In [ 1 ] , Alazard showed the incompressible limit for Navier-Stokes equations in the whole space. Note that in [ 1 ] , the solutions have the same states at the far fields. Recently, Huang et.al. [ 13 ] began to study the case that the solutions have different end states and found that the solutions of compressible Navier-Stokes equations converge to a nonlinear diffusion wave solution globally in time as Mach number goes to zero, which is related to the thermal creep flow. That is, the flow in diffusion wave is only driven by the variation of temperature. This phenomenon is quite different from the constant case. Since the diffusion wave is independent of the viscosity $\mu=0$ , we conjecture that the result of [ 13 ] is still valid without viscosity, that is $\mu=0$ in the system ( 1.1 ). Precisely speaking, we consider the non-viscous and heat-conductive gas in the following system $\displaystyle v_{t}-u_{x}=0,$ $\displaystyle u_{t}+P_{x}=0,$ (1.2) $\displaystyle(\theta+\frac{u^{2}}{2})_{t}+(Pu)_{x}=(\kappa\frac{\theta_{x}}{v})_{x},$ where the only difference with the system ( 1.1 ) is that $\mu=0$ . We will prove that the solution of system ( 1.2 ) converges to a nonlinear diffusion wave solution globally in time as Mach number tends to zero. Moreover, as the Mach number is suitably small, the flow is only driven by the variation of temperature. Let $\varepsilon$ be the compressibility parameter, which represents the maximum Mach number of the fluid. As in [ 26 ] , we set $t\rightarrow\varepsilon t,\quad x\rightarrow x,\quad u\rightarrow\varepsilon u,\quad\mu\rightarrow\varepsilon\mu,\quad\kappa\rightarrow\varepsilon\kappa.$ By the above changes of variables, system ( 1.2 ) is written as $\displaystyle v_{t}^{\varepsilon}-u_{x}^{\varepsilon}=0,$ $\displaystyle u_{t}^{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}P^{\varepsilon}_{x}=0,$ (1.3) $\displaystyle[\theta^{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}(\varepsilon u^{\varepsilon})^{2}]_{t}+(P^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon})_{x}=\kappa(\frac{\theta_{x}^{\varepsilon}}{v^{\varepsilon}})_{x}.$ As $\varepsilon\rightarrow 0$ , the limit of solutions of ( 1.3 ) is called the low Mach limit [ 16 , 17 ] . Similar to [ 1 , 13 , 26 ] , we assume that the pressure is a small perturbation of a given constant state $\bar{P}>0$ , i.e. $P=\bar{P}+O(\varepsilon),$ (1.4) and without loss of generality, we further assume $\bar{P}$ to be $1$ .
Formally, as the Mach number goes to zero, the limit system of ( 1.3 ) is $\displaystyle(2u-\kappa T_{x})_{x}=0,\,\,\rho=T^{-1},$ $\displaystyle u_{t}+\pi_{x}=0,$ (1.5) $\displaystyle\theta_{t}+u_{x}=\kappa(\frac{\theta_{x}}{v})_{x},$ and $v_{t}-u_{x}=0,$ in which $\pi_{x}$ is the limit of $\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}P^{\varepsilon}_{x}$ . We will study the low Mach limit and what happens in the limiting process when the background is not constant state, i.e. $(v^{\varepsilon},\theta^{\varepsilon})(x,t)\rightarrow(v_{\pm},\theta_{\pm}),\quad{\rm as}\,x\rightarrow\pm\infty,\quad{\rm with}\,\,\frac{\theta_{-}}{v_{-}}=\frac{\theta_{+}}{v_{+}},$ (1.6) where $\theta_{-}$ may not be equal to $\theta_{+}$ . Following [ 13 ] , we shall construct a spacial diffusive wave $(\bar{v},\bar{u},\bar{\theta},\bar{\pi})$ of ( 1.5 ) by choosing $(\bar{v},\bar{u},\bar{\theta})\triangleq(T,\frac{\kappa T_{x}}{2T},T),$ (1.7) where $T(\eta),\,\eta=\frac{x}{\sqrt{1+t}}$ is the unique self-similar solution of the following diffusion equation $T_{t}=(\frac{\kappa T_{x}}{2T})_{x},\qquad\lim_{x\rightarrow\pm\infty}T=\theta_{\pm},$ (1.8) and $\bar{\pi}$ is a solution of ( 1.5 ) 2 . For the existence and uniqueness of the self-similar solution of ( 1.8 ), see [ 2 ] and [ 3 ] . Set $\delta=|\theta_{+}-\theta_{-}|$ , then the $T(x,t)$ has the following asymptotic expression $T_{x}(x,t)=O(1)\delta(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{4d(\theta_{\pm})(1+t)}},\quad d(\theta_{\pm})=\frac{\kappa}{2\theta_{\pm}},\quad{\rm as}\,x\rightarrow\pm\infty.$ (1.9) (We will prove this approximation by the idea and method from [ 7 ] in Appendix .)
Because $(\bar{v},\bar{u},\bar{\theta})$ is not the solution of ( 1.3 ), there will be some non-integrated error terms in the system. It is necessary to introduce a new profile to approximate towards system ( 1.3 ). That is $(\tilde{v},\tilde{u},\tilde{\theta})\triangleq(\bar{v},\bar{u},\bar{\theta}-\frac{1}{2}(\varepsilon\bar{u})^{2}).$ (1.10) Then, we have $\|(\bar{v}-\tilde{v},\bar{u}-\tilde{u},\bar{\theta}-\tilde{\theta})\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\leqslant C\varepsilon^{2}(1+t)^{-1},$ which implies that $(\tilde{v},\tilde{u},\tilde{\theta})$ approximates the diffusive wave solution $(\bar{v},\bar{u},\bar{\theta})$ in $L^{2}-$ norm.
A direct calculation implies $\displaystyle\tilde{v}_{t}-\tilde{u}_{x}=0,$ $\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\tilde{u}_{t}+\tilde{P}_{x}=R_{1x},$ (1.11) $\displaystyle[\tilde{\theta}+\frac{1}{2}(\varepsilon\tilde{u})^{2}]_{t}+(\tilde{P}\tilde{u})_{x}=\kappa(\frac{\tilde{\theta}_{x}}{\tilde{v}})_{x}+R_{2x},$ in which $R_{1}=\varepsilon^{2}\frac{\kappa T_{t}}{2T}-\varepsilon^{2}\frac{\tilde{u}^{2}}{2T}=O(1)\delta\varepsilon^{2}(1+t)^{-1}e^{-\frac{c_{\pm}x^{2}}{1+t}},$ (1.12) and $R_{2}=\varepsilon^{2}\kappa\frac{\tilde{u}\tilde{u}_{x}}{T}-\varepsilon^{2}\frac{\tilde{u}^{3}}{2T}=O(1)\delta\varepsilon^{2}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}e^{-\frac{c_{\pm}x^{2}}{1+t}},$ (1.13) as $x\rightarrow\pm\infty$ . The initial data of ( 1.3 ) is given by $(v^{\varepsilon},u^{\varepsilon},\theta^{\varepsilon})|_{t=0}=(\tilde{v},\tilde{u},\tilde{\theta})(x,0).$ (1.14) Then we obtain the following global existence and uniform estimates. Theorem 1 (Uniform Estimates). Let $(\tilde{v},\tilde{u},\tilde{\theta})$ be the diffusive wave defined by ( 1.10 ) and set $\delta=|\theta_{+}-\theta_{-}|$ to be the wave strength. Then there exist constants $\varepsilon_{0}>0$ and $\delta_{0}>0$ , such that if $\varepsilon\leqslant\varepsilon_{0}$ and $\delta\leqslant\delta_{0}$ , the Cauchy problem ( 1.3 ) with the initial values ( 1.14 ) has a unique global smooth solution $(v^{\varepsilon},u^{\varepsilon},\theta^{\varepsilon})$ satisfying $\displaystyle\|(v^{\varepsilon}-\tilde{v},\varepsilon u^{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon\tilde{u},\theta^{\varepsilon}-\tilde{\theta})\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{2}$ $\displaystyle\leqslant$ $\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{3}\sqrt{\delta}(1+t)^{-1+C_{0}\sqrt{\delta}},$ $\displaystyle\|(v^{\varepsilon}-\tilde{v},\varepsilon u^{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon\tilde{u},\theta^{\varepsilon}-\tilde{\theta})_{x}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{2}$ $\displaystyle\leqslant$ $\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{\frac{8}{3}}\sqrt{\delta}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}+C_{0}\sqrt{\delta}},$ (1.15) $\displaystyle{\rm and}\qquad\quad\|(\theta^{\varepsilon}-\tilde{\theta})_{xx}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{2}$ $\displaystyle\leqslant$ $\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}\sqrt{\delta}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}+C_{0}\sqrt{\delta}},$ where $C$ and $C_{0}$ are constants independent of $\varepsilon$ and $\delta$ , and $\|\cdot\|_{L_{x}^{2}}$ denotes the $L^{2}-$ norm with respect to $x$ . Note that from ( 1.6 ), ( 1.10 ), ( 1.14 ) and ( 1.15 ), it is straightforward to see that $(v^{\varepsilon}-\tilde{v},\varepsilon u^{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon\tilde{u})\in(L_{t}^{\infty}(H_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R})))^{2}$ , $\theta^{\varepsilon}-\tilde{\theta}\in L_{t}^{\infty}(H_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}))$ . Hence the Gagliardo-Nirenberg’s inequalitiy $\displaystyle\|(v^{\varepsilon}-\tilde{v},\varepsilon u^{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon\tilde{u},\theta^{\varepsilon}-\tilde{\theta})\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}^{2}$ $\displaystyle\leqslant$ $\displaystyle C\|(v^{\varepsilon}-\tilde{v},\varepsilon u^{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon\tilde{u},\theta^{\varepsilon}-\tilde{\theta})\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\|(v^{\varepsilon}-\tilde{v},\varepsilon u^{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon\tilde{u},\theta^{\varepsilon}-\tilde{\theta})_{x}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}$ $\displaystyle{\rm and}\qquad\|(\theta^{\varepsilon}-\tilde{\theta})_{x}\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}^{2}$ $\displaystyle\leqslant$ $\displaystyle C\|(\theta^{\varepsilon}-\tilde{\theta})_{x}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\|(\theta^{\varepsilon}-\tilde{\theta})_{xx}\|_{L_{x}^{2}},$ immediately imply that Corollary 2 (Low Mach Limit). Under the assumptions of Theorem 1 , in addition, suppose $\delta_{0}\leqslant\frac{1}{16C_{0}}$ , we have, as $\varepsilon\rightarrow 0$ , $\displaystyle\|(v^{\varepsilon}-\tilde{v},\theta^{\varepsilon}-\tilde{\theta})\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}$ $\displaystyle\leqslant$ $\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{\frac{17}{12}}\delta^{\frac{1}{4}}(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rightarrow 0,$ $\displaystyle\|u^{\varepsilon}-\tilde{u}\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}$ $\displaystyle\leqslant$ $\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{\frac{5}{12}}\delta^{\frac{1}{4}}(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rightarrow 0,$ (1.16) $\displaystyle{\rm and}\quad\|(\theta^{\varepsilon}-\tilde{\theta})_{x}\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}$ $\displaystyle\leqslant$ $\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{\frac{5}{6}}\delta^{\frac{1}{4}}(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rightarrow 0.$ From Corollary 2 , we shall show the behaviors of solutions of ( 1.3 ) when the Mach number $\varepsilon$ is small. Without lose of generality, we assume that $\theta_{+}>\theta_{-}$ . Then for any given constant $\eta_{0}>0$ , by ( 1.9 ), there exists $C_{\eta_{0}}>0$ such that $T^{\prime}(\eta)>C_{\eta_{0}}\delta,\quad\textrm{for}\,\,|\eta|\leqslant\eta_{0}.$ From ( 1.7 ), ( 1.10 ) and ( 1.16 ), it follows that Corollary 3 (Driven by the Variation of Temperature). For the solution obtained by Theorem 1 , there exists constants $\varepsilon_{1}=\varepsilon_{1}(\eta_{0})\leqslant\varepsilon_{0}$ and $\tilde{C}_{0}>0$ , such that for $\varepsilon\leqslant\varepsilon_{1}$ , $\displaystyle 0<\frac{C_{\eta_{0}}\delta}{\tilde{C}_{0}\sqrt{1+t}}<\frac{\tilde{\theta}_{x}}{\tilde{C}_{0}}\leqslant u^{\varepsilon}\leqslant\tilde{C}_{0}\tilde{\theta}_{x},$ $\displaystyle 0<\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\theta}_{x}\leqslant\theta_{x}^{\varepsilon}\leqslant\frac{3}{2}\tilde{\theta}_{x},$ and $\bar{C}_{0}^{-1}\theta_{x}^{\varepsilon}\leqslant u^{\varepsilon}\leqslant\bar{C}_{0}\theta_{x}^{\varepsilon},\qquad\textrm{for}\,\,|x|<\eta_{0}(1+t)^{\frac{1}{2}},t\geqslant 0,$ (1.17) where $C_{1}$ is a positive constant depending only on $\theta_{\pm}$ . Remark 4. Due to $\tilde{u}=\frac{\kappa T_{x}}{2T}$ , the velocity $\tilde{u}$ of system ( 1.1 ) is proportional with the variation of temperature $\tilde{\theta}$ . Thus the estimate ( 1.17 ) shows that, as $\varepsilon$ is suitably small, the velocity $u^{\varepsilon}$ of system ( 1.3 ) is also proportional with the variation of temperature. Finally let us outline the proof of Theorem 1 . Since there is no viscosity in ( 1.3 ) 2 , the system ( 1.3 ) is less dissipative compared with the system ( 1.1 ). It is not trivial to obtain the higher order estimates. Indeed, even for the basic energy estimate, the derivative estimate for $\psi_{y}^{2}$ is missing, see ( 2.13 ) below. To prove Theorem 1 , we first obtain the estimates for $\|\zeta_{y}\|_{H^{1}}$ though the basic energy. Then, by the compensation method (cf. [ 19 ] ), we control simultaneously the estimates for $\|(\phi_{y},\psi_{y})\|_{H^{1}}$ by $\|\zeta_{y}\|_{H^{1}}$ . Motivated by [ 13 ] , a new type differential inequality is essentially used to obtain the desired a priori estimates. The rest of this paper will be arranged in the following way. Section 2 is devoted to the details of the proof, in which the energy estimates will be used, while Section 3 is Appendix to prove an approximation of a self-similar solution, which will be used in the proof of the main results. 2.
