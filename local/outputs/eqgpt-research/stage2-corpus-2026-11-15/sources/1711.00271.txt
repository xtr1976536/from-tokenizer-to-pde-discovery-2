SE–412 96, Göteborg, Sweden Email address: standarc@chalmers.se Abstract. We study stability and convergence of $hp$ -streamline diffusion (SD) finite element,
and Nitsche’s schemes for the three dimensional,
relativistic (3 spatial dimension and 3 velocities), time dependent
Vlasov-Maxwell system
and Maxwell’s equations, respectively.
For the $hp$ scheme for the Vlasov-Maxwell system,
assuming that the exact solution is in the Sobolev space $H^{s+1}(\Omega)$ , we derive global a priori error bound of
order ${\mathcal{O}}(h/p)^{s+1/2}$ ,
where $h(=\max_{K}h_{K})$ is the mesh parameter and $p(=\max_{K}p_{K})$ is the spectral order.
This estimate is based on the local version with $h_{K}=\mbox{ diam }K$ being the diameter of the phase-space-time element $K$ and $p_{K}$ is the spectral order (the degree of approximating finite element
polynomial) for $K$ .
As for the
Nitsche’s scheme, by a simple calculus of the field equations,
first we convert the Maxwell’s system to an elliptic type equation.
Then, combining the Nitsche’s method for the spatial discretization
with a second order time scheme, we obtain optimal convergence of ${\mathcal{O}}(h^{2}+k^{2})$ , where $h$ is the spatial mesh size and $k$ is the time step. Here, as in the classical literature, the second
order time scheme requires higher order regularity assumptions. Numerical justification of the results, in
lower dimensions, is presented
and is also the subject of a forthcoming computational work [ 20 ] . Key words and phrases: hp-method  and Streamline Diffusion  and Discontinuous Galerkin
 and Vlasov-Maxwell system  and Nitsche scheme 1. Introduction We study stability and convergence for some specific finite element
schemes for a model problem for the three dimensional,
relativistic, Vlasov-Maxwell (VM) system
with 3-dimensional spatial domain ( $\mathbf{x}\in\Omega_{x}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}$ )
and 3-dimensional velocities domain ( $\mathbf{v}\in\Omega_{v}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}$ ).
The objective is two-fold: i) Numerical investigations of the $hp$ -version of the streamline diffusion (SD)
finite element method for VM where both Maxwell’s
and Vlasov equations are discretized using a
space-velocity-time scheme both in $h$ (mesh size) and
in $p$ (spatial order) versions. In this part we derive optimal
a priori error bounds for a SD scheme in a $L_{2}$ -based norm. ii)
The study of the combined
effect of Nitsche’s symmetrization (cf [ 21 ] and [ 6 ] )
in the spatial scheme for a Galerkin method
and a time discretization, for a second order pde obtained through
the combined Maxwell’s fields. The SD method was suggested by Hughes and Brooks in [ 18 ] for the
fluid problems. The method was further developed
(by T. Houghs and co-workers) to include several engineering problems.
A through mathematical analysis was first given by Johnson et al in [ 19 ] in a study of the Navier-Stokes equations and was
extended to most pdes with hyperbolic nature, where, e.g., [ 2 ] - [ 5 ] and [ 24 ] are relevant in the present study.
In the SD method the test function is modified by adding a multiple
of the streaming part in the equation, in terms of the test function, to it.
Then, in the weak formulation we obtain a multiple of streaming
terms in test and trial functions.
This can be viewed as an extra diffusion term in the streaming direction in
the original equation. Hence, the name of the method
(the streamline diffusion). Such an extra diffusion would improve both the
stability and convergence properties of the underlying Galerkin scheme.
It is well known that the standard Galerkin method has a weaker convergence
property for the
hyperbolic problems: ${\mathcal{O}}(h^{s-1})$ versus ${\mathcal{O}}(h^{s})$ for the elliptic and parabolic problems with
exact solution in the Sobolev space $H^{s}(\Omega)$ .
The SD method improves this weak convergence to ${\mathcal{O}}(h^{s-1/2})$ (see [ 1 ] for Sobolev spaces of non-integer order)
and also, having an upwinding character, enhances the stability. These two properties are achieved by discontinuous Galerkin as well (see, e.g. [ 7 ] ).
The $hp$ -approach is to capture local behavior in the sense that: in
the vicinity of singularities refined mesh $h$ is combined with the
lower order (small $p$ ) polynomial approximations, whereas in more smooth
regions higher order polynomials (large $p$ ) and non-refined (large $h$ ) meshes
are used. In a sense the $hp$ -approach may be interpreted as a kind of
automatic adaptivity. The Vlasov-Maxwell (VM) system which describes the time evolution of
collisionless
plasma is formulated as $\displaystyle\partial_{t}f$ $\displaystyle+\hat{v}\cdot\nabla_{x}f+q(E+c^{-1}\hat{v}\times B)\cdot\nabla_{v}f=0,$ (1.1) $\displaystyle\partial_{t}E=c\nabla_{x}\times B-j,\qquad\nabla_{x}\cdot E=\rho,$ $\displaystyle\partial_{t}B=-c\nabla_{x}\times E,\qquad\nabla_{x}\cdot B=0$ with properly assigned initial data $f(0,x,v)=f^{0}(x,v)\geq 0$ , $E(0,x)=E^{0}(x)$ , $B(0,x)=B^{0}(x)$ .
Here $f$ is the density, in phase space, time of
particles with charge $q$ ,
mass $m$ and velocity $\hat{v}=(m^{2}+c^{-2}|v|^{2})^{-1/2}v\qquad(v\,\,\mbox{is momentum}).$ Further, $c$ is the speed of light and the charge and current densities $\rho$ and $j$ are given by $\rho(t,x)=4\pi\int qf\,dv\quad\mbox{and}\quad j(t,x)=4\pi\int qf\hat{v}\,dv.$ The phase-space variables may have different dimension: $(x,v)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d^{\prime}},\,d\leq d^{\prime}$ . The Vlasov-Maxwell equations arises in several branches of continuum physics,
e.g. in astrophysics or rarefied gas dynamics.
The main assumption underlying the model is that collisions are
rare and therefore negligible. In this setting the above system describes the
motion of a collisionless plasma, e.g., a high-temperature,
low-density, ionized gas. For a thorough mathematical study of
VM models we refer to
DiPerna and Lions [ 12 ] and a most recent work by Glassey and co-workers [ 14 ] - [ 15 ] and
the references therein. The results in [ 14 ] are for
a lower dimensional model where the interest lies in classical solutions,
and are based on compactness and regularity assumptions on the
initial density and fields. The main mathematical
concern in dealing with the Vlasov-Maxwell system is related to the
nonlinear term $(E+\hat{v}\times B)\cdot\nabla_{v}f$ which can be written in
the divergence free form, viz. $(E+\hat{v}\times B)\cdot\nabla_{v}f={\mbox{d}iv}_{v}\Big((E+\hat{v}\times B)f\Big)$ .
In [ 12 ] the nonlinear form $(E+\hat{v}\times B)f$ is analyzed. Numerical approaches for the VM system
have been considered by several authors in different setting.
The most relevant studies to this work are given by
Gamba and co-workers [ 9 ] devoted to
a discontinuous Galerkin approach, and Standar in [ 22 ] where
the stability and a priori error estimates for
the $h$ version of SD method for VM are derived.
As some related studies we mention the analysis of
a one dimensional model problem for the relativistic
VM system in an interval given by Filbet and co-workers in [ 13 ] . Also in a very recent work [ 11 ] Degond and co-workers study a particle-in-cell method
for the Vlasov-Maxwell system. An outline of this paper is as follows.
We gather notation and assumptions in Section 2.
In Section 3 we formulate the SD schemes for both Maxwell’s equations and the
Vlasov-Maxwell system. Section 4 is on stability and convergence of the $hp$ SD finite element method of the Maxwell’s equations, based on a
space-time iterative scheme. We insert such approximated field function
in the drift term in Vlasov-Maxwell equation and prove stability and
derive optimal convergence rates in the SD
phase-space-time discretization scheme. Section 5 is devoted to a
the study of a Nitsche
scheme combined with a time discretization
for a second order pde obtained from the Maxwell’s equations.
Here, we rely on a modified form of the Ritz projection and derive optimal
error estimates for the Nitsche scheme in spatial discretization.
Assuming additional regularities in time we also prove optimal convergence of
a second order time scheme for the fields.
Finally, in our concluding Section 6 we present some numerical tests
of the studied schemes in lower dimensional geometry. 2. Notation and Assumptions The divergence equations in ( 1.1 ) can be derived from the rest of
the equations, assuming that the initial data $E^{0}$ and $B^{0}$ satisfy
corresponding divergence equations.
Hence we will consider the following relativistic
Vlasov-Maxwell system in ${\mathbb{R}}^{d}$ (in the paper we focus on the dimension $d=3$ , but one can easily obtain
the analogous results for $d=2$ ) $\displaystyle\partial_{t}f$ $\displaystyle+\hat{v}\cdot\nabla_{x}f+(E+\hat{v}\times B)\cdot\nabla_{v}f=0,$ (2.1) $\displaystyle\partial_{t}E=\nabla_{x}\times B-j,$ $\displaystyle\partial_{t}B=-\nabla_{x}\times E$ with $\hat{v}=(1+|v|^{2})^{-1/2}v$ and $j(t,x)=\int f\hat{v}\,dv$ ,
where for simplicity we set the charge $q$ and all constants equal to one. Our objective is to use an iterative scheme to approximate the solution of
the Vlasov-Maxwell (henceforth referred as VM) equations.
First we take a guess for the density $f$ and then calculate the corresponding $j$ .
Next, we plug these quantities into the Maxwell’s equations
and solve these equations. Finally,
we solve the Vlasov equation with the such approximated $E$ and $B$ as coefficients. We start from the Maxwell’s part.
Set $E=(E_{1},E_{2},E_{3})^{T}\!$ , $B=(B_{1},B_{2},B_{3})^{T}\!$ , $j=(j_{1},j_{2},j_{3})^{T}\!$ .

not necessarily the same at each occurrence, and independent of the parameters
in the equations, unless otherwise explicitly specified. 3. hp-Streamline Diffusion Method Let $\Omega_{x}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}$ and $\Omega_{v}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}$ denote the space
and velocity domains,
respectively. We assume that $f(t,x,v)$ , $E_{i}(t,x)$ , $B_{i}(t,x)$ for $i=1,2,3$ have compact
supports in $\Omega_{x}$ and that $f(t,x,v)$ has compact support in $\Omega_{v}$ . Now we will introduce a finite element structure on $\Omega=\Omega_{x}\times\Omega_{v}$ .
Let $T_{h}^{x}=\{\tau_{x}\}$ and $T_{h}^{v}=\{\tau_{v}\}$ be finite element subdivisions
of $\Omega_{x}$ with elements $\tau_{x}$ and $\Omega_{v}$ with elements $\tau_{v}$ , respectively.
Then $T_{h}=T_{h}^{x}\times T_{h}^{v}=\{\tau_{x}\times\tau_{v}\}=\{\tau\}$ is a subdivision
of $\Omega$ .
Let $0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{M-1}<t_{M}=T$ be a partition of $[0,T]$ into
sub-intervals $I_{m}=(t_{m},t_{m+1}]$ , $m=0,\ldots,M-1$ .
Further let $\mathcal{C}_{h}$ be the corresponding subdivision of $Q_{T}=[0,T]\times\Omega$ into elements $K=I_{m}\times\tau$ , with $h_{K}=\textnormal{diam}\,K$ as the mesh parameter.
We also define a piecewise constant mesh function $h(t,x,v):=h_{K}$ , $(t,x,v)\in K$ . Finally, we
introduce $\mathcal{\tilde{C}}_{h}$ as the finite element subdivision of $[0,T]\times\Omega_{x}$ . Remark 3.1 . Henceforth, the discrete problems are the
finite element approximations for the equations ( 2.2 ) and
( 2.3 ) formulated for $(x,v,t)\in(0,T]\times{\Omega_{x}}\times{\Omega_{v}}$ ,
associated with initial and corresponding boundary data.
Here one may assume that $f$ has compact support in the
velocity space ${\mathbb{R}}_{v}^{d}$ , and hence assume homogeneous
Dirichlet boundary
condition for $\Omega_{v}$ . Thus to define an adequate finite element space we let $\mathcal{H}_{0}=\prod_{m=0}^{M-1}H_{0}^{1}(I_{m}\times\Omega_{x}\times\Omega_{v})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{\tilde{H}}_{0}=\prod_{m=0}^{M-1}H^{1}_{0}(I_{m}\times\Omega_{x}),$ where $H^{1}_{0}(I_{m}\times\Omega)=\{w\in H^{1};w=0\,\,\textnormal{on}\,\,\partial\Omega\}.$ Here $\Omega$ stands for either $\Omega_{x}$ or $\Omega_{x}\times\Omega_{v}$ . For $k=0,1,2,\ldots$ ,
we define the finite element spaces for the Maxwell’s
equations (resp. Vlasov equation) as the space of piecewise polynomials which are
continuous in $x$ (resp. in $x$ and $v$ ) and with possible discontinuities at the
interior time levels $t_{m},\,m=1,\ldots,M$ : $\tilde{V}_{h}=\{g\in[\mathcal{\tilde{H}}_{0}]^{6};g_{i}|_{\tilde{K}}\in P_{p_{\tilde{K}}}(I_{m})\times P_{p_{\tilde{K}}}(\tau_{x}),\,\forall\tilde{K}=I_{m}\times\tau_{x}\in\mathcal{\tilde{C}}_{h},\,1\leq i\leq 6\},$ with the extension for the Vlasov part and with $\tau=\tau_{x}\times\tau_{v}$ , viz: ${V}_{h}=\{g\in\mathcal{H}_{0};g|_{K}\in P_{p_{K}}(I_{m})\times P_{p_{K}}(\tau_{x})\times P_{p_{K}}(\tau_{v}),\,\forall K=I_{m}\times\tau\in\mathcal{C}_{h}\}.$ where $P_{p_{K}}(\cdot)$ is the set of polynomial of degree at most $p_{K}$ on the given set. In this setting we allow the degree of polynomial
to vary from cell to cell, hence we define the piecewise constant function $p(t,x,v):=p_{K}$ .
We shall also use some notation, viz. $(f,g)_{m}=(f,g)_{S_{m}},\qquad\|g\|_{m}=(g,g)_{m}^{1/2}$ and $\langle f,g\rangle_{m}=(f(t_{m},\ldots),g(t_{m},\ldots))_{\Omega},\qquad|g|_{m}=\langle g,g\rangle_{m}^{1/2},$ where $S_{m}=I_{m}\times\Omega$ , is the slab at $m$ -th level, $m=0,\ldots,M-1$ , and $\Omega$ stands for $\Omega_{x}$ in Maxwell’s equations and $\Omega_{x}\times\Omega_{v}$ for the Vlasov case. 3.1. the $i$ th step

find $f^{h,i}\in V_{h}$ such that for $m=0,1,\ldots,M-1$ , $\Big(\partial_{t}f^{h,i}+G(f^{h,i-1})\cdot\nabla f^{h,i},g+\delta(\partial_{t}g+G(f^{h,i-1})\cdot\nabla g)\Big)_{m}\\

Stability and Convergence of hp-SDFEM 4.1. Maxwell Equations Lemma 4.1 (M-coercivity) . The bilinear form $\tilde{\mathcal{B}}(\cdot,\,\cdot)$ satisfies the coercivity relation $\tilde{\mathcal{B}}(g,g)=|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|g|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|_{\mathrm{M}}^{2},\qquad\forall g\in\mathcal{\tilde{H}}_{0}.$ Proof. By definition of $\tilde{\mathcal{B}}$ we have that $\tilde{\mathcal{B}}(g,g)=\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}(\partial_{t}g+\sum_{l=1}^{3}M_{l}\partial_{l}g,g)_{m}\\
+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{\tilde{C}}_{h}}\!\!\delta_{K}\|\partial_{t}g+\sum_{l=1}^{3}M_{l}\partial_{l}g\|_{K}^{2}+\sum_{m=1}^{M-1}\langle[g],g_{+}\rangle_{m}+|g_{+}|^{2}_{0}.$ Integrating by parts we get that $\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}(\partial_{t}g,g)+\sum_{m=1}^{M-1}\langle[g],g_{+}\rangle_{m}+|g_{+}|^{2}_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\sum_{m=1}^{M-1}|[g]|^{2}_{m}+|g_{-}|^{2}_{M}+|g_{+}|^{2}_{0}\Big)$ and since $g(t,x)=0$ on $I\times\partial\Omega_{x}$ , we have that $\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}(M_{l}\partial_{l}g,g)_{m}=0,\quad\mbox{for }l=1,2,3.$ (4.1) Then, the proof follows immediately through adding all above terms.
∎ Lemma 4.2 (Poincaré-type M-estimate) . For any positive constant $C$ we have that for $g\in\mathcal{\tilde{H}}_{0}$ , $\|g\|^{2}_{m}\leq\Big(|g_{-}|^{2}_{m+1}+\frac{1}{C}\|\partial_{t}g+\sum_{l=1}^{3}M_{l}\partial_{l}g\|^{2}_{m}\Big)he^{2Ch}.$ Proof. For $t_{m}<t<t_{m+1}$ , we may write $\begin{split}\|g(t)\|^{2}_{\Omega_{x}}&=|g_{-}|^{2}_{m+1}-\int_{t}^{t_{m+1}}\frac{d}{ds}\|g(s)\|^{2}_{\Omega_{x}}ds\\
&=|g_{-}|^{2}_{m+1}-2\int_{t}^{t_{m+1}}(\partial_{s}g+\sum_{l=1}^{3}M_{l}\partial_{l}g,g)_{\Omega_{x}}ds\\
&\leq|g_{-}|^{2}_{m+1}+\frac{1}{C}\|\partial_{t}g+\sum_{l=1}^{3}M_{l}\partial_{l}g\|^{2}_{m}+C\int_{t}^{t_{m+1}}\|g\|^{2}_{\Omega_{x}},\\
\end{split}$ where in the second equality we used ( 4.1 ).
Finally, by Grönwall’s lemma we have that $\|g(t)\|^{2}_{\Omega_{x}}\leq\Big(|g_{-}|^{2}_{m+1}+\frac{1}{C}\|\partial_{t}g+\sum_{l=1}^{3}M_{l}\partial_{l}g\|^{2}_{m}\Big)e^{2Ch}.$ Now, integrating over $[t_{m},t_{m+1}]$ we obtain the desired result.
∎ We proceed to the error analysis.
First, let $\tilde{W}$ be an interpolant of $W$ in the finite dimensional
discrete function space $\tilde{V}_{h}$ and denote by $W^{h,i}$ a solution to ( 3.2 ).
Then we represent the error as the following split $\tilde{e}=W-W^{h,i}=(W-\tilde{W})-(W^{h,i}-\tilde{W})=\tilde{\eta}-\tilde{\xi},$ where $\tilde{\eta}=(\tilde{\eta}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{\eta}_{6})^{T}$ is the interpolation error
and $\tilde{\xi}=(\tilde{\xi}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{\xi}_{6})^{T}$ . To estimate the convergence rate for both the Maxwell’s and the Vlasov-Maxwell
equations we use the Theorem 3.2 from [ 5 ] , which are based on
classical interpolation estimates by [ 10 ] .
As a consequence of this Theorem we have the following bounds
for the interpolation error $\eta=f-\tilde{f}$ of a function $f\in H^{k+1}([0,T]\times\Omega)$ (where $\Omega$ stands for $\Omega_{x}$ in the Maxwell’s equations
and $\Omega_{x}\times\Omega_{v}$ for the Vlasov case)
and its gradient: $\displaystyle\|\eta\|^{2}$ $\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{K}\left(\frac{h_{K}}{2}\right)^{2s_{K}+2}\Phi_{1}(p_{K},s_{K})\|f\|^{2}_{s_{K}+1,K},$ (4.2) $\displaystyle\|\mathcal{D}\eta\|^{2}$ $\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{K}\left(\frac{h_{K}}{2}\right)^{2s_{K}}\Phi_{2}(p_{K},s_{K})\|f\|^{2}_{s_{K}+1,K},$ (4.3) where the sums are taken over all space-time
elements of the triangulation of the domain, $[0,T]\times\Omega$ , $0\leq s_{K}\leq\min(p_{K},k)$ , with $p_{K}$ being the local spectral order.
Closed formulas for $\Phi_{1}$ and $\Phi_{2}$ are given in Theorem 3.2 of [ 5 ] . A less involved formula for $\Phi_{1}$ can be found in [ 16 ] . Now we state the following convergence theorem for the Maxwell’s equations. Theorem 4.3 . Assume that $W\in H^{k+1}([0,T]\times\Omega)$ . Moreover
on each $K$ , the parameter $\delta_{K}$ satisfies $\delta_{K}=C_{1}\frac{h_{K}}{p_{K}}$ for some constant $C_{1}>0$ with $p_{K}h_{K}\leq C_{2}<1$ for some constant $C_{2}>0$ .
Then there exists a constant $C>0$ independent of $p_{K}$ , $h_{K}$ and $s_{K}$ such that $|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|W-W^{h,i}|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|_{\mathrm{M}}^{2}\leq C\!\!\sum_{K\in\mathcal{\tilde{C}}_{h}}\!\!h_{K}^{2s_{K}+1}p_{K}^{-1}\Phi_{\mathrm{M}}(p_{K},s_{K})\|W\|^{2}_{s_{K}+1,K}\\
+C\|f-f^{h,i-1}\|^{2}_{Q_{T}},$ where $\Phi_{\mathrm{M}}=\max(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2})$ with $\mathcal{N}=\textnormal{dim }\Omega_{x}+1$ for $\Phi_{1}$ and $\Phi_{2}$ (recall that M , as a subscript in the triple norm above,
is to emphasis that it concerns the triple norm of the Maxwell’s equations). Proof. We have by Lemma 4.1 and ( 3.3 ) that $|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|\tilde{\xi}|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|_{\mathrm{M}}^{2}=\tilde{\mathcal{B}}(\tilde{\xi},\tilde{\xi})=\tilde{\mathcal{B}}(\tilde{\eta},\tilde{\xi})-\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(b;\tilde{\xi})+\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(b^{h,i-1};\tilde{\xi}).$ We start with the term $\tilde{\mathcal{B}}(\tilde{\eta},\tilde{\xi})=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{\tilde{C}}_{h}}\!\!\Big(\partial_{t}\tilde{\eta}+\sum_{l=1}^{3}M_{l}\partial_{l}\tilde{\eta},\tilde{\xi}+\delta_{K}(\partial_{t}\tilde{\xi}+\sum_{l=1}^{3}M_{l}\partial_{l}\tilde{\xi})\Big)_{K}+\sum_{m=1}^{M-1}\langle[\tilde{\eta}],\tilde{\xi}_{+}\rangle_{m}+\langle\tilde{\eta}_{+},\tilde{\xi}_{+}\rangle_{0}.$ Partial integration gives the identities $(\partial_{t}\tilde{\eta},\tilde{\xi})_{m}=\int_{\Omega_{x}}[\,\tilde{\eta}\tilde{\xi}\,]_{t=t_{m}}^{t_{m+1}}dx-(\tilde{\eta},\partial_{t}\tilde{\xi})_{m}=\langle\tilde{\eta}_{-},\tilde{\xi}_{-}\rangle_{m+1}-\langle\tilde{\eta}_{+},\tilde{\xi}_{+}\rangle_{m}-(\tilde{\eta},\partial_{t}\tilde{\xi})_{m}$ and $(M_{l}\partial_{l}\tilde{\eta},\tilde{\xi})_{m}=-(\tilde{\eta},M_{l}\partial_{l}\tilde{\xi})_{m},$ since $\tilde{\eta}$ and $\tilde{\xi}$ have compact support in $\Omega_{x}$ .
Inserting these equations into the expression for $\tilde{\mathcal{B}}(\tilde{\eta},\tilde{\xi})$ we end up with the following equality $|\tilde{\mathcal{B}}(\tilde{\eta},\tilde{\xi})|=|\langle\tilde{\eta}_{-},\tilde{\xi}_{-}\rangle_{M}-\sum_{m=1}^{M-1}\langle\tilde{\eta}_{-},[\tilde{\xi}]\rangle_{m}\\
-\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}(\tilde{\eta},\partial_{t}\tilde{\xi}+\sum_{l=1}^{3}M_{l}\partial_{l}\tilde{\xi})_{m}+\!\sum_{K\in\mathcal{\tilde{C}}_{h}}\!\!\delta_{K}\Big(\partial_{t}\tilde{\eta}+\sum_{l=1}^{3}M_{l}\partial_{l}\tilde{\eta},\partial_{t}\tilde{\xi}+\sum_{l=1}^{3}M_{l}\partial_{l}\tilde{\xi}\Big)_{K}|.$ Further, using some standard inequalities it follows that $|\tilde{\mathcal{B}}(\tilde{\eta},\tilde{\xi})|\leq\frac{1}{16}|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|\tilde{\xi}|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|_{\mathrm{M}}^{2}+32\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}|\tilde{\eta}_{-}|^{2}_{m+1}+\!\sum_{K\in\mathcal{\tilde{C}}_{h}}\!\!\Big(\frac{32}{\delta_{K}}\|\tilde{\eta}\|^{2}_{K}+32\delta_{K}\|\partial_{t}\tilde{\eta}+\sum_{l=1}^{3}M_{l}\partial_{l}\tilde{\eta}\|^{2}_{K}\Big).$ Now let us estimate the second term $|\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(b^{h,i-1};\tilde{\xi})-\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(b;\tilde{\xi})|=\Big|\sum_{K\in\mathcal{\tilde{C}}_{h}}\!\!\Big(b^{h,i-1}-b,\tilde{\xi}+\delta_{K}(\partial_{t}\tilde{\xi}+\sum_{l=1}^{3}M_{l}\partial_{l}\tilde{\xi})\Big)_{K}\Big|\\
\leq\frac{1}{16}|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|\tilde{\xi}|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|_{\mathrm{M}}^{2}+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{\tilde{C}}_{h}}\!\!(\frac{C}{2}+8\delta_{K})\|b-b^{h,i-1}\|^{2}_{K}+\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\big(\frac{1}{2C}\|\tilde{\eta}\|^{2}_{m}+\frac{1}{2C}\|\tilde{e}\|_{m}^{2}\big).$ The above two inequalities and Lemma 4.2 , with properly chosen $C$ and the bound on $p_{K}h_{K}$ , imply the estimate $|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|\tilde{\xi}|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|_{\mathrm{M}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{8}|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|\tilde{\xi}|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|_{\mathrm{M}}^{2}+\frac{7}{16}|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|\tilde{e}|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|_{\mathrm{M}}^{2}+C\|f-f^{h,i-1}\|^{2}_{Q_{T}}\\
+C\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}(|\tilde{\eta}_{-}|^{2}_{m+1}+h|\tilde{e}_{-}|^{2}_{m+1})+C\!\sum_{K\in\mathcal{\tilde{C}}_{h}}\!\!\Big((1+\frac{1}{\delta_{K}})\|\tilde{\eta}\|^{2}_{K}+\delta_{K}\|\partial_{t}\tilde{\eta}+\sum_{l=1}^{3}M_{l}\partial_{l}\tilde{\eta}\|^{2}_{K}\Big).$ Hiding the $\tilde{\xi}$ -term on the right hand side in the $\tilde{\xi}$ -term on the left hand side,
gives us the following inequality $|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|\tilde{\xi}|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|_{\mathrm{M}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|\tilde{e}|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|_{\mathrm{M}}^{2}+C\|f-f^{h,i-1}\|^{2}_{Q_{T}}+C\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}(|\tilde{\eta}_{-}|^{2}_{m+1}+h|\tilde{e}_{-}|^{2}_{m+1})\\
+C\!\sum_{K\in\mathcal{\tilde{C}}_{h}}\!\!\Big((1+\delta_{K}^{-1})\|\tilde{\eta}\|^{2}_{K}+\delta_{K}\|\partial_{t}\tilde{\eta}+\sum_{l=1}^{3}M_{l}\partial_{l}\tilde{\eta}\|^{2}_{K}\Big).$ Thus, we have estimated $|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|\tilde{\xi}|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|_{\mathrm{M}}^{2}$ . This implies that $|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|\tilde{e}|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|_{\mathrm{M}}^{2}\leq|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|\tilde{\eta}|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|_{\mathrm{M}}^{2}+|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|\tilde{\xi}|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|_{\mathrm{M}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|\tilde{e}|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|_{\mathrm{M}}^{2}+C\|f-f^{h,i-1}\|^{2}_{Q_{T}}+C\sum_{m=1}^{M}h|\tilde{e}_{-}|^{2}_{m}\\
+C\Big(|\tilde{\eta}_{+}|^{2}_{0}+\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}|\tilde{\eta}_{-}|^{2}_{m+1}+\!\sum_{K\in\mathcal{\tilde{C}}_{h}}\!\!(\delta_{K}^{-1}\|\tilde{\eta}\|_{K}^{2}+\delta_{K}\|\partial_{t}\tilde{\eta}+\sum_{l=1}^{3}M_{l}\partial_{l}\tilde{\eta}\|^{2}_{K})+\sum_{m=1}^{M-1}|[\tilde{\eta}]|^{2}_{m}\Big).$ (4.4) Now we have to estimate the interpolation error terms: $\begin{split}J_{1}&:=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{\tilde{C}}_{h}}\!\!\Big(\delta_{K}^{-1}\|\tilde{\eta}\|_{K}^{2}+\delta_{K}\|\partial_{t}\tilde{\eta}+\sum_{l=1}^{3}M_{l}\partial_{l}\tilde{\eta}\|^{2}_{K}\Big),\\
J_{2}&:=|\tilde{\eta}_{+}|^{2}_{0}+\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}|\tilde{\eta}_{-}|^{2}_{m+1}+\sum_{m=1}^{M-1}|[\tilde{\eta}]|^{2}_{m}.\\
\end{split}$ For the term $J_{1}$ we use ( 4.2 ) and ( 4.3 ) to get $J_{1}\leq C\sum_{K\in\mathcal{\tilde{C}}_{h}}\!\!\left(\frac{h_{K}}{2}\right)^{2s_{K}}\Phi_{\mathrm{M}}(p_{K},s_{K})p_{K}^{-2}\big(\delta_{K}^{-1}h^{2}_{K}+\delta_{K}\big)\|W\|_{s_{K}+1,K}^{2}.$ (4.5) To estimate the term $J_{2}$ we use the trace
estimate combined with the inverse inequality and get $\|\tilde{\eta}\|_{\partial K}^{2}\leq C\Big(\big(\sum_{l=1}^{6}\|\nabla\tilde{\eta}_{l}\|_{K}\big)\|\tilde{\eta}\|_{K}+h_{K}^{-1}\|\tilde{\eta}\|_{K}^{2}\Big),$ (4.6) to obtain $J_{2}\leq C\sum_{K\in\mathcal{\tilde{C}}_{h}}\!\!\left[\left(\frac{h_{K}}{2}\right)^{s_{K}}\Phi_{\mathrm{M}}^{1/2}(p_{K},s_{K})\left(\frac{h_{K}}{2}\right)^{s_{K}+1}p_{K}^{-1}\Phi_{\mathrm{M}}^{1/2}(p_{K},s_{K})\right.\\
+\left.h_{K}^{-1}\left(\frac{h_{K}}{2}\right)^{2s_{K}+2}p_{K}^{-2}\Phi_{\mathrm{M}}(p_{K},s_{K})\right]\|W\|_{s_{k}+1,K}^{2},$ (4.7) where for all terms in $J_{2}$ we combined ( 4.6 )
with ( 4.2 ) and ( 4.3 ). Now, moving the triple norm of $\tilde{e}$ on the right hand side of ( 4.4 )
to the left hand side and using estimates ( 4.5 ) and ( 4.7 )
it follows that $|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|\tilde{e}|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|_{\mathrm{M}}^{2}\leq C\sum_{K\in\mathcal{\tilde{C}}_{h}}\!\!h_{K}^{2s_{K}+1}p_{K}^{-1}\Phi_{\mathrm{M}}(p_{K},s_{K})\|W\|^{2}_{s_{K}+1,K}\\
+C\|f-f^{h,i-1}\|^{2}_{Q_{T}}+C\sum_{m=1}^{M}h|\tilde{e}_{-}|^{2}_{m}.$ (4.8) The next step is to apply a discrete Grönwall lemma of the form:
suppose $\{a_{\ell}\}^{M}_{1}$ satisfies $a_{\ell}\leq C_{1}+C_{2}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}h,\qquad\ell=1,\ldots,M.$ If $h\leq 1/2C_{2}$ , then we have that $a_{\ell}\leq 2C_{1}e^{2C_{2}(\ell-1)h}\quad\mbox{for}\quad\ell=1,\ldots,M.$ The discrete Grönwall’s lemma yields $|\tilde{e}_{-}|^{2}_{\ell}\leq C\sum_{K\in\mathcal{\tilde{C}}_{h}}\!\!h_{K}^{2s_{K}+1}p_{K}^{-1}\Phi_{\mathrm{M}}(p_{K},s_{K})\|W\|^{2}_{s_{K}+1,K}\\
+C\|f-f^{h,i-1}\|^{2}_{Q_{T}}$ for $\ell=1,\ldots,M$ . Plugging these inequalities into ( 4.8 )
will give the stated error estimate and the proof is complete.

However, as we mentioned above, the analysis although lengthy

the Maxwell’s equations can be obtained using a symmetrizing
penalty approach known as Nitsche’s method.
This, however cannot be extended to the Vlasov part due
to the hyperbolic nature of the Vlasov equation.
Nevertheless, the advantages of the symmetrizing are
overwhelming.
Therefore below we include analysis of the Nitsche’s
approach for the Maxwell part. Recall that we have the Maxwell’s equations given by $\displaystyle E_{t}-\nabla_{x}\times B=-j,$ $\displaystyle B_{t}+\nabla_{x}\times E=0.$ We differentiate the first equation with respect to time to get $E_{tt}-\nabla_{x}\times B_{t}=-j_{t},$ (5.1) and plug the second Maxwell’s equation into the equation ( 5.1 )
to obtain $E_{tt}+\nabla_{x}\times(\nabla_{x}\times E)=-j_{t}.$ (5.2) Multiplying ( 5.2 ) with $g\in H(\textnormal{curl},\Omega_{x})=\{v\in L_{2}(\Omega_{x}):\nabla_{x}\times v\in L_{2}(\Omega_{x})\}$ and integrating over $\Omega_{x}$ yields $\int_{\Omega_{x}}E_{tt}\cdot g\,dx+\int_{\Omega_{x}}\nabla_{x}\times(\nabla_{x}\times E)\cdot g\,dx=-\int_{\Omega_{x}}j_{t}\cdot g\,dx.$ (5.3) Now recall the Green’s formula $\int_{\Omega_{x}}u\cdot\nabla_{x}\times v\,dx-\int_{\Omega_{x}}\nabla_{x}\times u\cdot v\,dx=-\int_{\Gamma_{x}}(u\times n)\cdot v\,ds,$ where $n=n(x)$ is the unit outward normal to the boundary at the point $x\in\partial\Omega_{x}$ .
Apply Green’s formula to ( 5.3 ) $\int_{\Omega_{x}}E_{tt}\cdot g\,dx+\int_{\Omega_{x}}\nabla_{x}\times E\cdot\nabla_{x}\times g\,dx-\int_{\Gamma_{x}}\nabla_{x}\times E\cdot(g\times n)\,ds=-\int_{\Omega_{x}}j_{t}\cdot g\,dx.$ This relation being non-symmetric
(see the contribution from the boundary terms)
causes severe restrictions in, e.g., deriving stability estimates.
To circumvent such draw-backs Nitsche introduced a symmetrized
scheme for elliptic and parabolic problems (see [ 21 ] ), which is also known as
the penalty method . This can be seen in, e.g., [ 8 ] and [ 23 ] .
In our case Nitsche’s method is performed
by the add of extra boundary terms making the bilinear form
symmetric and coercive, viz. $\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{x}}E_{tt}\cdot g\,dx+\int_{\Omega_{x}}\nabla_{x}\times E\cdot\nabla_{x}\times g\,dx-\int_{\Gamma_{x}}\nabla_{x}\times E\cdot(g\times n)\,ds$ $\displaystyle-\int_{\Gamma_{x}}(E\times n)\cdot\nabla_{x}\times g\,ds+\frac{\gamma}{h}\int_{\Gamma_{x}}E\cdot g\,ds=-\int_{\Omega_{x}}j_{t}\cdot g\,dx.$ Here $\gamma$ is a constant that will be specified later.
Now, we define the symmetric bilinear form $\displaystyle a(E,g):=$ $\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{x}}\nabla_{x}\times E\cdot\nabla_{x}\times g\,dx-\int_{\Gamma_{x}}\nabla_{x}\times E\cdot(g\times n)\,ds$ $\displaystyle-\int_{\Gamma_{x}}(E\times n)\cdot\nabla_{x}\times g\,ds+\frac{\gamma}{h}\int_{\Gamma_{x}}E\cdot g\,ds$ and the element space of piecewise linear polynomials $V^{x}_{h}=\{g\in H(\textnormal{curl},\Omega_{x}):g|_{\tau_{x}}\in P_{1}(\tau_{x}),\,\forall\tau_{x}\in T^{x}_{h}\}.$ Thus, we can formulate the semi-discrete problem as: for each fixed $t$ ,
find $E^{h}(t,\cdot)\in V^{x}_{h}$ , such that $(E_{tt}^{h},g)_{\Omega_{x}}+a(E^{h},g)=-(j_{t},g)_{\Omega_{x}}\quad\forall g\in V^{x}_{h}.$ (5.4) It is straightforward to observe the consistency of the method. Lemma 5.1 (Consistency) . The exact solution $E$ of ( 5.2 ) satisfies $(E_{tt},g)_{\Omega_{x}}+a(E,g)=-(j_{t},g)_{\Omega_{x}}\quad\forall g\in H(\textnormal{curl},\Omega_{x}).$ Now, defining the mesh dependent discrete norm $\|g\|^{2}_{h}:=\|\nabla_{x}\times g\|^{2}_{\Omega_{x}}+\|h^{-1/2}g\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{x}},$ we have $\displaystyle a(g,g)$ $\displaystyle=\|\nabla_{x}\times g\|^{2}_{\Omega_{x}}-2(\nabla_{x}\times g,g\times n)_{\Gamma_{x}}+\frac{\gamma}{h}\|g\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{x}}$ $\displaystyle\geq\|\nabla_{x}\times g\|^{2}_{\Omega_{x}}-2\|h^{1/2}\nabla_{x}\times g\|_{\Gamma_{x}}\|h^{-1/2}g\times n\|_{\Gamma_{x}}+\gamma\|h^{-1/2}g\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{x}}$ $\displaystyle\geq\|\nabla_{x}\times g\|^{2}_{\Omega_{x}}-\frac{1}{\alpha}\|h^{1/2}\nabla_{x}\times g\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{x}}-\alpha\|h^{-1/2}g\times n\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{x}}+\gamma\|h^{-1/2}g\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{x}}$ $\displaystyle\geq\frac{\alpha-\tilde{C}}{\alpha}\|\nabla_{x}\times g\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{x}}+(\gamma-4\alpha)\|h^{-1/2}g\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{x}},$ where in the last inequality we used the following trace estimate: $\|h^{1/2}\nabla_{x}\times g\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{x}}\leq\tilde{C}\|\nabla_{x}\times g\|^{2}_{\Omega_{x}}\quad\forall g\in V^{x}_{h}$ and the trivial inequality $\|h^{-1/2}g\times n\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{x}}\leq 4\|h^{-1/2}g\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{x}}\quad\forall g\in L_{2}(\Gamma_{x}).$ (5.5) Now, if we choose the constants $\gamma$ and $\alpha$ , such that $\gamma>4\alpha$ and $\alpha>\tilde{C}$ ,
then we have proved the following coercivity result. Lemma 5.2 (Coercivity) . If $\gamma$ is large enough, then there exists a constant $C>0$ ,
such that $a(g,g)\geq C\|g\|^{2}_{h}\quad\forall g\in V^{x}_{h}.$ To have continuity of the form $a(\cdot,\cdot)$ we need to define a mesh dependent triple norm as $|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|g|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|^{2}_{h}:=\|g\|^{2}_{h}+\|h^{1/2}\nabla_{x}\times g\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{x}}.$ Then we have the following lemma. and the inverse inequality for the triple norm to get $\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{m}-\mathbb{E}^{m-1}\leq$ $\displaystyle k\|\omega^{m}\|_{\Omega_{x}}\left(\|\bar{\partial}_{t}\theta^{m}\|_{\Omega_{x}}+\|\bar{\partial}_{t}\theta^{m-1}\|_{\Omega_{x}}\right)$ $\displaystyle+C_{a}\frac{k^{3}}{4}|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|\bar{\partial}_{t}^{2}Q_{h}E^{m}|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|_{h}\left(|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|\bar{\partial}_{t}\theta^{m}|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|_{h}+|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|\bar{\partial}_{t}\theta^{m-1}|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|_{h}\right)$ $\displaystyle\leq$ $\displaystyle k\|\omega^{m}\|_{\Omega_{x}}\left(\sqrt{\mathbb{E}^{m}}+\sqrt{\mathbb{E}^{m-1}}\right)$ $\displaystyle+C_{a}\frac{k^{3}h^{-2}}{4}\|\bar{\partial}_{t}^{2}Q_{h}E^{m}\|_{\Omega_{x}}\left(\|\bar{\partial}_{t}\theta^{m}\|_{\Omega_{x}}+\|\bar{\partial}_{t}\theta^{m-1}\|_{\Omega_{x}}\right)$ $\displaystyle\leq$ $\displaystyle\left(k\|\omega^{m}\|_{\Omega_{x}}+C_{a}\frac{k^{3}h^{-2}}{4}\|\bar{\partial}_{t}^{2}Q_{h}E^{m}\|_{\Omega_{x}}\right)\left(\sqrt{\mathbb{E}^{m}}+\sqrt{\mathbb{E}^{m-1}}\right).$ After cancellation it follows that $\sqrt{\mathbb{E}^{m}}\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}^{m-1}}+k\|\omega^{m}\|_{\Omega_{x}}+C_{a}\frac{k^{3}h^{-2}}{4}\|\bar{\partial}_{t}^{2}Q_{h}E^{m}\|_{\Omega_{x}}.$ Iterating the above inequality leads to $\sqrt{\mathbb{E}^{m}}\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}^{1}}+k\sum_{j=2}^{m}\|\omega^{j}\|_{\Omega_{x}}+C_{a}\frac{k^{3}h^{-2}}{4}\sum_{j=2}^{m}\|\bar{\partial}_{t}^{2}Q_{h}E^{j}\|_{\Omega_{x}}.$ (5.15) Now we estimate the terms on the right hand side.
Let us begin with $\omega^{j}$ and split it as $\omega^{j}=(Q_{h}-I)\bar{\partial}_{t}^{2}E^{j}+(\bar{\partial}_{t}^{2}E^{j}-E_{tt}^{j-1})=:\omega_{1}^{j}+\omega_{2}^{j}.$ We write $\omega_{1}^{j}$ in the following way $\omega_{1}^{j}=\frac{1}{k^{2}}(Q_{h}-I)(E^{j}-2E^{j-1}+E^{j-2})=\frac{1}{k^{2}}(Q_{h}-I)\left(\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}E_{t}\,dt-\int_{t_{j-2}}^{t_{j-1}}E_{t}\,dt\right).$ Summing over $j$ and using Lemma 5.4 gives $k\sum_{j=2}^{m}\|\omega_{1}^{j}\|_{\Omega_{x}}\leq\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=2}^{m}\int_{t_{j-2}}^{t_{j}}\|(Q_{h}-I)E_{t}\|_{\Omega_{x}}\,dt\leq\frac{2Ch^{2}}{k}\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\|E_{t}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{x})}\,dt.$ As for $\omega_{2}^{j}$ we use Taylor expansion of $E^{j}$ and $E^{j-2}$ in polynomials of degree 2 about $t_{j-1}$ .
Then, due to cancellations, we end up with $\omega_{2}^{j}=\frac{1}{6k^{2}}\left(\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}(t-t_{j-1})^{2}E_{ttt}\,dt-\int_{t_{j-2}}^{t_{j-1}}(t-t_{j-1})^{2}E_{ttt}\,dt\right).$ Once again summing over $j$ leads to $k\sum_{j=2}^{m}\|\omega_{2}^{j}\|_{\Omega_{x}}\leq\frac{1}{6k}\sum_{j=2}^{m}\int_{t_{j-2}}^{t_{j}}(t-t_{j-1})^{2}\|E_{ttt}\|_{\Omega_{x}}\,dt\leq\frac{k}{3}\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\|E_{ttt}\|_{\Omega_{x}}\,dt.$ The third term in ( 5.15 ) will be estimated as follows $\displaystyle\frac{k^{3}h^{-2}}{4}\sum_{j=2}^{m}\|\bar{\partial}_{t}^{2}Q_{h}E^{j}\|_{\Omega_{x}}$ $\displaystyle\leq\frac{k^{3}h^{-2}}{4}\sum_{j=2}^{m}\|\omega^{j}\|_{\Omega_{x}}+\|E_{tt}^{j-1}\|_{\Omega_{x}}.$ It remains to estimate $\mathbb{E}^{1}$ , which depends on how $\mathcal{E}^{0}$ and $\mathcal{E}^{1}$ are chosen.
Let $\mathcal{E}^{0}=Q_{h}E^{0}$ and assume that $\mathcal{E}^{1}$ is chosen such that $\|\theta^{1}\|_{\Omega_{x}}\leq C(h^{2}+k^{2})$ .
Then we have $\displaystyle\sqrt{\mathbb{E}^{1}}=\sqrt{\|\frac{\theta^{1}}{k}\|^{2}_{\Omega_{x}}+\frac{1}{4}a(\theta^{1},\theta^{1})}$ $\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{k}\|\theta^{1}\|_{\Omega_{x}}+\frac{1}{2}|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|\theta^{1}|\mkern-1.0mu|\mkern-1.0mu|_{h}$ $\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{k}+\frac{1}{2h}\right)\|\theta^{1}\|_{\Omega_{x}}\leq C\left(\frac{1}{k}+\frac{1}{2h}\right)(h^{2}+k^{2}).$ We combine the above estimates to get $\|\bar{\partial}_{t}\theta^{m}\|_{\Omega_{x}}\leq C(h+k)$ for $m=2,3,\ldots,M$ ,
assuming that $k$ is proportional to $h$ .
Finally, if we use the following estimate $\|\theta^{m}\|_{\Omega_{x}}\leq\|\theta^{1}\|_{\Omega_{x}}+k\sum_{j=2}^{m}\|\bar{\partial}_{t}\theta^{j}\|_{\Omega_{x}},$ then we also have that $\|\theta^{m}\|_{\Omega_{x}}\leq C(h+k)$ .
We sum up the results in the following theorem. Theorem 5.7 . Let $E$ and $\mathcal{E}^{m}$ be the solutions of

assuming moreover that $E_{t}\in L_{1}\left((0,T);H^{2}(\Omega_{x})\right)$ , $E_{ttt}\in L_{1}\left((0,T);L_{2}(\Omega_{x})\right)$ and that $\mathcal{E}^{1}$ is chosen such that $\|\theta^{1}\|_{\Omega_{x}}\leq C(h^{2}+k^{2})$ ,

Numerical Results Here we present some numerical results justifying the accuracy of our method.
We performed the calculations for the simplified case of one space variable
and two velocities variables, i.e. the one and one-half dimensional
Vlasov-Maxwell system (cf [ 22 ] ), which takes the following form: $\begin{array}[]{c}\partial_{t}f+v_{1}\partial_{x}f+(E_{1}+v_{2}B)\partial_{v_{1}}f+(E_{2}-v_{1}B)\partial_{v_{2}}f=0,\\
\partial_{t}E_{1}=-\int v_{1}fdv=-j_{1}(t,x),\\
\partial_{t}E_{2}+\partial_{x}B=-\int v_{2}fdv=-j_{2}(t,x),\\
\partial_{t}B+\partial_{x}E_{2}=0,\end{array}$ where $f=f(t,x,v_{1},v_{2})$ , $E_{1}=E_{1}(t,x)$ , $E_{2}=E_{2}(t,x)$ , $B=B(t,x)$ with $x\in\Omega_{x}\subset{\mathbb{R}}$ and $v=(v_{1},v_{2})\in\Omega_{v}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}$ .
We assume here the non-relativistic case of the Vlasov-Maxwell system,
since there is a wider literature available to compare the numerical test with.
We note that our theoretical results are also valid in this case. The initial conditions are given by $\begin{array}[]{c}f(0,x,v_{1},v_{2})=\frac{1}{\pi\beta}e^{-v_{1}^{2}/\beta}\left[\mu e^{-(v_{2}-v_{0,1})^{2}/\beta}+(1-\mu)e^{-(v_{2}+v_{0,2})^{2}/\beta}\right],\\
E_{1}(0,x)=E_{2}(0,x)=0,\quad B(0,x)=-b\sin(k_{0}x_{1}),\end{array}$ which corresponds to the streaming Weibel instability (cf [ 9 ] )

We perform the calculations for two sets of values of parameters: $\begin{array}[]{ll}\mbox{case 1:}&\mu=0.5,\ v_{0,1}=v_{0,2}=-0.3,\ k_{0}=0.2,\\
\mbox{case 2:}&\mu=1/6,\ v_{0,1}=-0.5,\ v_{0,2}=-0.1,\ k_{0}=0.2,\\

Vlasov-Maxwell system.
The first study is devoted to the $hp$ streamline diffusion method for the
relativistic Vlasov-Maxwell system in fully 3-dimensions.
Our objective is to present unified phase-space (for Maxwell’s equations)
and phase-space-time (for the Vlasov-Maxwell system) discretization schemes
that have optimal order convergence for the hyperbolic problems
( ${\mathcal{O}}(h^{s-1/2})$ for solutions in the Sobolev space $H^{s}(\Omega)$ )
with strong stability properties and adaptivity features.
The adaptivity in a priori regime is based on refining in the vicinity of
singularities combined with lower order approximating polynomials and
non-refined mesh with higher spectral order in smooth regions.
In this way we have constructed a finite
element mesh with several improving properties, e.g.
stability, convergence, and adaptivity, gathered in it. To our knowledge,
except in some work in convection-diffusion problems, see e.g. [ 17 ] and our study in [ 5 ] , such approach
is not considered for this type of equations elsewhere. The second study concerns a penalty method for the Maxwell’s equations, which
is based on a certain Nitsche type symmetrization scheme. In this part we have
combined the field equations to a second order pde. For this equation
we derive a second order spatial approximation for the Nitsche’s scheme.
We also prove a second order temporal discretization,
assuming a somewhat more regular-in-time field functions. Even this approach
is not considered in any other works for the VM system. The results are justified in lower dimensional cases through the accuracy
and the streaming Weibel instability tests presented in this paper
and through implementing some numerical examples in the forthcoming paper,
see [ 20 ] .
The full-dimensions are to expensive to experiment.
However, the theoretical analysis and numerical justifications in
low dimensions are indicating the robustness of the considered schemes. Acknowledgment The research of the first author was supported by the
