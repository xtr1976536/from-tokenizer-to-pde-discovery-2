In this paper, we consider the initial-boundary value problem of a three-component reaction-diffusion system with dual-dependent motility (which depends on both $v$ and $m$ ): $\begin{cases}u_{t}=\Delta[\gamma(v,m)u]+\alpha um,&x\in\Omega,~t>0,\\
v_{t}=\Delta v-v+(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}m)u,&x\in\Omega,~t>0,\\
m_{t}=\Delta m-um,&x\in\Omega,~t>0,\\
\nabla(u\gamma(v,m))\cdot\nu=\nabla v\cdot\nu=\nabla m\cdot\nu=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,~t>0,\\
(u,v,m)(x,0)=(u_{0},v_{0},m_{0})(x),&x\in\Omega,\end{cases}$ (*) where $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}$ is a bounded domain with a smooth boundary. Here $\alpha$ , $\theta_{1}$ and $\theta_{2}$ are non-negative constants, and $\nu$ denotes the outward unit normal vector on $\partial\Omega$ . The random motility function $\gamma(v,m)$ , depending on both the chemical concentration $v$ and the nutrient level $m$ , satisfies: • $\gamma(v,m)\in C^{3}\left([0,\infty)\times[0,\infty)\right)$ , $\gamma(v,m)>0$ , $\gamma_{v}(v,m)\leq 0$ , $\gamma_{m}(v,m)\geq 0$ on $[0,\infty)\times[0,\infty)$ ; moreover, $\lim\limits_{v\to+\infty}\gamma(v,m)=0$ for any $m\geq 0$ . For $\theta_{1}=0$ , we first establish the existence of global classical solutions with uniform-in-time bounds for the system ( * ), and further prove that the solution $(u,v,m)$ converges to the constant steady state $(u_{*},0,0)$ as $t\to\infty$ , where $u_{*}=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\left(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}+\alpha\|m_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\right)$ . For $\theta_{1}>0$ , we demonstrate the global boundedness of solutions under appropriate decay conditions on $\gamma(v,m)$ . We further show that there exists $\theta_{*}>0$ such that if $0<\theta_{1}\leq\theta_{*}$ , all solutions converge to $(u_{*},\theta_{1}u_{*},0)$ as $t\to\infty$ with $u_{*}$ defined as above. Our analysis employs refined energy estimates, moser iteration techniques, and careful exploitation of the structural properties of $\gamma(v,m)$ . These results characterize the solution behavior in both cases ( $\theta_{1}=0$ and $\theta_{1}>0$ ), highlighting the crucial role of dual-dependent motility in the system’s long-term dynamics. Introduction In this paper, we consider the initial-boundary value problem for the following three-component reaction-diffusion system with density-dependent motility $\begin{cases}u_{t}=\Delta[\gamma(v,m)u]+\alpha um,&x\in\Omega,~t>0,\\
v_{t}=\Delta v-v+(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}m)u,&x\in\Omega,~t>0,\\
m_{t}=\Delta m-um,&x\in\Omega,~t>0,\\
\nabla(u\gamma(v,m))\cdot\nu=\nabla v\cdot\nu=\nabla m\cdot\nu=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,~t>0,\\
(u,v,m)(x,0)=(u_{0},v_{0},m_{0})(x),&x\in\Omega,\end{cases}$ (1.1) where $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}(n\geq 1)$ is a bounded domain with smooth boundary and $\nu$ stands for the outward unit normal vector on $\partial\Omega$ . The parameters $\alpha\geq 0$ and $\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\geq 0$ . The motility response function $\gamma(v,m)$ depends on both chemical concentration $v$ and the nutrient $n$ , exhibiting chemical-immobilizing and resource-mobilizing properties in the sense that $\gamma_{v}(v,m)\leq 0\ \ \mathrm{and}\ \ \gamma_{m}(v,m)\geq 0.$ Such kind of dual-dependent motility function $\gamma(v,m)$ was first derived in [ 6 ] by considering dweller-wanderer interactions, where the organisms adjust their movement patterns (e.g., velocity, turning frequency, residence time) in response to dual environmental gradients: resource availability and pheromone concentration. This regulation hinges on two opposing yet complementary responses, which collectively optimize the trade-off between local exploitation (by dwellers) and global exploration (by wanderers) in a population. For the system ( 1.1 ), existing results [ 36 ] primarily focus on simplified cases where the motility function $\gamma$ depends exclusively on a single variable: either $\gamma(v,m)=\phi(v)$ or $\gamma(v,m)=\varphi(m)$ . Case 1: $\gamma(v,m)=\phi(v)$ . In this case, when $\theta_{2}=0$ , the system ( 1.1 ) reduces to the following form: $\begin{cases}u_{t}=\Delta[\phi(v)u]+\alpha um,&x\in\Omega,~t>0,\\
v_{t}=\Delta v-v+\theta_{1}u,&x\in\Omega,~t>0,\\
m_{t}=\Delta m-um,&x\in\Omega,~t>0,\\
\nabla(\phi(v)u)\cdot\nu=\nabla v\cdot\nu=\nabla m\cdot\nu=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,~t>0,\\
(u,v,m)(x,0)=(u_{0},v_{0},m_{0})(x),&x\in\Omega,\end{cases}$ (1.2) which was first proposed in [ 25 ] to gain a quantitative understanding of the periodic strip pattern formation. For the system ( 1.2 ), the global existence, uniform boundedness, and stabilization of classical solutions have been intensively investigated recently by virtue of two distinct methods: energy estimate [ 29 , 17 , 18 ] and comparison method [ 10 , 11 , 12 , 13 , 14 , 15 ] . More precisely, when $n=2$ and $\alpha=0$ , the first result on the global boundedness of classical solution for the system ( 1.2 ) was obtained by Tao and Winkler [ 29 ] under the assumption that $0<\phi_{0}\leq\phi(v)\leq\phi_{1}$ by using dual arguments. Based on the ingenious comparison method, Fujie and Jiang [ 12 ] relax the assumption $0<\phi_{0}\leq\phi(v)\leq\phi_{1}$ and establish global existence for any positive, non-increasing motility function $\phi(v)$ that converges to zero at infinity, even allowing the function to vanish asymptotically. Moreover, for any non-increasing motility function decaying slower than any negative exponential function at infinity, the uniform-in-time bound of classical solution was obtained [ 1 , 11 ] . If $\phi(v)=e^{-\chi v}$ for $\chi>0$ , a critical mass phenomenon was found [ 5 , 10 , 12 , 18 ] in the sense that if $\int_{\Omega}u_{0}dx<\frac{4\pi}{\chi\theta_{1}}$ , the solution with uniform-in-time bound exists, while the solution will blow up at infinity time if $\int_{\Omega}u_{0}dx>\frac{4\pi}{\chi\theta_{1}}$ . For higher dimensions ( $n\geq 3$ ), it has been proved that the system ( 1.2 ) with $\alpha=0$ has a unique global classical solution for arbitrary positive motility function [ 13 ] . The uniform-in-time boundedness of classical solution was also studied extensively for both the simplify parabolic-elliptic case [ 1 , 11 , 14 ] or the full parabolic-parabolic case [ 11 , 12 ] . This results imply that $\phi(s)\sim s^{-k}$ as $s\to\infty$ with any $k<\frac{n}{n-2}$ seems to the optimal condition for the existence of global classical of solution with uniform-in time bound when $n\geq 3$ . The results on global existence, uniformly boundedness and stabilization of constant steady state also been extended to the case $\alpha>0$ , see [ 17 , 12 ] . At last, we should mention that except the global boundedness and stabilization of classical solution for the system ( 1.2 ), there also some results on the weak solution [ 8 , 29 ] . There also some interesting results on the system ( 1.2 ) with logistic growth including boundedness [ 10 , 16 ] , non-constant steady state [ 31 , 27 ] and traveling wave [ 23 ] . Case 2: $\gamma(v,m)=\varphi(m)$ . In this case, the component $v$ decouples from $u,m$ and hence the system ( 1.1 ) becomes $\begin{cases}u_{t}=\Delta[\varphi(m)u]+\alpha um,&x\in\Omega,~t>0,\\
m_{t}=\Delta m-um,&x\in\Omega,~t>0,\\
\nabla(\varphi(m)u)\cdot\nu=\nabla m\cdot\nu=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,~t>0,\\
(u,m)(x,0)=(u_{0},m_{0})(x),&x\in\Omega.\end{cases}$ (1.3) The model ( 1.3 ) has been used to describe the bacterial populations how adjust their motility in response to the concentration of surrounding nutrients consumed upon contact. For instance, motility may increase under starvation conditions [ 7 ] or decrease when nutrients are scarce, such as in poor agar environments [ 22 ] . Compared with chemotaxis-production interaction system ( 1.2 ), few results are available for the system ( 1.3 ) due to the lack of some favorable structural properties. The solution behaviors of ( 1.3 ) depend on the fact that $\varphi(m)$ is degenerate or not. More precisely, if $\varphi^{\prime}(m)\leq 0$ , the system ( 1.3 ) can be viewed as the simplest predator–prey system with density-dependent motility [ 20 , 19 ] . If $1\leq n\leq 2$ , it has been proved in [ 21 , 32 ] that the system ( 1.3 ) has a unique global classical solution $(u,m)$ , which converges to the constant steady state $(u_{*},0)$ with $u_{*}=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}u_{0}dx+\frac{\alpha}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}m_{0}dx$ . The results obtained in [ 21 , 32 , 24 ] are based on the fact $\varphi(m)$ is strictly positive throughout $[0,\infty)$ and hence non-degenerate occurs. In contrast to the case $\varphi^{\prime}(m)\leq 0$ , the solution behavior is much more complex due to the possible degeneracy of $\varphi(m)$ . In fact, if $\varphi(m)>0$ but $\varphi(0)=0$ , the global classical solution of the system ( 1.3 ) still exist in the case of $1\leq n\leq 2$ , which however will approach a non-constant steady state $(u_{\infty},0)$ as $t\to\infty$ when $m_{0}$ is appropriately small [ 35 ] . These results in [ 21 , 32 , 24 ] imply that the degeneracy of signal-dependent motility plays an important role for the existence of non-constant large-time patterns in related taxis-consumption systems. Beyond the existence of global classical solution for the system ( 1.3 ) in lower dimensions ( $1\leq n\leq 2$ ), the existence of global very weak solution for any dimensions has also been established in [ 24 , 34 ] for any $\phi\in C^{3}([0,\infty))$ satisfying $\varphi>0$ on $[0,\infty)$ . To our knowledge, there is few result on the chemotaxis model with motility response function $\gamma(v,m)$ depending on both chemical concentration $v$ and the nutrient $m$ . In this paper, we assume that the motility function $\gamma(v,m)$ satisfies the following properties: (H1) $\gamma(v,m)\in C^{3}([0,\infty)\times[0,\infty))$ , $\gamma(v,m)>0$ and $\gamma_{v}(v,m)\leq 0$ , $\gamma_{m}(v,m)\geq 0$ on $[0,\infty)\times[0,\infty)$ . Moreover, for any $m\geq 0$ , it holds that $\lim\limits_{v\to+\infty}\gamma(v,m)=0.$ In this paper, we aim to address several fundamental questions regarding the behavior of solutions to the three-component reaction-diffusion system with a density-dependent motility function $\gamma(v,m)$ that depends on both the chemical concentration $v$ and the nutrient $m$ . Specifically, we investigate the conditions on $\gamma(v,m)$ under which solutions exist globally in time, whether these solutions stabilize to constant or non-constant steady states as time evolves, and how the interplay between chemical sensing and nutrient sensing influences the formation of spatial patterns. These questions are motivated by the need to extend previous studies that focused on simplified cases where $\gamma$ depends solely on $v$ or $m$ , and to provide a more comprehensive understanding of systems where the motility is regulated by multiple environmental factors. Throughout this paper, we assume that the initial value satisfies $(u_{0},v_{0},m_{0})\in[W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)]^{3},\quad u_{0}\geq 0,\quad u_{0}\not\equiv 0,\quad m_{0}\geq 0,\quad v_{0}>0\quad\text{in}\ \bar{\Omega}.$ (1.4) Next, we will investigate the global boundedness and large time behavior of classical solutions to ( 1.1 ) for two distinct cases: $\theta_{1}=0$ and $\theta_{1}>0$ . If $\theta_{1}=0$ , then we have the following results on the global dynamics for the solution behavior. Theorem 1.1 (Boundedness and stabilization: $\theta_{1}=0$ ) . Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}$ be a bounded domain with smooth boundary and $\theta_{1}=0$ . Suppose that $\gamma$ satisfies the assumption (H1), and that the initial data $(u_{0},v_{0},m_{0})$ satisfies ( 1.4 ). Then the system ( 1.1 ) has a unique non-negative global classical solution $(u,v,m)\in[C(\bar{\Omega}\times[0,\infty))\cap C^{2,1}(\bar{\Omega}\times(0,\infty))]^{3}$ satisfying $\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}}+\|m(\cdot,t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\leq C,$ where $C>0$ is a constant independent of $t$ . Moreover, it holds that $\lim_{t\to\infty}(\|u(\cdot,t)-u_{*}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|m(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}})=0,$ where $u_{*}=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}+\alpha\|m_{0}\|_{L^{1}})$ . Remark 1.1 . The results in Theorem 1.1 imply that the solution will not blow up in two-dimensional spaces and no pattern formation occurs if the chemical signal produced by food. Remark 1.2 . In Theorem 1.1 , we assume that $\gamma(v,m)\in C^{3}([0,\infty)\times[0,\infty))$ . However, when $\theta_{1}=0$ , from Theorem 1.1 , we know that $v$ converges to zero as $t\to\infty$ . This raises an interesting problem: what can be said about the global dynamics for the case $\gamma(v,m)=m/v^{k}$ ? Remark 1.3 . In Theorem 1.1 , we only establish the global existence and boundedness of solutions in two-dimensional spaces. An intriguing open question is whether we can derive the global boundedness of solutions for the system ( 1.1 ) with $\theta_{1}=0$ in higher dimensions. If $\theta_{1}>0$ , the behavior of solutions exhibits distinct characteristics. We now elaborate on our findings regarding the global boundedness and stabilization of solutions for the system ( 1.1 ) with $\theta_{1}>0$ . Theorem 1.2 (Boundedness and stabilization: $\theta_{1}>0$ ) . Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}$ be a bounded domain with smooth boundary and $\theta_{1}>0$ . If $\gamma$ satisfies the assumption (H1) and there exists $\chi>0$ such that $\liminf_{s\to+\infty}e^{\chi s}\gamma(s,m)>0,\quad\text{for all}\ m\geq 0,$ (1.5) and the initial data $(u_{0},v_{0},m_{0})$ satisfies ( 1.4 ) with $\|u_{0}+\alpha m_{0}\|_{L^{1}}<\frac{4\pi}{\chi(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}\|m_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}})}.$ Then the system ( 1.1 ) has a unique global classical solution with uniform-in-time bound. Moreover, there exists a constant $\theta_{*}>0$ such that if $0<\theta_{1}\leq\theta_{*}$ , then $\lim_{t\to\infty}\left(\|u(\cdot,t)-u_{*}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|v(\cdot,t)-\theta_{1}u_{*}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|m(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)=0,$ where $u_{*}=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}+\alpha\|m_{0}\|_{L^{1}})$ . In particular, if $\gamma$ satisfies the assumptions (H1) and ( 1.5 ) for any $\chi>0$ , then the system ( 1.1 ) still has a unique classical solution with uniform-in-time bound for any initial data $(u_{0},v_{0},m_{0})$ satisfying ( 1.4 ). Remark 1.4 . Comparing the results in Theorem 1.1 ( $\theta_{1}=0$ ) and Theorem 1.2 ( $\theta_{1}>0$ ), we observe that the presence of chemical production ( $\theta_{1}>0$ ) leads to different asymptotic behavior, with the chemical concentration $v$ stabilizing to $\theta_{1}u_{*}$ rather than zero. 2. Local existence and Preliminaries We first establish the existence of local solutions to ( 1.1 ) by employing the Schauder fixed point theorem along with the parabolic regularity theory [ 16 ] or the Amann’s theorem [ 4 , 3 ] . For brevity, we omit the details of the proof. Lemma 2.1 (Local existence) . Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}$ be a bounded domain with smooth boundary. Assume that $\gamma$ satisfies (H1) and the initial data $(u_{0},v_{0},m_{0})$ satisfies ( 1.4 ).
Then there exists $T_{\max}\in(0,\infty]$ such that the problem ( 1.1 ) has a unique non-negative classical solution $(u,v,m)\in[C(\bar{\Omega}\times[0,T_{\max}))\cap C^{2,1}(\bar{\Omega}\times(0,T_{\max}))]^{3}$ . If $T_{\max}<\infty$ , then $\limsup_{t\nearrow T_{\max}}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}=\infty.$ With the local existence of solution in hand, we establish some basic estimates of solutions for the system ( 1.1 ). Lemma 2.2 . Let $(u,v,m)$ be the solution of ( 1.1 ) obtained in Lemma 2.1 , then we have $0<\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\leq\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}}\leq\|u_{0}+\alpha m_{0}\|_{L^{1}},\quad\text{for all}\ t\in[0,T_{\max}),$ (2.1) and $0\leq m(x,t)\leq\|m_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}:=m^{*},\quad(x,t)\in\bar{\Omega}\times[0,T_{\max}),$ (2.2) as well as $\|m(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\ \text{is decreasing in}\ t.$ (2.3) Proof. Integrating the first equation of ( 1.1 ) and using the non-negativity of $u$ and $m$ , we derive $\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}u=\alpha\int_{\Omega}um,$ (2.4) which gives $\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}}\geq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}>0.$ (2.5) On the other hand, multiplying the third equation of ( 1.1 ) by $\alpha$ , and integrating it by parts and adding it to ( 2.4 ), one has $\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}(u+\alpha m)=0$ and hence $\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}}\leq\int_{\Omega}(u+\alpha m)=\int_{\Omega}(u_{0}+\alpha m_{0}).$ (2.6) Then the combination of ( 2.5 ) and ( 2.6 ) gives ( 2.1 ). Applying the parabolic comparison principle and noting the non-negativity of $m$ and $u$ , we obtain ( 2.2 ) directly. At last, we shall employ the maximum principle to prove ( 2.3 ). For any fixed $t_{0}\in[0,T_{\max})$ and $(x,t)\in\Omega\times[0,T_{\max}-t_{0})$ , we set $z(x,t)=m(x,t+t_{0})-\|m(\cdot,t_{0})\|_{L^{\infty}}.$ Then we can check that $z$ satisfies $\left\{\begin{aligned} &z_{t}-\Delta z+uz\leq 0,&&\quad x\in\Omega,\ t\in(0,T_{\max}-t_{0}),\\
&\nabla z\cdot\nu=0,&&\quad x\in\partial\Omega,\ t\in(0,T_{\max}-t_{0}),\\
&z(x,0)=m(x,t_{0})-\|m(\cdot,t_{0})\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 0,&&\quad x\in\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.$ Thus, invoking the maximum principle, we deduce that $m(x,t+t_{0})\leq\|m(\cdot,t_{0})\|_{L^{\infty}}\ \ \ \mathrm{for}\ \ (x,t)\in\Omega\times[0,T_{\max}-t_{0}),$ (2.7) which gives ( 2.3 ), due to the arbitrary of $t_{0}$ .

Proof of Theorem 1.1 : Boundedness and stabilization with $\theta_{1}=0$ In this section, we shall study the global boundedness and global stabilization for the system ( 1.1 ) with $\theta_{1}=0$ under the assumption that $\gamma(v,m)$ satisfies (H1). From Lemma 2.1 , we obtain $v\geq 0$ , which combined with the assumption (H1) of $\gamma$ and ( 2.2 ), gives a positive constant $\gamma_{1}>0$ such that $0<\gamma(v,m)\leq\gamma_{1}.$ (3.1) However, the condition $0<\gamma(v,m)$ does not exclude the possible of degeneracy for the diffusion of $u$ . Noting that $\gamma_{v}(v,m)\leq 0$ and hence to prevent degeneracy, the key point is to prove that $v$ admits an upper bound. In fact, if $\theta_{1}=0$ , the following result provides the lower and upper bound for $v$ . Lemma 3.1 . If $\theta_{1}=0$ , then the solution $(u,v,m)$ of ( 1.1 ) obtained in Lemma 2.1 satisfies $0\leq v(x,t)\leq C_{1},\quad(x,t)\in\bar{\Omega}\times[0,T_{\max}),$ (3.2) where $C_{1}>0$ is a constant independent of $t$ . Proof. The fact $v\geq 0$ has been proved by Lemma 2.1 . We only need to show the upper bound of $v$ .
Multiplying the third equation of ( 1.1 ) by $\theta_{2}$ and adding the result to the second equation of ( 1.1 ), one has $(v+\theta_{2}m)_{t}=\Delta(v+\theta_{2}m)-v,\quad(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T_{\max}).$ Letting $z=v+\theta_{2}m$ , we deduce that $z$ satisfies the following system $\begin{cases}z_{t}-\Delta z+z=\theta_{2}m,\quad&x\in\Omega,\ t\in(0,T_{\max}),\\
\nabla z\cdot\nu=0,\quad&x\in\partial\Omega,\ t\in(0,T_{\max}),\\
z(x,0)=(v+\theta_{2}m)(x,0)=v_{0}(x)+\theta_{2}m_{0}(x),\quad&x\in\Omega.\end{cases}$ (3.3) Then applying the variation-of-constants formula, from ( 3.3 ) we obtain $z(x,t)=e^{(\Delta-1)t}z_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(\Delta-1)(t-s)}\theta_{2}m(x,s)ds,$ which, together with the well-known semigroup estimate ( [ 33 ] ) and the fact ( 2.2 ), gives $\begin{split}\|z(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}&\leq\|e^{(\Delta-1)t}z_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|e^{(\Delta-1)(t-s)}\theta_{2}m(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{\infty}}ds\\
&\leq k_{1}e^{-\lambda_{1}t}\|z_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\theta_{2}k_{1}\|m_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{1}(t-s)}ds\\
&\leq k_{1}\left(1+\frac{\theta_{2}}{\lambda_{1}}\right)\|z_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},\quad t\in[0,T_{\max}),\end{split}$ (3.4) where $k_{1}$ is a positive constant depending only on $\Omega$ and $\lambda_{1}>0$ is the first nonzero eigenvalue of $-\Delta$ in $\Omega$ .
Therefore, we derive from ( 3.4 ) that $\|v+\theta_{2}m\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq c_{1},$ which, combined with the fact $\theta_{2}>0$ and the non-negativity of $m$ , implies $\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq c_{2}$ for $t\in[0,T_{\max})$ . Hence, we complete the proof of Lemma 3.1 .
∎ 3.1. Global boundedness: $\theta_{1}=0$ In this subsection, we shall prove the global boundedness of solution for the system ( 1.1 ) with $\theta_{1}=0$ . Under the assumption (H1) and the facts ( 3.2 ) and ( 2.2 ), it holds that $0<\gamma_{0}\leq\gamma(v,m)\leq\gamma_{1},\quad|\gamma_{v}(v,m)|\leq\gamma_{2},\quad|\gamma_{m}(v,m)|\leq\gamma_{3}.$ (3.5) Lemma 3.2 . Let $(u,v,w)$ be the solution obtained in Lemma 2.1 .
Then there exists a positive constant $C_{2}>0$ independent of $t$ such that $\int_{t}^{t+\tau}\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\leq C_{2},\quad\text{for all}\ t\in(0,\widetilde{T}_{\max}),$ (3.6) where $\tau:=\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{2}T_{\max}\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{T}_{\max}:=T_{\max}-\tau.$ (3.7) Proof. Let $\mathcal{B}$ be a self-adjoint realization of $-\Delta$ defined on $D(\mathcal{B}):=\left\{\phi\in W^{2,2}(\Omega)\cap L^{2}(\Omega)\Big|\int_{\Omega}\phi=0\ \text{and}\ \frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\nu}=0\ \text{on}\ \partial\Omega\right\}.$ Let $\bar{f}(t)=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}f(x,t)dx$ , we infer from ( 1.1 ) that $(u+\alpha m-\bar{u}-\alpha\bar{m})_{t}=-\mathcal{B}\left(u\gamma(v,m)+\alpha m-\overline{u\gamma(v,m)}-\alpha\bar{m}\right).$ (3.8) Multiplying ( 3.8 ) by $\mathcal{B}^{-1}(u+\alpha m-\bar{u}-\alpha\bar{m})$ and integrating the result by parts, we obtain $\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}&\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|\mathcal{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(u+\alpha m-\bar{u}-\alpha\bar{m})|^{2}\\
=&-\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{B}\left(u\gamma(v,m)+\alpha m-\overline{u\gamma(v,m)}-\alpha\bar{m}\right)\mathcal{B}^{-1}(u+\alpha m-\bar{u}-\alpha\bar{m})\\
=&-\int_{\Omega}\left(u\gamma(v,m)+\alpha m-\overline{u\gamma(v,m)}-\alpha\bar{m}\right)(u+\alpha m-\bar{u}-\alpha\bar{m})\\

&\leq c_{1}.\end{split}$ (3.9) By observing $\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(u+\alpha m-\bar{u}-\alpha\bar{m})=0$ and invoking the Poincar $\acute{\text{e}}$ inequality and the property $\|m(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|m_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}$ , we derive $\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}|\mathcal{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(u+\alpha m-\bar{u}-\alpha\bar{m})|^{2}&\leq c_{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\mathcal{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(u+\alpha m-\bar{u}-\alpha\bar{m})|^{2}\\

∎ Lemma 3.5 . Let $(u,v,m)$ be the solution of the system ( 1.1 ) with $\theta_{1}=0$ obtained in Lemma 2.1 . Auxiliary functions and properties To obtain the lower and upper bound for $v$ , we first introduce two auxiliary functions $w$ and $S$ , and then present some properties of the auxiliary functions as well as the connection between $v$ and the auxiliary functions $w$ and $S$ . Here the auxiliary function $w(x,t)$ is the unique non-negative solution of the following Helmholtz equation $\begin{cases}-\Delta w+w=u,&x\in\Omega,\ t>0,\\
\nabla w\cdot\nu=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,\ t>0.\end{cases}$ (4.1) Similarly, $S(x,t)$ is the unique non-negative solution of the following system $\begin{cases}-\Delta S+S=u+\alpha m,&x\in\Omega,\ t>0,\\

Moreover, for all $(x,t)\in\Omega\times[0,T_{\max})$ , we have $w(x,t)=\mathcal{A}^{-1}[u(x,t)]\geq 0,$ (4.2) and $S(x,t)=\mathcal{A}^{-1}[(u+\alpha m)(x,t)]\geq 0,$ (4.3) where the non-negativity of $S$ and $w$ follows from the non-negativity of $u+\alpha m$ and $u$ by the comparison principle. Define $S_{0}(x)\triangleq S(x,0)=\mathcal{A}^{-1}[u_{0}+\alpha m_{0}](x)\quad\text{and}\quad w_{0}(x)\triangleq w(x,0)=\mathcal{A}^{-1}[u_{0}](x),$ and then $S_{0}$ and $w_{0}$ both belong to $W^{3,\infty}(\Omega)$ by the regularity assumption ( 1.4 ) on the initial conditions.

On the other hand, multiplying $-\Delta w+w=u$ by $w$ and integrating the result by parts, we derive $\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{\Omega}uw.$ (4.33) Therefore, adding ( 4.32 ) into ( 4.33 ), and applying Young’s inequality, we have $\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}(\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+(\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+\int_{\Omega}\gamma(v,m)u^{2}\\
&\leq(\alpha m^{*}+\gamma_{1}+1)\int_{\Omega}uw\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\gamma(v,m)u^{2}+\frac{(\alpha m^{*}+\gamma_{1}+1)^{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{w^{2}}{\gamma(v,m)},\end{split}$ which, in conjunction with $\gamma(v,m)\geq\gamma(v,0)>0$ by the non-negativity of $m$ and the assumption (H1), gives $\frac{d}{dt}(\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+(\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+\int_{\Omega}\gamma(v,m)u^{2}\leq c_{1}\int_{\Omega}\frac{w^{2}}{\gamma(v,0)},$ (4.34) where $c_{1}=(\alpha m^{*}+\gamma_{1}+1)^{2}$ .
Noting that $\gamma$ satisfies the assumptions ( 4.29 ) and (H1), then we fix $\eta\geq 0$ , there exist $b>0$ and $s_{b}>0$ depending on $b$ and $\chi$ such that $\gamma^{-1}(s,\eta)\leq be^{\chi s}+\gamma^{-1}(s_{b},\eta),\quad\text{for all}\ s\geq 0.$ (4.35) From ( 4.35 ) and $v\leq(1+\varepsilon)(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}m^{*})S+(1+\theta_{1})K_{\varepsilon}$ for any $\varepsilon>0$ in ( 4.17 ), we deduce that $\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\frac{w^{2}}{\gamma(v,0)}&\leq\int_{\Omega}[be^{\chi v}+\gamma^{-1}(s_{b},0)]w^{2}\\
&\leq\int_{\Omega}[be^{\chi(1+\varepsilon)(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}m^{*})S+\chi(1+\theta_{1})K_{\varepsilon}}+\gamma^{-1}(s_{b},0)]w^{2}\\
&\leq be^{\chi(1+\theta_{1})K_{\varepsilon}}\left(\int_{\Omega}e^{p\chi(1+\varepsilon)(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}m^{*})S}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{\Omega}w^{2p^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}+\gamma^{-1}(s_{b},0)\int_{\Omega}w^{2},\end{split}$ (4.36) where $p>1$ is close to 1 satisfying $\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1$ . Combining $\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}}\leq\|u_{0}+\alpha m_{0}\|_{L^{1}}$ in ( 2.1 ) with the fact that $w$ satisfies ( 4.1 ), we invoke ( 2.9 ) in Lemma 2.3 such that $\left(\int_{\Omega}w^{2p^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\leq c_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{\Omega}w^{2}\leq c_{2}.$ (4.37) On the other hand, noting that $\|S(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}}=\|u_{0}+\alpha m_{0}\|_{L^{1}}:=\Lambda$ and ( 4.30 ), we can find some sufficient small $\varepsilon>0$ and fix $p>1$ close to 1 such that $(1+\varepsilon)p\Lambda<\frac{4\pi}{\chi(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}m^{*})}$ and hence $\chi(1+\varepsilon)(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}m^{*})p<\frac{4\pi}{\Lambda}.$ (4.38) Since ( 4.38 ) holds, we infer from ( 2.10 ) in Lemma 2.3 that $\left(\int_{\Omega}e^{p\chi(1+\varepsilon)(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}m^{*})S}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq c_{3}.$ (4.39) Therefore, inserting ( 4.37 ) and ( 4.39 ) into ( 4.36 ), one has $\int_{\Omega}\frac{w^{2}}{\gamma(v,0)}\leq c_{4}e^{c_{5}\theta_{1}},$ which, substituted it into ( 4.34 ), gives a constant $c_{6}>0$ independent of $t$ and $\theta_{1}$ such that $\frac{d}{dt}(\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+(\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+\int_{\Omega}\gamma(v,m)u^{2}\leq c_{6}e^{c_{5}\theta_{1}}.$ (4.40) Letting $Z(t)=\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}$ and $h(t)=c_{6}e^{c_{5}\theta_{1}}$ and employing ( 4.40 ), we obtain $Z^{\prime}(t)+Z(t)\leq h(t)\quad\text{for all}\ t\in(0,T_{\max}),$ which implies $\|\nabla w(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|w(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq c_{7}e^{c_{8}\theta_{1}},$ (4.41) where $c_{7}$ and $c_{8}$ are positive constants independent of $t$ and $\theta_{1}$ . Moreover, integrating ( 4.40 ) over the interval $(t,t+\tau)$ and using ( 4.41 ), we derive ( 4.31 ) directly. Then we complete the proof of Lemma 4.4 .
∎ Remark 4.1 . If $\gamma(v,m)$ satisfies ( 4.29 ) for any $\chi>0$ , then we can directly obtain ( 4.39 ) and hence ( 4.31 ) holds without any smallness assumption on the initial data. Lemma 4.5 . Under the assumptions in Lemma 4.4 , it follows that $\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{3}(1+\theta_{1})e^{C_{4}\theta_{1}},\quad\text{for}\ t\in(0,T_{\max}),$ (4.42) where $C_{3}$ and $C_{4}$ are positive constants independent of $\theta_{1}$ and $t$ . Proof. Using the continuous embedding of $W^{2,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Omega)$ ( $p>1$ ) and the standard elliptic regularity, we derive from ( 4.1 ) that $\|w(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq c_{1}\|w(\cdot,t)\|_{W^{2,p}}\leq c_{2}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{p}},$ (4.43) with some $1<p<2$ such that $\frac{p}{2-p}$ is larger than 1 but near 1. Moreover, we can use the Hölder inequality to derive $\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{p}}\leq\|u\gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}(v,m)\|_{L^{2}}\|\gamma^{-\frac{1}{2}}(v,m)\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{2-p}}},$ which substituted into ( 4.43 ) gives $\|w(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq c_{2}\|u\gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}(v,m)\|_{L^{2}}\|\gamma^{-\frac{1}{2}}(v,m)\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{2-p}}}.$ (4.44) Under the facts ( 4.35 ) and $v\leq(1+\varepsilon)(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}m^{*})S+(1+\theta_{1})K_{\varepsilon}$ for any $\varepsilon>0$ in ( 4.17 ), we obtain $\|\gamma^{-\frac{1}{2}}(v,m)\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{2-p}}}\leq c_{3}\left[\int_{\Omega}\left(be^{\chi(1+\theta_{1})K_{\varepsilon}}\right)^{\frac{p}{2-p}}e^{\chi(1+\varepsilon)(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}m^{*})S\frac{p}{2-p}}+(\gamma^{-1}(s_{b},0))^{\frac{p}{2-p}}\right]^{\frac{2-p}{2p}}$ (4.45) Noting $\|S(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}}=\|(u_{0}+\alpha m_{0})\|_{L^{1}}:=\Lambda$ and using the condition ( 4.30 ) that $\Lambda=\|u_{0}+\alpha m_{0}\|_{L^{1}}<\frac{4\pi}{\chi(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}m^{*})},$ we can choose some $\varepsilon>0$ sufficient small such that $(1+\varepsilon)\frac{p}{2-p}\Lambda<\frac{4\pi}{\chi(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}m^{*})}$ and hence $\chi(1+\varepsilon)(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}m^{*})\frac{p}{2-p}<\frac{4\pi}{\Lambda}.$ (4.46) Utilizing ( 4.46 ), we infer from ( 2.10 ) in Lemma 2.3 that $\int_{\Omega}e^{\chi(1+\varepsilon)(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}m^{*})S\frac{p}{2-p}}\leq c_{6},$ which substituted into ( 4.45 ) gives $\|w(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq c_{9}e^{c_{8}\theta_{1}}\left(\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\gamma(v,m)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\gamma(v,m)+c_{10}e^{c_{11}\theta_{1}}$ (4.48) With ( 4.31 ) in hand, we can derive from ( 4.48 ) that $\int_{t}^{t+\tau}\|w(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\int_{t}^{t+\tau}\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\gamma(v,m)dxds+c_{10}e^{c_{11}\theta_{1}}\leq c_{12}e^{c_{13}\theta_{1}},\quad\text{for all}\ t\in(0,T_{\max}-\tau),$ where $\tau$ is defined in ( 3.7 ) and hence for any fixed $x\in\Omega$ , $\int_{t}^{t+\tau}w(x,s)ds\leq\int_{t}^{t+\tau}\|w(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{\infty}}ds\leq c_{12}e^{c_{13}\theta_{1}}.$ (4.49) Moreover, in view of $0\leq m\leq m^{*}$ in ( 2.2 ) and $0<\gamma(v,m)\leq\gamma_{1}$ in ( 3.1 ), applying the elliptic comparison principle to ( 4.5 ), we obtain $w_{t}+u\gamma(v,m)=\mathcal{A}^{-1}[u\gamma(v,m)+\alpha um]\leq(\gamma_{1}+\alpha m^{*})w,$ which, in conjunction with the non-negativity of $u$ , implies $w_{t}\leq(\gamma_{1}+\alpha m^{*})w.$ (4.50) For any $t\in(0,T_{\max})$ , we deduce from ( 4.49 ) that there exists $t_{0}\in((t-\tau)_{+},t)$ satisfying $t_{0}\geq 0$ and $t_{0}\in(0,T_{\max}-\tau)$ , such that $w(\cdot,t_{0})\leq c_{14}e^{c_{13}\theta_{1}}.$ (4.51) Then integrating ( 4.50 ) over $(t_{0},t)$ and employing ( 4.51 ) and $t\leq t_{0}+\tau\leq t_{0}+1$ , we obtain $w(\cdot,t)\leq w(\cdot,t_{0})e^{\int_{t_{0}}^{t}(\gamma_{1}+\alpha m^{*})ds}\leq c_{15}e^{c_{13}\theta_{1}}.$ (4.52) Owing to ( 4.6 ) for $t\in[0,\tau]$ and ( 4.52 ), we infer that $w(x,t)\leq c_{16}e^{c_{13}\theta_{1}},\quad\text{for}\ t\in[0,T_{\max}).$ (4.53) Hence, it follows from ( 4.53 ) and $v\leq(1+\theta_{1})\mathcal{K}_{1}w$ in ( 4.18 ) that $v(x,t)\leq(1+\theta_{1})\mathcal{K}_{1}w(x,t)\leq c_{17}(1+\theta_{1})e^{c_{13}\theta_{1}},\quad\text{for}\ t\in[0,T_{\max}),$ which yields ( ∎ Remark 4.2 . For any bounded positive constant $\theta_{1}$ , we can assume without loss of generality that $0<\theta_{1}\leq c_{1}$ . Consequently, from Lemma 4.5 , we know that there exists a constant $c_{2}>0$ independent of $\theta_{1}$ such that $0<v\leq c_{2}.$ (4.54) For clarity, we denote $\gamma_{0}$ as the lower bound of $\gamma(v,m)$ . Under the assumption (H1) on $\gamma(v,m)$ and using ( 4.54 ), there exist constants $\gamma_{i}(i=0,1,2,3)$ independent of $t$ and $\theta_{1}$ such that $0<\gamma_{0}\leq\gamma(v,m)\leq\gamma_{1},\quad|\gamma_{v}(v,m)|\leq\gamma_{2},\quad\text{and}\quad|\gamma_{m}(v,m)|\leq\gamma_{3}.$ (4.55) Next, we shall use arguments similar to those for $\theta_{1}=0$ to obtain the boundedness of $\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}$ . For brevity, we only present the outline and omit the details of the proof. More precisely, we establish the following results. Lemma 4.6 . Suppose that the conditions in Lemma 4.4 hold. Let $\theta_{1}>0$ and $(u,v,m)$ be the solution of the system ( 1.1 ) obtained in Lemma 2.1 . Then it holds that $\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq M:=C_{5}(1+\theta_{1})^{12}e^{C_{6}(1+\theta_{1})^{6}}\quad\text{for all}\ t\in(0,T_{\max}),$ where the constants $C_{5},C_{6}>0$ are independent of $t$ and $\theta_{1}$ . Proof. By virtue of ( 4.55 ), we can adopt arguments similar to those used in proving Lemma 3.2 to show that there exists a constant $c_{1}>0$ independent of $t$ such that $\int_{t}^{t+\tau}\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\leq c_{1},$ (4.56) where $\tau:=\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{2}T_{\max}\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{T}_{\max}:=T_{\max}-\tau.$ Analogously to the proof of Lemma 3.3 , we derive the following inequalities: $\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2}+2\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2}+\int_{\Omega}|\Delta v|^{2}\leq\int_{\Omega}[(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}m)u]^{2}\leq(1+\theta_{1})^{2}(1+\theta_{2}m^{*})^{2}\int_{\Omega}u^{2},$ (4.57) and $\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla m|^{2}+2\int_{\Omega}|\nabla m|^{2}+\int_{\Omega}|\Delta m|^{2}\leq 2\|m_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\int_{\Omega}u^{2}+2\|m_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}|\Omega|.$ (4.58) Invoking ( 4.56 ) and applying Lemma 2.4 to ( 4.57 ) and ( 4.58 ), we find two positive constants $c_{2}$ and $c_{3}$ independent of $\theta_{1}$ and $t$ such that $\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2}+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla m|^{2}\leq c_{2}(1+\theta_{1})^{2}\quad\text{for all}\ t\in(0,T_{\max}),$ (4.59) and $\int_{t}^{t+\tau}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta v|^{2}+\int_{t}^{t+\tau}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta m|^{2}\leq c_{3}(1+\theta_{1})^{2}\quad\text{for all}\ t\in(0,\widetilde{T}_{\max}).$ (4.60) Using ( 4.59 ) and ( 4.60 ), we employ arguments similar to those in Lemma 3.4 to obtain $\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq c_{4}e^{c_{5}(1+\theta_{1})^{6}}.$ Finally, following the standard steps in Lemmas 3.5 and 3.6 , we conclude that $\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq c_{6}(1+\theta_{1})^{12}e^{c_{7}(1+\theta_{1})^{6}}.$ This completes the proof of Lemma 4.6 .
∎ Proof of Theorem 1.2 : Boundedness . For any $\theta_{1}>0$ , from Lemma 4.6 , we can find a constant $M>0$ independent of $t$ such that $\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq M$ , which combined with the local existence results in Lemma 2.1 proves the existence of global classical solution with uniform-in-time bound stated in Theorem 1.2 .
∎ 4.3. Global stabilization: $\theta_{1}>0$ In this subsection, we proceed to analyze the asymptotic behavior of solutions for the system ( 1.1 ) with $\theta_{1}>0$ . First, we should point out that for $\theta_{1}>0$ , the result in Lemma 3.10 still holds and hence $\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\|m(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}=0.$ (4.61) Henceforth, we only focus on the convergence of $u$ and $v$ . In fact, we establish the following results: Lemma 4.7 . Let $(u,v,m)$ be the solution of the system ( 1.1 ) with $\theta_{1}>0$ obtained in Theorem 1.2 . Then there exists a $\theta_{*}>0$ such that if $0<\theta_{1}\leq\theta_{*}$ we have $\lim_{t\to\infty}(\|u(\cdot,t)-u_{*}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|v(\cdot,t)-\theta_{1}u_{*}\|_{L^{\infty}})=0,$ (4.62) where $u_{*}=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int u_{0}+\frac{\alpha}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}m_{0}$ . Proof. As in Lemma 3.11 , we first rewrite the first equation of the system ( 1.1 ) as $(u-u_{*})_{t}=\Delta(u\gamma(v,m))+\alpha um,$ and then multiply it by $u-u_{*}$ and integrate it by parts to derive that $\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}(u-u_{*})^{2}+\int_{\Omega}\gamma(v,m)|\nabla u|^{2}=-\int_{\Omega}\gamma_{v}u\nabla u\cdot\nabla v-\int_{\Omega}\gamma_{m}u\nabla u\cdot\nabla m+\alpha\int_{\Omega}um(u-u_{*}),$ which, together with the facts ( 4.55 ) and $\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq M$ in Lemma 4.6 , gives $\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}(u-u_{*})^{2}+2\gamma_{0}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\\
&\leq 2M\gamma_{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u||\nabla v|+2M\gamma_{3}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u||\nabla m|+2\alpha M^{2}\int_{\Omega}m\\
&\leq\gamma_{0}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{2M^{2}(\gamma_{2}^{2}+\gamma_{3}^{2})}{\gamma_{0}}\int_{\Omega}(|\nabla v|^{2}+|\nabla m|^{2})+2\alpha M^{2}\int_{\Omega}m,\end{split}$ and then $\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}(u-u_{*})^{2}+\gamma_{0}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}&\leq\frac{2M^{2}(\gamma_{2}^{2}+\gamma_{3}^{2})}{\gamma_{0}}\int_{\Omega}(|\nabla v|^{2}+|\nabla m|^{2})+2\alpha M^{2}\int_{\Omega}m.\end{split}$ (4.63) Recalling that $\bar{u}=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}u=u_{*}-\frac{\alpha}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}m=u_{*}-\alpha\bar{m}$ , we use the Poincar $\acute{\text{e}}$ inequality with the constant $C_{p}>0$ to find $\int_{\Omega}(u-u_{*})^{2}\leq 2\int_{\Omega}(u-\bar{u})^{2}+2\alpha^{2}\int_{\Omega}\bar{m}^{2}\leq 2C_{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}+2\alpha^{2}m^{*}\int_{\Omega}m,$ which, multiplied by $\frac{\gamma_{0}}{2C_{p}}$ and substituted into ( 4.63 ), yields $\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}(u-u_{*})^{2}+\frac{\gamma_{0}}{2C_{p}}\int_{\Omega}(u-u_{*})^{2}\leq&\frac{2M^{2}(\gamma_{2}^{2}+\gamma_{3}^{2})}{\gamma_{0}}\int_{\Omega}(|\nabla v|^{2}+|\nabla m|^{2})\\
&+\frac{\gamma_{0}\alpha^{2}m^{*}+2\alpha M^{2}C_{p}}{C_{p}}\int_{\Omega}m.\end{split}$ (4.64) Moreover, we rewrite the second equation of the system ( 1.1 ) as $(v-\theta_{1}u_{*})_{t}=\Delta v-(v-\theta_{1}u_{*})+\theta_{1}(u-u_{*})+\theta_{2}um,$ (4.65) then multiply ( 4.65 ) by $(v-\theta_{1}u_{*})$ and integrate it by parts to obtain $\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}(v-\theta_{1}u_{*})^{2}+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2}\\
&\leq-\int_{\Omega}(v-\theta_{1}u_{*})^{2}+\theta_{1}\int_{\Omega}(u-u_{*})(v-\theta_{1}u_{*})+\theta_{2}\int_{\Omega}um(v-\theta_{1}u_{*})\\
&\leq-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(v-\theta_{1}u_{*})^{2}+\frac{\theta_{1}^{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}(u-u_{*})^{2}+c_{2}\theta_{2}M\int_{\Omega}m,\end{split}$ which implies $\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}(v-\theta_{1}u_{*})^{2}+\int_{\Omega}(v-\theta_{1}u_{*})^{2}+2\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2}\leq\theta_{1}^{2}\int_{\Omega}(u-u_{*})^{2}+2c_{2}\theta_{2}M\int_{\Omega}m.$ (4.66) On the other hand, multiplying the third equation of ( 1.1 ) by $m$ and integrating it by parts, we derive $\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}m^{2}=-2\int_{\Omega}|\nabla m|^{2}-2\int_{\Omega}um^{2},$ which, combined with $0\leq m(x,t)\leq m^{*}$ , gives $\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}m^{2}+\int_{\Omega}m^{2}+2\int_{\Omega}|\nabla m|^{2}\leq m^{*}\int_{\Omega}m.$ (4.67) Therefore, multiplying ( 4.66 ) and ( 4.67 ) by $\frac{M^{2}(\gamma_{2}^{2}+\gamma_{3}^{2})}{\gamma_{0}}$ respectively, and adding them into ( 4.64 ), we have $\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}\left\{\int_{\Omega}(u-u_{*})^{2}+\frac{M^{2}(\gamma_{2}^{2}+\gamma_{3}^{2})}{\gamma_{0}}\int_{\Omega}[(v-\theta_{1}u_{*})^{2}+m^{2}]\right\}\\
&\ \ \ +\left[\frac{\gamma_{0}}{2C_{p}}-\frac{M^{2}(\gamma_{2}^{2}+\gamma_{3}^{2})\theta_{1}^{2}}{\gamma_{0}}\right]\int_{\Omega}(u-u_{*})^{2}+\frac{M^{2}(\gamma_{2}^{2}+\gamma_{3}^{2})}{\gamma_{0}}\int_{\Omega}[(v-\theta_{1}u_{*})^{2}+m^{2}]\\
&\leq\left[\frac{M^{2}(\gamma_{2}^{2}+\gamma_{3}^{2})(2c_{2}\theta_{2}M+m^{*})}{\gamma_{0}}+\frac{\gamma_{0}\alpha m^{*}+2\alpha M^{2}C_{p}}{C_{p}}\right]\int_{\Omega}m.\end{split}$ (4.68) By the definitions of $M$ in Lemma 4.6 , and $\gamma_{0},\gamma_{2}$ and $\gamma_{3}$ in ( 4.55 ), we derive that there exists a constant $\theta_{*}>0$ such that if $0<\theta_{1}<\theta_{*}$ that $M_{1}:=\frac{\gamma_{0}}{2C_{p}}-\frac{M^{2}(\gamma_{2}^{2}+\gamma_{3}^{2})\theta_{1}^{2}}{\gamma_{0}}>0.$ (4.69) Letting $Y(t):=\int_{\Omega}(u-u_{*})^{2}+\frac{M^{2}(\gamma_{2}^{2}+\gamma_{3}^{2})}{\gamma_{0}}\int_{\Omega}[(v-\theta_{1}u_{*})^{2}+m^{2}],$ then utilizing ( 4.68 ) and noting ( 4.69 ), we can find a constant $c_{3}>0$ such that $Y^{\prime}(t)+c_{3}Y(t)\leq\left[\frac{M^{2}(\gamma_{2}^{2}+\gamma_{3}^{2})(2c_{2}\theta_{2}M+m^{*})}{\gamma_{0}}+\frac{\gamma_{0}\alpha m^{*}+2\alpha M^{2}C_{p}}{C_{p}}\right]|\Omega|\|m(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}},$ which, coupled with $\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\|m(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}=0$ , gives $\lim_{t\to\infty}(\|u(\cdot,t)-u_{*}\|_{L^{2}}+\|v(\cdot,t)-\theta_{1}u_{*}\|_{L^{2}})=\lim_{t\to\infty}Y(t)=0.$ (4.70) Applying the parabolic regularity, we obtain that $\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}}+\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\leq c_{4}\ \ \mathrm{for}\ \ t\geq 1.$ (4.71) Hence, we invoke the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality to conclude ( 4.62 ) by ( 4.70 ) and ( 4.71 ).
∎ Proof of Theorem 1.2 : Stabilization. From ( 4.61 ) and Lemma 4.7 , we complete the global stabilization of the constant steady state $(u_{*},\theta_{1}u_{*},0)$ .
