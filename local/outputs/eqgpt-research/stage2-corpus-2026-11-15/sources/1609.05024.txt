Einsteinstr. 62, D 48149 Münster, Germany. e-mail: {judith.berendsen,martin.burger,jan.pietschmann}@wwu.de Martin Burger Jan-Frederik Pietschmann Abstract The aim of this paper is to study a PDE model for two diffusing species interacting by local size exclusion and global attraction. This leads to a nonlinear degenerate cross-diffusion system, for which we provide a global existence result. The analysis is motivated by the formulation of the system as a formal gradient flow for an appropriate energy functional consisting of entropic terms as well as quadratic nonlocal terms. Key ingredients are entropy dissipation methods as well as the recently developed boundedness by entropy principle.
Moreover, we investigate phase separation effects inherent in the cross-diffusion model by an analytical and numerical study of minimizers of the energy functional and their asymptotics to a previously studied case as the diffusivity tends to zero. Finally we briefly discuss coarsening dynamics in the system, which can be observed in numerical results and is motivated by rewriting the PDEs as a system of nonlocal Cahn-Hilliard equations. 1 Introduction Mathematical models with local repulsion and global attraction received strong attention in the last decades, in particular motivated by applications in biology ranging from bacterial chemotaxis (cf. e.g. [ 17 , 25 , 47 , 55 ] ) to macroscopic motion of animal groups (cf. e.g. [ 10 , 15 , 31 , 52 ] ) as well as applications in other fields of science (cf. e.g. [ 62 , 45 ] ). The macroscopic modelling of the density evolution leads to partial differential equations with nonlinear diffusion and an additional nonlocal term. The majority of such models can be formulated as metric gradient flows for the density $\rho$ with some energy functional consisting of a local and a nonlocal interaction term $E(\rho)=\int_{\Omega}e(\rho)-\rho(K*\rho)~dx,$ (1.1) where $e$ is a convex functional, $K$ a nonnegative interaction kernel, and $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}$ . Throughout the paper, $\Omega$ may always be unbounded and we will explicitly remark when its boundedness is needed. Various results on the analysis of energy minimizers respectively stationary states (cf. [ 2 , 5 , 16 , 19 , 22 , 26 , 27 , 29 , 32 , 39 , 50 , 58 , 59 ] ) and the gradient flow dynamics of the form (cf. [ 6 , 9 , 8 , 16 , 28 , 30 , 36 , 65 ] ) $\partial_{t}\rho=\nabla\cdot(M(\rho)\nabla E^{\prime}(\rho))$ (1.2) have been achieved in the last years, which led to a good understanding of such models and phenomena. Much less is known however in the case of multi-species systems, which received most attention only recently (cf. e.g. [ 1 , 14 , 13 , 20 , 44 , 54 , 57 , 60 ] ). With different species, the modelling leads to nonlinear degenerate cross-diffusion systems for the densities of all species, again with some nonlocal terms. The majority of work was concerned with the derivation of models including formal and computational studies, rigorous results are so far mainly available without the nonlocal interaction terms. First rigorous studies of stationary problems (cf. [ 24 , 18 , 33 ] ) show interesting phase separation phenomena, whose dynamics seems rather unexplored so far. In this paper we hence study a nonlocal cross-diffusion model for two species called red (density $r$ ) and blue (density $b$ ) for simplicity, which can be derived from a lattice-based microscopic model with size exclusion (cf. [ 20 , 57 ] ): $\displaystyle\partial_{t}r$ $\displaystyle=\nabla\cdot(\varepsilon(1-\rho)\nabla r+\varepsilon r\nabla\rho+r(1-\rho)\left[\nabla(c_{11}K\ast r-K\ast b)+\nabla V\right]),$ (1.3) $\displaystyle\partial_{t}b$ $\displaystyle=\nabla\cdot(D\varepsilon(1-\rho)\nabla b+D\varepsilon b\nabla\rho+Db(1-\rho)\left[\nabla(c_{22}K\ast b-K\ast r)+\nabla V\right]),$ (1.4) either on the whole space or in a bounded domain supplemented with no-flux boundary conditions. The positive parameter $\varepsilon$ regulates the strength of the diffusion relative to the nonlocal convection terms.
Here $K$ is again the interaction kernel, and the constants $c_{ii}<0$ , $i=1,2$ measure the strength of self-interaction, while the strength of the cross-interaction is scaled to unity (in accordance with the notation of [ 33 ] ). The time scaling is chosen such that $r$ has a unit diffusion coefficient, and $D$ is the potentially different diffusion coefficient of $b$ . The function $\rho$ is the nonnegative total density $\rho=r+b,$ (1.5) naturally bounded from above by one.
This system (for $V\equiv 0$ ) is a gradient flow for the energy functional $\displaystyle F^{\varepsilon}(r,\,b)=\varepsilon F^{E}(r,b)+F^{0}(r,b).$ (1.6) consisting of the nonlocal interaction $\displaystyle F^{0}(r,\,b)=\int_{\Omega}c_{11}r(K*r)-r(K*b)-b(K*r)+c_{22}b(K*b)\;dx$ (1.7) and the entropy term $\displaystyle F^{E}(r,\,b)=\int_{\Omega}r\log r+b\log b+(1-\rho)\log(1-\rho)\;dx.$ (1.8) For $\Omega$ unbounded we need a confining potential $V$ and the energy is modified to $\displaystyle E^{\varepsilon}(r,\,b)=F^{\varepsilon}(r,\,b)+F^{C}(r,\,b),\qquad F^{C}(r,\,b)=\int_{\Omega}(r+b)V\;dx.$ (1.9) We shall from now on always use the letter $F$ to denote the energy without confining potential and $E$ if the potential is present.
This energy is to be considered on the set of bounded densities with given mass $\displaystyle{\cal A}=\{(r,\,b)\in L^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{+})^{2}:$ (1.10) $\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}r\;dx=m_{r},\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}b\;dx=m_{b},\rho=r+b\leq 1\text{ for a.e. }x\in\Omega\},$ which can be shown to be invariant under the dynamics of ( 1.3 ), ( 1.4 ).
The special case of minimizing $F^{0}$ on ${\cal A}$ was recently investigated by Cicalese et. al. [ 33 ] and it appears obvious that $F^{\varepsilon}$ is the natural entropic version and ( 1.3 ), ( 1.4 ) the natural dynamic model leading to such a minimization problem in the large time asymptotics. 1.1 Connection to Cahn-Hilliard In order to see the inherent phase separation in ( 1.3 ), ( 1.4 ) we rewrite it as a system of nonlocal Cahn-Hilliard equations. For this sake let $K$ be nonnegative and integrable with $k=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}K(x)~dx,$ (1.11) which allows to define the nonlocal Laplacian $\Delta_{K}$ as a negative semidefinite operator via $-\Delta_{K}u=u-\frac{1}{k}K\ast u.$ (1.12) Then ( 1.3 ), ( 1.4 ) becomes $\displaystyle\partial_{t}r=\nabla\cdot(kr(1-\rho)\nabla(c_{11}\Delta_{K}r-\Delta_{K}b+\partial_{r}W(r,b)))$ (1.13) $\displaystyle\partial_{t}b=\nabla\cdot(Dkb(1-\rho)\nabla(c_{22}\Delta_{K}b-\Delta_{K}r+\partial_{b}W(r,b)))$ (1.14) with the multi-well potential $W(r,b)=\varepsilon(r\log r+b\log b+(1-\rho)\log(1-\rho))+\frac{c_{11}}{2}r^{2}-rb+\frac{c_{22}}{2}b^{2}-\frac{c_{11}}{2}r-\frac{c_{22}}{2}b.$ (1.15) Note that for $\varepsilon=0$ the potential has three global minimizers $(r,b)$ in the corners of the unit triangle, i.e. $(0,1)$ , $(1,0)$ and $(0,0)$ . The first two correspond to segregated states and the third one to void, hence those are the main structures to be expected as energy minimizers and in the long time asympotics. For small $\varepsilon>0$ the minima are shifted to the interior of the unit triangle and we thus expect less pronounced segregation. For large $\varepsilon$ the energy becomes convex and hence we expect mixing instead of segregation. As in other Cahn-Hilliard equations we expect dynamics at two time scales: a short one where clusters - in this case separated in red and blue - appear, and a large one where coarsening dynamics of the clusters appear. In the single species case of the model ( 1.3 ), ( 1.4 ), e.g. obtained for $b\equiv 0$ , the coarsening dynamics has been discussed in detail in [ 21 , 34 ] , coarsening rates for a related nonlocal model were derived in [ 61 ] . Related work for systems of local Cahn-Hilliard equations (partly with constant mobility) was carried out in [ 12 , 37 , 40 , 41 , 44 ] , supplemented by numerical simulations in [ 4 , 53 ] . 1.2 Organization of the paper The aim of this work is to analyse the entropic regularization, with parameter $\varepsilon$ , of the functional $F^{0}$ . We takle this problem from two different perspectives: First we consider the regularized energy from a variational point of view. Second, we study the system of non-local cross-diffusion equations which naturally occur as the gradient flow with respect to this energy. To this end, we will start by discussing the energy minimization of $F^{\varepsilon}$ respectively $E^{\varepsilon}$ , we briefly comment on the results in [ 33 ] and minor extensions, moreover we verify the $\Gamma$ -convergence of $E^{\varepsilon}$ to $E^{0}$ . We also prove the existence of minimizers of $E^{\varepsilon}$ and their convergence. This is supplemented by a numerical study of the variational problem based on energy minimization via splitting methods. We further show the existence of weak solutions for the transient model ( 1.3 ), ( 1.4 ), extending the results of [ 20 ] to nonlocal interactions and simplifying the line of the proof via appropriate time discretization with regularizing terms in primal and dual (entropy) variables. Moreover, we provide a regularity result in the case of equal diffusivities and verify consistency of the stationary problem with the energy minimization. Finally we study the dynamics in the particularly relevant case of $\varepsilon\rightarrow 0$ by formal and numerical methods. In the single species case metastable coarsening dynamics of clusters are already studied in detail (cf. [ 34 , 21 ] ) and we obtain similar behaviour in the multi-species setting. 2 Minimizers of the Energy Functionals We start by stating assumptions on the interaction kernel $K$ and the potential $V$ . While the Coulomb kernel (or Newton-Potential) is a prominent example for our model, we can extend our arguments to a more general class. Definition 2.2 (Admissible Kernel) . For $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}$ , possibly unbounded, we say that a kernel $K$ is admissible if the following conditions are satisfied (K1) $K\in W^{1,1}_{loc}(\Omega)$ . (K2) K is radially symmetric, i.e. $K(x)=k(|x|)$ and $k$ is non-increasing. (K3) As $x\to 0$ and $x\to\infty$ , $K$ behaves at most as singular as the Coulomb kernel. Since $V$ should act as a confining potential, we impose the following growth condition at infinity. (V1) Let $V\in W^{1,\infty}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})$ be nonnegative almost everywhere and such that $\liminf_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{V(x)}{|x|^{2}}>0.$ 2.1 Existence of Minimizers Let us first summarize the results of Cicalese et. al. concerning the existence of a minimizer of solely the functional $F^{0}$ on the whole space $\mathbb{R}^{N}$ . Theorem 2.3 ( [ 33 ] ) . Let $c_{11},\,c_{22}\leq 0$ and $K\in L_{loc}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})$ and ${\cal A}$ as defined in ( 1.10 ). Then there exists a minimizer $(r^{0},\,b^{0})\in\mathcal{A}$ to the functional $\displaystyle F^{0}(r,\,b)=\int_{\Omega}c_{11}r(K*r)-r(K*b)-b(K*r)+c_{22}b(K*b)\,dx.$ More precisely let $(r_{n},\,b_{n})$ be a minimizing sequence. Then there is a unique sequence of translations $(\tau_{n})\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}$ such that up to a subsequence $(r_{n}(\cdot-\tau_{n}),b_{n}(\cdot-\tau_{n}))\to(r^{0},\,b^{0})$ as $n\to\infty$ . Remark 2.4 . Note that due to the translation invariance of $F^{0}$ , the results of [ 33 ] allow for arbitrary shifts of the minimizers. Adding a confining potential, i.e. replacing $F^{0}$ by $E^{0}=F^{0}+F^{C}$ , however, does not change their result and removes this technicality. Next, we extend this existence result to the functionals $F^{\varepsilon}$ and $E^{\varepsilon}$ , respectively. Theorem 2.5 . (Existence of Minimizer) 
Let $c_{11},\,c_{22}\leq 0$ and $K$ be admissible in the sense of definition 2.2 . Then there exists at least one minimizer $(\underline{r},\,\underline{b})\in\mathcal{A}$ to the functional $\displaystyle E^{\varepsilon}(r,b)$ $\displaystyle=\varepsilon F^{E}(r,b)+F^{0}(r,b)+F^{C}(r,b).$ More precisely any minimizing sequence $(r_{n},\,b_{n})$ converges $L^{1}(\Omega)-$ weakly to a minimizer $(\underline{r},\,\underline{b})$ of $E^{\varepsilon}$ as $n\to\infty$ . If, in addition, $\Omega$ is bounded, the same result holds for the functional $F^{\varepsilon}$ , i.e. the energy without confining potential. While the proof of Theorem 2.3 uses Lions’ concentration compactness principle, our approach is based on Dunford-Pettis, making use of the confining potential when $\Omega$ is unbounded. ∎ 2.2 Structure of Energy Minimizers The energy minimizers of $F^{0}$ on ${\cal A}$ could be characterized explicitly in some important cases in [ 33 ] using radial symmetrization techniques. In particular they showed that there is no phase separation in the case of cross-attraction being larger than the self-attractions, i.e. $c_{11}>-1$ and $c_{22}>-1$ . On the other hand they obtain a strong phase separation result if $c_{11}+c_{22}<-2$ and one of the self-attractions is weak ( $c_{ii}>-1$ ). In this case the energy minimizer consists of the strongly attracting species having density one in a ball and the other one having density in a spherical shell around it. The case of strong self-attraction of both species, $c_{11}<-1$ , $c_{22}<-1$ was left open in multiple dimension however, noticing that already in dimension one the minimizer is not spherically symmetric. We supplement these results by a generic phase separation result, whose proof closely follows a related result in [ 18 ] as well as numerical simulations for $N=2$ in Section 3 : Proposition 2.8 . Let $(\underline{r},\underline{b})\in{\cal A}$ be a minimizer of $F^{0}$ on this admissible set, for $K$ being a radially strictly decreasing kernel and $c_{11}+c_{22}<-2$ .
Then the intersection of the supports of $\underline{r}$ and $\underline{b}$ on $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}$ possibly unbounded has zero Lebesgue measure. Proof. Assume by contradiction that there exists an set $D$ of positive Lebesgue measure and $\delta_{0}\in(0,\frac{1}{2})$ such that $\underline{r}>\delta_{0}$ and $\underline{b}>\delta_{0}$ on $D$ . Let $d$ be the diameter and $R<\frac{d}{6}$ sufficiently small. Then there exist two balls $B_{R}(x_{1})$ and $B_{R}(x_{2})$ such that $|x_{1}-x_{2}|>4R$ and the intersection of both balls with $D$ has nonzero Lebesgue measure. Define $S_{i}=B_{R}(x_{i})\cap D$ . For $|\delta|<\min\{{\delta_{0}}{|S_{1}|},{\delta_{0}}{|S_{2}|})$ define variations $r^{\delta}$ and $b^{\delta}$ that coincide with $\underline{r}$ respectively $\underline{b}$ outside $S_{1}\cup S_{2}$ and $r^{\delta}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\underline{r}+\frac{\delta}{|S_{1}|}&\text{in }S_{1}\\
\underline{r}-\frac{\delta}{|S_{2}|}&\text{in }S_{2}\end{array}\right.\qquad b^{\delta}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\underline{b}-\frac{\delta}{|S_{1}|}&\text{in }S_{1}\\
\underline{b}+\frac{\delta}{|S_{2}|}&\text{in }S_{2}\end{array}\right.$ It is straightforward to see that $(r^{\delta},b^{\delta})\in{\cal A}$ and $\displaystyle F^{0}(r^{\delta},b^{\delta})$ $\displaystyle=F^{0}(\underline{r},\underline{b})$ $\displaystyle\quad+\delta\ell(\underline{r},\underline{b})+\delta^{2}(c_{11}+c_{22}+2)\Big(\frac{1}{|S_{1}|^{2}}\int_{S_{1}}\int_{S_{1}}K(x-y)~dx~dy$ $\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{|S_{2}|^{2}}\int_{S_{2}}\int_{S_{2}}K(x-y)~dx~dy-\frac{2}{|S_{1}|~|S_{2}|}\int_{S_{1}}\int_{S_{2}}K(x-y)~dx~dy\Big)$ for some bounded linear functional $\ell$ . From $F^{0}(r^{\delta},b^{\delta})\geq F^{0}(\underline{r},\underline{b})$ we conclude in the limit $\delta\rightarrow 0$ we have $\ell(\underline{r},\underline{b})=0$ . The strict radial decrease of the kernel $K$ implies $\frac{1}{|S_{i}|^{2}}\int_{S_{i}}\int_{S_{i}}K(x-y)~dx~dy\geq\inf_{|z|\leq 2R}K(z)$ for $i\in\{1,2\}$ , and $\frac{2}{|S_{1}|~|S_{2}|}\int_{S_{1}}\int_{S_{2}}K(x-y)~dx~dy<2\sup_{|z|\geq 2R}K(z)\leq 2\inf_{|z|\leq 2R}K(z),$ hence the above difference of integrals is positive. With $c_{11}+c_{22}+2<0$ we then conclude for finite $\delta$ that $F^{0}(r^{\delta},b^{\delta})<F^{0}(\underline{r},\underline{b}),$ which is a contradiction.
∎ The entropic terms in the energy $F^{\varepsilon}$ counter this separation effect on the minimizers. We can show this complete change of the situation on bounded domains. Lemma 2.9 . For further studies of the detailed shapes of the minimizers we refer to the numerical examples in Section 5 . Theorem 2.12 ( $\Gamma$ -Convergence) . Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}$ be an open and bounded domain.
As $\varepsilon\to 0$ and for all $(r,b)\in{\cal A}$ , the functional $\displaystyle E^{\varepsilon}(r,b)$ $\Gamma$ -converges to the functional $\displaystyle E^{0}(r,b)$ with respect to the weak topology in $[L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})]^{2}$ . Furthermore, minimizers $(r^{\varepsilon},b^{\varepsilon})$ of $E^{\varepsilon}$ converge to minimizers $(r_{0},b_{0})$ of $E^{0}$ as $\varepsilon\to 0$ . Proof. To prove $\Gamma$ -convergence, we have to check a $\liminf-$ inequality and the existence of a recovery sequence, see [ 11 , definition 1.5] for details. To this end consider arbitrary sequences $r^{\varepsilon},\,b^{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{A}$ that weakly converge to some $(r,b)\in\mathcal{A}$ as $\varepsilon\to 0$ . Since on $\mathcal{A}$ , the functional $F^{E}$ is uniformly bounded, is follows that $\varepsilon F^{E}$ converges to zero. On the other hand, we know that the interaction terms $F^{0}$ are weakly lower semicontinuous and thus we have $\displaystyle E^{0}(r,b)$ $\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}E^{\varepsilon}(r_{\varepsilon},b_{\varepsilon}).$ (2.10) Choosing for every $(r,b)\in\mathcal{A}$ the constant sequence as recovery sequence, and using once more the fact that $\varepsilon F^{E}$ converges to zero as $\varepsilon\to 0$ for all $(r,b)\in\mathcal{A}$ we have $\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}E^{\varepsilon}(r_{\varepsilon},b_{\varepsilon})=E^{0}(r,b).$ This implies the $\Gamma$ -convergence of $E^{\varepsilon}$ to $E^{0}$ . The fact that the set $\mathcal{A}$ is precompact with respect to the weak topology in $L^{2}$ trivially implies the coerciveness of $E^{\varepsilon}$ and therefore, [ 11 , Theorem 1.21] , we have the convergence of minimizers as $\varepsilon\to 0$ with respect to the weak topology in $L^{2}$ .

As a consequence of the rescaling we find up to higher order terms in $\lambda$ $\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}K(x-y)r(x)b(y)~dx~dy$ $\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}K(\frac{\tilde{x}-\tilde{y}}{\lambda})\tilde{r}(\tilde{x})\tilde{b}(\tilde{y})\lambda^{-2N}~d\tilde{x}~d\tilde{y}$ $\displaystyle\approx\lambda^{-N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}k\tilde{r}(\tilde{x})\tilde{b}(\tilde{x})-C\lambda^{2}\nabla\tilde{r}(\tilde{x})\cdot\nabla\tilde{b}(\tilde{x})~d\tilde{x},$ with $k$ defined in ( 1.11 ), $C$ being related to the second moment of $K$ . With similar approximation of the self-interaction terms the energy $\lambda^{N}F^{0}$ equals up to second order $\tilde{F}^{\lambda}(\tilde{r},\tilde{b})=\lambda^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\frac{a_{11}}{2}|\nabla\tilde{r}|^{2}+\frac{a_{22}}{2}|\nabla\tilde{b}|^{2}+a_{12}\nabla\tilde{r}\cdot\nabla\tilde{b})~d\tilde{x}+k\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}W(\tilde{r},\tilde{b})~d\tilde{x},$ with $W$ as in ( 1.15 ) with $\varepsilon=0$ and $a_{11}=-c_{11}C,\quad a_{22}=-c_{22}C,\quad a_{12}=C.$ We see that for $c_{11},\,c_{22}>1$ the symmetric $2\times 2$ matrix $A$ with diagonal entries $a_{ii}$ und off-diagonal entries $a_{12}$ is symmetric. Hence, we may perform a change of variables to $(u,v)=A^{1/2}(\tilde{r},\tilde{b})$ and obtain the diagonalized problem $G^{\lambda}(u,v)=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2}-kW(A^{-1/2}(u,v))~d\tilde{x}$ to be minimized on the transformed unit interval ${\cal B}=\{(u,v)~|~A^{-1/2}(u,v)\in{\cal A}\}.$ The asymptotics of problems of this form has been investigated by Baldo [ 3 ] , who showed $\Gamma$ -convergence to a multi-phase problem, the limiting functional being a sum of weighted perimeters between the pure phases. Translated to our setting we can conclude that $\tilde{F}^{\lambda}$ $\Gamma$ -converges to a functional of the form $\tilde{F}^{0}(R,B)=d_{RB}\text{Per}(\partial R\cap\partial B)+d_{RV}\text{Per}(\partial R\setminus\partial B)+d_{BV}\text{Per}(\partial B\setminus\partial R)$ (2.11) and weights $d_{RB}$ , $d_{RV}$ , $d_{BV}$ that can be derived from the behaviour of the potential $W$ , i.e. the values $c_{11}$ , $c_{22}$ , on the unit triangle. The following estimates hold for a constant $\tilde{C}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}$ and $r_{\tau},\,b_{\tau}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)$ $\displaystyle\|\sqrt{1-\rho_{\tau}}\nabla\sqrt{r_{\tau}}\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\sqrt{1-\rho_{\tau}}\nabla\sqrt{b_{\tau}}\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,L^{2}(\Omega))}$ $\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C},$ (4.62) $\displaystyle\|\sqrt{1-\rho_{\tau}}\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,H^{1}(\Omega))}+\|\rho_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,H^{1}(\Omega))}$ $\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}$ (4.63) $\displaystyle\sqrt{\tau}(\|u_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}((0,T);H^{1}(\Omega))}+\|v_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}((0,T);H^{1}(\Omega))})$ $\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}$ (4.64) Lemma 4.3 . The discrete time derivatives of $r_{\tau}$ and $b_{\tau}$ are uniformly bounded $\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}\|r_{\tau}-\sigma_{\tau}r_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,H^{-1}(\Omega))}+\frac{1}{\tau}\|b_{\tau}-\sigma_{\tau}b_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}((0,T),\,H^{-1}(\Omega))}\leq C.$ (4.65) The first lemma is a direct consequence of the discrete in time entropy inequality ( 4.1 ), see [ 57 , 23 ] for details. The second results from using the estimates of the first one in the weak formulation ( 4.1 ). Now we apply a special version of the Aubins-Lions lemma specifically designed for piecewise constant interpolations, cf. [ 35 , Theorem 1] . Using the Gelfand triple $H^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow H^{-1}(\Omega),$ this theorem implies that ( 4.65 ) together with the boundedness of $\rho_{\tau}$ in $L^{2}((0,T);H^{1}(\Omega))$ is sufficient to obtain a strongly converging subsequence such that (without relabeling) $\displaystyle\rho_{\tau}\to\rho\text{ in }L^{2}((0,T);L^{2}(\Omega))\text{ as }\tau\to 0.$ This implies $\displaystyle\sqrt{1-\rho_{\tau}}\to\sqrt{1-\rho}\quad\text{in}\quad L^{4}((0,T),\,L^{4}(\Omega)).$ But since the space $L^{4}((0,T),\,L^{4}(\Omega))$ embeds continuously in $L^{2}((0,T),\,L^{2}(\Omega)),$ the function $\sqrt{1-\rho_{\tau}}$ must also converge strongly to $\sqrt{1-\rho}$ in $L^{2}((0,T),\,L^{2}(\Omega))$ . Note that ( 4.64 ) immediately implies $\displaystyle\tau u_{\tau},\,\tau v_{\tau}\to 0\text{ as }\tau\to 0\text{ strongly in }L^{2}((0,T);H^{1}(\Omega)),$ $\displaystyle\tau\nabla u_{\tau},\,\tau\nabla v_{\tau}\to 0\text{ as }\tau\to 0\text{ strongly in }L^{2}((0,T);[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{N}).$ Finally, since $r_{\tau},\,b_{\tau}$ are uniformly bounded in $L^{\infty}(\Omega)$ , we know that there exist subsequences such that $\displaystyle r_{\tau}\rightharpoonup^{*}r,\;b_{\tau}\rightharpoonup^{*}b\quad\text{ in }L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{\infty}(\Omega)).$ (4.66) In order to be able to pass to the limit in ( 4.1 ), we rewrite $\displaystyle(1-\rho_{\tau})\nabla r_{\tau}+r_{\tau}\nabla\rho_{\tau}=\sqrt{1-\rho_{\tau}}\nabla(\sqrt{1-\rho_{\tau}}r_{\tau})-3\sqrt{1-\rho_{\tau}}r_{\tau}\nabla\sqrt{1-\rho_{\tau}}$ The first term on the right hand side can be dealt with by further expanding the term $\nabla(\sqrt{1-\rho_{\tau}}r_{\tau})$ , the fact that $\sqrt{1-\rho_{\tau}}$ converges strongly, using the bounds ( 4.62 ) and the fact ( 4.66 ). For the second term, one has to prove strong convergence of $\sqrt{1-\rho_{\tau}}r_{\tau},$ which can be done either by using the Kolmogorov-Riesz theorem, see [ 20 ] or again a generalized Aubins-Lions lemma, see [ 64 ] . Note that this strong convergence also allows us to identify the corresponding, weakly converging gradient.

Let $T>0$ , $\varepsilon>0$ , $\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}$ and the assumptions on $V$ , $K$ and the constants $c_{11}$ and $c_{22}$ as in Theorem 2.5 . Then, the system ( 4.7 )–( 4.8 ),
with initial conditions $\displaystyle r(\cdot,\,0)=r_{0}\quad\text{and}\quad b(\cdot,\,0)=b_{0}$ with $(r_{0},b_{0})\in\mathcal{M}$ and the additional requirement that the second moment of both $r_{0}$ and $b_{0}$ are bounded. Then there exists a weak solution $(r,\,b)$ in $\displaystyle W=(L^{2}((0,T),\,L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))\cap H^{1}((0,\,T),\,H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})))^{2}$ such that $\displaystyle\rho,\,\sqrt{1-\rho}r,\,\sqrt{1-\rho}b\in L^{2}((0,\,T),\,H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})),$ and furthermore $0\leq r,\,b$ and $\rho\leq 1$ almost everywhere. Proof. The proof is based on the same a priori estimates as in the bounded domain case and thus we only sketch the additional arguments. Indeed, the main ingredient is the entropy inequality, ( 4.11 ) which, due to the uniform $L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}$ -bounds on $r$ and $b$ also holds on $\mathbb{R}^{N}$ . This in particular implies the second moment bound on $r$ and $b$ which in turn yields the compactness of the embedding from $H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})$ to $L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})$ . Indeed, taking a sequence $u_{n}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})$ we have, for some $u\in L^{2}$ and fixed $R>0$ that $\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(u-u_{n})^{2}\;dx=\int_{B_{R}(0)}(u-u_{n})^{2}\;dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}(0)}(u-u_{n})^{2}\;dx.$ Using the $L^{\infty}$ -bound on $u_{n}$ and $u$ , we can estimate the second term on the right hand side and obtain $\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(u-u_{n})^{2}\;dx\leq\int_{B_{R}(0)}(u-u_{n})^{2}\;dx+\frac{1}{R^{2}}\|u-u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}(0)}|x|^{2}(u-u_{n})\;dx.$ Taking the $\limsup_{n\to\infty}$ , we see that the first term on the right hand side vanishes due to the compactness of $H^{1}(B_{R}(0))\hookrightarrow L^{2}(B_{R}(0))$ for fixed $R$ . Taking, in a second step the limit $R\to\infty$ yields the assertion. Now we generate a sequence of approximate solutions $r_{n}$ and $b_{n}$ by solving the problem $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B_{n}(0)}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\partial_{t}r_{n}\\
\partial_{t}b_{n}\end{array}\right)\cdot\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\Phi_{1}\\
\Phi_{2}\\

∎ For $D=1$ , we have the following regularity result for $\rho$ which may serve as a first step in proving uniqueness of solutions via a fixed point argument in that case. Proposition 4.5 (Regularity of $\rho$ for $D=1$ ) . Assume that the diffusion coefficient $D$ is one, and let $(r,b)$ be a weak solution to ( 4.7 )–( 4.8 ). Then, on a bounded Lipschitz domain $\Omega$ , there are positive constants $C_{1},C_{2}$ such that the sum $r+b=\rho$ satisfies the following a priori estimate $\displaystyle\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{1,p}(\Omega))}\leq C_{1}+C_{2}\|\rho_{0}\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)},$ for all $2\leq p<\infty.$ Proof. For $D=1$ , we can add equations ( 4.7 ) and ( 4.8 ) to obtain an equation for $\rho$ given as $\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho-\varepsilon\Delta\rho=-\nabla\cdot h$ (4.77) with $\displaystyle h:=r(1-\rho)(c_{11}\nabla K\ast r-K\ast b)+b(1-\rho)(c_{22}\nabla K\ast b-K\ast r).$ and initial datum $\displaystyle\rho(\cdot,0)=\rho_{0}\text{ in }W^{1,p}(\Omega).$ Using the regularity of $r$ and $b$ as well as the properties of $K$ , we can estimate $h$ as follows $\displaystyle\|h(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}$ $\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla K\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\left[(c_{11}-1)\|r(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+(c_{22}-1)\|b(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right]=C$ Since the constant on the right hand side does not depend on $t$ we can take the supremum over all $t\in[0,T]$ and conclude that $h\in L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{\infty}(\Omega))$ . Thus applying the $L^{p}$ theory for linear parabolic equation, see e.g. [ 51 , Chapter VII] , yields the assertion.

On the right a cut through the axis $y=0$ is shown. 6 Outlook: Coarsening Dynamics In the following we further investigate the coarsening dynamics of the system ( 1.3 ), ( 1.4 ) as $\varepsilon\rightarrow 0$ . Let us first of all discuss the case $\varepsilon=0$ , which is characterized by a very large set of stationary solutions. Indeed, every pair $(\overline{r},\overline{b})$ with $\overline{r}(1-\overline{r}-\overline{b})=\overline{b}(1-\overline{r}-\overline{b})=0$ (6.1) is a stationary solution, but not necessarily stable under the dynamics. In the case for a single species, stability was characterized from an entropy condition in [ 21 ] , noticing that the model for $\varepsilon=0$ consists of nonlinear hyperbolic conservation laws with nonlocal terms. A generalization of the entropy condition to the system case seems out of reach, hence we revisit the single species case ( $b=0$ in our notation) and rederive the stability condition from an optimization argument. Intuitively a stationary state is stable if it is a local minimizer of the energy, and using a Lagrange multiplier $\lambda_{r}$ for the mass constraint we easily arrive at the conditions $\displaystyle c_{11}K*r(x)+\lambda_{r}\geq 0$ $\displaystyle\quad\text{if }r(x)=0$ $\displaystyle c_{11}K*r(x)+\lambda_{r}\leq 0$ $\displaystyle\quad\text{if }r(x)=1.$ Thus, at an interface between a region with $r=1$ inside and $r=0$ outside the normal derivative of $S=c_{11}K*r$ is nonpositive, which is exactly the entropy condition derived in [ 34 , 21 ] . In the system case we find by analogous arguments with Lagrange parameters $\lambda_{r}$ and $\lambda_{b}$ for the masses $\displaystyle c_{11}K*r(x)-K*b+\lambda_{r}\geq 0$ $\displaystyle\quad\text{if }r(x)=0,$ $\displaystyle c_{22}K*b(x)-K*r+\lambda_{b}\geq 0$ $\displaystyle\quad\text{if }b(x)=0,$ $\displaystyle c_{11}K*r(x)-K*b+\lambda_{r}\leq 0$ $\displaystyle\quad\text{if }\rho(x)=1,$ $\displaystyle c_{22}K*b(x)-K*r+\lambda_{b}\leq 0$ $\displaystyle\quad\text{if }\rho(x)=1.$ Hence we can effectively derive conditions on the sign of the normal derivatives of $\displaystyle S_{r}=c_{11}K*r-K*b\text{ and }S_{b}=c_{22}K*b-K*r$ on interfaces between regions with $r=1$ , $b=1$ , or $\rho=0$ (voids). On interfaces between red or blue and void the same analysis as in [ 21 ] applies, on interfaces between red and blue sign conditions on the derivatives of $S_{b}$ and $S_{r}$ need to hold simultaneously.
Such stable configurations will lead to similar stationary solutions or metastable dynamics in the case of small positive $\varepsilon$ . We finally mention that for the coarsening of interfaces in the metastable case analogous laws as in [ 34 , 21 ] can be derived by multiscale asymptotic expansions, whose details we leave to future research. In numerical experiments the coarsening dynamics is well observed, see Figure 8 , where one obtains local unmixing followed by coarsening. In the case of small self-attraction as shown in Figure 9 a different kind of coarsening dynamics is obtained, which is not characterized by $r$ and $b$ attaining values close to $1$ , but a mixed phase is coarsening versus the void regions. In this case a further analysis is quite open and an interesting issue for the future.
