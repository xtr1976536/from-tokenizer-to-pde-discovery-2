Abstract This paper investigates the spreading properties of globally defined bounded positive solutions of a chemotaxis system featuring a logistic source and consumption: $\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}u=\Delta u-\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)+u(a-bu),\quad&(t,x)\in{(0},\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
&{\tau\partial_{t}v}=\Delta v-uv,\quad&(t,x)\in{(0},\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N},\end{aligned}\right.$ where $u$ represents the population density of a biological species, and $v$ denotes the density of a chemical substance. We show that the presence of the chemical does not hinder the spreading of the species in general, and it does not accelerate the spreading speed under conditions that $v(0,\cdot)$ decays spatially or the parameters satisfy $0<-\chi\ll 1$ and $\tau=1$ . In the proof, we establish a novel connection between $u$ and $v$ using the sup- and inf-convolution techniques for viscosity solutions. Additionally, our numerical simulations reveal a noteworthy phase transition in $\chi$ : for $v(0,\cdot)$ uniformly distributed across space, the spreading speed accelerates only when $\chi$ surpasses a critical positive value. {\tau v_{t}}=\Delta v-uv,\quad&(t,x)\in{(0},\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N},\end{cases}$ (1.1) where $\chi$ is a constant that can be either positive or negative, and $a$ , $b$ , and $\tau$ are positive constants. In ( 1.1 ), $u(t,x)$ denotes the population density of some biological species and $v(t,x)$ denotes the population density of some chemical substance at time $t$ and space location $x$ ; $\chi$ is referred to as the chemotaxis sensitivity coefficient; the reaction term $u(a-bu)$ is referred to as a logistic source; and $\tau$ is linked to the diffusion rate of the chemical substance.
The chemotaxis sensitivity coefficient $\chi>0$ indicates that the biological species is being attracted by the chemical signal, and $\chi<0$ indicates that the biological species is repelled by the chemical signal. Chemotaxis is a phenomenon in which the movement of living organisms or cells is partially oriented along the gradient of certain chemicals. This phenomenon plays an important role in many biological processes, such as embryo formation and tumor development. The development of mathematical models for chemotaxis dates back to the pioneering works of Keller and Segel in the 1970s ( [ 22 , 23 , 24 ] ).
Since these pioneering works, a large amount of research has been carried out on various chemotaxis models. See, for example, [ 18 , 19 , 21 , 33 , 42 , 47 ] , etc. for the study of global existence and finite-time blow-up in the following minimal chemotaxis model on a bounded domain, $\begin{cases}u_{t}=\Delta u-\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v),\quad&x\in\Omega,\cr\tau v_{t}=\Delta u+g(u,v)-h(u,v)v,\quad&x\in\Omega,\cr\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}=\frac{\partial v}{\partial n}=0,\quad&x\in\partial\Omega,\end{cases}$ (1.2) where $\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N}$ is a smooth bounded domain. Before diving into a more detailed discussion of this problem and the history of past developments, let us briefly describe the main theoretical results of the paper for the benefit of the readers. Firstly, we show a lower bound for spreading speeds of solutions to ( 1.1 ) with general non-negative initial data $u_{0}$ in Theorem 1.1 . This lower bound equals the spreading speed when $\chi=0$ , that is, when chemotaxis is absent. Theorem 1.2 states that the chemical substance does not induce infinitely fast spreading of $u$ and $v$ . Finally, we prove that the upper bound of the spreading speed is the same as the lower bound, showing no acceleration due to chemotaxis, under certain conditions: when $v(0,\cdot)$ decays in space (see Theorem 1.3 (1)), and when $0<-\chi\ll 1$ and $\tau=1$ (see Theorem 1.3 (2)). Let us highlight the proof of Theorem 1.3 (2), where we uncover a novel connection between the population densities of the species $u$ and the chemical substance $v$ . Specifically, we introduce the variables $\zeta:=1-v$ and $w:=\zeta^{p}/u\qquad\text{ for some }p>1,$ and we show that, under the conditions of the theorem, $w$ is uniformly finite in both space and time after a short initial period.
For more details, we refer the readers to the discussion in Remark 1.3 (4) and Lemma 5.3 . Now, we discuss the existing literature on ( 1.2 ).
In [ 42 , 47 ] , ( 1.2 ) was considered with $\tau=1$ , $g(u,v)=0$ , $h(u,v)=u$ and this model is referred to as chemotaxis model with consumption. It is proved in [ 42 , Theorem 1.1] that this equation has a unique globally defined bounded classical solution with non-negative initial functions $(u_{0},v_{0})\in(W^{1,p}(\Omega))^{2}$ for some $p>N$ provided
that $0<\|v_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\cdot\chi<\frac{1}{6(N+1)}.$ (1.3) The authors in [ 47 ] proved that any globally defined bounded positive classical solutions of ( 1.2 ) converge to $(\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}u_{0},0)$ as $t\to\infty$ .
In [ 18 , 19 , 21 , 33 ] , ( 1.2 ) is studied with $g(u,v)=\mu u,$ and $h(u,v)=\lambda$ ( $\mu,\lambda>0).$ It is known that finite-time blow-up does not occur when $N=1$ and may occur when $N\geq 2$ (see [ 19 ] , [ 33 ] , etc.). We refer the reader to [ 20 , 29 , 34 , 35 , 44 , 46 , 48 ] , etc. for the study of the following chemotaxis model with a logistic source on a bounded domain, $\begin{cases}u_{t}=\Delta u-\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)+u(a-bu),\quad&x\in\Omega,\cr\tau v_{t}=\Delta u+g(u,v)-h(u,v)v,\quad&x\in\Omega,\cr\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}=\frac{\partial v}{\partial n}=0,\quad&x\in\partial\Omega.\end{cases}$ (1.4) Assume $\tau=1,$ $g(u,v)=0,$ and $h(u,v)=u$ . It is proved in [ 44 , Theorem 3.3] that
( 1.4 ) has a unique globally defined bounded classical solution with non-negative initial functions $(u_{0},v_{0})\in(W^{1,p}(\Omega))^{2}$ for some $p>N$ , provided
that
( 1.3 ) holds. It is also proved in [ 29 ] that this equation has a unique globally defined bounded classical solution with non-negative initial $(u_{0},v_{0})\in(C(\bar{\Omega})\times C^{1}(\bar{\Omega}))$ provided that $\chi\|v_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}$ is small relative to $b$ (see [ 29 , Theorem 1.1] ), and that any positive bounded globally defined classical solution converges to $(\frac{a}{b},0)$ as $t\to\infty$ (see [ 29 , Theorem 1.2] ).
Furthermore, if $\tau=1,g(u,v)=\mu u,$ and $h(u,v)=\lambda$ , it is known (see [ 20 , 46 ] ) that finite-time blow-up does not occur provided $b>{N|\chi|\mu}/{4}.$ The study of chemotaxis models on the whole space can provide some deep insight into the influence of chemotaxis on the evolution of biological species from some different angles. For instance, it can help determine whether chemotaxis would speed up or slow down the invasion of the biological species. Also, there have been numerous studies investigating chemotaxis models on the entire space. For instance, refer to [ 5 , 12 , 13 , 15 , 16 , 30 , 31 , 37 , 38 , 39 , 40 , 41 , 45 ] , etc. for investigations into the following chemotaxis model: $\begin{cases}u_{t}=\Delta u-\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)+u(a-bu),&x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N},\\
{\tau v_{t}}=\Delta v-\lambda v+\mu u,&x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\,\,v(0,x)=v_{0}(x)&x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}.\end{cases}$ (1.5) Observe that, in the absence of chemotaxis (i.e., $\chi=0$ ), the dynamic of ( 1.5 ) is governed by the following reaction-diffusion equation, $u_{t}=\Delta u+u(a-bu),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}.$ (1.6) The equation is also known as Fisher-KPP equation
due to the pioneering works of Fisher [ 8 ] and Kolmogorov, Petrowsky, Piskunov [ 27 ] on traveling wave solutions and take-over properties.
The spreading properties of ( 1.6 ) are well-established.
Equation ( 1.6 ) has traveling wave solutions $u(t,x)=\phi(x\cdot\xi-ct)$ ( $\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}$ )
connecting $\frac{a}{b}$ and $0$ $(\phi(-\infty)=\frac{a}{b},\phi(\infty)=0)$ for all speeds $c\geq 2\sqrt{a}$ and there is no such traveling wave solutions of slower speeds. For any given bounded $u_{0}\in C({\mathbb{R}}^{N},{\mathbb{R}}^{+})$ with
nonempty compact support, $\lim_{t\to\infty}\sup_{|x|\leq c^{\prime}t}\left|u(t,x)-\frac{a}{b}\right|=0\quad\forall\,c^{\prime}<2\sqrt{a},$ (1.7) and $\lim_{t\to\infty}\sup_{|x|\geq c^{\prime\prime}t}u(t,x)=0\quad\forall\,c^{\prime\prime}>2\sqrt{a}$ (1.8) (see [ 2 , 3 ] ).
Thanks to ( 1.7 ) and ( 1.8 ), in literature, the minimal wave speed $c^{*}:=2\sqrt{a}$ is also called the spreading speed of ( 1.6 ). Considering ( 1.5 ), it is an important question whether the chemotaxis speeds up or slows down the spreading of the biological species.
When $\tau=1$ , it is proved in [ 41 ] that if $b>\frac{N\chi\mu}{4},$ chemotaxis neither speed up nor slow down the spreading speed of the biological species in the sense that ( 1.7 ) and ( 1.8 ) hold for any solution $(u(t,x),v(t,x))$ of ( 1.5 ) with initial data $(u_{0}(\cdot),v_{0}(\cdot))$ whose supports are nonempty and compact. When $\tau=0$ , it is proved in [ 40 ] that if $b>\chi\mu$ and $\big(1+\frac{(\sqrt{a}-\sqrt{\lambda})_{+}}{2(\sqrt{a}+\sqrt{\lambda})}\big)\chi\mu\leq b,$ then again chemotaxis neither slows down nor speeds up the spreading speed. Assuming $\tau=0$ and $N=1$ , the authors of [ 12 ] proved that negative chemotaxis speeds up the spatial spreading of the biological species in the sense that the minimal wave speed of traveling waves of ( 1.5 ) must be greater than $c^{*}=2\sqrt{a}$ when $-\chi$ is sufficiently large. Very recently, the authors of the current paper studied the global existence of the chemotaxis models ( 1.1 ) and ( 1.5 ) in a unified way. Sufficient conditions are provided for positive classical solutions to exist globally and stay bounded (see [ 14 , Theorems 1.2-1.4] .
In this paper, we focus on the study of asymptotic behavior
of globally defined bounded solutions of ( 1.1 ), specially, spreading speeds of globally defined bounded positive solutions of ( 1.1 ). In the following, we introduce some notations and definitions in subsection 1.1, state our main theoretical results, and discuss novel ideas and techniques
in subsection 1.2, and present some numerical observations in subsection 1.3. 1.1 Notations and definitions In this subsection, we introduce some standing notations and the definition of spreading speeds.
Let us define $X:=C_{\rm unif}^{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})=\big\{u\in C({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\,|\,u(x)\,\,\text{is uniformly continuous in}\,\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\,\,{\rm and}\,\,\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|u(x)|<\infty\big\}$ equipped with the norm $\|u\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|u(x)|$ . We also set $X^{+}:=\{u\in X\,|\,u\geq 0\},$ and $X_{c}^{+}:=\big\{u\in X^{+}\,|\,{\rm supp}(u)\not=\emptyset,\,\,{\rm supp}(u)\,\,\text{is compact}\big\}.$ Next, we let $X_{1}:=C_{\rm unif}^{1,b}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})=\left\{u\in X\,\big|\,\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\in X\,\,{\rm for}\,\,i=1,2,\cdots,N\right\}$ equipped with the norm $\|u\|_{X_{1}}:=\|u\|_{\infty}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\|_{\infty}$ . We write $X_{1}^{+}:=X_{1}\cap X^{+}$ .
Finally, we use $C_{\rm unif}^{2+\alpha,b}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})$ to denote all $u\in X_{1}$ such that $\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}$ are uniformly bounded and $\alpha$ -Hölder continuous for $i,j=1,2,\ldots,N$ ,
and use the notation $C_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}):=\left\{u\in C({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\,\big|\,\lim_{|x|\to\infty}u(x)=0\right\}.$ For any given $(u_{0},v_{0})\in X^{+}\times X_{1}^{+}$ , we denote by $(u(t,x;u_{0},v_{0}),v(t,x;u_{0},v_{0}))$ the classical solution of ( 1.1 ) satisfying $\lim_{t\to 0+}(\|u(t,\cdot;u_{0},v_{0})-u_{0}(\cdot)\|_{X}+\|v(t,\cdot;u_{0},v_{0})-v_{0}(\cdot)\|_{X_{1}})=0,$ $u(\cdot,\cdot;u_{0},v_{0})\in C([0,T_{\max}),X)\cap C^{1}((0,T_{\max}),X),$ $v(\cdot,\cdot;u_{0},v_{0})\in C([0,T_{\max}),X_{1})\cap C^{1}((0,T_{\max}),X_{1}).$ Note that, by the comparison principle for parabolic equations, for every $(u_{0},v_{0})\in X^{+}\times X_{1}^{+}$ , it always holds that $u(t,x;u_{0},v_{0})\geq 0$ and $v(t,x;u_{0},v_{0})\geq 0$ for all $t>0$ in the existence interval of the solution. The objective of this paper is to study the asymptotic behavior of globally defined positive solutions. We introduce the following definitions.
For given $(u_{0},v_{0})\in X^{+}\times X_{1}^{+}$ , assuming that $(u(t,x;u_{0},v_{0}),v(t,x;u_{0},v_{0}))$ exists for all $t>0$ , let $S_{\rm low}(u_{0},v_{0}):=\{c\,|\,c>0,\,\,\liminf_{t\to\infty}\inf_{|x|\leq ct}u(t,x;u_{0},v_{0})>0\}$ and $S_{\rm up}(u_{0},v_{0}):=\{c\,|\,c>0,\,\,\limsup_{t\to\infty}\sup_{|x|\geq ct}u(t,x;u_{0},v_{0})=0\}.$ We define $c_{\rm low}^{*}(u_{0},v_{0}):=\sup\{c\,|\,c\in S_{\rm low}(u_{0},v_{0})\}$ and $c_{\rm up}^{*}(u_{0},v_{0}):=\inf\{c|\,c\in S_{\rm up}(u_{0},v_{0})\},$ where $c_{\rm low}^{*}(u_{0},v_{0})=0$ if $S_{\rm low}(u_{0},v_{0})=\emptyset$ and $c_{\rm up}^{*}(u_{0},v_{0})=\infty$ if $S_{\rm up}(u_{0},v_{0})=\emptyset$ .
By the definition of $c_{\rm low}^{*}(u_{0},v_{0})$ and $c_{\rm up}^{*}(u_{0},v_{0})$ , if $c_{\rm low}^{*}(u_{0},v_{0})>0$ , then $\liminf_{t\to\infty}\inf_{|x|\leq{c^{\prime}}t}u(t,x;u_{0},v_{0})>0\quad\forall\,0<{c^{\prime}}<c^{*}_{\rm low}(u_{0},v_{0}),$ and if $c_{\rm up}^{*}(u_{0},v_{0})<\infty$ , then $\lim_{t\to\infty}\sup_{|x|\geq{c^{\prime\prime}}t}u(t,x;u_{0},v_{0})=0\quad\forall\,{c^{\prime\prime}}>c^{*}_{\rm up}(u_{0},v_{0}).$ If $c_{\rm low}^{*}(u_{0},v_{0})=c_{\rm up}^{*}(u_{0},v_{0})$ , then $c^{*}(u_{0},v_{0}):=c_{\rm low}^{*}(u_{0},v_{0})$ is called
the spreading speed of
the solution $(u(t,x;u_{0},v_{0}),v(t,x;u_{0},v_{0}))$ .
It is well known that when $\chi=0$ , we have $c_{\rm low}^{*}(u_{0},v_{0})=c_{\rm up}^{*}(u_{0},v_{0})=2\sqrt{a}$ for any $u_{0}\in X_{c}^{+}$ and $v_{0}\in X_{1}^{+}$ (see [ 2 , 3 ] ). Many interesting questions arise when $\chi\not=0$ .
For example, whether $c_{\rm low}^{*}(u_{0},v_{0})$ is positive and $c_{\rm up}^{*}(u_{0},v_{0})$ is finite;
whether $c_{\rm low}^{*}(u_{0},v_{0})\geq 2\sqrt{a}$ , in other words, whether the chemotaxis does not
slow down the population’s spreading; whether $c_{\rm up}^{*}(u_{0},v_{0})\leq 2\sqrt{a}$ , that is, whether
the chemotaxis does not speed up the population’s spreading; whether $c_{\rm low}^{*}(u_{0},v_{0})=c_{\rm up}^{*}(u_{0},v_{0})$ , that is, the population has a single spreading speed; in the case $c_{\rm low}^{*}(u_{0},v_{0})>0$ , whether $u(t,x;u_{0},v_{0})$ converges to $\frac{a}{b}$ in the region $|x|<ct$ for any $0<c<c_{\rm low}^{*}(u_{0},v_{0})$ , which is strongly related to the stability of the constant equilibrium $(\frac{a}{b},0)$ ; and how $\chi$ affects the spreading properties; etc.
The goal of this paper is to answer some of these questions. 1.2 Main results, biological interpretations, and techniques In this subsection, we state our main results on
the asymptotic behavior of globally defined positive solutions, give some biological interpretations of the results, and highlight the techniques employed/developed in the proofs of the results. Our first main result provides a lower bound of the spatial spreading speeds of solutions to ( 1.1 ) with general non-negative initial data $u_{0}$ . Theorem 1.1 (Lower bound of spreading speeds) . Suppose that $u_{0}\in X^{+}$ and $v_{0}\in X^{+}_{1}$ , and $(u(t,x;u_{0},v_{0}),v(t,x;u_{0},v_{0}))$ is a globally defined bounded solution of ( 1.1 ).
If ${\rm supp}(u_{0})={\rm cl}\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\,|\,u_{0}(x)>0\}$ is nonempty, then the following hold. (1) $c_{\rm low}^{*}(u_{0},v_{0})\geq 2\sqrt{a}$ , or equivalently, $\liminf_{t\to\infty}\inf_{|x|\leq{c^{\prime}}t}u(t,x;u_{0},v_{0})>0\quad\forall\,0<{c^{\prime}}<2\sqrt{a}.$ (1.9) (2) $\lim_{t\to\infty}\sup_{|x|\leq{c^{\prime}}t}\left|u(t,x;u_{0},v_{0})-\frac{a}{b}\right|=0\quad\forall\,0<{c^{\prime}}<c^{*}_{\rm low}(u_{0},v_{0}),$ (1.10) and $\lim_{t\to\infty}\sup_{|x|\leq{c^{\prime}}t}v(t,x;u_{0},v_{0})=0\quad\forall\,0<{c^{\prime}}<c^{*}_{\rm low}(u_{0},v_{0}).$ (1.11) Remark 1.1 . (1) As it is mentioned in the above, when $\chi=0$ , $c_{\rm low}^{*}(u_{0},v_{0})=2\sqrt{a}$ for any $u_{0}\in X_{c}^{+}$ and $v_{0}\in X_{1}^{+}$ (in this case, $u(t,x;u_{0},v_{0})$ is independent of $v_{0}$ ).
Note that, when $\chi<0$ , the chemical substance is a chemorepellent, and when $\chi>0$ , the chemical substance is a chemoattractant. Theorem 1.1 reveals an important biological observation: chemical
substance does not slow down the propagation of the biological species with nonzero initial distribution even when the chemical substance is a chemorepellent. (2) ( 1.9 ) is proved by nontrivially modified arguments of [ 41 , Theorem 1.2(1)] .
The key idea is to show that, for any given $0<c^{\prime}<2\sqrt{a}$ , $u(t,x+c\xi t;u_{0},v_{0})$ is bounded away from zero uniformly in $0<c<c^{\prime}$ , $\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}$ , $|x|\leq l$ for some $l>0$ , and $t\gg 1$ (see Lemmas 3.1 , 3.2 , and 3.3 ).
In the proof, we make use of special Harnack inequalities for bounded solutions of ( 1.1 ) established in Lemmas 2.3 and 2.4 . Specifically, the local-in-time Harnack inequality, as stated in Lemma 2.3 , is a slight generalization of [ 4 , Theorem 1.2] and [ 13 , Lemma 2.2] , which address advection Fisher-KPP equations.
Additionally, our approach employs the principal eigenvalue and eigenfunction of some linearized operator for $u$ , and the comparison principal for parabolic equations. The following theorem is on upper bound of the spatial spreading speeds of solutions to ( 1.1 ) with compactly supported $u_{0}$ . Theorem 1.2 (Upper bound of spreading speeds) . Suppose that ${u_{0}\in X_{c}^{+}}$ and ${v_{0}\in X^{+}_{1}}$ , and $(u(t,x;u_{0},v_{0}),v(t,x;u_{0},v_{0}))$ is a globally defined bounded solution of ( 1.1 ). Then we have (1) $c_{\rm up}^{*}(u_{0},v_{0})<\infty$ . Moreover, for any $c^{\prime\prime}>c_{\rm up}^{*}(u_{0},v_{0})$ , there is $M>0$ such that $u(t,x;u_{0},v_{0})\leq Me^{-\sqrt{a}(|x|-c^{\prime\prime}t)}\quad\forall\,t>0,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}.$ (1.12) (2) There exist $C,\gamma>0$ depending on $c{{}^{\prime\prime}}-c^{*}_{up}(u_{0},v_{0})$ such that $\sup_{|x|\geq{c^{\prime\prime}}t}|v(t,x;u_{0},v_{0})-V(t,x;v_{0})|\leq Ce^{-\gamma t}.\quad\forall\,t>0,\,\,{c^{\prime\prime}}>c^{*}_{\rm up}(u_{0},v_{0}),$ where $V(t,x):=V(t,x;v_{0})$ is the solution of $\begin{cases}\tau V_{t}=\Delta V,\quad&x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N},\,t>0\cr V(0,x)=v_{0}(x),\quad&x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}.\end{cases}$ (1.13) Remark 1.2 . (1) Theorem 1.2 implies that the chemical substance does not drive the biological species spreads infinitely fast. (2) Theorem 1.2 (1) is proved by the application of special Harnack inequalities for bounded solutions of ( 1.1 ) established in Lemmas 2.3 and 2.4 . The exponential decay property ( 1.12 ) for $u(t,x;u_{0},v_{0})$ and the representation of $v(t,x;u_{0},v_{0})$ via the Duhamel’s principle are the key ingredients in the proof of Theorem 1.2 (2). We point out that the methods developed in the proofs of Theorems 1.1 and 1.2 can be applied to the study of the asymptotic dynamics of the following modified chemotaxis model for $\sigma>0$ (( 1.1 ) corresponds to the case when $\sigma=1$ ), $\begin{cases}u_{t}=\Delta u-\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)+u(a-bu^{\sigma}),\quad&(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N},\\
\tau v_{t}=\Delta v-uv,\quad&(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}.\end{cases}$ (1.14) Theorem 1.1 and Theorem 1.2 with $\frac{a}{b}$ being replaced by $(\frac{a}{b})^{{1}/{\sigma}}$ hold for globally defined bounded positive classical solutions of ( 1.14 ). Our last two theorems discuss various sufficient conditions for
the existence of spreading speed of globally defined bounded solutions of ( 1.1 )
and ( 1.14 ). Theorem 1.3 (Existence of spreading speeds) . Suppose that $u_{0}\in X^{+}_{c}$ and $v_{0}\in X^{+}_{1}$ , and $(u(t,x;u_{0},v_{0}),v(t,x;u_{0},v_{0}))$ is a globally defined bounded solution of ( 1.1 ). (1) If $v_{0}\in C_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})$ or $v_{0}\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})$ for some $p\geq 1$ , then $c^{*}_{\rm low}(u_{0},v_{0})=c^{*}_{\rm up}(u_{0},v_{0})=2\sqrt{a}.$ (2) Further assume $\tau=1$ , and that $u_{0}\in X^{+}_{1}$ , $v_{0}\in C_{\rm unif}^{2+\alpha,b}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})$ for some $\alpha>0$ , and $1-v_{0}\in X_{c}^{+}$ . Then there exists $\chi_{0}>0$ such that
for any $-\chi_{0}<\chi<0$ , we have $c^{*}_{\rm low}(u_{0},v_{0})=c^{*}_{\rm up}(u_{0},v_{0})=2\sqrt{a}.$ Theorem 1.4 . Suppose that $(u(t,x;u_{0},v_{0}),v(t,x;u_{0},v_{0}))$ is a globally defined bounded solution
of ( 1.14 ). Assume that $\tau=1$ , $\sigma\in(0,1)$ , $1-v_{0}$ , $u_{0}\in X_{c}^{+}\cap X_{1}$ , and $v_{0}\in C_{\rm unif}^{2+\alpha,b}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})$ for some $\alpha>0$ . Then there exists $\chi_{0}>0$ such that if $|\chi|<\chi_{0}$ , the conclusion of Theorem 1.3 (2) holds the same. Remark 1.3 . (1) Note that the biological species gradually consumes the chemical substance over time. Consequently, if initially, the chemical substance does not occupy the entire space to a certain extent, as indicated by conditions such as $v_{0}\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})$ for some $p\geq 1$ or $v_{0}\in C_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})$ , it intuitively follows that the spreading of the species should be unaffected. This intuition aligns with the findings of Theorem 1.3 (1). (2) Recall that when $\chi<0$ (resp. $\chi>0$ ), the chemical substance acts as a chemorepellent (resp. chemoattractant). If $v_{0}$ is not negligible as $|x|\to\infty$ , it is reasonable to expect that negative chemotaxis wouldn’t enhance the spread of the biological species, while a chemoattractant might accelerate it. This expectation is partly supported by our Theorem 1.3 (2) when $\chi<0$ but $|\chi|\ll 1$ . However, the situation regarding chemoattractants is more complicated. Indeed, as confirmed by numerical simulations (refer to Section 6 ), a phase transition emerges: there exists a critical threshold, denoted as $\chi^{*}>0$ , such that the spread accelerates when $\chi>\chi^{*}$ , while it maintains a constant speed of $2\sqrt{a}$ when $\chi<\chi^{*}$ . Although Theorem 1.4 provides another evidence, the problem remains open. (3) The additional smooth assumption $u_{0}\in X^{+}_{1}$ and $v_{0}\in C_{\rm unif}^{2+\alpha,b}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})$ guarantees the boundedness of $\|\nabla u(t,\cdot;u_{0},v_{0})\|_{\infty}$ and $\|\Delta v(t,\cdot;u_{0},v_{0})\|_{\infty}$ up to $t=0$ , which is a technical assumption used in our proof. (4) To establish Theorem 1.3 (2) and Theorem 1.4 , we explore a novel connection between the population densities of the species $u$ and the chemical substance $v$ . Specifically, we introduce the variables $\zeta:=1-v$ and $w:=\zeta^{p}/u\qquad\text{ for some }p>1,$ and $w$ turns out to satisfy a favorable parabolic equation, see the proof of Lemma 5.3 .
Leveraging the smoothing effect inherent in the parabolic equation, we are able to show $\zeta^{p}\lesssim u$ which leads to the estimates of $\zeta$ and $\nabla\zeta$ in terms of $u^{{1}/{p}}$ .
However, a significant difficulty arises in this approach: since $u_{0}$ and $\zeta_{0}:=1-v_{0}$ are known to have compact support only, $w$ is not well-defined at time $0$ . To circumvent this obstacle, we prove that $\zeta^{p}\lesssim u$ at some small positive times by using the comparison principle and employing the inf- and sup- convolution technique for viscosity solutions.
Having $w$ be finite over at least a small positive time interval, we propagate this property to all subsequent times through the parabolic equation satisfied by $w$ . Linking the two density variables $u$ and $v$ is essential in the analysis of spreading properties. 1.3 Numerical simulations and biological indications As mentioned before, numerical experiments indicate a phase transition, that is there exists a positive critical value of $\chi$ , beyond which the spreading speed accelerates, while it remains to be $2\sqrt{a}$ when $\chi<\chi^{*}$ . ∎ 2.3 Viscosity solutions In this subsection, we briefly recall viscosity solutions. We refer readers to [ 6 ] for more details. This notion of solutions as well as the comparison principle will be one of the main tools we use in Section 5.2 . Consider the following parabolic type equation: $u_{t}+F(t,x,u,\nabla u,D^{2}u)=0.$ (2.11) Let $\mathcal{S}^{N}$ denote the set of $N\times N$ symmetric matrices with the spectral norm. We say that $F$ is uniformly elliptic, if there exists $\Lambda>0$ such that for any positive semi-definite matrix $P\in\mathcal{S}^{N}$ , and any $(t,x,u,p,X)\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times\mathcal{S}^{N}$ , $\Lambda\text{ \rm Tr}(P)\leq F(t,x,u,p,X)-F(t,x,u,p,X+P).$ We assume $F$ to be continuous and uniformly elliptic. Now we recall the definition of viscosity solutions. Let $\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{N}$ be open and $T>0$ . (i) We say that an upper semicontinuous (resp. lower semicontinuous) function $u:(0,T)\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}$ is a (viscosity) subsolution (resp. (viscosity) supersolution) to ( 2.11 ) if the following holds:
for any smooth function $\phi$ in $\Omega$ such that $u-\phi$ has a local maximum (resp. minimum) at $(t_{0},x_{0})\in(0,T)\times\Omega$ , we have $\partial_{t}u(t_{0},x_{0})+{F}(t_{0},x_{0},u(t_{0},x_{0}),\nabla\phi(t_{0},x_{0}),D^{2}\phi(t_{0},x_{0}))\leq 0$ $\left(\text{resp. }\partial_{t}u(t_{0},x_{0})+{F}(t_{0},x_{0},u(t_{0},x_{0}),\nabla\phi(t_{0},x_{0}),D^{2}\phi(t_{0},x_{0}))\geq 0\right).$ (ii) We say that a continuous function $u:(0,T)\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}$ is a (viscosity) solution to ( 2.11 ) if it is both a subsolution and a supersolution. It is easy to see that a classical solution is a viscosity solution. For the purpose of the paper, we take $F(t,x,u,\nabla u,D^{2}u)=-\Delta u+f(t,x)\cdot\nabla u+g(t,x,u)$ (2.12) where $f,g$ are uniformly continuous and bounded functions. It is easy to check that the operator satisfies the condition (3.14) in [ 6 ] .
Consequently, we have the following comparison principle. Lemma 2.5 . Let $T>0$ and let $F$ be given in ( 2.12 ). Let $u^{+}:(0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\to{\mathbb{R}}$ and $u^{-}:(0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\to{\mathbb{R}}$ be, respectively, a supersolution and a subsolution to ( 2.11 ). If $u^{+}(0,\cdot)\geq u^{-}(0,\cdot)$ and $\inf_{t\in(0,T)}\liminf_{|x|\to\infty}(u^{+}(t,x)-u^{-}(t,x))\geq 0$ , then $u^{+}(t,x)\geq u^{-}(t,x)\quad\text{ for all }(t,x)\in(0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}.$ We refer readers to Sections 5D and 8 [ 6 ] for the proof and for more general cases. 2.4 Convergence to the constant equilibrium In this subsection, we prove the convergence of globally defined bounded solutions with strictly positive initial data to the constant solution $(\frac{a}{b},0)$ . The result implies that there are no other positive stationary solutions $(u(x),v(x))$ of ( 1.1 ) with $\inf_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}u(x)>0$ rather than $(\frac{a}{b},0)$ . Proposition 2.6 (Convergence to constant equilibrium) . Suppose that $u_{0}\in X^{+}$ and $v_{0}\in X^{+}_{1}$ , and $(u(t,x;u_{0},v_{0}),v(t,x;u_{0},v_{0}))$ is a globally defined bounded solution ( 1.1 ).
If $\inf_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}u_{0}(x)>0$ , then $\lim_{t\to\infty}u(t,x;u_{0},v_{0})=\frac{a}{b},\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}v(t,x;u_{0},v_{0})=0\quad\text{uniformly in}\,\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}.$ Proof. For any fixed $\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{a}{|\chi|})$ , by Lemma 2.4 , there are $T=T(\varepsilon,{\|v_{0}\|_{\infty}})\geq 1$ and $C=C(\varepsilon,{\chi})>0$ such that for any $t\geq T,$ we have $u_{t}\geq\Delta u-\chi\nabla u\cdot\nabla v-|\chi|u(\varepsilon+Cu^{1/2})+au-bu^{2}.$ (2.13) We first claim that $\delta_{T}:=\inf_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}u(T,x;u_{0},v_{0})>0.$ It follows from [ 14 , Proposition 2.1] that $u(t,\cdot;u_{0},v_{0})$ is uniformly continuous in $L^{\infty}$ -norm as $t\to 0$ .
Since $\inf_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}u_{0}(x)>0$ , there is $0<t_{1}<T$ such that $\delta_{1}:=\inf_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}u(t_{1},x;u_{0},v_{0})>0.$ It follows from Lemma 2.2 that ${M}:=\max\left\{\sup_{t\in[t_{1},T],x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\Delta v(t,x;u_{0},v_{0})|,\sup_{t_{1}\leq t\leq T,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}u(t,x;u_{0},v_{0})\right\}<\infty.$ We then have $\begin{cases}u_{t}\geq\Delta u-\chi\nabla v\cdot\nabla u-|\chi|{M}u+au-b{M}u,\quad t_{1}<t<T,\,\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\cr u(t_{1},x)\geq\delta_{1},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}.\end{cases}$ (2.14) Note that $u$ is a classical solution, and $|\nabla u|$ and $|\nabla v|$ are uniformly bounded and continuous on $[t_{1},T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}$ . Thus, by viewing $\nabla v(t,x;u_{0},v_{0})$ as a given function, the comparison principle for parabolic equations (see e.g., [ 36 , Proposition 52.10] ) yields $u(t,x;u_{0},v_{0})\geq e^{(-|\chi|{M}+a-b{M})(t-t_{1})}\delta_{1}\quad\forall\,t_{1}\leq t\leq T,\,\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}.$ This implies that $\delta_{T}\geq e^{(-|\chi|{M}+a-b{M})(T-t_{1})}\delta_{1}>0$ . The claim then follows. Next, let $\underline{u}(t)$ be the solution to the ODE $\begin{cases}\underline{u}_{t}=-|\chi|\underline{u}(\varepsilon+C\underline{u}^{1/2})+\underline{u}(a-b\underline{u})\cr\underline{u}(T)=\delta_{T}.\end{cases}$ (2.15) Note that $-|\chi|\varepsilon+a>0.$ Hence $\underline{u}=0$ is an unstable solution of ( 2.15 ), and there exists $\varepsilon_{0}>0$ such that $\underline{u}(t;\delta_{T})\geq\varepsilon_{0},\qquad\forall\,t>T.$ Then, by the comparison principle, we have $u(t,x;u_{0},v_{0})\geq\underline{u}(t;\delta_{T})\geq\varepsilon_{0},\quad\forall\,t\geq T.$ (2.16) This shows that $\tau v_{t}\leq\Delta v-\varepsilon_{0}v$ for all $t\geq T$ .
Thus, by comparing $v$ with $\bar{v}(t)$ which solves the ODE $\tau\bar{v}_{t}=-\varepsilon_{0}\bar{v}$ with $\bar{v}(T)=\|v(T,\cdot;u_{0},v_{0})\|_{\infty}\leq\|v_{0}\|_{\infty}$ ,
we obtain ${0\leq v(t,x)\leq\bar{v}(t)\leq\|v_{0}\|_{\infty}e^{-\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{\tau}(t-T)},\qquad\forall t\geq T.}$ (2.17) In particular, we proved $\lim_{t\to\infty}v(t,x;u_{0},v_{0})=0\quad\text{uniformly in}\,\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}.$ (2.18) Now, we prove that $\lim_{t\to\infty}u(t,x;u_{0},v_{0})=a/b\quad\text{uniformly in}\,\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}.$ (2.19) Suppose for contradiction that
there exist $\delta_{0}>0$ , a sequence $t_{n}\to\infty$ , and $x_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}$ such that $|u(t_{n},x_{n};u_{0},v_{0})-a/b|>\delta_{0}.$ (2.20) Define $u_{n}(t,x):=u(t+t_{n},x+x_{n};u_{0},v_{0})\quad\text{and}\quad v_{n}(t,x)=v(t+t_{n},x+x_{n};u_{0},v_{0}),\quad t\geq-t_{n}.$ By ( 2.18 ), $\lim_{n\to\infty}v_{n}(t,x)=0\quad\text{locally uniformly in}\,t\in{\mathbb{R}},\,\text{and uniformly in}\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}.$ (2.21) By Lemma 2.2 , for any bounded subset $I\subset{\mathbb{R}}$ , there is $n_{0}\geq 1$ such that $\{w_{n}(t,x)\}_{n\geq n_{0}}$ is uniformly bounded and equi-continuous on $I\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}$ , where $w_{n}(t,x)=u(t+t_{n},x+x_{n})$ , $\partial_{x_{i}}u(t+t_{n},x+x_{n};u_{0},v_{0})$ , $\partial_{t}u(t+t_{n},x+x_{n};u_{0},v_{0})$ , $\partial^{2}_{x_{i}x_{j}}u(t+t_{n},x+x_{n};u_{0},v_{0})$ , $v(t+t_{n},x+x_{n};u_{0},v_{0})$ , $\partial_{x_{i}}v(t+t_{n},x+x_{n};u_{0},v_{0})$ , $\partial_{t}v(t+t_{n},x+x_{n}$ ; $u_{0},v_{0})$ or $\partial^{2}_{x_{i}x_{j}}v(t+t_{n},x+x_{n};u_{0},v_{0})$ for $1\leq i,j\leq N$ . Therefore, $\tilde{u}$ satisfies $\tilde{u}_{t}=\Delta\tilde{u}+a\tilde{u}-b\tilde{u}^{2},\qquad t\in{\mathbb{R}},\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}.$ By ( 2.16 ) and the global boundedness of $(u(t,x;u_{0},v_{0}),v(t,x;u_{0},v_{0}))$ , there exists $K>\varepsilon_{0}$ such that $0<\varepsilon_{0}\leq\tilde{u}(t,x)\leq K,\quad\forall\,t\in{\mathbb{R}},\,\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}.$ Set $\underline{u}_{0}:=\inf_{(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}}\tilde{u}$ and $\overline{u}_{0}:=\sup_{(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}}\tilde{u}.$ For every $t_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}},$ let $\underline{u}(\cdot;t_{0})$ and $\overline{u}(\cdot;t_{0})$ be the solutions of $\begin{cases}\frac{d}{dt}\overline{u}=\overline{u}(a-b\overline{u}),\ \ t>t_{0}\\
\overline{u}(t_{0};t_{0})=\overline{u}_{0}\end{cases}$ and $\begin{cases}\frac{d}{dt}\underline{u}=\underline{u}(a-b\underline{u}),\ \ t>t_{0}\\
\underline{u}(t_{0};t_{0})=\underline{u}_{0},\end{cases}$ respectively. Then for every $t_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}$ , $\lim_{t\to\infty}\overline{u}(t;t_{0})=\lim_{t\to\infty}\underline{u}(t;t_{0})=\frac{a}{b}.$ (2.23) Since $0<\underline{u}_{0}\leq\tilde{u}(t,x)\leq\overline{u}_{0}$ for every $(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1},$ we obtain that $\underline{u}(t-t_{0};0)=\underline{u}(t;t_{0})\leq\tilde{u}(t,x)\leq\overline{u}(t;t_{0})=\overline{u}(t-t_{0};0)\ \ \forall\ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N},\ t\geq t_{0}.$ Taking limit as $t_{0}\to-\infty$ on both sides and using ( 2.23 ) imply $\tilde{u}(t,x)\equiv a/b,$ which contradicts with ( 2.20 ).
Therefore, ( 2.19 ) holds, and
Proposition 2.6 is proved.

By ( A.2 ), ( A.3 ), and ( A.4 ), for $\beta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})$ and any $\delta\in(0,1)$ , there is $C=C(\beta,\delta)>0$ such that $\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{\beta}$ $\displaystyle\leq\|T(t)u_{0}\|_{\beta}+|\chi|\int_{0}^{t}\left\|T(t-s)\nabla\cdot(u(s)\nabla v(s))\right\|_{\beta}ds+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|T(t-s)u(s)(1+a-bu(s))\right\|_{\beta}ds$ $\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-\beta}\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}+|\chi|C\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{t-s}{2}\right)^{-\beta}e^{-(1-\delta)(t-s)}\left\|T\left(\frac{t-s}{2}\right)\nabla\cdot(u(s)\nabla v(s))\right\|_{\infty}ds$ $\displaystyle\qquad+C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\beta}e^{-(1-\delta)(t-s)}\|u(s)(1+a-bu(s))\|_{\infty}ds$ $\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-\beta}\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}+|\chi|C\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{t-s}{2}\right)^{-\beta-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-(1-\delta)(t-s)}\|(u(s)\nabla v(s))ds\|_{\infty}$ $\displaystyle\qquad+C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\beta}e^{-(1-\delta)(t-s)}\|u(s)(1+a-bu(s))\|_{\infty}ds$ $\displaystyle\leq C_{t_{0}}\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}+|\chi|CMM_{1}+CM(1+a+bM),$ where the last inequality holds for $t\geq t_{0}>0$ . So for these $t$ , $u(t)$ is uniformly bounded in $X^{\beta}$ . Then, the uniform Hölder continuity of $u(t,\cdot)$ for each $t\geq t_{0}$ follows from the following continuous embeddings (see [ 17 , Exercise 9] ) $\displaystyle X^{\beta}\subset C_{\rm unif}^{\lfloor\alpha\rfloor,\alpha-\lfloor\alpha\rfloor,b}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\quad\text{if}\quad 0\leq\alpha<2\beta.$ (A.5) Now, for any fixed $0<\beta<\frac{1}{2}$ , choose $\tilde{\beta}\in(\beta,\frac{1}{2})$ . Recall $I_{i}$ with $i=0,1,2$ from ( A.2 ).
Then, by [ 17 , Theorem 1.4.3] , ( A.3 ) and ( A.4 ), there exist $C=C(\beta,\delta)$ such that for any $t\geq t_{0}$ and $h>0$ , we have $\displaystyle\|I_{0}(t+h)-I_{0}(t)\|_{\beta}=\|\big(T(h)-I\big)T(t)u_{0}\|_{\beta}\leq Ch^{\tilde{\beta}-\beta}\|T(t)u_{0}\|_{\tilde{\beta}}\leq Ch^{\tilde{\beta}-\beta}t^{-\tilde{\beta}}\|u_{0}\|_{\infty},$ $\displaystyle\quad\,\|I_{1}(t+h)-I_{1}(t)\|_{\beta}$ $\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|\big(T(h)-I\big)T(t-s)\nabla\cdot(u(s)\nabla v(s))\|_{\beta}ds+\int_{t}^{t+h}\|T(t+h-s)\nabla\cdot(u(s)\nabla v(s))\|_{\beta}ds$ $\displaystyle\leq Ch^{\tilde{\beta}-\beta}\int_{0}^{t}\|T(t-s)\nabla\cdot(u(s)\nabla v(s))\|_{\tilde{\beta}}ds+C\int_{t}^{t+h}(t+h-s)^{-\beta-\frac{1}{2}}\|u(s)\|_{\infty}\|\nabla v(s)\|_{\infty}ds$ $\displaystyle\leq Ch^{\tilde{\beta}-\beta}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\tilde{\beta}-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-(1-\delta)(t-s)}\|u(s)\|_{\infty}\|\nabla v(s)\|_{\infty}ds+CMM_{1}h^{\frac{1}{2}-\beta}$ $\displaystyle\leq CMM_{1}h^{\tilde{\beta}-\beta}+CMM_{1}h^{\frac{1}{2}-\beta},$ and $\displaystyle\|I_{2}(t+h)-I_{2}(t)\|_{\beta}$ $\displaystyle\leq Ch^{\beta}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(1-\delta)(t-s)}(t-s)^{-\beta}\|u(s)\|_{\infty}\left[1+a+\|u(s)\|_{\infty}\right]ds$ $\displaystyle\quad+C\int_{t}^{t+h}(t+h-s)^{-\beta}\|u(s)\|_{\infty}\left[1+a+\|u(s)\|_{\infty}\right]ds$ $\displaystyle\leq CM(1+a+M)(h^{\beta}+h^{1-\beta}).$ Hence, the mapping $[t_{0},\infty)\ni t\mapsto u(t)\in X^{\beta}$ is uniformly Hölder continuous.
This, together with ( A.5 ), implies that $u$ is Hölder continous in both space and time. Furthermore, the Hölder norm is uniformly finite for all $t\geq t_{0}$ . Now, since $u$ is Hölder continuous with uniform bound for $t\geq t_{0}$ , we can apply the classical parabolic estimates (see Theorems 5 and 10 in Chapter 3 of [ 10 ] ) and use the equation of $v$ to get that ( 2.1 ) holds
for $w=v(t,x)$ , $\partial_{x_{i}}v(t,x)$ , $\partial_{t}v(t,x)$ or $\partial^{2}_{x_{i}x_{j}}v(t,x)$ for $1\leq i,j\leq N$ .
Then applying the classical parabolic estimates and using the equation of $u$ , we have that ( 2.1 ) holds for $w=u(t,x)$ , $\partial_{x_{i}}u(t,x)$ , $\partial_{t}u(t,x)$ , $\partial^{2}_{x_{i}x_{j}}u(t,x)$ . Finally, assume that $v_{0}$ and $u_{0}$ are, respectively, uniformly bounded in $C_{\rm unif}^{2+\alpha,b}$ and $X_{1}$ . Then $\|T(t)u_{0}\|_{\beta}\leq C_{\beta}$ uniformly for all $t\geq 0$ when $\beta<\frac{1}{2}$ . Then the above argument yields that $u(t,x)$ is Hölder continuous uniformly for all $t\in[0,\infty)$ and $x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}$ . The boundary Schauder estimates (see e.g., [ 28 ] and [ 43 , Theorem 3.2] ) then yield that ( 2.1 ) holds
for $w=v(t,x)$ , $\partial_{x_{i}}v(t,x)$ , $\partial_{t}v(t,x)$ or $\partial^{2}_{x_{i}x_{j}}v(t,x)$ for $1\leq i,j\leq N$ .
∎ Proof of Lemma 2.3 . By Lemma 2.2 , we have $A=A(\chi,\|v_{0}\|_{X_{1}},\|u\|_{\infty}):=\sup_{t>1}\left(\|\nabla v(t,\cdot)\|_{\infty}+\|\Delta v(t,\cdot)\|_{\infty}\right)<\infty.$ We first prove ( 2.2 ). To this end, for given $t_{0}\geq 1$ , let $t_{1}:=t_{0}-\frac{1}{2}$ and let $\bar{u}(t,x)$ be the solution of the equation $\begin{cases}\bar{u}_{t}=\Delta\bar{u}-\chi\nabla v(t,x)\cdot\nabla\bar{u},\qquad&(t,x)\in(t_{1},\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N},\\
\bar{u}(t_{1},\cdot)=u(t_{1},\cdot),&x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}.\end{cases}$ It follows from the comparison principle for parabolic equations that $0\leq\bar{u}(t,x)\leq\|u(t_{1},\cdot)\|_{\infty}\leq\|u\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{s\geq 0}\|u(s,\cdot)\|_{\infty}\quad\forall\,(t,x)\in(t_{1},\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}.$ Let $\psi(t,x)$ and $\phi(t,x)$ be defined by $\psi(t,x)=\min\big\{1,({a}/{b})^{1/\sigma}/\|u\|_{\infty}\big\}\,e^{-|\chi|A(t-t_{1})}\,\bar{u}(t,x)$ and $\phi(t,x)=e^{(a+A|\chi|)(t-t_{1})}\,\bar{u}(t,x),$ respectively.
Then, we have for $(t,x)\in(t_{1},\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}$ , $\displaystyle\psi_{t}=-|\chi|A\psi+\Delta\psi-\chi\nabla v\cdot\nabla\psi\leq\Delta\psi-\chi\nabla\cdot(\psi\nabla v)\leq\Delta\psi-\chi\nabla\cdot(\psi\nabla v)+\psi(a-b\psi^{\sigma}),$ and $\displaystyle\phi_{t}$ $\displaystyle=(a+|\chi|A)\phi+\Delta\phi-\chi\nabla v\cdot\nabla\phi\geq\Delta\phi-\chi\nabla\cdot(\phi\nabla v)+a\phi$ $\displaystyle\geq\Delta\phi-\chi\nabla\cdot(\phi\nabla v)+\phi(a-b\phi^{\sigma}).$ Since $\psi(t_{1},\cdot)\leq\phi(t_{1},\cdot)=\bar{u}(t_{1},\cdot)=u(t_{1},\cdot),$ the comparison principle yields $\psi(t,\cdot)\leq u(t,\cdot)\leq\phi(t,\cdot)\quad\forall\,t\geq t_{1}.$ Note that $\bar{u}(t,x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\Gamma(t,t_{1},x,z)u(t_{1},z)dz,$ where $\Gamma(t,t_{1},x,z)$ is the fundamental solution of the equation $\begin{cases}\Gamma_{t}=\Delta_{x}\Gamma-\chi\nabla_{x}v\cdot\nabla_{x}\Gamma,\qquad\qquad\text{ for }(t,x)\in(s,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N},\\
\Gamma(t=t_{1},t_{1},x,z)=\delta_{z}(x).\end{cases}$ Hence $\displaystyle\min\left\{1,({a}/{b})^{1/\sigma}/\|u\|_{\infty}\right\}\,e^{-|\chi|A(t-t_{1})}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\Gamma(t,t_{1},x,z)u(t_{1},z)dz\leq u(s^{\prime},x)$ (A.6) $\displaystyle\leq e^{(a+A|\chi|)(t-t_{1})}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\Gamma(t,t_{1},x,z)u(t_{1},z)dx\quad\forall\,t\geq t_{1},\,\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}.$ Let $p>1$ , $q$ be the conjugate exponent to $p$ and let $\alpha:=(1+p)/(2p)$ . From the proof of [ 13 , (4.25)] , for any fixed $s_{0},R>0$ , there exists a constant $C_{0}>0$ depending only on $s_{0}$ , $R$ , $p$ , $A$ , $N$ and an upper bound of $|\chi|$ such that $\Gamma(t_{0},t_{1},x,z)^{\alpha p}\leq C_{0}\,\Gamma(t,t_{1},y,z)\,\,\forall\,t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+s_{0}],\,\,|x-y|\leq R,\,\,z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}.$ (A.7) By [ 1 , Theorem 10] , there exists $C_{1}\geq 1$ , depending only $A$ , $N$ and an upper bound of $|\chi|$ , such that for any $x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}$ and $0\leq s<t$ with $t-s\leq\frac{1}{2}$ , $({C_{1}(t-s)^{N/2}})^{-1}e^{-C_{1}\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t-s}}\leq\Gamma(t,s,x,y)\leq{C_{1}}{(t-s)^{-N/2}}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{C_{1}(t-s)}}.$ This and $t_{0}=t_{1}+\frac{1}{2}$ imply that there is $C_{2}(q,\chi,N)$ such that $\|\Gamma(t_{0},t_{1},x,\cdot)^{1-\alpha}\|_{q}\leq C_{2}\quad\forall\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}.$ (A.8) By ( A.6 ) and Hölder’s inequality, we have $\displaystyle u(t_{0},x)$ $\displaystyle\leq e^{(a+A|\chi|)/2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\Gamma(t_{0},t_{1},x,z)\,u(t_{1},z)\,dz$ $\displaystyle\leq e^{(a+A|\chi|)/2}\,\|u\|_{\infty}^{1/q}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\left(u(t_{1},z)\,\Gamma(t_{0},t_{1},x,z)^{\alpha p}\right)^{1/p}\Gamma(t_{0},t_{1},x,z)^{(1-\alpha)}dz$ $\displaystyle\leq e^{(a+A|\chi|)/2}\,\|u\|_{\infty}^{1/q}\,\|\Gamma(t_{0},t_{1},x,\cdot)^{1-\alpha}\|_{q}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}u(t_{1},z)\Gamma(t_{0},t_{1},x,z)^{\alpha p}dz\right)^{1/p}.$ Using ( A.7 ) and ( A.8 ) yields for $t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+s_{0}]$ , $\displaystyle u(t_{0},x)$ $\displaystyle\leq C_{2}C_{0}^{1/p}e^{(a+A|\chi|)/2}\,\|u\|_{\infty}^{1/q}\,\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}u(t_{1},z)\Gamma(t,t_{1},y,z)dz\right)^{1/p}$ $\displaystyle\leq C_{2}C_{0}^{1/p}e^{(a+A|\chi|)/2+|\chi|A(\frac{1}{2}+s_{0})/p}\max\left\{\|u\|_{\infty}^{1/q},\|u\|_{\infty}\left({b}/{a}\right)^{1/(\sigma p)}\right\}u(t,y)^{1/p},$ where in the last inequality, we applied ( A.6 ). We can conclude with ( 2.2 ). Next, we prove ( 2.3 ). By a priori estimate for parabolic equations (see [ 32 , Theorem 7.22] ) and
the anisotropic Sobolev embedding for $q=N+3$ (see [ 7 , Lemma A3] ), there is $C_{3}=C_{3}(A,\chi,R,N)>0$ such that for any $(t,x)\in(1,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}$ , $\begin{split}|\nabla u(t,x)|&\leq C_{3}\,\left(\|u\|_{L^{q}([t-\frac{1}{2},t]\times B_{R}(x))}+\|u(a-bu)\|_{L^{q}([t-\frac{1}{2},t]\times B_{R}(x))}\right)\\
&\leq C_{3}\,\|u\|_{L^{\infty}([t-\frac{1}{2},t]\times B_{R}(x))}\,(1+a+b\|u\|_{\infty}).\end{split}$ By ( 2.2 ), for any $p\in(1,\infty)$ , there exists a constant $C>0$ that depends only on $a$ , $b$ , $R$ , $p$ , $A$ , $N$ , $\|u\|_{\infty}$ , and an upper bound of $|\chi|$ such that $|\nabla u(t,x)|\leq{C\,u(t,y)^{1/p}}$ for all $|x-y|\leq R$ , which proves ( 2.3 ).
