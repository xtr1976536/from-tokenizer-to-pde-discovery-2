In this paper we analyze the Kuramoto-Sivashinsky equation (KSE), a model of flame-front propagation, on a two-dimensional square torus of arbitrary size $2L$ with $L>\pi$ . In this case, the linearized equation at the origin admits a finite number of growing modes, which corresponds to the positive eigenvalues of the linear operator $-\Delta^{2}-\Delta$ .
The problem of analyzing the long-time behavior of solutions of the 2D KSE in two spatial dimensions remains largely open; the only global existence results are for sufficiently small tori, or for sufficiently anisotropic and thin domains, due to the lack of good a priori estimates.
The main purpose of this paper is to analyze the instability around growing modes at the nonlinear level. More precisely, we consider the maximal growing mode $\lambda_{0}$ and we show that there is a finite dimensional manifold of initial data of size ${\varepsilon}$ arbitrarily small such that the corresponding solutions become of size $O(1)$ over a time-scale of order $\log({\varepsilon}^{-1})$ . The proof is based on several ingredients such as a sharp quantitative construction of an approximate solution bifurcating from the maximal linearly growing mode, a fixed point argument with exponential weights to construct local in time solutions, and a continuation argument based on sharp energy estimates and para-differential calculus. Introduction In this work, we study the long-time dynamics of the Kuramoto-Sivashinsky equation (KSE for short) in two space dimensions under periodic boundary conditions. The KSE is a model for long-wave instability in dissipative systems and was derived from a coordinate-free model of interface dynamics for flame-front propagation in combustion [ 25 , 26 , 35 ] . Because the KSE models the dynamics of an interface, the most physical dimensions are 1 and 2. The KSE is a non-linear, hyperdissipative evolution equation that has both a scalar form for a potential $v$ with a non-linearity in conservative form, and a vector form for its gradient with a Burgers-type nonlinearity.
We employ the scalar form of the equation that can be written as: $\partial_{t}v+\Delta^{2}v+\Delta v+\Pi(|\nabla v|^{2})=0,\quad x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{T}}^{2}_{L}:={\mathbb{T}}_{L}\times{\mathbb{T}}_{L},$ where ${\mathbb{T}}_{L}:={\mathbb{R}}/(2L{\mathbb{Z}})\simeq[-L,L],$ and $\Pi v:=v-\frac{1}{2L}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}_{L}}v(x)\,dx$ , $v:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}$ is $(2L)$ -periodic with respect to $x=(x_{1},x_{2}).$ We note that we have chosen to use a square torus,
rather than allowing two independent dimensions such as $[0,L_{1}]\times[0,L_{2}].$ This is primarily for simplicity, so as not to over-complicate the proofs to be presented.
An additional benefit, though, is that this choice also highlights how our results do not depend on any anisotropy in the problem. In particular, we are not relying on any thinness of the domain,
and thus our results are genuinely two-dimensional. We shall assume that (1.1) $L>\pi\,,$ which gives rise to growing modes for the linearized operator.
By rescaling the space variables $x$ , we can set the problem on the standard torus ${\mathbb{T}}^{2}$ . Namely, we rescale $x\mapsto\omega x\quad\text{where}\quad\omega:=\frac{\pi}{L},$ and we set $v(x)={\varphi}(\omega x),$ with ${\varphi}:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}\quad 2\pi-\text{periodic in both variables.}$ The equation for ${\varphi}$ becomes (1.2) $\partial_{t}{\varphi}+\Delta^{2}_{\omega}{\varphi}+\Delta_{\omega}{\varphi}+\omega^{2}\Pi(|\nabla{\varphi}|^{2})=0,\quad x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{T}}^{2},$ where $\Delta_{\omega}:=\omega^{2}\Delta,\quad\Delta_{\omega}^{2}:=\omega^{4}\Delta^{2}=\omega^{4}(\partial_{x_{1}}^{2}+\partial_{x_{2}}^{2})^{2}\,.$ Written in terms of frequency, $\omega,$ the condition ( 1.1 )
becomes (1.3) $0<\omega<1\,.$ We study the instability for the Cauchy problem (1.4) $\begin{cases}&\partial_{t}{\varphi}={\mathcal{L}}_{\omega}{\varphi}-\omega^{2}\Pi(|\nabla{\varphi}|^{2}),\quad x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{T}}^{2}\\
&{\varphi}(0,x)={\varphi}_{in}(x),\end{cases}$ where ${\mathcal{L}}_{\omega}:=-\Delta_{\omega}^{2}-\Delta_{\omega}\,.$ More precisely, we shall prove that for any $0<{\varepsilon}\ll 1$ arbitrarily small, there exists a finite dimensional manifold of initial data of size $O({\varepsilon})$ in the Sobolev space $H^{s}$ , for which there exists a unique solution ${\varphi}$ up to time $T_{\varepsilon}\simeq\log({\varepsilon}^{-1})$ such that $\|{\varphi}(T_{\varepsilon})\|_{H^{s}}=O(1)$ . For this result to hold, $s$ has to be taken large enough, specifically bigger than $4$ . We shall prove this result in the general case in which the linearized equation can have an arbitrary number of growing modes (positive eigenvalues of the linear operator ${\mathcal{L}}_{\omega}$ ). The only hypothesis on the size of the torus, i.e. ( 1.1 ), guarantees that there is at least one strictly positive eigenvalue of ${\mathcal{L}}_{\omega}$ , as already mentioned. The problem of studying the long-time dynamics of the Kuramoto-Sivashinsky equation (KSE) is far from being understood in dimension greater than 1. A main difficulty in proving global existence is the lack of a maximum principle for the KSE, due to the presence of the biharmonic operator. In dimension $d=1$ , it is possible to obtain an a priori estimate on the $L^{2}$ norm of $u$ due to the special structure of the non-linearity, which can be written in divergence form. The $L^{2}$ control allows to prove global existence by a continuation argument [ 39 ] . The one-dimensional global existence result of [ 39 ] allows exponential growth of solutions, because the linearized operator $\partial_{t}+\Delta^{2}+\Delta$ admits exponentially growing modes for large enough periods.
It has then been demonstrated that this exponential growth does not actually occur, and solutions which start near the zero solution remain near the zero solution [ 16 ] , [ 31 ] ; this is sometimes described as stability of the
zero solution in the one-dimensional KSE, but this is not stability in the usual sense in that it is not possible to control solutions to remain arbitrarily close to the zero
solution. For many well-studied equations such as the incompressible
Navier-Stokes or Euler equations, the availability of an $L^{2}$ estimate is independent of dimension. This situation
is markedly different for the KSE.
In fact, there is no known global estimate for any $L^{p}$ norm in $d>1$ , and this is a primary reason why the issue of global existence of solutions to the KSE is still open. There is an extensive literature concerning the KSE in dimension $d=1$ . While we do not survey the full literature in the one-dimensional case, we mention results concerning analyticity of solutions (see [ 8 , 19 ] and references therein), optimal bounds on the growth of the $L^{2}$ norm as a function of the period $L$ [ 7 , 13 , 14 , 15 , 32 , 36 , 37 , 38 ] (see also [ 20 ] ), and inertial manifolds [ 11 , 21 ] . There are few results for the higher-dimensional KSE. Short-time existence and analyticity is known to hold in the full space with data in certain $L^{p}$ spaces [ 6 ] (see also [ 20 ] ). For $d=2$ , global existence holds for thin or sufficiently anisotropic domains [ 5 , 24 , 30 , 34 ] . A first isotropic result was [ 2 ] , in which Ambrose-Mazzucato studied the KSE on ${\mathbb{T}}^{2}$ and proved short-time existence and analyticity with a bound on the analyticity radius for data in $L^{2}$ and in the Wiener algebra. They established global existence
of mild solutions for mean-free data sufficiently small in $L^{2}$ and in the Wiener algebra, but only in the absence of growing modes, which happens in a two-dimensional torus $[0,L_{1}]\times[0,L_{2}]$ when $L_{1},\;L_{2}<2\pi$ . Subsequently, Ambrose and Mazzucato extended this to global existence of small solutions
when $L_{1}$ and $L_{2}$ are each slightly larger than $2\pi,$ so that there is a linearly growing mode
present in both spatial directions [ 3 ] ; this is the only isotropic global result for the higher-dimensional
KSE which allows a linearly growing mode in each spatial direction.
In [ 10 ] , Feng-Mazzucato proved existence of mild solutions for sufficiently small data $\phi(0)$ in $L^{2}$ on an arbitrary interval of time $[0,T]$ in the absence of growing modes. In [ 1 ] , solutions of the KSE in arbitrary dimension are proved to exist when
the initial data has low regularity, but the solutions are only global in the absence of linearly
growing modes. We also mention a detailed numerical study of the two-dimensional KSE [ 22 ] . We only consider the classical form of KSE, and not any modified or generalized KSE, for which more results are known (see e.g. [ 12 , 29 ] ).
One recent modified equation is the anisotropically reduced KSE [ 27 ] , in which a maximum principle holds for
one component of the gradient of the solution; another anisotropic modification
of the KSE is studied in [ 41 ] .
In [ 10 ] , Feng and Mazzucato also considered the KSE with additional linear advection and prove that global existence can be achieved in the presence of growing modes and for arbitrary data, if the advecting field is relaxation enhancing with sufficiently small dissipation time. We also mention [ 9 ] where the advection term is given by a shear flow. Another work on equations similar in some ways to the KSE
is [ 4 ] , in which the question
of global existence or singularity formation was studied for hyperviscous equations of Hamilton-Jacobi-type. We also mention the work [ 23 ] where a 1D KSE-type equation with a dispersive term is considered, showing that the dispersive term weakens the global bounds. In the present paper, we study the KSE in the case of the two dimensional periodic box ${\mathbb{T}}^{2}_{L}:=({\mathbb{R}}/(2L{\mathbb{Z}}))^{2}$ with $L>\pi$ , so that we allow the presence of an arbitrary large number of linearly growing modes. We rigorously prove instability, namely, we construct initial data of arbitrarily small size $O({\varepsilon})$ such that in finite time $T\simeq\log({\varepsilon}^{-1})$ the corresponding solutions become of order $O(1)$ (in the $H^{s}$ topology). Our approach is inspired by the technique developed in [ 17 ] , in which the author proves some nonlinear instability results for the Euler and Prandtl equations (see also [ 18 ] ). Throughout the paper, we use standard notation to represent function spaces. In particular, $H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})$ , $s\in{\mathbb{R}}$ , is the standard $L^{2}$ -based Sobolev space equipped with the norm $\|u\|_{H^{s}}:=\Big(\sum_{\xi\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}|\widehat{u}(\xi)|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\langle\xi\rangle:=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}},$ and we denote by $H^{s}_{0}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})$ the space of $H^{s}$ functions with zero average. For any $T>0$ , we denote by ${\mathcal{C}}^{0}([0,T],H^{s}_{0}({\mathbb{T}}^{d}))$ the space of continuous functions $[0,T]\to H^{s}_{0}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})\,,\quad t\mapsto u(t),$ equipped by the sup norm $\|u\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}_{T}H^{s}_{x}}:=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}\,.$ For any $k\geq 1$ integer we also denote by ${\mathcal{C}}^{k}([0,T],H^{s}_{0}({\mathbb{T}}^{d}))$ the space of the ${\mathcal{C}}^{k}$ maps $[0,T]\to H^{s}_{0}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})\,,\quad t\mapsto u(t),$ equipped by the norm $\|u\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{k}_{T}H^{s}_{x}}:=\sum_{n=0}^{k}\|\partial_{t}^{n}u\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}_{T}H^{s}_{x}}\,.$ Given two Banach spaces $X$ and $Y$ , we denote by ${\mathcal{B}}(X,Y)$ the space of bounded linear operators from $X$ to $Y$ equipped with the standard operator norm $\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(X,Y)}$ . If $X=Y$ , we use the notation ${\mathcal{B}}(X)\equiv{\mathcal{B}}(X,X)$ . Given ${\mathcal{H}}$ a Hilbert space and a linear operator $A:{\mathcal{D}}(A)\to{\mathcal{H}}$ where ${\mathcal{D}}(A)$ is a dense subspace of ${\mathcal{H}}$ , we denote by $A^{*}$ the standard adjoint operator of $A$ . Given some parameters $a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}>0$ , we use the following notation. We write $A\lesssim_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}}B$ if there exists a positive constant $C(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})>0$ such that $A\leq C(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})B\,.$ 2. Statement of the main result and ideas of the proof We expand a function $u\in L^{2}_{0}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})$ in Fourier series as $u(x)\equiv u(x_{1},x_{2})=\sum_{(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus\{0\}}\widehat{u}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})e^{i(x_{1}\xi_{1}+x_{2}\xi_{2})}=\sum_{\xi\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus\{0\}}\widehat{u}(\xi)e^{{\rm i}x\cdot\xi}.$ (We recall that $L^{2}_{0}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})$ is the subspace of mean-free elements, therefore $\widehat{u}(0)=0$ .) We consider the linear operator ${\mathcal{L}}_{\omega}=-\Delta_{\omega}^{2}-\Delta_{\omega}$ . For any $\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus\{0\}$ , one has that (2.1) $\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{\omega}(e^{{\rm i}x\cdot\xi})$ $\displaystyle=\lambda_{\omega}(\xi)e^{{\rm i}x\cdot\xi}\,,$ $\displaystyle\lambda_{\omega}(\xi)$ $\displaystyle:=-\omega^{4}|\xi|^{4}+\omega^{2}|\xi|^{2}=-\omega^{2}|\xi|^{2}(\omega^{2}|\xi|^{2}-1)\,,\quad\xi\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus\{0\}\,.$ The spectrum of ${\mathcal{L}}_{\omega}$ is then given by $\sigma({\mathcal{L}}_{\omega})=\Big\{\lambda_{\omega}(\xi)=-\omega^{2}|\xi|^{2}(\omega^{2}|\xi|^{2}-1):\xi\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus\{0\}\Big\}\,.$ Since $0<\omega<1$ , one has that (2.2) $\lambda_{\omega}(1,0)=\lambda_{\omega}(0,1)=\omega^{2}(1-\omega)(\omega+1)>0.$ Therefore there is at least one strictly positive eigenvalue of ${\mathcal{L}}_{\omega}$ . We split the spectrum $\sigma({\mathcal{L}}_{\omega})$ of the linear operator ${\mathcal{L}}_{\omega}$ as $\displaystyle\sigma({\mathcal{L}}_{\omega})=\sigma_{+}({\mathcal{L}}_{\omega})\cup\sigma_{-}({\mathcal{L}}_{\omega})\,,$ $\displaystyle\sigma_{+}({\mathcal{L}}_{\omega}):=\Big\{\lambda\in\sigma({\mathcal{L}}_{\omega}):\lambda\geq 0\Big\}\,,$ $\displaystyle\sigma_{-}({\mathcal{L}}_{\omega}):=\Big\{\lambda\in\sigma({\mathcal{L}}_{\omega}):\lambda<0\Big\}\,.$ By ( 2.2 ), one has that $\sigma_{+}({\mathcal{L}}_{\omega})\neq\emptyset$ and there is at least one eigenvalue in $\sigma_{+}({\mathcal{L}}_{\omega})$ that is strictly positive. A direct calculation shows that (2.3) $\displaystyle\sigma_{+}({\mathcal{L}}_{\omega})$ $\displaystyle=\Big\{\lambda_{\omega}(\xi):0<|\xi|\leq\frac{1}{\omega}\Big\}\,,$ $\displaystyle\sigma_{-}({\mathcal{L}}_{\omega})$ $\displaystyle=\Big\{\lambda_{\omega}(\xi):|\xi|>\frac{1}{\omega}\Big\}.$ Hence $\sigma_{+}({\mathcal{L}}_{\omega})$ is non-empty, finite and $\sigma_{-}({\mathcal{L}}_{\omega})$ is infinite. Moreover there could be some integer vectors $\xi\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus\{0\}$ such that $|\xi|=\frac{1}{\omega}$ . For such vectors $\xi$ one has that $\lambda_{\omega}(\xi)=0$ . We denote by $\lambda_{0}$ the maximum (strictly positive) eigenvalue of ${\mathcal{L}}_{\omega}$ , namely, (2.4) $\lambda_{0}:={\rm max}\,\sigma_{+}({\mathcal{L}}_{\omega})={\rm max}\,\sigma({\mathcal{L}}_{\omega})>0\,.$ Then $\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{\omega}(\xi_{0})\quad\text{for some}\quad\xi_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus\{0\}\quad\text{with}\quad 0<|\xi_{0}|<\frac{1}{\omega}\,.$ The eigenspace associated to $\lambda_{0}$ is given by $E(\lambda_{0}):={\rm span}\big\{e^{{\rm i}x\cdot\xi}:|\xi|=|\xi_{0}|\big\}\,.$ We focus on initial conditions for ( 1.4 ) that are Fourier localized around
wavenumber $|\xi_{0}|$ ; that is, we take ${\varphi}_{in}(x)=\sum_{|\xi|=|\xi_{0}|}\widehat{\varphi}_{in}(\xi)e^{{\rm i}x\cdot\xi}$ , with $\widehat{\varphi}_{in}(\xi)=\overline{\widehat{\varphi}_{in}(-\xi)}$ , which guarantees that ${\varphi}_{in}$ is real valued. We then have that ${\mathcal{L}}_{\omega}{\varphi}_{in}=\lambda_{0}{\varphi}_{in}$ . For future use, for any $n\geq 2$ we also introduce the finite dimensional spaces (2.5) $E_{n}:={\rm span}\Big\{e^{{\rm i}x\cdot\xi}:1\leq|\xi|\leq n|\xi_{0}|\Big\}\,.$ We will study the Cauchy problem: (2.6) $\begin{cases}\partial_{t}{\varphi}={\mathcal{L}}_{\omega}{\varphi}-\omega^{2}\Pi(|\nabla{\varphi}|^{2}),\\
{\varphi}(0,x)={\varepsilon}{\varphi}_{in}(x),\end{cases}$ where $0<{\varepsilon}\ll 1$ is small enough. The main result of this paper is the following theorem. Theorem 2.1 . Let $s\geq 4$ and let $\omega$ satisfy ( 1.3 ). There are two constants $\delta_{0}\equiv\delta_{0}(s,\omega),{\varepsilon}_{0}\equiv{\varepsilon}_{0}(s,\omega)>0$ such that for any ${\varepsilon}\in(0,{\varepsilon}_{0})$ , for any initial ${\varepsilon}{\varphi}_{in}$ with ${\varphi}_{in}\in E(\lambda_{0})$ normalized in $H^{s}$ , i.e. $\|{\varphi}_{in}\|_{H^{s}}=1$ , there exist a time $T_{\varepsilon}$ with $\frac{1}{2\lambda_{0}}\log({\varepsilon}^{-1})<T_{\varepsilon}<\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}}\log({\varepsilon}^{-1}),$ and a unique solution ${\varphi}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{0}([0,T_{\varepsilon}],H^{s}_{0}({\mathbb{T}}^{2}))\cap{\mathcal{C}}^{1}([0,T_{\varepsilon}],H^{s-4}_{0}({\mathbb{T}}^{2}))$ of the Cauchy problem ( 2.6 ) satisfying $\|{\varphi}(T_{\varepsilon})\|_{H^{s}}\geq\delta_{0}\,.$ We must compare this result to the global existence result of [ 3 ] .
With the present notation, for $\omega$ quite close to $1$ and satisfying ( 1.3 ), and with small initial
data, the result of of [ 3 ] applies. Then, solutions exist for all time
and remain small, proportional to the size of the initial data, which is consistent with Theorem 2.1 .
Indeed, as $\omega\rightarrow 1^{-},$ we would have ${\varepsilon}_{0}\rightarrow 0$ and
correspondingly $\delta_{0}\rightarrow 0$ as well. The next subsection is devoted to illustrating the ideas of the proof of Theorem 2.1 . 2.1. Ideas of the proof We consider initial data ${\varepsilon}{\varphi}_{in}$ , arbitrarily small, i.e. $0<{\varepsilon}\ll 1$ where ${\varphi}_{in}$ is an eigenfunction of ${\mathcal{L}}_{\omega}$ , normalized in $H^{s}$ , associated to the biggest (positive) eigenvalue of ${\mathcal{L}}_{\omega}$ , i.e. ${\mathcal{L}}_{\omega}{\varphi}_{in}=\lambda_{0}{\varphi}_{in},\quad\|{\varphi}_{in}\|_{H^{s}}=1\,.$ We note that neglecting the nonlinearity, the linear problem $\begin{cases}\partial_{t}{\varphi}_{1}={\mathcal{L}}_{\omega}{\varphi}_{1}\\
{\varphi}_{1}(0,x)={\varepsilon}{\varphi}_{in}(x)\end{cases}$ admits the solution ${\varphi}_{1}(t,x)={\varepsilon}e^{\lambda_{0}t}{\varphi}_{in}(x)\,.$ Hence $\|{\varphi}_{1}(0)\|_{H^{s}}={\varepsilon}\ll 1$ . First, we explore when this linear solution becomes of order $O(1)$ in $H^{s}$ , namely, for which time $T_{0}\equiv T_{0}({\varepsilon})$ one has that $\|{\varphi}_{1}(T_{0})\|_{H^{s}}={\varepsilon}e^{\lambda_{0}T_{0}}=1\,.$ Clearly this happens when $T_{0}:=\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}}\log({\varepsilon}^{-1})\,.$ Informally, the goal is then to extend this very simple mechanism for the linearized equation to the nonlinear problem. We shall prove that there exists a time $T\simeq\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}}\log({\varepsilon}^{-1})$ , more precisely $T_{0}/2<T<T_{0}$ , and a unique solution of ( 2.6 ) such that $\|{\varphi}(0)\|_{H^{s}}={\varepsilon}$ and $\|{\varphi}(T)\|_{H^{s}}=O(1)$ . This “instability time” $T$ is strictly smaller than the “instability time” $T_{0}$ given by the linearized problem, an effect of the nonlinear term appearing in the KSE. We look for such an instability time $T$ as $T\equiv T_{\sigma}\equiv T_{\sigma}({\varepsilon})=T_{0}-\sigma,$ where $\sigma\equiv\sigma(s,\omega)\gg 0$ has to be chosen large enough, but independently of ${\varepsilon}$ . We observe that given $\sigma>0$ , $T_{0}/2<T_{\sigma}<T_{0}$ is satisfied provided that $0<{\varepsilon}<{\varepsilon}_{0}:=e^{-2\lambda_{0}\sigma}\,.$ We stress that this is the only condition that we require on ${\varepsilon}$ along the whole paper. We construct the solution ${\varphi}(t,x)$ in such a way that, over the time interval $[0,T_{\sigma}]$ , it holds ${\varphi}(t,x)\simeq{\varepsilon}e^{\lambda_{0}t}{\varphi}_{in}(x)$ . More precisely, we prove the quantitative estimate $\sup_{t\in[0,T_{\sigma}]}\|{\varphi}(t)-{\varepsilon}e^{\lambda_{0}t}{\varphi}_{in}\|_{H^{s}}\leq C(s,\omega)e^{-2\lambda_{0}\sigma}\,.$ This estimate implies that, for $\sigma\gg 0$ large enough, but independent of ${\varepsilon}$ , one has that $\|{\varphi}(T_{\sigma})\|_{H^{s}}\geq\delta_{0}:=\frac{1}{2}e^{-\lambda_{0}\sigma}\,.$ In order to prove this estimates, the proof is essentially divided into three steps: (1) Construction of an approximate solution ${\varphi}_{app}$ close to the linear solution ${\varepsilon}e^{\lambda_{0}t}{\varphi}_{in}$ ; (2) Local in time existence of a solution close to the approximate solution ${\varphi}_{app}$ ; (3) Continuation argument up to time $T_{\sigma}\simeq\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}}\log({\varepsilon}^{-1})$ and instability estimate. In the following, we shall describe these three steps. Step 1. Construction of the approximate solution ${\varphi}_{app}$ . In Section 3 , we will construct a ${\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}$ approximate solution at any order of the Cauchy problem ( 2.6 ) as a regular expansion in power of ${\varepsilon}$ . That is, we look for ${\varphi}_{app}(t,x)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}{\varepsilon}^{n}{\varphi}_{n}(t,x),$ where $N\in\mathbb{N}$ is arbitrary.
By inserting this ansatz into the equation, one finds that ${\varphi}_{1},\ldots,{\varphi}_{N}$ can be recursively determined by solving linear equations. Actually ${\varepsilon}{\varphi}_{1}$ is the solution of the linearized problem with initial data ${\varepsilon}{\varphi}_{in}$ , whereas for $n\geq 2$ , ${\varphi}_{n}(t,x)$ solves the linear inhomogeneous Cauchy problem ( 3.6 ). The key point is to quantify the exponential growth in time of all the ${\varphi}_{n}$ , $n=2,\ldots,N$ with sharp bounds. We shall prove that $\|{\varphi}_{n}(t)\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{s,n,\omega}e^{n\lambda_{0}t},\quad\forall t\geq 0,\quad\forall s\geq 0,\quad n=1,\ldots,N.$ We exploit the following property of the quadratic nonlinearity: if $u$ is a function supported on the Fourier modes $|\xi|\leq N$ and $v$ is supported on the Fourier modes $|\xi|\leq M$ , then the nonlinearity ${\mathcal{Q}}(u,v)$ is supported on the Fourier modes $|\xi|\leq N+M$ . The estimated above is proved in Lemma 3.3 , by performing an induction argument of the Fourier coefficients of ${\varphi}_{n}$ , by using variation of the constants and using that $\lambda_{0}$ is the maximum positive eigenvalue of the linear operator ${\mathcal{L}}_{\omega}$ . The error term $r_{N}(t,x)$ is then bounded in Lemma 3.5 , where it is proved that $\|r_{N}(t)\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{s,N,\omega}{\varepsilon}^{N+1}e^{(N+1)\lambda_{0}t},\quad\forall t\in[0,T_{\sigma}],\quad\forall s\geq 0\,.$ In deriving these estimates, the elementary fact that, since ${\varepsilon}e^{\lambda_{0}T_{0}}=1$ , for any $0\leq t\leq T_{\sigma}=T_{0}-\sigma$ ${\varepsilon}e^{\lambda_{0}t}\leq e^{-\lambda_{0}\sigma}\ll 1\quad\text{for}\quad\sigma\gg 0\quad\text{large enough,}$ is used repeatedly. Step 2. Local in time existence close to ${\varphi}_{app}$ . In Section 4 , we will prove existence up to $O(1)$ times of solutions ${\varphi}(t,x)\simeq{\varphi}_{app}(t,x)$ . To this end, we write ${\varphi}={\varphi}_{app}+u$ and we solve the Cauchy problem ( 4.1 ), that is, $\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u={\mathcal{L}}_{\omega}u+2{\mathcal{Q}}({\varphi}_{app},u)+{\mathcal{Q}}(u,u)+r_{N}\\
u(0,x)=0\,.\end{cases}$ In Proposition 4.1 , we prove existence of smooth solutions up to time $T=O(1)$ by a fixed point argument in a weighted space that keeps track of the time exponential growth due to the maximal unstable eigenvalue $\lambda_{0}$ . More precisely, we employ the following norm (see ( 4.5 )): $\|u\|_{s,T}:=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}e^{-N\lambda_{0}t}\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}.$ The local solution $u$ then will satisfy (2.7) $\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}\leq{\varepsilon}^{N}e^{N\lambda_{0}t},\quad\forall t\in[0,T]\,.$ The solution is a “mild” solution, that is, a fixed point of the map $\Phi$ given in ( 4.11 ).
As customary for semilinear parabolic equation, control of the integral term that contains the nonlinearity is obtained through smoothing estimates for the semigroup $e^{t{\mathcal{L}}_{\omega}}$ , $t>0$ , specifically that that gives a gain of one derivative (see Lemma 4.3 ). This bound degenerates at the origin as for the biharmonic operator, that is, $\sim\frac{1}{t^{\frac{1}{4}}}$ . In order to estimate all the terms appearing in the Duhamel formula with respect to the norm $\|\cdot\|_{s,T}$ , we establish quantitative bounds involving the error term $r_{N}$ and the nonlinearity in Lemmata 4.4 , 4.5 . Finally, the fixed point argument on $\Phi$ is implemented in Lemma 4.6 . Step 3. Continuation argument up to time $T_{\sigma}\simeq\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}}\log({\varepsilon}^{-1})$ and instability estimate. In Section 5 , we discuss how to extend the local solution $u$ of the Cauchy problem ( 4.1 ), satisfying ( 2.7 ) , up to time $T_{\sigma}\simeq\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}}\log({\varepsilon}^{-1})$ . This argument is by contradiction. We call $T_{*}$ the maximal time for which $u$ is the solution of ( 4.1 ) satisfying ( 2.7 ). We then assume by contradiction that $T_{*}\leq T_{\sigma}$ and we perform a sharp energy estimate over the time interval $[0,T_{*}]$ . Since the nonlinearity contains derivatives, we use the theory of para-differential operators in order to avoid a possible loss of derivatives in the energy estimates. We collect all the facts needed on para-differential operators in Appendix A . First of all, we paralinearize ( 4.1 ) and we reduce it to the para-differential problem ( 5.6 ), which is (2.8) $\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u={\mathcal{L}}_{\omega}u+T_{A}\cdot\nabla u+f_{N}(t)\\
u(0,x)=0\end{cases}$ where $A(t)$ and $f_{N}(t)$ (that clearly depend on $u$ ) satisfy the estimates $\displaystyle\|A(t)\|_{H^{s-1}}\lesssim_{s}{\varepsilon}e^{\lambda_{0}t}\,,\quad\forall t\in[0,T_{*}]$ $\displaystyle\|f_{N}(t)\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{s,N,\omega}{\varepsilon}^{N+1}e^{(N+1)\lambda_{0}t},\qquad\forall t\in[0,T_{*}]$ which is true under our hypothesis that $s\geq 4$ . The para-product operator $T_{a}$ , given a suffciently regular Sobolev function $a$ , is defined in ( A.3 ). Then by performing an energy estimate on ( 2.8 ), we obtain that $\frac{d}{dt}\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\leq\lambda_{0}\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+C_{0}{\varepsilon}^{2N+1}e^{(2N+1)\lambda_{0}t}\,,\quad\forall t\in[0,T_{*}]$ for some constant $C_{0}\equiv C_{0}(s,N,\omega)>0$ large enough. It follows that $\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}\leq C{\varepsilon}^{N+\frac{1}{2}}e^{(N+\frac{1}{2})\lambda_{0}t},\quad\forall t\in[0,T_{*}]$ for some constant $C\equiv C(s,N,\omega)>0$ . Then for any $s\geq 0$ , $u\in H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})$ , one has $\Big\|\Big[T_{a}\partial_{x_{k}}+(T_{a}\partial_{x_{k}})^{*}\Big]u\Big\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{s}\|a\|_{H^{s_{0}+1}}\|u\|_{H^{s}}\,.$ Proof. Then $\displaystyle(T_{a}\partial_{x_{k}})^{*}+T_{a}\partial_{x_{k}}$ $\displaystyle=-\partial_{x_{k}}T_{a}^{*}+T_{a}\partial_{x_{k}}=-\partial_{x_{k}}T_{a}+T_{a}\partial_{x_{k}}-\partial_{x_{k}}{\mathcal{R}}_{a}$ $\displaystyle=-T_{a}\partial_{x_{k}}+T_{a}\partial_{x_{k}}-T_{\partial_{x_{k}}a}-\partial_{x_{k}}{\mathcal{R}}_{a}=-T_{\partial_{x_{k}}a}-\partial_{x_{k}}{\mathcal{R}}_{a}\,.$ By Lemma A.1 , since $a\in H^{s_{0}+1}$ , $s_{0}>\frac{d}{2}$ , one has that for any $s\geq 0$ , $\|T_{\partial_{x_{k}}a}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(H^{s})}\lesssim_{s}\|\partial_{x_{k}}a\|_{H^{s_{0}}}\lesssim_{s}\|a\|_{H^{s_{0}+1}}\,.$ Moreover, applying Lemma A.4 with $\rho=1$ gives for any $s\geq 0$ , $u\in H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})$ , $\displaystyle\|\partial_{x_{k}}{\mathcal{R}}_{a}u\|_{H^{s}}$ $\displaystyle\leq\|{\mathcal{R}}_{a}u\|_{H^{s+1}}\lesssim\|a\|_{H^{s_{0}+1}}\|u\|_{H^{s}}\,,$ which concludes the proof.
∎ Lemma A.6 . Let ${\mathcal{S}}_{M}$ be the mollifier defined in ( 5.16 ) and let $s_{0}>\frac{d}{2}$ , $a\in H^{s_{0}+1}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})$ . Then for any $s\geq 0$ , one has that $\|[{\mathcal{S}}_{M},T_{a}\partial_{x_{k}}]\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(H^{s})}\lesssim_{s}\|a\|_{H^{s_{0}+1}}$ . Proof. Let $A:=[{\mathcal{S}}_{M},T_{a}\partial_{x_{k}}]$ . A direct calculation shows that $\widehat{Au}(\eta)=\sum_{\xi\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}g(\eta,\xi)\widehat{a}(\eta-\xi)\widehat{u}(\xi),$ where $g(\eta,\xi):={\rm i}\big(\chi_{M}(\eta)-\chi_{M}(\xi)\big)\xi_{k}\psi_{\delta}(\eta-\xi\,,\,\xi)\,.$ By the properties of the cut-off function $\psi_{\delta}$ , one has that ${\rm supp}(g)\subseteq\Big\{(\eta,\xi)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\langle\eta-\xi\rangle\leq\delta\langle\xi\rangle\Big\}\,.$ Moreover, by the properties of the cut off function $\chi_{M}$ in ( 5.13 )-( 5.15 ) and by the mean value theorem $|\chi_{M}(\eta)-\chi_{M}(\xi)|\lesssim\langle\xi+\theta(\eta-\xi)\rangle^{-1}|\eta-\xi|$ for some $\theta\in[0,1]$ . Since $\langle\eta-\xi\rangle\leq\delta\langle\xi\rangle$ , by choosing $\delta$ small enough, we have that $\langle\xi+\theta(\eta-\xi)\rangle\gtrsim\langle\xi\rangle$ . Therefore, $|\chi_{M}(\eta)-\chi_{M}(\xi)|\lesssim\langle\xi\rangle^{-1}\langle\eta-\xi\rangle\,.$ This last bound actually implies that $|g(\eta,\xi)|\lesssim\langle\eta-\xi\rangle\,.$ Next, on the support of $m$ it holds $\langle\eta\rangle\lesssim\langle\eta-\xi\rangle+\langle\xi\rangle\lesssim\langle\xi\rangle\,$ By the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, using that $2s_{0}>d$ and hence $\sum_{\xi}\langle\eta-\xi\rangle^{-2s_{0}}=\sum_{k}\langle k\rangle^{-2s_{0}}=C(s_{0})<+\infty$ , it follows that $\displaystyle\|Au\|_{H^{s}}^{2}$ $\displaystyle\lesssim_{s}\sum_{\eta\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\Big(\sum_{\xi\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\langle\eta-\xi\rangle^{s_{0}+1}|\widehat{a}(\eta-\xi)||\widehat{u}(\xi)|\frac{1}{\langle\eta-\xi\rangle^{s_{0}}}\Big)^{2}$ $\displaystyle\lesssim_{s}\sum_{\eta,\xi\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}|\widehat{u}(\xi)|^{2}\langle\eta-\xi\rangle^{2(s_{0}+1)}|\widehat{a}(\eta-\xi)|^{2}\lesssim_{s}\|a\|_{H^{s_{0}+1}}^{2}\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}$ which gives the claimed bound.
