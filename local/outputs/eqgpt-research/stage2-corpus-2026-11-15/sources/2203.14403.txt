An improvement of [ 18 ] on the blow-up region and the lifespan estimate of a weakly coupled system of wave equations with damping and mass in the scale-invariant case and with time-derivative nonlinearity is obtained in this article. Indeed, thanks to a better understanding of the dynamics of the solutions, we give here a better characterization of the blow-up region. Furthermore, the techniques used in this article may be extended to other systems and interestingly they simplify the proof of the blow-up result in [ 3 ] which is concerned with the single wave equation in the same context as in the present work. Key words and phrases: blow-up, critical curve, lifespan, nonlinear wave equations, semilinear weakly coupled system, scale-invariant damping, time-derivative nonlinearity. 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification 35L71, 35B44 1. Introduction The weakly coupled system of semilinear wave equations in the presence of damping and mass terms in scale-invariant case with time derivative nonlinearity reads as follows: (1.1) $\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle u_{tt}-\Delta u+\frac{\mu_{1}}{1+t}u_{t}+\frac{\nu_{1}^{2}}{(1+t)^{2}}u=|\partial_{t}v|^{p},&\quad(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times[0,\infty),\\
\displaystyle v_{tt}-\Delta v+\frac{\mu_{2}}{1+t}v_{t}+\frac{\nu_{2}^{2}}{(1+t)^{2}}v=|\partial_{t}u|^{q},&\quad(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times[0,\infty),\\
u(x,0)={\varepsilon}f_{1}(x),\ \ v(x,0)={\varepsilon}f_{2}(x),&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
u_{t}(x,0)={\varepsilon}g_{1}(x),\ \ v_{t}(x,0)=\varepsilon g_{2}(x),&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\end{cases}$ where $\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\nu^{2}_{1}$ and $\nu^{2}_{2}$ are nonnegative constants. The positive parameter ${\varepsilon}$ characterizes the size of the initial
data, and $f_{1},f_{2},g_{1}$ and $g_{2}$ are positive functions assumed to be compactly supported on $B_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}(0,R),R>0$ . Naturally, along this article , we suppose that $p,q>1$ . First, we recall the Glassey exponent $p_{G}$ which is given by (1.2) $p_{G}=p_{G}(N):=1+\frac{2}{N-1}.$ It is well-known that the aforementioned critical value, $p_{G}$ , characterizes the threshold between the global existence ( $p>p_{G}$ ) and the nonexistence ( $p\leq p_{G}$ ) regions; see e.g. [ 7 , 8 , 10 , 20 , 23 , 26 ] . In this paragraph, we recall some results related the massless case for a single equation. We start by mentioning the blow-up result for the solution of a single equation inherited from ( 1.1 ) without mass term. Indeed, Lai and Takamura showed in [ 13 ] an upper bound estimate of the lifespan. Later, in [ 18 ] , Palmieri and Tu enhanced this result by extending the blow-up region for $p$ in the case of a single equation (with mass term). More precisely, they obtain a blow-up result for $p\in(1,p_{G}(N+\sigma(\mu,0))]$ where (1.3) $\sigma=\sigma(\mu,\nu):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\mu+1-\sqrt{\delta}&\textnormal{if}&\delta\in[0,1),\\
\mu&\text{if}&\delta\geq 1,\end{array}\right.$ and $\delta$ is given by ( 1.5 ) below. Note that the result in [ 18 ] was recently improved in [ 6 ] by extending the upper bound for $p$ from $p_{G}(N+\sigma(\mu,0))$ to $p_{G}(N+\mu)$ . Recently, in [ 3 ] , the case of the scale-invariant damped equation with mass and time-derivative nonlinearity was studied; this reads (1.4) $\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle u_{tt}-\Delta u+\frac{\mu}{1+t}u_{t}+\frac{\nu^{2}}{(1+t)^{2}}u=|u_{t}|^{p},\quad\mbox{in}\ {\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times[0,\infty),\\
u(x,0)={\varepsilon}f(x),\ u_{t}(x,0)={\varepsilon}g(x),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}.\end{array}\right.$ Let us introduce the following quantity, that we assume to be positive, (1.5) $\delta=\delta(\mu,\nu):=(\mu-1)^{2}-4\nu^{2}.$ Furthermore, we define (1.6) $\sigma=\sigma(\mu,\nu):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\mu+1-\sqrt{\delta}&\textnormal{if}&\delta\in[0,1),\\
\mu&\text{if}&\delta\geq 1.\end{array}\right.$ Using a functional approach, a blow-up result is proven in [ 3 ] for ( 1.4 ), improving thus the one obtained in [ 18 ] . In fact, the blow-up interval, $p\in(1,p_{G}(N+\sigma)]$ ( $\sigma$ is given by ( 1.6 )), is ameliorated in comparison with [ 18 ] , to show the blow-up inside the region $p\in(1,p_{G}(N+\mu)]$ , with $\delta\in(0,1)$ . However, for $\delta\geq 1$ , the two results, in [ 3 ] and [ 18 ] , are the same. In relationship with these works, we also mention the articles [ 6 , 13 ] where the massless case is investigated. Now, letting $\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}=\nu_{1}=\nu_{2}=0$ in ( 1.1 ) we find the following coupled system: (1.7) $\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\Delta u=|\partial_{t}v|^{p},&(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times[0,\infty),\\
v_{tt}-\Delta v=|\partial_{t}u|^{q},&(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times[0,\infty),\\
u(x,0)={\varepsilon}f_{1}(x),\ \ v(x,0)={\varepsilon}f_{2}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
u_{t}(x,0)={\varepsilon}g_{1}(x),\ \ v_{t}(x,0)=\varepsilon g_{2}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.\end{cases}$ For the global existence of solutions to ( 1.7 ), we refer the reader to [ 11 ] . However, the blow-up of ( 1.7 ) has been the subject of several works; see e.g. [ 1 , 9 , 15 , 16 ] . More precisely, the critical (in the sense of interface between blow-up and global existence) curve for $p,q$ is given by (1.8) $\Upsilon(N,p,q):=\max(\Lambda(N,p,q),\Lambda(N,q,p))=0,$ where (1.9) $\displaystyle\Lambda(N,p,q):=\frac{p+1}{pq-1}-\frac{N-1}{2}.$ Under some assumptions, the solution $(u,v)$ of ( 1.7 ) blows up in finite time $T({\varepsilon})$ for small initial data (of size ${\varepsilon}$ ), namely (1.10) $T({\varepsilon})\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}C{\varepsilon}^{-\Upsilon(N,p,q)}&\text{if}&\Upsilon(N,p,q)>0,\\
\exp(C{\varepsilon}^{-(pq-1)})&\text{if}&\Upsilon(N,p,q)=0,\ p\neq q,\\
\exp(C{\varepsilon}^{-(p-1)})&\text{if}&\Upsilon(N,p,q)=0,\ p=q.\end{array}\right.$ In the context of the present work, Palmieri and Tu [ 18 ] proved a blow-up result for the system ( 1.1 ). More precisely, the authors in [ 18 ] proved that there is blow-up for the system ( 1.1 ) for $p,q$ satisfying (1.11) $\Omega(N,\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},p,q):=\max(\Lambda(N+\sigma_{1},p,q),\Lambda(N+\sigma_{2},q,p))\geq 0,$ where $\Lambda$ is given by ( 1.9 ) and $\sigma_{i}=\sigma(\mu_{i},\nu_{i}),i=1,2$ is given by ( 1.6 ). Indeed, for small initial data (of size ${\varepsilon}$ ), the solution $(u,v)$ of ( 1.1 ) blows up in finite time $T({\varepsilon})$ that is bounded as (1.12) $T({\varepsilon})\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}C{\varepsilon}^{-\Omega(N,\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},p,q)}&\text{if}&\Omega(N,\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},p,q)>0,\\
\exp(C{\varepsilon}^{-(pq-1)})&\text{if}&\Omega(N,\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},p,q)=0,\\
\exp(C{\varepsilon}^{-\min\left(\frac{pq-1}{p+1},\frac{pq-1}{q+1}\right)})&\text{if}&\Lambda(N+\sigma_{1},p,q)=\Lambda(N+\sigma_{2},p,q)=0.\end{array}\right.$ Concerning the case of coupled equations, namely ( 1.1 ) without mass terms ( $\nu_{1}=\nu_{2}=0$ ), an improvement was obtained in [ 5 ] for the blow-up results and the lifespan. The results in [ 5 ] are ameliorating the ones in [ 18 ] . In this article, we refine the results in [ 18 ] when at least one of the coefficients $\delta_{i},i=1,2$ , is in $[0,1)$ . In fact, for positive values of $\delta_{i}$ ( $i=1,2$ ), we extend the results obtained in [ 18 ] to show that the new blow-up region does not depend on the mass parameters $\nu_{i},i=1,2$ (and hence nor on $\sigma_{i},i=1,2$ ). A similar observation was concluded for the case of a one equation that we studied in [ 3 ] , and the aim here is to extend this examination to the case of the coupled system ( 1.1 ). But, although the obtaining of similar results for damped coupled systems with mass terms is predictable, the situation is somehow more delicate. Indeed, the techniques used in [ 3 ] are now longer effective for the system ( 1.1 ). To overcome this difficulty, we first write the linear problem associated with ( 1.1 ) which reduces in this case to the following single equation: (1.13) $\displaystyle\displaystyle w^{L}_{tt}-\Delta w^{L}+\frac{\mu_{i}}{1+t}w^{L}_{t}+\frac{\nu_{i}^{2}}{(1+t)^{2}}w^{L}=0,$ where $w$ stands for $u^{L}$ or $v^{L}$ , the solutions of the linear problem associated with ( 1.1 ). Clearly, the equation ( 1.13 ) is invariant under the following transformation: (1.14) $\displaystyle\tilde{w}^{L}(x,t)=w^{L}(\alpha x,\alpha(1+t)-1),\ \alpha>0.$ Taking advantage of the aforementioned invariance properties, we refine our choice for a functional family that is indexed by a positive parameter $\eta$ . This judicious choice implies a better description of the dynamics of the solution of ( 1.1 ). More precisely, we obtain, for $\eta$ large enough, the coercivity of the functional that will be introduced later on to show the blow-up results. Thanks to the above observations, we finally enhance the result on the blow-up region, defined by ( 1.11 ) and obtained in [ 18 ] , by showing that the critical curve is characterized by (1.15) $\Omega(N,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},p,q)=\max(\Lambda(N+\mu_{1},p,q),\Lambda(N+\mu_{2},q,p))\geq 0,$ where $\Lambda$ is given by ( 1.9 ). The outline of this article is presented as follows. First, we introduce in Section 2 the weak formulation of ( 1.1 ) in the energy space. Then, in the same section, we state our main result. Some technical lemmas are proven in Section 3 . Finally, we show the proof of the main result in Section 4 . 2. Main Result The aim of this section is to state our main result for which we will write the equivalent of the system ( 1.1 ) in the corresponding energy space. More precisely, the weak formulation associated with ( 1.1 ) reads as follows: Definition 2.1 . We say that $(u,v)$ is an energy solution of
( 1.1 ) on $[0,T)$ if $\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u,v\in\mathcal{C}([0,T),H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}))\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,T),L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})),\\
\ u_{t}\in L^{q}_{loc}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}),\ v_{t}\in L^{p}_{loc}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N})\end{array}\right.$ satisfies, for all $\Phi,\tilde{\Phi}\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times[0,T))$ and all $t\in[0,T)$ , the following equations: (2.1) $\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}u_{t}(x,t)\Phi(x,t)dx-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}u_{t}(x,0)\Phi(x,0)dx-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}u_{t}(x,s)\Phi_{t}(x,s)dx\,ds\\
\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\nabla u(x,s)\cdot\nabla\Phi(x,s)dx\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{\mu_{1}}{1+s}u_{t}(x,s)\Phi(x,s)dx\,ds\\
\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{\nu_{1}^{2}}{(1+s)^{2}}u(x,s)\Phi(x,s)dx\,ds=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|v_{t}(x,s)|^{p}\Phi(x,s)dx\,ds,\end{array}$ and (2.2) $\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}v_{t}(x,t)\tilde{\Phi}(x,t)dx-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}v_{t}(x,0)\tilde{\Phi}(x,0)dx-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}v_{t}(x,s)\tilde{\Phi}_{t}(x,s)dx\,ds\\
\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\nabla v(x,s)\cdot\nabla\tilde{\Phi}(x,s)dx\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{\mu_{2}}{1+s}v_{t}(x,s)\tilde{\Phi}(x,s)dx\,ds\\
\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{\nu_{2}^{2}}{(1+s)^{2}}v(x,s)\tilde{\Phi}(x,s)dx\,ds=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|u_{t}(x,s)|^{q}\tilde{\Phi}(x,s)dx\,ds,\end{array}$ together with the conditions $u(x,0)=\varepsilon f_{1}(x)$ and $v(x,0)=\varepsilon f_{2}(x)$ being satisfied in $H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})$ . After some elementary computations, ( 2.1 ) and ( 2.2 ) can be written, respectively, in the following way (2.3) $\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\big[u_{t}(x,t)\Phi(x,t)-u(x,t)\Phi_{t}(x,t)+\frac{\mu_{1}}{1+t}u(x,t)\Phi(x,t)\big]dx\\
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}u(x,s)\left[\Phi_{tt}(x,s)-\Delta\Phi(x,s)-\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\left(\frac{\mu}{1+s}\Phi(x,s)\right)+\frac{\nu_{1}^{2}}{(1+s)^{2}}\Phi(x,s)\right]dx\,ds\\
\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|u_{t}(x,s)|^{p}\Phi(x,s)dx\,ds+{\varepsilon}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\big[-f_{1}(x)\Phi_{t}(x,0)+\left(\mu_{1}f_{1}(x)+g_{1}(x)\right)\Phi(x,0)\big]dx,\end{array}$ and (2.4) $\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\big[v_{t}(x,t)\tilde{\Phi}(x,t)-v(x,t)\tilde{\Phi}_{t}(x,t)+\frac{\mu_{2}}{1+t}u(x,t)\tilde{\Phi}(x,t)\big]dx\\
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}v(x,s)\left[\tilde{\Phi}_{tt}(x,s)-\Delta\tilde{\Phi}(x,s)-\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\left(\frac{\mu_{2}}{1+s}\tilde{\Phi}(x,s)\right)+\frac{\nu_{2}^{2}}{(1+s)^{2}}\tilde{\Phi}(x,s)\right]dx\,ds\\
\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|v_{t}(x,s)|^{p}\tilde{\Phi}(x,s)dx\,ds+{\varepsilon}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\big[-f_{2}(x)\Phi_{t}(x,0)+\left(\mu_{2}f_{2}(x)+g_{2}(x)\right)\tilde{\Phi}(x,0)\big]dx.\end{array}$ Remark 2.1 . Since $f$ and $g$ are supported on $B_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}(0,R)$ , one can see that $\mbox{\rm supp}(u),\mbox{\rm supp}(v)\ \subset\{(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times[0,\infty):|x|\leq t+R\}$ . Consequently, one can choose any test function $\Phi$ which is not necessarily compactly supported. In the following, we state the main result of this article. Theorem 2.2 . Let $p,q>1$ . For $i=1,2$ , let $\mu_{i},\nu_{i}^{2}>0$ , with $\delta_{i}:=\delta(\mu_{i},\nu_{i})>0$ (see ( 1.5 )),
such that (2.5) $\Omega(N,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},p,q)\geq 0,$ where $\Omega$ is defined by ( 1.11 ). Assume that $f_{1},f_{2}\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})$ and $g_{1},g_{2}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})$ are non-negative functions which are compactly supported on $B_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}(0,R)$ ,
and do not vanish everywhere. Furthermore, we suppose that (2.6) $\frac{\mu_{i}-1-\sqrt{\delta_{i}}}{2}f_{i}(x)+g_{i}(x)\geq 0,\quad i=1,2.$ Let $(u,v)$ be an energy solution of ( 2.1 )-( 2.2 ) on $[0,T_{\varepsilon})$ such that $\mbox{\rm supp}(u),{\rm supp}(v)\ \subset\{(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times[1,\infty):|x|\leq t+R\}$ .
Then, there exists a constant ${\varepsilon}_{0}={\varepsilon}_{0}(f_{1},f_{2},g_{1},g_{2},N,R,p,q,\mu_{1},\mu_{2})>0$ such that $T_{\varepsilon}$ verifies (2.7) $T({\varepsilon})\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}C{\varepsilon}^{-\Omega(N,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},p,q)}&\text{if}&\Omega(N,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},p,q)>0,\\
\exp(C{\varepsilon}^{-(pq-1)})&\text{if}&\Omega(N,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},p,q)=0,\\
\exp(C{\varepsilon}^{-\min\left(\frac{pq-1}{p+1},\frac{pq-1}{q+1}\right)})&\text{if}&\Lambda(N+\mu_{1},p,q)=\Lambda(N+\mu_{2},q,p)=0.\end{array}\right.$ where $C$ is a positive constant independent of ${\varepsilon}$ and $0<{\varepsilon}\leq{\varepsilon}_{0}$ . Remark 2.2 . The blow-up result in Theorem 2.2 exhibits the new region obtained for the critical curve for $p,q$ as a shift of the dimension by $\mu_{1},\mu_{2}$ . We believe that this new blow-up region delimitation coincides with the critical one. Of course, a rigorous confirmation should be proved by a global existence result. Remark 2.3 . The result in Theorem 2.2 holds true for the following generalized system: (2.8) $\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle u_{tt}-\Delta u+\left(b_{1}(t)+\frac{\mu_{1}}{1+t}\right)u_{t}+\left(c_{1}(t)+\frac{\nu_{1}^{2}}{(1+t)^{2}}\right)u=|\partial_{t}v|^{p},&\ (x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times[0,\infty),\\
\displaystyle v_{tt}-\Delta v+\left(b_{2}(t)+\frac{\mu_{2}}{1+t}\right)v_{t}+\left(c_{2}(t)+\frac{\nu_{2}^{2}}{(1+t)^{2}}\right)v=|\partial_{t}u|^{q},&\ (x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times[0,\infty),\end{cases}$ where $b_{i}(t),(1+t)c_{i}(t)$ belong to $L^{1}(0,\infty)$ and $\mu_{i},\nu_{i}$ are such that $\delta_{i}>0$ ; $i=1,2$ . The proof of the generalized damping case ( 2.8 ) can be performed by mimicking the one of Theorem 2.2 with the necessary modifications. Remark 2.4 . The techniques used in this article can be of course adapted in other contexts. More precisely, the case of a single equation corresponding to ( 1.1 ) can be simplified by taking into account the invariance property ( 1.14 ) which is related to the introduction of the parameter $\eta$ . Furthermore, one can use the aforementioned techniques to study systems like ( 1.1 ) with mixed nonlinearities, this will be the subject of a forthcoming work which will somehow constitute an extension of our previous works [ 4 , 5 , 6 ] , see also [ 13 , 17 ] . 3. Some auxiliary results We first introduce a positive test function which is defined as (3.1) $\psi_{i}^{\eta}(x,t):=\rho_{i}^{\eta}(t)\phi^{\eta}(x),\ i=1,2,\ \forall\ \eta>0,$ where (3.2) $\phi^{\eta}(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\int_{S^{N-1}}e^{\eta x\cdot\omega}d\omega&\mbox{for}\ N\geq 2,\\
e^{\eta x}+e^{-\eta x}&\mbox{for}\ N=1.\end{array}\right.$ Note that the function $\phi^{\eta}(x)$ is introduced in [ 25 ] and $\rho_{i}^{\eta}(t)$ , [ 15 , 19 , 21 , 22 ] , is solution of (3.3) $\frac{d^{2}\rho_{i}^{\eta}(t)}{dt^{2}}-\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\mu_{i}}{1+t}\rho_{i}^{\eta}(t)\right)+\left(\frac{\nu_{i}^{2}}{(1+t)^{2}}-\eta^{2}\right)\rho_{i}^{\eta}(t)=0,\ i=1,2.$ From the literature, it is well-known that the expression of $\rho_{i}^{\eta}(t)$ is given by (see the Appendix for more details), (3.4) $\rho_{i}^{\eta}(t)=(\eta(t+1))^{\frac{\mu_{i}+1}{2}}K_{\frac{\sqrt{\delta_{i}}}{2}}(\eta(t+1)),\ i=1,2,$ where $K_{\nu}(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-t\cosh\zeta)\cosh(\nu\zeta)d\zeta,\ \nu\in\mathbb{R}.$ Furthermore, we have that the function $\phi^{\eta}(x)$ satisfies $\Delta\phi^{\eta}=\eta^{2}\phi^{\eta}.$ One can easily see that the function $\psi_{i}^{\eta}(x,t)$ fulfills the following conjugate equation: (3.5) $\partial^{2}_{t}\psi_{i}^{\eta}(x,t)-\Delta\psi_{i}^{\eta}(x,t)-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\frac{\mu_{i}}{1+t}\psi_{i}^{\eta}(x,t)\right)+\frac{\nu_{i}^{2}}{(1+t)^{2}}\psi_{i}^{\eta}(x,t)=0.$ In what follows and through this article, the constant $C$ stands for any generic positive number which may depend on the data ( $p,q,\mu_{i},N,R,f_{i},g_{i}$ ) i=1,2 but not on $\varepsilon$ and whose value may change from line to line. However, in some occurrences and when it is necessary, we will precise the dependence of the constant $C$ on the parameters involved in this work. Now, we state without proof the following lemma which gives a useful estimate for the function $\psi_{i}^{\eta}(x,t)$ . Lemma 3.1 ( [ 25 ] ) . Let $r>1$ .
Then, there exists a constant $C=C(\eta,N,R,r)>0$ such that (3.6) $\int_{|x|\leq t+R}\Big(\phi^{\eta}(x)\Big)^{r}dx\leq Ce^{rt}(1+t)^{\frac{(2-r)(N-1)}{2}},\quad\forall\ t\geq 0.$ In order to show the blow-up result later on, the following functionals are introduced here. (3.7) $F_{1}^{\eta}(t):=e^{-\eta t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}u(x,t)\phi^{\eta}(x)dx,\quad F_{2}^{\eta}(t):=e^{-\eta t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}v(x,t)\phi^{\eta}(x)dx,$ and (3.8) $\tilde{F}_{1}^{\eta}(t):=e^{-\eta t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\partial_{t}u(x,t)\phi^{\eta}(x)dx,\quad\tilde{F}_{2}^{\eta}(t):=e^{-\eta t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\partial_{t}v(x,t)\phi^{\eta}(x)dx,$ where $\eta$ is a positive constant that will be determined later on. We also define the multiplier $m_{i}(t)$ for $i=1,2$ as follows: (3.9) $m_{i}(t)=(1+t)^{\mu_{i}},\quad i=1,2.$ Hence, the next two lemmas give the first lower bounds for $F_{i}^{\eta}(t)$ and $\tilde{F}_{i}^{\eta}(t)$ ,  i=1,2, respectively. Lemma 3.2 . Assume that the assumption in Theorem 2.2 holds.
