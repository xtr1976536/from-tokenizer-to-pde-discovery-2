Kuntal Bhandari $\,{}^{\dagger}$ Date: August 24, 2026 Abstract. This paper is concerned with the existence of insensitizing controls for a nonlinear coupled system of two Korteweg-de Vries (KdV) equations, typically known as the Hirota-Satsuma system.
The idea is to look for controls such that some functional of the states (the so-called sentinel) is insensitive to the small perturbations of initial data. Since the system is coupled,
we consider a sentinel in which we observe both components of the system in a localized observation set. By some classical argument, the insensitizing problem is then reduced to a null-control problem for an extended system where the number of equations is doubled. We study the null-controllability for the linearized model associated to that extended system by means of a suitable Carleman estimate which is proved in this paper. Finally, the local null-controllability of the extended (nonlinear) system is obtained by applying the inverse mapping theorem, and this implies the required insensitizing property for the concerned model. Key words and phrases: Korteweg-de Vries system, insensitizing control, Carleman estimate, observability, inverse mapping theorem 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification 35K52 - 35Q53 - 93B05 - 93B07 - 93B35 1. Introduction 1.1. Statement of the problem In this article, we study an insensitizing control problem for the Hirota-Satsuma system of two coupled Korteweg-de Vries (KdV) equations. In $1981$ , R. Hirota and J. Satsuma [ 32 ] have proposed a system of two coupled KdV equations, namely $\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{t}-\frac{1}{2}u_{xxx}-3uu_{x}+6vv_{x}=0,\\
v_{t}+v_{xxx}+3uv_{x}=0,\end{cases}$ which describes the interactions of two long waves with different dispersion relations. They have presented a soliton solution of the above system, and shown that it has two- and three-soliton solutions under a special connection between the dispersion relations of the two long waves; we refer [ 32 ] for more details. Let us describe the problem on which we are going to work in the present article. Let $T>0$ be given finite time and $L>0$ be any finite length. Denote $Q_{T}:=(0,T)\times(0,L)$ . We further assume two non-empty open sets $\omega\subset(0,L)$ and $\mathcal{O}\subset(0,L)$ verifying $\mathcal{O}\cap\omega\neq\emptyset$ , and that there is a non-empty open set $\omega_{0}$ such that $\omega_{0}\subset\subset\mathcal{O}\cap\omega$ . We now consider the following coupled KdV system: (1.1) $\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{t}-\frac{1}{2}u_{xxx}-3uu_{x}+6vv_{x}=h_{1}\mathds{1}_{\omega}+{\xi}_{1}&\text{in }Q_{T},\\
v_{t}+v_{xxx}+3uv_{x}=h_{2}\mathds{1}_{\omega}+\xi_{2}&\text{in }Q_{T},\\
u(t,0)=u(t,L)=u_{x}(t,0)=0&\text{for }t\in(0,T),\\
v(t,0)=v(t,L)=v_{x}(t,L)=0&\text{for }t\in(0,T),\\
u(0)=u_{0}+\tau\widehat{u}_{0},\ \,v(0)=v_{0}+\tau\widehat{v}_{0}&\text{in }(0,L),\end{cases}$ where $u=u(t,x)$ and $v=v(t,x)$ are the state variables, $h_{i}=h_{i}(t,x)$ , $i=1,2$ are localized control functions acting on the subset $\omega$ , and $\xi_{i}=\xi_{i}(t,x)$ , $i=1,2$ , are given external source terms. In ( 1.1 ) the initial states $(u(0),v(0))$ are partially unknown in the following sense: – $(u_{0},v_{0})\in[L^{2}(0,L)]^{2}$ are given, and – $(\widehat{u}_{0},\widehat{v}_{0})\in[L^{2}(0,L)]^{2}$ are unknown which satisfy $\|\widehat{u}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,L)}=\|\widehat{v}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,L)}=1$ . They represent some uncertainty on the initial data. – $\tau\in\mathbb{R}$ is some unknown parameter which is small enough. Our aim is to study the insensitizing control problem for the system ( 1.1 ). This topic has been originally introduced by J.-L. Lions [ 36 ] which concerns with the existence of controls that make a certain functional (depending on the state variables) insensible
with respect to small perturbations of the initial data. In our case, we consider the following functional (the so-called sentinel ) defined on the set of solutions to ( 1.1 ), given by (1.2) $\displaystyle J_{\tau}(u,v):=\frac{1}{2}\iint_{(0,T)\times\mathcal{O}}|u|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\iint_{(0,T)\times\mathcal{O}}|v|^{2},$ where $\mathcal{O}$ is the so-called observation domain . Then, the insensitizing control problem associated to ( 1.1 ) can be stated as follows. Let $(u_{0},v_{0})\in[L^{2}(0,L)]^{2}$ and $(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in[L^{2}(Q_{T})]^{2}$ be given. We say that the control functions $(h_{1},h_{2})\in[L^{2}((0,T)\times\omega)]^{2}$ insensitize the sentinel functional $J_{\tau}$ given by ( 1.2 ), if (1.3) $\left.\frac{\partial J_{\tau}(u,v)}{\partial\tau}\right|_{\tau=0}=0,\quad\forall\,(\widehat{u}_{0},\widehat{v}_{0})\in[L^{2}(0,L)]^{2}\textnormal{ with }\|\widehat{u}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,L)}=\|\widehat{v}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,L)}=1.$ Thus, it amounts to find a pair of control functions $(h_{1},h_{2})\in[L^{2}((0,T)\times\omega)]^{2}$ such that the uncertainty in the initial data does not affect the measurement of $J_{\tau}$ . When the condition ( 1.3 ) holds for a pair of controls $(h_{1},h_{2})$ , the sentinel $J_{\tau}$ is said to be locally insensitive to the perturbations of initial data. In other words, ( 1.3 ) indicates that
the sentinel does not detect the variations
of the given initial data $(u_{0},v_{0})$ by small unknown perturbation $(\tau\widehat{u}_{0},\tau\widehat{v}_{0})$ in the observation domain $\mathcal{O}$ . 1.2. Bibliographic comments To begin with, the controllability of dispersive systems is gaining its popularity among several researchers; the first internal controllability results regarding the single KdV equations have been presented by Russell and Zhang [ 41 , 42 ] in the periodic domains. We also cite the work [ 35 ] by Rosier and Zhang related to this topic. In [ 26 ] , Glass and Guerrero proved the exact internal controllability of the KdV equation when the control acts in a neighborhood of the left endpoint. Later on, Capistrano-Filho, Pazoto and Rosier [ 12 ] established a Carleman estimate yielding an observability inequality to conclude the internal controllability for the KdV equations.
More recently, the internal null-controllability of a generalized Hirota-Satsuma system (which is a coupled system of three KdV equations with a first order coupling) has been proved by Carreño, Cerpa and Crépeau in [ 15 ] . On the other hand, the boundary controllability for KdV equations has been intensively investigated by several renowned researchers, see for instance, [ 18 , 19 , 43 , 21 , 27 , 22 , 40 ] . Most of those works were concerned with the system (1.4) $\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{t}+u_{x}+u_{xxx}+uu_{x}=0&\text{in }(0,T)\times(0,L),\\
u(t,0)=p_{1}(t),\ u(t,L)=p_{2}(t),\ u_{x}(t,L)=p_{3}(t)&\text{in }(0,T),\end{cases}$ where $p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}$ are control inputs. In particular,
Rosier [ 40 ] proved that the linearized model of ( 1.4 ) is exactly controllable with a control $p_{3}\in L^{2}(0,T)$ (and $p_{1}=p_{2}=0$ ) if and only if $L$ does not belong to the following countable set of critical length $\displaystyle\mathcal{N}=\left\{\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{3}}\sqrt{k^{2}+kl+l^{2}}\,:\,k,l\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\right\}.$ He has also shown that when the linearized equation is controllable, the same holds true for the nonlinear equation. But the converse is not necessarily true,
as it has been proved in [ 18 , 19 , 22 ] that the nonlinear KdV equation is still
controllable even if $L\in\mathcal{N}$ . Finally, we refer [ 11 , 39 , 20 ] where the boundary controllability of some system of KdV equations have been addressed. Let us talk about the insensitizing control problems for pdes. We mention that the pioneer results concerning the existence of insensitizing controls were obtained by de Teresa in [ 23 ] , and by Bodart et al. in [ 5 ] for the linear and semilinear heat equations. Since then, many works have been devoted to study the insensitizing problem from different perspectives. The authors in [ 7 , 6 , 8 ] study such problems for linear and semilinear heat equations with different types of nonlinearities and/or boundary conditions. In the direction of wave equation, Alabau-Boussouira [ 1 ] proved the existence of exact insensitizing controls for the scalar wave equation. Guerrero [ 29 ] has considered an insensitizing control problem for the linear parabolic equation where the sentinel functional is dependent on the gradient of the solution. Later, he has studied such control problem for the Stokes equation [ 28 ] where the sentinel is taken in terms of the curl of solution .
In the context of insensitizing problems for Navier-Stokes equations we quote the works [ 30 , 17 ] , and for the Boussinesq system we cite [ 16 , 14 ] . We also refer [ 10 ] where the insensitizing control problem for a phase field system has been explored. Furthermore, a numerical study for the insensitizing property of semilinear parabolic equations has been pursued in [ 9 ] . It is worth mentioning that insensitizing problems for the fourth-order parabolic equations have been treated in [ 33 ] and with respect to shape variations in [ 38 , 25 ] . We also bring up a recent work [ 13 ] where the authors studied the insensitizing property for the fourth-order dispersive nonlinear Schrödinger equation with cubic nonlinearity.
Last but not the least, we address a very recent work [ 4 ] where the insensitizing control problems for the stabilized Kuramoto-Sivashinsky system have been analyzed. In the current work, we investigate the insensitizing property of the so-called Hirota-Satsuma model, which is basically a nonlinear coupled KdV system as mentioned earlier, and to the best of our knowledge, this problem has not been addressed in the literature. 1.3. Main results As mentioned earlier, our goal is to prove the existence of control functions $(h_{1},h_{2})$ which insensitize the functional $J_{\tau}$ given by ( 1.2 ).
In this regard, our control result is the following. Theorem 1.1 . Assume that $\mathcal{O}\cap\omega\neq\emptyset$ and $u_{0}\equiv v_{0}\equiv 0$ . Then, there exist constants $C>0$ and $\delta>0$ such that for any $(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in[L^{2}(Q_{T})]^{2}$ satisfying (1.5) $\|e^{C/t}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\|_{[L^{2}(Q_{T})]^{2}}\leq\delta,$ one can prove the existence of control functions $(h_{1},h_{2})\in[L^{2}((0,T)\times\omega)]^{2}$ which insensitize the functional $J_{\tau}$ in the sense of ( 1.3 ). To prove the above theorem, we shall equivalently establish the result given by Theorem 1.3 below. In fact, adapting the arguments in [ 5 , Proposition 1] or [ 24 , Appendix] , it can be proved that the insensitivity condition ( 1.3 ) is equivalent to a null-control problem for an extended system, which is in our case given by (1.6) $\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{t}-\frac{1}{2}u_{xxx}-3uu_{x}+6vv_{x}=h_{1}\mathds{1}_{\omega}+\xi_{1}&\text{in }Q_{T},\\
v_{t}+v_{xxx}+3uv_{x}=h_{2}\mathds{1}_{\omega}+\xi_{2}&\text{in }Q_{T},\\
u(t,0)=u(t,L)=u_{x}(t,0)=0&\text{for }t\in(0,T),\\
v(t,0)=v(t,L)=v_{x}(t,L)=0&\text{for }t\in(0,T),\\
u(0)=u_{0},\ \ v(0)=v_{0}&\text{in }(0,L),\end{cases}$ (1.7) $\displaystyle\begin{cases}-p_{t}+\frac{1}{2}p_{xxx}-3pu_{x}+3qv_{x}=u\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{O}}&\text{in }Q_{T},\\
-q_{t}-q_{xxx}+6pv_{x}=v\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{O}}&\text{in }Q_{T},\\
p(t,0)=p(t,L)=p_{x}(t,L)=0&\text{for }t\in(0,T),\\
q(t,0)=q(t,L)=q_{x}(t,0)=0&\text{for }t\in(0,T),\\
p(T)=0,\ \ q(T)=0&\text{in }(0,L),\end{cases}$ and we have the following result. Proposition 1.2 . A pair of control functions $(h_{1},h_{2})\in[L^{2}((0,T)\times\omega)]^{2}$ verifies the insensitivity condition ( 1.3 ) for the sentinel ( 1.2 ) if and only if the associated solution to ( 1.6 )–( 1.7 ) satisfies (1.8) $(p(0),q(0))=(0,0)\quad\textnormal{in }(0,L).$ In what follows, we only focus on studying the controllability properties for the $4\times 4$ forward-backward system ( 1.6 )–( 1.7 ). Indeed, we prove the following theorem which is the main result of our paper. Theorem 1.3 (Local null-controllability of the extended system) . Assume that $\mathcal{O}\cap\omega\neq\emptyset$ and $u_{0}\equiv v_{0}\equiv 0$ . Then, there exist constants $C>0$ and $\delta>0$ such that for any $(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in[L^{2}(Q_{T})]^{2}$ verifying (1.9) $\|e^{C/t}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\|_{[L^{2}(Q_{T})]^{2}}\leq\delta,$ there exist control functions $(h_{1},h_{2})\in[L^{2}((0,T)\times\omega)]^{2}$ such that the solution $(u,v,p,q)$ to ( 1.6 )–( 1.7 ) satisfies $p(0)=q(0)=0$ in $(0,L)$ . As usual, to prove Theorem 1.3 , one needs to first establish a global null-controllability result for the linearized (around zero) model associated to ( 1.6 )–( 1.7 ). More precisely, we consider the following system: (1.10) $\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{t}-\frac{1}{2}u_{xxx}=h_{1}\mathds{1}_{\omega}+f_{1}&\text{in }Q_{T},\\
v_{t}+v_{xxx}=h_{2}\mathds{1}_{\omega}+f_{2}&\text{in }Q_{T},\\
u(t,0)=u(t,L)=u_{x}(t,0)=0&\text{for }t\in(0,T),\\
v(t,0)=v(t,L)=v_{x}(t,L)=0&\text{for }t\in(0,T),\\
u(0)=u_{0},\ \ v(0)=v_{0}&\text{in }(0,L),\end{cases}$ (1.11) $\displaystyle\begin{cases}-p_{t}+\frac{1}{2}p_{xxx}=u\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{O}}+f_{3}&\text{in }Q_{T},\\
-q_{t}-q_{xxx}=v\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{O}}+f_{4}&\text{in }Q_{T},\\
p(t,0)=p(t,L)=p_{x}(t,L)=0&\text{for }t\in(0,T),\\
q(t,0)=q(t,L)=q_{x}(t,0)=0&\text{for }t\in(0,T),\\
p(T)=0,\ \ q(T)=0&\text{in }(0,L).\end{cases}$ with given right hand sides $(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})$ from some certain space (specified later). Note that, the controls $(h_{1}\mathds{1}_{\omega},h_{2}\mathds{1}_{\omega})$ act directly in the equations of $(u,v)$ while the equations of $(p,q)$ are indirectly controlled via the couplings $(u\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{O}},v\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{O}})$ . At this point, one can observe that the condition $\mathcal{O}\cap\omega\neq\emptyset$ is necessary to obtain the required
null-controllability result for the extended system ( 1.10 )–( 1.11 ), in other words, the insensitizing property for the main system ( 1.1 ). As we know, proving the null-controllability of ( 1.10 )–( 1.11 ) is equivalent to determine the observability property of its adjoint system, which is given by (1.12) $\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\eta_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\eta_{xxx}=\zeta\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{O}}+g_{1}&\text{in }Q_{T},\\
-\psi_{t}-\psi_{xxx}=\theta\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{O}}+g_{2}&\text{in }Q_{T},\\
\eta(t,0)=\eta(t,L)=\eta_{x}(t,L)=0&\text{for }t\in(0,T),\\
\psi(t,0)=\psi(t,L)=\psi_{x}(t,0)=0&\text{for }t\in(0,T),\\
\eta(T)=0,\ \ \psi(T)=0&\text{in }(0,L),\end{cases}$ (1.13) $\displaystyle\begin{cases}\zeta_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\zeta_{xxx}=g_{3}&\text{in }Q_{T},\\
\theta_{t}+\theta_{xxx}=g_{4}&\text{in }Q_{T},\\
\zeta(t,0)=\zeta(t,L)=\zeta_{x}(t,0)=0&\text{for }t\in(0,T),\\
\theta(t,0)=\theta(t,L)=\theta_{x}(t,L)=0&\text{for }t\in(0,T),\\
\zeta(0)=\zeta_{0},\ \ \theta(0)=\theta_{0}&\text{in }(0,L).\end{cases}$ with given $(\zeta_{0},\theta_{0})\in[L^{2}(0,L)]^{2}$ and source terms $(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},g_{4})$ from some suitable space, specified later. The problem amounts to establish a suitable Carleman estimate satisfied by the state variables
of the $4\times 4$ system ( 1.12 )–( 1.13 ) with only two observation terms, namely $\eta$ and $\psi$ . We shall discuss it at length in Section 3 . Paper Organization The paper is organized as follows.
Section 2 contains the well-posedness results of the underlying coupled KdV systems. Then, in Section 3 we prove a suitable Carleman estimate for the $4\times 4$ adjoint system ( 1.12 )–( 1.13 ).
The Carleman estimate (see Theorem 3.3 ) then yields an observability inequality which is obtained in Subsection 4.1 , precisely Proposition 4.2 . In Subsection 4.2 , we establish the null-controllability of the linearized model ( 1.10 )–( 1.11 ), thanks to the appropriate observability inequality. Afterthat, in Section 5 ,
we prove the local null-controllability of the system ( 1.6 )–( 1.7 ), which is precisely the proof of Theorem 1.3 . Finally, we conclude our paper by providing several remarks in Section 6 . Notation Throughout the paper, $C>0$ denotes a generic constant that may vary line to line and depend on $\mathcal{O}$ , $\omega$ , $L$ and $T$ . 2. Well-posedness results 2.1. Functional setting and well-posedness of single KdV equation We start by introducing the following functional spaces: (2.1) $\displaystyle X_{0}:=L^{2}(0,T;H^{-2}(0,L)),\quad\ X_{1}:=L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}_{0}(0,L)),$ $\displaystyle\widetilde{X}_{0}:=L^{1}(0,T;H^{-1}(0,L)),\quad\ \widetilde{X}_{1}:=L^{1}(0,T;H^{3}(0,L)\cap H^{2}_{0}(0,L)),$ and (2.2) $\displaystyle Y_{0}:=L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(0,L))\cap\mathcal{C}^{0}([0,T];H^{-1}(0,L)),$ $\displaystyle Y_{1}:=L^{2}(0,T;H^{4}(0,L))\cap\mathcal{C}^{0}([0,T];H^{3}(0,L)),$ which are equipped with their usual norms. For each $\mu\in[0,1]$ , we further define the interpolation spaces (see for instance [ 3 , 37 ] ): (2.3) $X_{\mu}:=(X_{0},X_{1})_{[\mu]},\quad\ \widetilde{X}_{\mu}:=(\widetilde{X}_{0},\widetilde{X}_{1})_{[\mu]},\quad\ Y_{\mu}:=(Y_{0},Y_{1})_{[\mu]}.$ In particular, we have (2.4) $\displaystyle\begin{cases}X_{\frac{1}{4}}=L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(0,L)),\quad\widetilde{X}_{\frac{1}{4}}=L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(0,L)),\\
Y_{\frac{1}{4}}=L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(0,L))\cap\mathcal{C}^{0}([0,T];L^{2}(0,L)),\end{cases}$ (2.5) $\displaystyle\begin{cases}X_{\frac{1}{2}}=L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(0,L)),\quad\widetilde{X}_{\frac{1}{2}}=L^{1}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(0,L)),\\
Y_{\frac{1}{2}}=L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(0,L))\cap\mathcal{C}^{0}([0,T];H^{1}(0,L)).\end{cases}$ Also, one can observe that for any $\nu\in(0,1]$ , (2.6) $\displaystyle\begin{cases}X_{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\nu}{4}}=L^{2}(0,T;H^{\nu}(0,L)),\quad\widetilde{X}_{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\nu}{4}}=L^{1}(0,T;H^{1+\nu}(0,L)),\\
Y_{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\nu}{4}}=L^{2}(0,T;H^{2+\nu}(0,L))\cap\mathcal{C}^{0}([0,T];H^{1+\nu}(0,L)).\end{cases}$ Let us now consider the single KdV equation given by (2.7) $\displaystyle\begin{cases}y_{t}\pm y_{xxx}=f&\text{in }Q_{T},\\
y(t,0)=y(t,L)=y_{x}(t,L)=0&\text{for }(0,T),\\
y(0,x)=y_{0}(x)&\text{in }(0,L).\end{cases}$ with given source term $f$ and initial data $y_{0}$ . We recall the following known results for ( 2.7 ). Lemma 2.1 ( [ 26 , Section 2.2.2] ) . For given $y_{0}\in L^{2}(0,L)$ and $f\in F$ with $F=X_{\frac{1}{4}}$ or $\widetilde{X}_{\frac{1}{4}}$ , the system ( 2.7 ) admits a unique solution $y\in Y_{\frac{1}{4}}$ , and in addition, there exists a constant $C>0$ such that (2.8) $\displaystyle\|y\|_{Y_{\frac{1}{4}}}\leq C\left(\|y_{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,L)}+\|f\|_{F}\right).$ Lemma 2.2 ( [ 26 , Section 2.3.1] ) . For given $y_{0}\in H^{3}(0,L)$ with $y_{0}(0)=y_{0}(L)=y_{0}^{\prime}(L)=0$ , and $f\in F$ with $F=X_{1}$ or $\widetilde{X}_{1}$ , the system ( 2.7 ) admits a unique solution $y\in Y_{1}$ . In addition, there exists a constant $C>0$ such that (2.9) $\displaystyle\|y\|_{Y_{1}}\leq C\left(\|y_{0}\|_{H^{3}(0,L)}+\|f\|_{F}\right).$ Lemma 2.3 ( [ 26 , Section 2.3.2] ) . Let $y_{0}\equiv 0$ and $\mu\in[1/4,1]$ . Then, for given $f\in F$ with $F=X_{\mu}$ or $\widetilde{X}_{\mu}$ , the system ( 2.7 ) admits a unique solution $y\in Y_{\mu}$ , and moreover, there exists a constant $C>0$ such that (2.10) $\displaystyle\|y\|_{Y_{\mu}}\leq C\|f\|_{F}.$ Note that the above results are also applicable for the adjoint equation to ( 2.7 ) which is backward in time. 2.2. Well-posedness of the $4\times 4$ linearized system and its adjoint We state the following results. Proposition 2.4 . Let $(u_{0},v_{0})\in[L^{2}(0,L)]^{2}$ , $(h_{1},h_{2})\in[L^{2}((0,T)\times\omega)]^{2}$ and $(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})\in[F]^{4}$ with $F=X_{\frac{1}{4}}$ or $\widetilde{X}_{\frac{1}{4}}$ be given. Then, the system ( 1.10 )–( 1.11 ) possesses a unique solution $(u,v,p,q)\in\big[Y_{\frac{1}{4}}\big]^{4}$ . In addition, there exists a constant $C>0$ such that (2.11) $\displaystyle\|(u,v,p,q)\|_{[Y_{\frac{1}{4}}]^{4}}\leq C\Big(\|(u_{0},v_{0})\|_{[L^{2}(0,L)]^{2}}+\|(h_{1},h_{2})\|_{[L^{2}((0,T)\times\omega)]^{2}}+\|(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})\|_{[F]^{4}}\Big),$ where $Y_{\frac{1}{4}}$ is defined by ( 2.4 ). Proof. The proof of above proposition can be made in the following way. First, we use Lemma 2.1 to the set of equations ( 1.10 ) to show that $(u,v)\in\big[Y_{\frac{1}{4}}\big]^{2}$ along with the estimate (2.12) $\displaystyle\|(u,v)\|_{[Y_{\frac{1}{4}}]^{2}}\leq C\Big(\|(u_{0},v_{0})\|_{[L^{2}(0,L)]^{2}}+\|(h_{1},h_{2})\|_{[L^{2}((0,T)\times\omega)]^{2}}+\|(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{[F]^{2}}\Big).$ Then, using $(u\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{O}},v\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{O}})$ as source terms in the equations of $(p,q)$ given by ( 1.11 ), and combining with ( 2.12 ) we get the required estimate ( 2.11 ).
∎ Similar result holds for the adjoint system ( 1.12 )–( 1.13 ). Proposition 2.5 . Let $(\zeta_{0},\theta_{0})\in[L^{2}(0,L)]^{2}$ and $(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},g_{4})\in[F]^{4}$ with $F=X_{\frac{1}{4}}$ or $\widetilde{X}_{\frac{1}{4}}$ be given. Then, the system ( 1.12 )–( 1.13 ) admits a unique solution $(\eta,\psi,\zeta,\theta)\in\big[Y_{\frac{1}{4}}\big]^{4}$ and moreover, there exists a constant $C>0$ such that (2.13) $\displaystyle\|(\eta,\psi,\zeta,\theta)\|_{[Y_{\frac{1}{4}}]^{4}}\leq C\Big(\|(\zeta_{0},\theta_{0})\|_{[L^{2}(0,L)]^{2}}+\|(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},g_{4})\|_{[F]^{4}}\Big).$ 2.3. Well-posedness of the $4\times 4$ nonlinear system Using a fixed point theorem, we now prove the well-posedness of our $4\times 4$ nonlinear system ( 1.6 )–( 1.7 ). Proposition 2.6 . Let $T>0$ and $L>0$ . Then, there exists some positive real number $\delta_{0}$ such that for every $(u_{0},v_{0})\in[L^{2}(0,L)]^{2}$ , $(h_{1},h_{2})\in[L^{2}((0,T)\times\omega)]^{2}$ and $(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in[L^{2}(Q_{T})]^{2}$ , satisfying (2.14) $\displaystyle\|(u_{0},v_{0})\|_{[L^{2}(0,L)]^{2}}+\|(h_{1},h_{2})\|_{[L^{2}((0,T)\times\omega)]^{2}}+\|(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\|_{[L^{2}(Q_{T})]^{2}}\leq\delta_{0},$ the system ( 1.6 )–( 1.7 ) possesses a unique solution $\displaystyle(u,v,p,q)\in\big[Y_{\frac{1}{4}}\big]^{4},$ where $Y_{\frac{1}{4}}$ is defined by ( 2.4 ). Before going to the proof of above proposition, we prove the following lemma. Lemma 2.7 . Let $y_{1},y_{2}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(0,L))$ . Concluding remarks In this work, we have proved the existence of insensitizing controls for a system of two KdV equations, notably known as Hirota-Satsuma system.
The insensitizing problem is reformulated to an equivalent null-control problem for the $4\times 4$ system
( 1.6 )–( 1.7 ) with the action of only two localized controls .
To prove this result, we first established a Carleman estimate (see Theorem 3.3 ) for the adjoint system ( 1.12 )–( 1.13 ) to the linearized problem ( 1.10 )–( 1.11 ). This helped us to find a suitable observability inequality, namely ( 4.4 ), which lead to prove the global null-controllability of the linearized model ( 1.10 )–( 1.11 ). Finally, using the inverse mapping theorem, we conclude the local null-controllability of the system ( 1.6 )–( 1.7 ), and that implies the main result of this paper, i.e., Theorem 1.1 . Let us now present some concluding remarks concerning the problem addressed in this work. 1. Choice of initial data. As in other insensitizing problems, the assumption on the zero initial condition in Theorem 1.1 (i.e., $u_{0}=v_{0}=0$ ) is roughly related to the fact that system ( 1.6 )–( 1.7 ) is composed by forward and backward equations.
In fact, it has already been indicated in the work [ 24 ] (see also [ 23 ] ) that it is difficult to treat every initial data while studying the insensitizing control problems. 2. Condition on observation region. The assumption $\mathcal{O}\cap\omega\neq\emptyset$ is essential to prove a suitable Carleman estimate and hence the observability inequality (see Theorem 3.3 and Proposition 4.2 in this paper), which are the main ingredients for the proof of Theorem 1.3 . However, in [ 34 ] the authors proved that in the simpler case of heat equation, this condition is not necessary if one deals with an $\varepsilon$ -insensitizing problem (that is, $\Big|\frac{\partial J_{\tau}(u,v)}{\partial\tau}\big|_{\tau=0}\Big|\leq\varepsilon$ ). This remains an open problem for our case. 3. Less observation term in sentinel functional. Recall that, we have considered the following sentinel functional in this paper, (6.1) $\displaystyle J_{\tau}(u,v)=\frac{1}{2}\iint_{(0,T)\times\mathcal{O}}|u|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\iint_{(0,T)\times\mathcal{O}}|v|^{2}.$ If we drop any one of the two observations, then the extended linearized system ( 1.10 )–( 1.11 ) will not be controllable anymore by the methodology used in this paper. For instance, by choosing the sentinel functional $\displaystyle\widehat{J}_{\tau}(u,v)=\frac{1}{2}\iint_{(0,T)\times\mathcal{O}}|u|^{2}$ would lead the extended system as $\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{t}-\frac{1}{2}u_{xxx}-3uu_{x}+6vv_{x}=h_{1}\mathds{1}_{\omega}+\xi_{1}\ &\text{in }Q_{T},\\
v_{t}+v_{xxx}+3uv_{x}=h_{2}\mathds{1}_{\omega}+\xi_{2}\ &\text{in }Q_{T},\\
-p_{t}+\frac{1}{2}p_{xxx}-3pu_{x}+3qv_{x}=u\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{O}}\ &\text{in }Q_{T},\\
-q_{t}+q_{xxx}+6pv_{x}=0\ &\text{in }Q_{T},\end{cases}$ with the same initial-boundary conditions as in ( 1.6 )–( 1.7 ). But the associated linearized model to the above system is not null-controllable with two controls $(h_{1},h_{2})$ due to the lack of linear coupling in the equation of $q$ . Thus, the choice of ( 6.1 ) is somewhat necessary to study the insensitizing control property for our system ( 1.1 ), at least by means of the strategy developed in this paper. 4. Reduction of control input. It would be a challenging task if we reduce the number of controls in the system ( 1.1 ). As for instance, if we drop the action of $h_{2}$ in the second equation of ( 1.1 ), it is quite clear that the extended linearized system ( 1.10 )–( 1.11 ) cannot be controllable since there is no influence of control force in the equations of $v$ and $q$ .
Therefore, dealing with only one control instead of two in the main system ( 1.1 ) is really delicate and it needs further attention. Generally speaking, for the purpose of studying insensitizing control problems, we need to tackle with the controllability of an extended system where the number of controls are already less than the equations (in our case, we have two controls $(h_{1},h_{2})$ acting in the $4\times 4$ system ( 1.6 )–( 1.7 )).
Thus, reducing the number of controls in the main system would lead more obstacle to achieve the required controllability criterion for the extended system.
Similar phenomenon has also been addressed in [ 4 ] in the context of insensitizing problem for a coupled fourth- and second-order parabolic system, and in [ 31 ] in the framework of hierarchic control problems.
