, uniqueness 2020 MSC 35K55, 35K67, 35A02, 35Q92, 92C17. 1 Introduction The aim of this paper is to study the uniqueness of global weak solutions to the initial/boundary-value problem $\left\{\begin{aligned} &u_{t}=(uvu_{x})_{x}-(u^{2}vv_{x})_{x}+uv,\;\;&x\in\Omega,\;t>0,\\
&v_{t}=v_{xx}-uv,\;\;&x\in\Omega,\;t>0,\\
&uvu_{x}-u^{2}vv_{x}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\,v_{x}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,&t>0,\\
&u|_{t=0}=u^{0},\,v|_{t=0}=v^{0},&x\in\Omega,\\
\end{aligned}\right.$ (1) where $\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}$ is a bounded open interval, which describes the evolution of food-consuming bacterial populations in nutrient-poor environments. The equations have been proposed to describe the dynamics in collections of Bacillus subtilis ( [ 1 ] , [ 3 ] ).
It has been shown by Winkler [ 2 ] that ( 1 ) with assumption $\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mbox{~$u^{0}\in C^{\vartheta}(\overline{\Omega})$ for some $\vartheta\in(0,1)$,~with~$u^{0}\geq 0$ and~$\int_{\Omega}\ln u^{0}dx>-\infty$, }\\
&\mbox{~$v^{0}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)$ satisfies $v^{0}>0$ in~$\overline{\Omega}$ }\end{aligned}\right.$ (2) admits a global weak solution $(u,v)$ in the sense that for any $\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}([0,\infty)\times\overline{\Omega})$ , $\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}u\varphi_{t}dxdt-\int_{\Omega}u^{0}\varphi(\cdot,0)dx$ (3) $\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}uvu_{x}\varphi_{x}dxdt+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}u^{2}vv_{x}\varphi_{x}dxdt+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}uv\varphi dxdt,$ $\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}v\varphi_{t}dxdt-\int_{\Omega}v^{0}\varphi(\cdot,0)dx=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}v_{x}\varphi_{x}dxdt-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}uv\varphi dxdt,$ (4) which satisfies $\left\{\begin{aligned} &0\leq u\in C^{0}([0,\infty)\times\overline{\Omega})\cap L^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times\Omega),~u_{x}\in L^{2}_{loc}([0,\infty)\times\overline{\Omega}),~\\
&v\in C^{0}([0,\infty)\times\overline{\Omega})\cap C^{1,2}((0,\infty)\times\overline{\Omega})\cap L^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times\Omega),~v_{x}\in L^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times\Omega),~\\
&\mbox{for any fixed}~T,~v\geq c(T)>0.\end{aligned}\right.$ (5) The two-dimensional case was treated by Zhang and Li [ 4 ] . Our main result reads as follows. Theorem 1 . Let $(u,v)$ be a weak solution to ( 1 )-( 2 ) satisfying ( 3 )-( 5 ). If $1/u^{0}\in L^{1}(\Omega)$ , then $(u,v)$ is unique. Without loss of generality, we suppose that $\Omega=(0,1)$ . The key idea of the proof is the use of $w(t,x)=\int_{0}^{x}(u(t,y)+v(t,y))dy$ , such that the uniqueness of $(u,v)$ is equivalent to that of $(w,v)$ . More precisely, it follows from the $L^{1}(\Omega)$ -conservation of $u+v$ that $w(t,0)\equiv 0$ and $w(t,1)\equiv\|u^{0}+v^{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}$ which imply that $(w_{1}-w_{2})|_{\partial\Omega}=0$ in Lemma 4. The boundedness of $1/u$ in $L^{\infty}(0,\infty;L^{1}(\Omega))$ (Lemma 2 ) is used to control the term $\frac{v_{1}^{2}(v_{1}-v_{2})^{2}}{u_{1}+u_{2}}$ in ( 18 ) of Lemma 5 . We obtain Lemma 5 from Lemmas 2 - 4 and hence the uniqueness of $(w,v)$ by Gronwall’s inequality. 2 Proof of Theorem 1 Lemma 2 .
