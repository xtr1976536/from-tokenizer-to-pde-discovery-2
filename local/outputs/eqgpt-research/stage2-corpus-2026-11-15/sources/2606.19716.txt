University of California, Santa Barbara, CA 93106, USA. xy6@ucsb.edu Abstract We propose and analyze a structure-preserving numerical method for the $2\tfrac{1}{2}$ -dimensional (2.5D) electron magnetohydrodynamics system with fractional dissipation on the periodic torus. The method works directly with the magnetic field components and combines this component formulation with the gradient recovery operator of [T. Chu, H. Guo, and Z. Zhang, SIAM J. Numer. Anal., 63 (2025), pp. 23–53]. We establish discrete energy stability for a semi-implicit structure-preserving formulation and use an explicit-Hall integrating-factor implementation for efficient computation on periodic grids. The fractional dissipation is treated exactly in Fourier space, and the in-plane divergence constraint is enforced by a spectral Hodge projection. Numerical experiments demonstrate second-order spatial convergence and stable Hall-driven dynamics across several benchmark tests. keywords Electron magnetohydrodynamics, gradient recovery, fractional Laplacian, structure preserving, energy stability. † † runningheads: A gradient recovery method for EMHD / H. Yang MSC 65M60, 65M12, 76W05, 76E25. 1 Introduction The electron magnetohydrodynamics (EMHD) system $\partial_{t}\bm{B}+\nabla\times\!\bigl((\nabla\times\bm{B})\times\bm{B}\bigr)=\nu\Delta\bm{B},\qquad\nabla\cdot\bm{B}=0,$ (1) describes the evolution of a magnetic field $\bm{B}$ in a plasma regime where electron motion dominates and ions provide a neutralizing background. The model is widely used to study rapid magnetic reconnection and electron-scale magnetic dynamics in space and laboratory plasmas. In particular, the Hall effect allows electron motion to decouple from ion motion below the ion inertial length, leading to reconnection rates that can become largely independent of resistivity [ 13 , 2 , 29 , 27 ] . The nonlinear Hall term $\nabla\times((\nabla\times\bm{B})\times\bm{B})$ in ( 1 ) contains one more spatial derivative than the nonlinear terms in standard MHD. This higher-order structure underlies many distinctive features of EMHD and makes the model considerably more challenging to analyze and discretize. On the physics side, the Hall term supports dispersive whistler waves, promotes open X-point structures in reconnection regions, and can generate elongated current sheets depending on the dominant dissipation mechanism [ 3 , 25 , 11 ] . On the mathematical side, the non-resistive system remains poorly understood: local well-posedness in general settings is open, while recent works have shown ill-posedness mechanisms and well-posedness results under special structures or partial dissipation [ 6 , 22 , 23 ] . The $2.5$ D setting and its physical relevance. We focus on the $2\tfrac{1}{2}$ -dimensional ( $2.5$ D) setting, in which the magnetic field has three components $(B_{x},B_{y},B_{z})$ but depends only on the two spatial variables $(x,y)$ . This is the minimal nontrivial geometry for EMHD: it retains the essential Hall coupling between the in-plane field and the out-of-plane component while avoiding the full complexity of a fully three-dimensional computation. In particular, it preserves current-sheet and magnetic-island structures arising in reconnection problems [ 25 , 3 , 12 , 1 ] . This reduction is therefore not merely a simplifying assumption: the Hall term still retains the derivative structure responsible for the main analytical and numerical difficulties, while the computational cost remains far below that of a fully three-dimensional simulation. Moreover, the fractional-dissipation model studied here lies in a regime where available well-posedness theory applies [ 4 ] . Thus the $2.5$ D system provides a natural testbed for the structure-preserving scheme developed below. Component formulation. Rather than using scalar flux-function variables, we formulate the method directly in terms of the magnetic field components $(B_{x},B_{y},B_{z})$ . Under the 2.5D assumption, the curl is $\nabla\times\bm{B}=(\partial_{y}B_{z},\,-\partial_{x}B_{z},\,J)$ with $J=\partial_{x}B_{y}-\partial_{y}B_{x}$ the out-of-plane current. Expanding the Hall term component by component yields the closed system $\displaystyle\partial_{t}B_{x}+\partial_{y}(\bm{B}\cdot\nabla B_{z})+\Lambda^{\alpha}B_{x}$ $\displaystyle=0,$ (2a) $\displaystyle\partial_{t}B_{y}-\partial_{x}(\bm{B}\cdot\nabla B_{z})+\Lambda^{\alpha}B_{y}$ $\displaystyle=0,$ (2b) $\displaystyle\partial_{t}B_{z}+\bm{B}\cdot\nabla J+\Lambda^{\alpha}B_{z}$ $\displaystyle=0,$ (2c) with $\nabla\cdot\bm{B}=0$ , where $\Lambda^{\alpha}=(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}$ is the fractional Laplacian with $\alpha\in(1,2]$ . This is the system discretized in the rest of the paper. The in-plane pair $(B_{x},B_{y})$ is driven by gradients of $B_{z}$ ; the out-of-plane component $B_{z}$ is advected by the in-plane current $J$ . Local well-posedness of ( 2 ) follows from Chae–Wan–Wu [ 4 ] , whose analysis requires fractional dissipation of order $\alpha>1$ ; 1 1 1 In our convention $\Lambda^{\alpha}=(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}$ , equivalently $(-\Delta)^{\beta}$ with $\beta>1/2$ . our range $\alpha\in(1,2]$ lies in this regime. Relation to the scalar formulation. The same 2.5D dynamics can also be written in scalar variables by setting $\bm{B}=\nabla\times(a\bm{e}_{z})+b\bm{e}_{z}$ , where $a$ is the flux function ( $B_{x}=\partial_{y}a$ , $B_{y}=-\partial_{x}a$ ) and $b=B_{z}$ , giving $\displaystyle\partial_{t}a+a_{y}b_{x}-a_{x}b_{y}$ $\displaystyle=\mu\Delta a,$ (3a) $\displaystyle\partial_{t}b-a_{y}\Delta a_{x}+a_{x}\Delta a_{y}$ $\displaystyle=\nu\Delta b,$ (3b) with current $J=-\Delta a$ and resistivities $\mu,\nu\geq 0$ . This form is convenient for PDE analysis, since the in-plane divergence constraint is built in and the Hall cancellation that underpins energy estimates is transparent. Local well-posedness under partial resistivity ( $\mu>0$ or $\nu>0$ ) is due to Dai–Babaei [ 6 , 7 ] ; see [ 8 , 9 , 10 , 22 , 23 , 24 , 20 , 21 ] for the broader literature. For low-order finite element discretization, however, ( 3 ) is less convenient than the component formulation: the Hall term in ( 3b ) is third order in the flux function, through $a_{x}\Delta a_{y}-a_{y}\Delta a_{x}=\bm{B}_{xy}\cdot\nabla J$ . This leads to a parabolic time-step restriction $\tau\lesssim h^{2}/|\nabla a|$ , far more severe than the hyperbolic $\tau\lesssim h/|\bm{B}|$ associated with the component system, and compounds the error of approximating $J_{h}$ and then differentiating it again. In ( 2 ), by contrast, $J_{h}$ is computed directly from first derivatives of $(B_{x,h},B_{y,h})$ , and the remaining Hall structure involves only second-order discrete operators. These considerations motivate our choice of the component formulation ( 2 ) as the primary computational representation. Gradient recovery methods. Gradient recovery is a classical post-processing technique in finite element analysis: from a $C^{0}$ finite element solution, whose gradient is generally discontinuous across element interfaces, one reconstructs a smoother and often superconvergent gradient. Two classical constructions are the superconvergent patch recovery (SPR) [ 32 ] and the polynomial-preserving recovery (PPR) [ 31 ] ; recovered gradients also yield asymptotically exact a posteriori error estimators [ 26 ] . Recovery techniques have further been used in $C^{0}$ linear discretizations of fourth-order problems [ 19 ] , high-frequency wave propagation [ 16 ] , elliptic interface problems [ 17 , 15 , 18 ] , and the spectral computation of topological edge modes in photonic graphene [ 14 ] . In the present work, we employ the recovery operator [ 5 ] for a different purpose: not as an error estimator, but as a constructive device that makes the second-order discrete Hall operators computable on $C^{0}$ linear elements, for which the required second derivatives are otherwise unavailable. Numerical approach and contributions. The key insight is that the component formulation ( 2 ) reduces the Hall derivative count from three to two, and the gradient recovery operator $G_{h}$ of Chu, Guo, and Zhang [ 5 ] then provides an accurate and stable way to approximate the remaining second-order Hall structure using $C^{0}$ linear elements. A semi-implicit treatment of the Hall term with an exact integrating-factor method for the fractional Laplacian gives unconditional diffusion stability, leaving only the hyperbolic CFL $\tau\lesssim h/|\bm{B}|$ from the explicit Hall evaluation. The present work combines the 2.5D component formulation with the gradient recovery framework [ 5 ] , leading to a practical discretization based on $C^{0}$ linear elements and an efficient Fourier realization on periodic grids. For the analysis, we introduce a semi-implicit structure-preserving formulation and establish discrete energy stability estimates. Numerical experiments demonstrate second-order spatial convergence and illustrate stable Hall-driven dynamics across several benchmark problems. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 presents the spatial discretization, the recovery gradient operator, the discrete Hall nonlinearities, and the time discretization. Section 3 establishes the discrete energy stability theory, including Hall cancellation and unconditional $L^{2}$ stability, and discusses a formal higher-order $H_{h}^{s}$ estimate. Section 4 describes the Fourier realization of $G_{h}$ , the integrating-factor treatment of $\Lambda^{\alpha}$ , and the spectral Hodge projection. Section 5 presents the numerical experiments. Appendix A introduces the BDF2 modified-energy stability theorem and its numerical verification. 2 Algorithm Description This section develops the spatial and temporal discretization of the component system ( 2 ). We first introduce a $C^{0}$ linear finite element space together with the gradient recovery operator that makes the second-order discrete Hall terms computable, then assemble the associated discrete Hall and current operators, and finally present the semi-implicit time discretization. The construction keeps the lowest-order Hall cancellation exact at the discrete level, which is the structural property exploited in the stability analysis of Section 3 . 2.1 Spatial discretization and the recovery operator Let $\mathcal{T}_{h}$ be a shape-regular triangulation of $\Omega=[0,2\pi]^{2}$ with mesh size $h$ . Define the $C^{0}$ linear finite element space $V_{h}=\{v_{h}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(\Omega;\mathcal{T}_{h}):v_{h}\text{ continuous at all }p\in\mathcal{N}_{h}\},$ where $\mathcal{N}_{h}$ is the set of nodes (vertices) of $\mathcal{T}_{h}$ . $V_{h}^{\mathrm{per}}$ denotes the subset of $V_{h}$ with periodic boundary condition. For each $v_{h}\in V_{h}$ , let $\mathcal{E}_{h}$ denote the sets of all edges, and let $m_{e}$ and $\mathcal{K}_{e}$ denote the midpoint and the union of the two triangles sharing edge $e$ . Associate a Morley finite element function $w_{h}\in M_{h}$ by $w_{h}(p)=v_{h}(p)\;\forall\,p\in\mathcal{N}_{h},\qquad\frac{\partial w_{h}}{\partial\bm{n}_{e}}(m_{e})=\bm{n}_{e}\cdot\frac{1}{|\mathcal{K}_{e}|}\int_{\mathcal{K}_{e}}\nabla v_{h}\,dx\;\forall\,e\in\mathcal{E}_{h},$ (4) where $\bm{n}_{e}$ is the unit normal to $e$ . The gradient recovery operator $G_{h}:V_{h}\to\mathcal{P}_{1}(\Omega;\mathcal{T}_{h})^{2}$ is defined by $G_{h}v_{h}|_{T}=\nabla w_{h}|_{T},\quad\text{for all}\quad T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}.$ We introduce $G_{h}$ here on a general shape-regular triangulation; on the uniform periodic grid used in our computations it admits an explicit Fourier realization, derived in Section 4 . The key property of $G_{h}$ is captured in the following lemma. Lemma 1 (Lemma 3.3 of [ 5 ] ). For any $v_{h}\in V_{h}$ , $G_{h}v_{h}\in V_{h}^{\mathrm{CR}}\times V_{h}^{\mathrm{CR}}$ , and $G_{h}v_{h}(m_{e})=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{K}_{e}|}\int_{\mathcal{K}_{e}}\nabla v_{h}\,dx\qquad\forall\,e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}.$ (5) This formula makes $G_{h}$ cheap to compute (one patch average per edge) and avoids assembling Morley basis functions in practice. The stability equivalence $\left\|\nabla v_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\|G_{h}v_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}$ (Lemma 3.7 of [ 5 ] ) and the discrete Poincaré inequality $\left\|\nabla v_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\left\|DG_{h}v_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}$ hold on shape-regular meshes without uniformity assumptions. Panel (a) shows the diamond-shaped patch $\mathcal{K}_{e}=T_{1}\cup T_{2}$ formed by the two triangles sharing interior edge $e$ : the midpoint $m_{e}$ lies on $e$ , the outward unit normal $\bm{n}_{e}$ points from $T_{2}$ into $T_{1}$ , and the patch-average formula ( 5 ) assigns to $m_{e}$ the area-weighted mean of $\nabla v_{h}$ over the patch. The in-plane pair $(B_{x,h},B_{y,h})$ satisfies the divergence-free constraint $\nabla\cdot_{xy}\bm{B}_{h}=0$ , while $B_{z,h}$ is a free scalar field. 1. Discrete in-plane curl $J_{h}\big|_{T}\;=\;\bigl(G_{h}B_{y,h}\cdot\bm{e}_{x}-G_{h}B_{x,h}\cdot\bm{e}_{y}\bigr)\big|_{T},\qquad T\in\mathcal{T}_{h},$ giving a piecewise-constant scalar $J_{h}=\partial_{x,h}B_{y,h}-\partial_{y,h}B_{x,h}$ (the $z$ -component of $\nabla\times_{h}\bm{B}_{h}$ ). 2. Discrete Hall nonlinearity Expanding $\nabla\times\bigl((\nabla\times\bm{B})\times\bm{B}\bigr)$ component-by-component under $\partial_{z}=0$ separates the three equations as follows. • In-plane ( $i=x,y$ ): the Hall force is $\pm\partial_{i}\bigl(\bm{B}\cdot\nabla B_{z}\bigr)$ , treated semi-implicitly: $\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{h}^{xy}[\bm{B}_{h}^{n},B_{z,h}^{n+1}]_{x}$ $\displaystyle=\partial_{y,h}\bigl(\bm{B}_{h}^{n}\cdot G_{h}B_{z,h}^{n+1}\bigr),$ (6) $\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{h}^{xy}[\bm{B}_{h}^{n},B_{z,h}^{n+1}]_{y}$ $\displaystyle=-\partial_{x,h}\bigl(\bm{B}_{h}^{n}\cdot G_{h}B_{z,h}^{n+1}\bigr).$ • Out-of-plane ( $i=z$ ): the Hall force is $\bm{B}\cdot\nabla J$ ,
treated semi-implicitly: $\mathcal{N}_{h}^{z}[\bm{B}_{h}^{n},\bm{B}_{h}^{n+1}]=\bm{B}_{h}^{n}\cdot G_{h}J_{h}^{n+1},\qquad J_{h}^{n+1}=\partial_{x,h}B_{y,h}^{n+1}-\partial_{y,h}B_{x,h}^{n+1}.$ (7) Treating one factor of $\bm{B}$ explicitly linearizes each Hall term and reduces the problem to three coupled second-order linear systems in $\bm{B}_{h}^{n+1}$ . 3. Discrete fractional Laplacian On the uniform periodic grid the fractional Laplacian $\Lambda^{\alpha}=(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}$ is applied exactly in Fourier space (e.g., [ 30 ] ): $\widehat{(\Lambda^{\alpha}f_{h})}_{k_{x},k_{y}}=|k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2}|^{\alpha/2}\,\hat{f}_{k_{x},k_{y}}.$ (8) This is exact for any $\alpha>0$ and avoids the $O(h^{2})$ stencil error of the standard five-point approximation. 4. Edge-penalty bilinear form To control jumps of the recovered gradient across edges, define (following [ 5 , Eq. (4.4)] ) $b_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}h_{e}^{-1}\bigl(\llbracket G_{h}u_{h}\rrbracket,\llbracket G_{h}v_{h}\rrbracket\bigr)_{L^{2}(e)},$ (9) where $\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket$ denotes the jump across $e$ . Remark 2 . In contrast to the stabilization term in [ 5 ] , the boundary penalty term is omitted here because periodic boundary conditions allow every edge to be treated as an interior edge. 2.3 Time discretization Given $\bm{B}_{h}^{n}\in(V_{h}^{\mathrm{per}})^{3}$ , find $\bm{B}_{h}^{n+1}\in(V_{h}^{\mathrm{per}})^{3}$ such that for all $(\phi_{x},\phi_{y},\phi_{z})\in(V_{h}^{\mathrm{per}})^{3}$ : $\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}\bigl(B_{x,h}^{n+1}-B_{x,h}^{n},\phi_{x}\bigr)$ $\displaystyle+\bigl(\mathcal{N}_{h}^{xy}[\bm{B}_{h}^{n},B_{z,h}^{n+1}]_{x},\phi_{x}\bigr)+\bigl(\Lambda_{h}^{\alpha}B_{x,h}^{n+1},\phi_{x}\bigr)+\sigma\,b_{h}(B_{x,h}^{n+1},\phi_{x})=0,$ (10) $\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}\bigl(B_{y,h}^{n+1}-B_{y,h}^{n},\phi_{y}\bigr)$ $\displaystyle+\bigl(\mathcal{N}_{h}^{xy}[\bm{B}_{h}^{n},B_{z,h}^{n+1}]_{y},\phi_{y}\bigr)+\bigl(\Lambda_{h}^{\alpha}B_{y,h}^{n+1},\phi_{y}\bigr)+\sigma\,b_{h}(B_{y,h}^{n+1},\phi_{y})=0,$ (11) $\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}\bigl(B_{z,h}^{n+1}-B_{z,h}^{n},\phi_{z}\bigr)$ $\displaystyle+\bigl(\mathcal{N}_{h}^{z}[\bm{B}_{h}^{n},\bm{B}_{h}^{n+1}],\phi_{z}\bigr)+\bigl(\Lambda_{h}^{\alpha}B_{z,h}^{n+1},\phi_{z}\bigr)+\sigma\,b_{h}(B_{z,h}^{n+1},\phi_{z})=0,$ (12) followed by the in-plane divergence-free projection (e.g., [ 28 ] ) $\bigl(B_{x,h}^{n+1},B_{y,h}^{n+1}\bigr)\leftarrow P_{h}^{\mathrm{div}}\bigl(B_{x,h}^{n+1},B_{y,h}^{n+1}\bigr).$ The semi-implicit formulation leads to coupled linear subproblems at each step: the in-plane pair $(B_{x},B_{y})$ is driven by $\nabla B_{z}^{n+1}$ through ( 10 )–( 11 ), and $B_{z}$ is driven by $J^{n+1}$ of $(B_{x}^{n+1},B_{y}^{n+1})$ through ( 12 ). In the implementation of Section 4 , we use an integrating-factor variant in which all Hall forces are evaluated explicitly at time level $n$ , replacing the coupled linear solve by three independent Fourier updates. The semi-implicit coupled system ( 10 )–( 12 ) is the structure-preserving model underlying the stability analysis of Section 3 ; it is not solved directly in our experiments, which instead use the explicit-Hall integrating-factor implementation of Section 4 . The Hodge projection in Step 3 maintains $\max|\nabla\cdot_{xy}\bm{B}_{h}|$ near round-off ( $10^{-16}$ – $10^{-13}$ , scaling with the field amplitude) throughout. 3 Stability analysis This section presents the energy mechanism behind the scheme. The argument follows the dyadic energy method of [ 4 ] , adapted to the periodic discrete operators used here. The essential point is that the Hall term must be discretized in a form that preserves the skew-symmetry of $\nabla\times((\nabla\times\bm{B})\times\bm{B})$ . In the continuum, several componentwise forms are equivalent by the product rule and by $\nabla\cdot\bm{B}=0$ . At the discrete level these identities are no longer automatic. For the stability proof we therefore use the curl–cross-product form below. Let $\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)_{h}$ denote the periodic grid inner product and assume that $D_{x},D_{y}$ are periodic summation-by-parts operators, $\left(D_{x}f,g\right)_{h}=-\left(f,D_{x}g\right)_{h},\qquad\left(D_{y}f,g\right)_{h}=-\left(f,D_{y}g\right)_{h}.$ (13) On the uniform periodic grid this is satisfied by the Fourier realization of $G_{h}$ , whose symbols are purely imaginary and odd. Define the discrete $2.5$ D curl by $C_{h}\bm{B}_{h}:=\bigl(D_{y}B_{z,h},\,-D_{x}B_{z,h},\,D_{x}B_{y,h}-D_{y}B_{x,h}\bigr).$ (14) Then ( 13 ) implies the discrete curl identity $\left(C_{h}\bm{U}_{h},\bm{V}_{h}\right)_{h}=\left(\bm{U}_{h},C_{h}\bm{V}_{h}\right)_{h},$ (15) which is the periodic analogue of integration by parts for the curl operator. For $\bm{U}_{h},\bm{V}_{h}\in(V_{h}^{\mathrm{per}})^{3}$ , define the linearized Hall operator $\mathcal{H}_{h}(\bm{U}_{h};\bm{V}_{h}):=C_{h}\bigl((C_{h}\bm{U}_{h})\times\bm{V}_{h}\bigr),$ (16) where $\bm{U}_{h}$ is the implicit argument and $\bm{V}_{h}$ is the frozen explicit field. This is the discrete counterpart of $\nabla\times((\nabla\times\bm{B})\times\bm{B})$ . Remark 3 . The component formula $H_{x}=D_{y}(\bm{B}_{xy}\cdot\nabla_{h}B_{z})$ , $H_{y}=-D_{x}(\bm{B}_{xy}\cdot\nabla_{h}B_{z})$ , $H_{z}=\bm{B}_{xy}\cdot\nabla_{h}J$ is equivalent to the continuum Hall term only after using the product rule and $\nabla\cdot\bm{B}_{xy}=0$ . If this reduced component form is kept in the code, the out-of-plane term should be interpreted in conservative weak form, $\displaystyle\left(H_{z,h}(\bm{U}_{h};\bm{V}_{h}),\phi_{h}\right)_{h}$ $\displaystyle=-\left(J_{h}(\bm{U}_{h}),V_{x,h}D_{x}\phi_{h}+V_{y,h}D_{y}\phi_{h}\right)_{h},$ (17) $\displaystyle J_{h}(\bm{U}_{h})$ $\displaystyle=D_{x}U_{y,h}-D_{y}U_{x,h},$ so that the lowest-order Hall cancellation is exact. The high-order Chae–Wan–Wu estimate is cleanest for the curl–cross-product form ( 16 ), and the theorem below is stated for that form. Let $L_{b,h}$ be the symmetric non-negative operator induced by the edge penalty, namely, $\left(L_{b,h}u_{h},v_{h}\right)_{h}=b_{h}(u_{h},v_{h}).$ The structure-preserving semi-implicit step is $\frac{\bm{B}_{h}^{n+1,*}-\bm{B}_{h}^{n}}{\tau}+\mathcal{H}_{h}(\bm{B}_{h}^{n+1,*};\bm{B}_{h}^{n})+\Lambda_{h}^{\alpha}\bm{B}_{h}^{n+1,*}+\sigma L_{b,h}\bm{B}_{h}^{n+1,*}=0,$ (18) followed by the spectral Hodge projection $\displaystyle\bm{B}_{h}^{n+1}$ $\displaystyle=P_{h}^{\mathrm{div}}\bm{B}_{h}^{n+1,*},$ (19) $\displaystyle\left\|P_{h}^{\mathrm{div}}\bm{U}_{h}\right\|_{0,h}$ $\displaystyle\leq\left\|\bm{U}_{h}\right\|_{0,h}.$ (20) The projection is an orthogonal projection in the grid $L^{2}$ inner product; therefore Lemma 4 (Discrete Hall cancellation). The unconditional $L^{2}$ stability of Theorem 5 , by contrast, is fully rigorous and independent of this transfer. Remark 9 . The Chae–Wan–Wu cancellation reduces the Hall contribution to a commutator that costs only one half derivative above the $H_{h}^{s}$ energy level, namely $H_{h}^{s+1/2}$ . The dissipation generated by $\Lambda_{h}^{\alpha}=(-\Delta_{h})^{\alpha/2}$ controls $H_{h}^{s+\alpha/2}$ . Therefore the interpolation step ( 31 ) requires $s+1/2<s+\alpha/2$ , which is exactly $\alpha>1$ . In the notation of [ 4 ] , the diffusion is written as $(-\Delta)^{\alpha}$ , so the corresponding condition is $\alpha>1/2$ . Remark 10 . The integrating-factor Euler update $\widehat{\bm{B}}^{n+1}_{\bm{k}}=e^{-|\bm{k}|^{\alpha}\tau}\left(\widehat{\bm{B}}^{n}_{\bm{k}}-\tau\widehat{\mathcal{H}[\bm{B}^{n}]}_{\bm{k}}\right)$ is unconditionally stable for the fractional diffusion substep, but it does not inherit the exact nonlinear energy identity of Theorem 5 . Even if $\left(\mathcal{H}[\bm{B}_{h}^{n}],\bm{B}_{h}^{n}\right)_{h}=0$ , the explicit Hall update gives $\left\|\bm{B}_{h}^{n}-\tau\mathcal{H}[\bm{B}_{h}^{n}]\right\|_{0,h}^{2}=\left\|\bm{B}_{h}^{n}\right\|_{0,h}^{2}+\tau^{2}\left\|\mathcal{H}[\bm{B}_{h}^{n}]\right\|_{0,h}^{2}.$ Thus the diffusion part is unconditional, whereas the fully explicit Hall part remains subject to a Hall CFL condition. The unconditional nonlinear energy estimate is a property of the semi-implicit curl-form discretization ( 18 ). A second-order BDF2 variant of the scheme, which satisfies an analogous modified-energy stability theorem, is described in Appendix A . 4 Implementation on the Uniform Periodic Grid All computations are carried out on the uniform $N\times N$ periodic grid on $\Omega=[0,2\pi]^{2}$ with grid spacing $h=2\pi/N$ . On this grid the recovery operators admit an exact Fourier realization, so all differential operators are evaluated via FFTs. While the analysis of Section 3 is carried out for the semi-implicit curl-form discretization ( 18 ), all numerical experiments in Section 5 use the explicit-Hall integrating-factor implementation described below. The two schemes are based on the same Hall operator and differential identities, but the explicit-Hall implementation is unconditionally stable only for the fractional-diffusion substep and does not inherit the exact nonlinear energy identity of Theorem 5 (see Remark 10 ). We use the explicit-Hall implementation in the experiments because it is computationally simpler on the periodic grid, reducing each step to a few FFTs, while the semi-implicit formulation is what exposes the discrete Hall cancellation and supplies the stability mechanism. Spectral realization of the recovery gradient $G_{h}$ On the uniform $N\times N$ Cartesian grid, every interior edge patch $\mathcal{K}_{e}$ consists of two right triangles whose union is an $h\times h$ square. In this geometry the patch average of $\nabla v_{h}$ over $\mathcal{K}_{e}$ reduces to a fixed linear combination of nodal values. Summing over all contributing patches yields the nine-point stencil $(G_{h}f)^{x}_{i,j}=\frac{1}{4h}\Bigl[\bigl(f_{i+1,j}-f_{i-1,j}\bigr)+\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(f_{i+1,j+1}-f_{i-1,j+1}\bigr)+\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(f_{i+1,j-1}-f_{i-1,j-1}\bigr)\Bigr],$ (32) with the $y$ -component given by symmetry. This is a separable convolution: a central difference in $x$ followed by the averaging filter $\tfrac{1}{4}[\delta_{j-1}+2\delta_{j}+\delta_{j+1}]$ in $y$ . Taking the 2D DFT with integer wavenumbers $(k_{x},k_{y})\in\{-N/2+1,\ldots,N/2\}^{2}$ , the Fourier symbol of $G_{h}$ is $\widehat{(G_{h}f)^{x}}_{k_{x},k_{y}}=\sigma^{x}(k_{x},k_{y})\,\hat{f}_{k_{x},k_{y}},\qquad\sigma^{x}(k_{x},k_{y})=-\frac{i\sin(k_{x}h)}{h}\cdot\frac{1+\cos(k_{y}h)}{2}.$ (33) Remark 11 (Accuracy and aliasing suppression). The exact spectral $x$ -derivative has symbol $ik_{x}$ . The stencil symbol satisfies $\sigma^{x}(k_{x},k_{y})=-ik_{x}\Bigl[1-\tfrac{(k_{x}h)^{2}}{6}+O((k_{x}h)^{4})\Bigr]\Bigl[1-\tfrac{(k_{y}h)^{2}}{4}+O((k_{y}h)^{4})\Bigr],$ so $\sigma^{x}=-ik_{x}+O((kh)^{2})$ for resolved modes. At the Nyquist frequency $k_{y}=N/2$ , the averaging factor $(1+\cos(\pi))/2=0$ damps the corresponding near-Nyquist modes, providing a mild built-in filtering effect; this is not, however, a full de-aliasing rule for nonlinear products. In the code, ( 32 ) is applied via numpy.fft.fft2 / ifft2 , which is bit-for-bit equivalent to direct stencil application but generalizes trivially to any Fourier-diagonal operator. Fractional Laplacian via integrating factor On the uniform periodic grid, $\Lambda^{\alpha}$ is applied exactly in Fourier space by ( 8 ). The integrating-factor update $\hat{B}^{n+1}_{\bm{k}}=e^{-|\bm{k}|^{\alpha}\tau}\bigl(\hat{B}^{n}_{\bm{k}}-\tau\,\widehat{\mathcal{H}[\bm{B}^{n}]}_{\bm{k}}\bigr),$ (34) where $\mathcal{H}$ denotes the explicit Hall term, handles dissipation exactly regardless of $\tau$ and $h$ . The remaining practical time-step restriction is the hyperbolic Hall CFL $\tau\lesssim h/\max|\bm{B}|$ . Remark 12 (Unconditional stability of the IF method). For any $\alpha>0$ and $\tau,h>0$ , the integrating factor $e^{-|\bm{k}|^{\alpha}\tau}\in(0,1]$ for all $\bm{k}$ . This means the diffusion step in ( 34 ) is unconditionally stable: regardless of how large $\tau$ is, no Fourier mode grows from the dissipation. In contrast, a fully explicit treatment of the fractional diffusion would require a parabolic time-step restriction $\tau\lesssim h^{\alpha}/C$ . Divergence-free projection After the Hall update, the in-plane components acquire a spurious divergence of order $O(\tau h)$ . This is corrected by a spectral Hodge projection: solve $\Delta\phi=\nabla\cdot\bm{B}$ in Fourier space, $\hat{\phi}_{\bm{k}}=-\frac{\widehat{(\nabla\cdot\bm{B})}_{\bm{k}}}{|\bm{k}|^{2}},\quad|\bm{k}|\neq 0,$ and set $\bm{B}\leftarrow\bm{B}-\nabla\phi$ . This reduces $\max|\nabla\cdot\bm{B}_{h}|$ to the round-off floor ( $10^{-16}$ – $10^{-13}$ , scaling with the field amplitude and its gradients) and costs two forward and two inverse FFTs. The out-of-plane component $B_{z}$ requires no projection. Coupling summary The in-plane pair $(B_{x},B_{y})$ is forced by $\nabla B_{z}$ through the Hall terms ( 6 ); $B_{z}$ is forced by the in-plane current $J=\partial_{x}B_{y}-\partial_{y}B_{x}$ through ( 7 ). In the explicit-Hall implementation, all three components use only the time- $n$ state in the Hall force evaluation, so the coupling within a step is one-directional. The complete update is: (i) Evaluate Hall forces $(H_{x}^{n},H_{y}^{n},H_{z}^{n})$ using ( 33 ). (ii) Apply integrating-factor step ( 34 ): unconditionally stable. (iii) Project $(B_{x}^{n+1},B_{y}^{n+1})$ via spectral Hodge: divergence to round-off. 5 Numerical Examples All experiments use the periodic domain $\Omega=[0,2\pi]^{2}$ on a uniform $N=128$ grid ( $h=2\pi/128\approx 0.049$ ) with time step $\tau=0.001$ and the integrating-factor Euler scheme of Section 4 . Appendix A Stability of a BDF2 variant The energy framework of Section 3 extends to a second-order-in-time variant based on the Backward Differentiation Formula of order 2 (BDF2). The numerical experiments are based primarily on the integrating-factor Euler implementation; the results below provide mathematical support for future use of higher-order time stepping. For problems where a larger time step is desirable, the energy framework extends naturally to the second-order Backward Differentiation Formula (BDF2) scheme. Given $\bm{B}_{h}^{n}$ and $\bm{B}_{h}^{n-1}$ , let $\widetilde{\bm{B}}_{h}^{n}:=2\bm{B}_{h}^{n}-\bm{B}_{h}^{n-1}$ be the second-order extrapolation. The BDF2 semi-implicit step is $\frac{3\bm{B}_{h}^{n+1,*}-4\bm{B}_{h}^{n}+\bm{B}_{h}^{n-1}}{2\tau}+\mathcal{H}_{h}(\bm{B}_{h}^{n+1,*};\widetilde{\bm{B}}_{h}^{n})+\Lambda_{h}^{\alpha}\bm{B}_{h}^{n+1,*}+\sigma L_{b,h}\bm{B}_{h}^{n+1,*}=0,$ (39) followed by the Hodge projection $\bm{B}_{h}^{n+1}=P_{h}^{\mathrm{div}}\bm{B}_{h}^{n+1,*}$ . Define the BDF2 modified magnetic energy $\mathcal{E}_{h}^{n}:=\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\left\|\bm{B}_{h}^{n}\right\|_{0,h}^{2}+\left\|2\bm{B}_{h}^{n}-\bm{B}_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{0,h}^{2}\Bigr).$ (40) The factor $\left\|2\bm{B}_{h}^{n}-\bm{B}_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{0,h}^{2}$ is the standard BDF2 correction term that makes the modified energy equivalent to $\left\|\bm{B}_{h}^{n}\right\|_{0,h}^{2}$ up to a constant factor. Theorem 13 (BDF2 modified-energy stability). Under the assumptions of Theorem 5 , if $\bm{B}_{h}^{n}$ and $\bm{B}_{h}^{n-1}$ are in the range of $P_{h}^{\mathrm{div}}$ , then every step of ( 39 ) satisfies $\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{h}^{n+1,*}$ $\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\bm{B}_{h}^{n+1,*}-2\bm{B}_{h}^{n}+\bm{B}_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{0,h}^{2}+2\tau\left\|\Lambda_{h}^{\alpha/2}\bm{B}_{h}^{n+1,*}\right\|_{0,h}^{2}$ $\displaystyle\quad+2\tau\sigma\sum_{i=x,y,z}b_{h}(B_{i,h}^{n+1,*},B_{i,h}^{n+1,*})=\mathcal{E}_{h}^{n},$ (41) where $\mathcal{E}_{h}^{n+1,*}$ is defined with $\bm{B}_{h}^{n+1,*}$ in place of $\bm{B}_{h}^{n+1}$ . Consequently, $\mathcal{E}_{h}^{n+1}+2\tau\left\|\Lambda_{h}^{\alpha/2}\bm{B}_{h}^{n+1,*}\right\|_{0,h}^{2}+2\tau\sigma\sum_{i=x,y,z}b_{h}(B_{i,h}^{n+1,*},B_{i,h}^{n+1,*})\leq\mathcal{E}_{h}^{n}.$ (42) Proof. Take the inner product of ( 39 ) with $\bm{B}_{h}^{n+1,*}$ . The Hall term vanishes by Lemma 4 (applied with the frozen field $\widetilde{\bm{B}}_{h}^{n}$ in place of $\bm{V}_{h}$ ). The BDF2 time-difference identity $\displaystyle 2\left(3a-4b+c,a\right)_{h}=\left\|a\right\|_{0,h}^{2}+\left\|2a-b\right\|_{0,h}^{2}-\left\|b\right\|_{0,h}^{2}-\left\|2b-c\right\|_{0,h}^{2}+\left\|a-2b+c\right\|_{0,h}^{2}$ with $a=\bm{B}_{h}^{n+1,*}$ , $b=\bm{B}_{h}^{n}$ , $c=\bm{B}_{h}^{n-1}$ , multiplied by $2\tau$ , gives ( 41 ). The projected estimate ( 42 ) follows because $P_{h}^{\mathrm{div}}$ is orthogonal and $\bm{B}_{h}^{n}$ is already in its range, so $\left\|\bm{B}_{h}^{n+1}\right\|_{0,h}\leq\left\|\bm{B}_{h}^{n+1,*}\right\|_{0,h}$ and $\left\|2\bm{B}_{h}^{n+1}-\bm{B}_{h}^{n}\right\|_{0,h}\leq\left\|2\bm{B}_{h}^{n+1,*}-\bm{B}_{h}^{n}\right\|_{0,h}$ . Remark 14 (Comparison of the two schemes). Both the backward-Euler scheme ( 18 ) and the BDF2 scheme ( 39 ) are unconditionally stable in their respective energy norms, and both treat the Hall nonlinearity with a single frozen explicit factor, so both require the Hall CFL $\tau\lesssim h/\max|\bm{B}_{h}|$ for accuracy rather than stability. The backward-Euler scheme is first-order in time and simpler to start (no previous-step value required). The BDF2 scheme carries a larger stability constant and requires a warm-start Euler step, but its modified-energy identity ( 41 ) retains the same unconditional character. In the numerical experiments of Section 5 we use the integrating-factor Euler implementation as the primary scheme; Table 4 records the modified-energy behavior of the BDF2 variant for completeness.
