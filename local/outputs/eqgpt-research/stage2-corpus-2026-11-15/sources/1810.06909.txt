The well-posedness of a chemotaxis system with indirect signal production in a two-dimensional domain is shown, all solutions being global unlike the classical Keller-Segel chemotaxis system. Nevertheless, there is a threshold value $M_{c}$ of the mass of the first component which separates two different behaviours: solutions are bounded when the mass is below $M_{c}$ while there are unbounded solutions starting from initial conditions having a mass exceeding $M_{c}$ . This result extends to arbitrary two-dimensional domains a previous result of Tao & Winkler (2017) obtained for radially symmetric solutions to a simplified version of the model in a ball and relies on a different approach involving a Liapunov functional. Introduction Chemotaxis models have been derived in [ 19 , 21 , 24 ] to describe the spreading and aggregative behaviour of the mountain pine beetle which has a major impact on the forest industry in North America. These models describe the space and time evolution of the density $u$ of flying beetles, the density $v$ of nesting beetles, and the concentration $w$ of beetle pheromone (chemoattractant). The main behavioural difference between flying and nesting beetles is that the latter do not disperse in space while the former move randomly in space, their motion being biased by high gradients of the pheromone concentration. A very important feature in the model is that the pheromone is produced by the nesting beetles and not by the flying ones, though it influences only the motion of the latter. This is in sharp contrast with the classical Keller-Segel model where the chemical inducing a bias in the motion of the species is produced directly by the species itself. As we shall see below, this feature alters significantly the dynamics, at least in two space dimensions. A simplified version of the models derived in [ 19 , 21 , 24 ] is considered in [ 23 ] and reads (1.1a) $\displaystyle\partial_{t}u$ $\displaystyle=\mathrm{div}\left(\nabla u-u\nabla w\right)\;\;\text{ in }\;\;(0,\infty)\times\Omega\ ,$ (1.1b) $\displaystyle\nu\varepsilon\partial_{t}v$ $\displaystyle=u-v\;\;\text{ in }\;\;(0,\infty)\times\Omega\ ,$ (1.1c) $\displaystyle 0$ $\displaystyle=D\Delta w-\langle v\rangle+v\;\;\text{ in }\;\;(0,\infty)\times\Omega\ ,$ supplemented with no-flux boundary conditions for $u$ and $w$ (1.1d) $\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{n}=\nabla w\cdot\mathbf{n}=0\;\;\text{ on }\;\;(0,\infty)\times\partial\Omega\ ,$ the normalization condition for $w$ (1.1e) $\langle w\rangle=0\;\;\text{ in }\;\;(0,\infty)\ ,$ and non-negative initial conditions $(u^{in},v^{in})$ for $(u,v)$ . Here $\Omega$ is a smooth bounded domain of $\mathbb{R}^{2}$ , $\nu$ , $\varepsilon$ , and $D$ are positive parameters, and $\langle v\rangle$ in ( 1.1c ) and $\langle w\rangle$ in ( 1.1e ) denote the mean value with respect to the space variable of $v$ and $w$ , respectively. Recall that, for $z\in L^{1}(\Omega)$ , its mean value is given by $\langle z\rangle:=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}z(x)\ \mathrm{d}x\ .$ A Jäger-Luckhaus version of the Keller-Segel chemotaxis system [ 14 ] is recovered from ( 1.1 ) by setting $\nu\varepsilon=0$ and reads, since $v=u$ in that case, (1.2a) $\displaystyle\partial_{t}u$ $\displaystyle=\mathrm{div}\left(\nabla u-u\nabla w\right)\;\;\text{ in }\;\;(0,\infty)\times\Omega\ ,$ (1.2b) $\displaystyle 0$ $\displaystyle=D\Delta w-\langle u\rangle+u\;\;\text{ in }\;\;(0,\infty)\times\Omega\ ,$ supplemented with no-flux boundary conditions for $u$ and $w$ (1.2c) $\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{n}=\nabla w\cdot\mathbf{n}=0\;\;\text{ on }\;\;(0,\infty)\times\partial\Omega\ ,$ the normalization condition for $w$ (1.2d) $\langle w\rangle=0\;\;\text{ in }\;\;(0,\infty)\ ,$ and a non-negative initial condition $u^{in}$ for $u$ . Observe that, in ( 1.2 ), the chemoattractant is produced directly by the species with density $u$ , instead of being produced through another species as in ( 1.1 ). A first consequence of this feature is that all solutions to ( 1.1 ) are global [ 23 , Theorem 1.1] . This global existence property contrasts markedly with the situation for ( 1.2 ) for which the following is known: solutions to ( 1.2 ) are global when either $\|u^{in}\|_{1}<4\pi D$ or $\Omega$ is a ball, $u^{in}$ is radially symmetric, and $\|u^{in}\|_{1}>8\pi D$ [ 5 , 14 , 15 ] . Finite time blowup may take place when either $\|u^{in}\|_{1}>4\pi D$ or $\Omega$ is a ball, $u^{in}$ is radially symmetric, and $\|u^{in}\|_{1}>8\pi D$ [ 14 , 15 , 16 , 20 ] , see also the survey [ 12 ] . A striking feature of the dynamics of ( 1.1 ), uncovered in [ 23 ] , is that, though all solutions to ( 1.1 ) are global, a threshold phenomenon occurs in infinite time. More precisely, when $\Omega$ is a ball, say $\Omega=B_{1}(0):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\ :\ |x|<1\}$ , and the initial conditions $(u^{in},v^{in})$ are radially symmetric, so that the solution $(u,v,w)$ of ( 1.1 ) is also radially symmetric for all times, it is shown in [ 23 , Theorems 1.2 & 1.3] that all (radially symmetric) solutions are actually bounded when $\|u^{in}\|_{1}<8\pi D$ while there are unbounded solutions emanating from initial conditions satisfying $\|u^{in}\|_{1}>8\pi D$ , the $L^{\infty}$ -norm of $u(t)$ growing up as $t\to\infty$ at an exponential rate. The approach of [ 23 ] exploits the classical fact that the specific structure of ( 1.1 ) and the radial symmetry of the solutions allow one to reduce ( 1.1 ) to a single nonlocal parabolic equation for the cumulative distribution function $U$ defined by $U(t,|x|):=\int_{0}^{\sqrt{|x|}}\sigma\bar{u}(t,\sigma)\ \mathrm{d}\sigma\ ,\qquad(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times B_{1}(0)\ ,$ where $\bar{u}(t,|x|):=u(t,x)$ for $(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times B_{1}(0)$ . Even though the parabolic equation solved by $U$ involves a nonlocal term, it turns out that such a powerful tool as the comparison principle becomes available, see [ 23 , Lemma 4.2] , and the proofs of [ 23 , Theorems 1.2 & 1.3] rely on the construction of suitable supersolutions and subsolutions. This technique is however restricted to ( 1.1 ) in a radially symmetric setting and does not allow one to handle more general initial conditions or to consider an arbitrary domain $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}$ . Neither does it extend to the version of ( 1.1 ) involving degradation of the chemoattractant (1.3a) $\displaystyle\partial_{t}u$ $\displaystyle=\mathrm{div}\left(\nabla u-u\nabla w\right)\;\;\text{ in }\;\;(0,\infty)\times\Omega\ ,$ (1.3b) $\displaystyle\nu\varepsilon\partial_{t}v$ $\displaystyle=u-v\;\;\text{ in }\;\;(0,\infty)\times\Omega\ ,$ (1.3c) $\displaystyle 0$ $\displaystyle=D\Delta w-\delta w+v\;\;\text{ in }\;\;(0,\infty)\times\Omega\ ,$ with $\delta>0$ , supplemented with no-flux boundary conditions for $u$ and $w$ and non-negative initial conditions $(u^{in},v^{in})$ for $(u,v)$ , nor to its parabolic counterpart (1.4a) $\displaystyle\partial_{t}u$ $\displaystyle=\mathrm{div}\left(\nabla u-u\nabla w\right)\;\;\text{ in }\;\;(0,\infty)\times\Omega\ ,$ (1.4b) $\displaystyle\nu\varepsilon\partial_{t}v$ $\displaystyle=u-v\;\;\text{ in }\;\;(0,\infty)\times\Omega\ ,$ (1.4c) $\displaystyle\nu\partial_{t}w$ $\displaystyle=D\Delta w-\delta w+v\;\;\text{ in }\;\;(0,\infty)\times\Omega\ ,$ supplemented with no-flux boundary conditions for $u$ and $w$ (1.4d) $\left(\nabla u-u\nabla w\right)\cdot\mathbf{n}=\nabla w\cdot\mathbf{n}=0\;\;\text{ on }\;\;(0,\infty)\times\partial\Omega\ ,$ and initial conditions (1.5) $(u,v,w)(0)=(u^{in},v^{in},w^{in})\;\;\text{ in }\;\;\Omega\ .$ The main contribution of this paper is to show that the systems ( 1.1 ), ( 1.3 ), and ( 1.4 ) share the same infinite time threshold phenomenon uncovered in [ 23 ] , without restrictions on the two-dimensional domain $\Omega$ and the initial data. This requires a different argument and we actually show that ( 1.1 ), ( 1.3 ), and ( 1.4 ) all possess a Liapunov functional, and that the properties of this Liapunov functional provide insight on the boundedness or unboundedness of the solutions. From now on, we shall focus on ( 1.4 )-( 1.5 ) and will return briefly to ( 1.1 ) and ( 1.3 ) in Section 5 . Introducing (1.6) $L(r):=r\ln{r}-r+1\geq 0\ ,\qquad r\geq 0\ ,$ and (1.7a) $\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{0}(u,w)$ $\displaystyle:=\int_{\Omega}\left[L(u)-uw\right]\ \mathrm{d}x+\frac{D}{2}\|\nabla w\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}\|w\|_{2}^{2}\ ,$ (1.7b) $\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u,v,w)$ $\displaystyle:=\mathcal{E}_{0}(u,w)+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\|-D\Delta w+\delta w-v\|_{2}^{2}\ ,$ the main observation is that $\mathcal{E}$ is a Liapunov functional for ( 1.4 )-( 1.5 ), see Lemma 3.2 . Interestingly, $\mathcal{E}_{0}$ is a Liapunov functional for the classical Keller-Segel system [ 9 , 17 ] (1.8a) $\displaystyle\partial_{t}u$ $\displaystyle=\mathrm{div}\left(\nabla u-u\nabla w\right)\;\;\text{ in }\;\;(0,\infty)\times\Omega\ ,$ (1.8b) $\displaystyle\nu\partial_{t}w$ $\displaystyle=D\Delta w-\delta w+u\;\;\text{ in }\;\;(0,\infty)\times\Omega\ ,$ (1.8c) $\displaystyle\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{n}=\nabla w\cdot\mathbf{n}=0\;\;\text{ on }\;\;(0,\infty)\times\partial\Omega\ ,$ (1.8d) $\displaystyle(u,w)(0)=(u^{in},w^{in})\;\;\text{ in }\;\;\Omega\ ,$ and, as such, its properties have been thoroughly studied [ 9 , 10 , 11 , 13 , 17 ] . In particular, introducing the set (1.9) $\mathcal{A}_{M}:=\left\{(u,w)\in L_{1}(\Omega)\times W_{2}^{1}(\Omega)\ :\ u\geq 0\ ,\ w\geq 0\ ,\ \|u\|_{1}=M\right\}$ for $M\geq 0$ , it is known that (1.10) $\begin{array}[]{lcl}\displaystyle{\inf_{(u,w)\in\mathcal{A}_{M}}\mathcal{E}_{0}(u,w)}>-\infty&\text{ if }&M\in[0,4\pi D]\ ,\\

Indeed, given $M>0$ , global existence and blowup of solutions to ( 1.8 ) are intimately related to the finiteness or not of the infimum of $\mathcal{E}_{0}$ on $\mathcal{A}_{M}$ [ 9 , 10 , 11 , 13 , 17 ] . As we shall see below, the property ( 1.10 ) also plays an important role in the dynamics of ( 1.4 )-( 1.5 ), a feature which is actually not so surprising as there is a close connection between ( 1.4 )-( 1.5 ) and ( 1.8 ). Indeed, ( 1.8 ) can formally be derived from ( 1.4 )-( 1.5 ) by setting $\varepsilon=0$ . We now describe our results on ( 1.4 )-( 1.5 ) and begin with its well-posedness. Then the system ( 1.4 )-( 1.5 ) has a unique non-negative weak solution $(u,v,w)$ in $W^{1}_{3}$ defined on $[0,\infty)$ satisfying $\displaystyle u$ $\displaystyle\in C([0,\infty);W_{3,\mathcal{B}}^{2\theta}(\Omega))\cap C^{1}([0,\infty);W^{2\theta-2}_{3,\mathcal{B}}(\Omega))\ ,$ $\displaystyle v$ $\displaystyle\in C^{1}([0,\infty);W^{1}_{3}(\Omega))\ ,$ $\displaystyle w$ $\displaystyle\in C([0,\infty);W_{3,\mathcal{B}}^{2}(\Omega))\cap C^{1}([0,\infty);L_{3}(\Omega))\ ,$ and (1.14) $\|u(t)\|_{1}=M:=\|u^{in}\|_{1}\ ,\qquad t\geq 0\ .$ In particular, $(u(t),v(t),w(t))\in\mathcal{I}_{M,\theta}$ for all $t\geq 0$ . Furthermore, $u\in C((0,\infty);W_{3,\mathcal{B}}^{2}(\Omega))\cap C^{1}((0,\infty);L_{3}(\Omega))\ .$ Several approaches may be used to deal with the well-posedness of ( 1.4 )-( 1.5 ). Since ( 1.4b ) can be solved explicitly to find $v$ in terms of $u$ , one possibility would be to solve the parabolic system ( 1.4a )-( 1.4c )-( 1.4d )-( 1.5 ) with a source term which is nonlocal with respect to time. However, since an abstract theory is developed in [ 2 ] to handle systems coupling parabolic equations and ordinary differential equations, we rather follow this route which provides the local well-posedness of ( 1.4 )-( 1.5 ) in $W_{3}^{1}(\Omega)$ and may cope as well with nonlinear reaction terms in ( 1.4b ) or ( 1.4c ). The parabolic regularising effects of both ( 1.4a ) and ( 1.4c ) are then used to prove that it is actually a strong solution for positive times. Several estimates are next needed to prove that the solution is global, the positivity of $\varepsilon$ being of utmost importance already in Lemma 2.3 . Having established the global well-posedness of ( 1.4 )-( 1.5 ), we next turn to qualitative information on the dynamics of ( 1.4 )-( 1.5 ) which is the main goal of this paper. We first study the boundedness of solutions, thereby extending [ 23 , Theorem 1.2] to ( 1.4 )-( 1.5 ) in an arbitrary domain $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}$ . Theorem 1.2 . Let $\theta\in(5/6,1)$ and consider initial conditions $(u^{in},v^{in},w^{in})\in\mathcal{I}_{M,\theta}$ . We denote the corresponding solution to ( 1.4 )-( 1.5 ) given by Theorem 1.1 by $(u,v,w)$ . (a) Assume further that $M=\|u^{in}\|_{1}\in(0,4\pi D)$ . Then (1.15) $\sup_{t\geq 0}\left\{\|u(t)\|_{\infty}+\|v(t)\|_{\infty}+\|w(t)\|_{W_{\infty}^{1}}\right\}<\infty\ .$ (b) Assume further that $\Omega$ is the ball $B_{r}(0)$ of radius $r>0$ centered at $x=0$ , $(u^{in},v^{in},w^{in})$ are radially symmetric, and $M=\|u^{in}\|_{1}\in(0,8\pi D)$ . Then ( 1.15 ) also holds true. The proof of Theorem 1.2 (a) relies on the properties of the Liapunov functional $\mathcal{E}$ (defined in ( 1.7b )) which are derived from the properties of $\mathcal{E}_{0}$ (defined in ( 1.7a )) [ 9 , 17 ] . The main observation is that, when $(u,w)$ belongs to the set $\mathcal{A}_{M}$ defined in ( 1.9 ) and $M\in(0,4\pi D)$ , it follows from the Moser-Trudinger inequality [ 8 ] that $\mathcal{E}_{0}(u,w)\geq\frac{4\pi D-M}{8\pi}\|\nabla w\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}\|w\|_{2}^{2}-b$ for some constant $b\geq 0$ which depends only on $\Omega$ , $D$ , $\delta$ , $M$ , and $\|w\|_{1}$ . Therefore, an upper bound on $\mathcal{E}_{0}(u,w)$ entails an estimate on the $W_{2}^{1}$ -norm of $w$ which, in turn, allows us to derive further estimates and deduce the boundedness of the solution. Similar arguments are used to prove Theorem 1.2 (b). The final result deals with the unboundedness of some solutions provided $\|u^{in}\|_{1}$ is large enough. Theorem 1.3 . Let $\theta\in(5/6,1)$ . (a) Given $M\in(4\pi D,\infty)\setminus 4\pi D\mathbb{N}$ , there are non-negative functions $(u^{in},v^{in},w^{in})\in\mathcal{I}_{M,\theta}$ for which the corresponding solution $(u,v,w)$ to ( 1.4 )-( 1.5 ) given by Theorem 1.1 satisfies (1.16) $\limsup_{t\to\infty}\|u(t)\|_{\infty}=\infty\ .$ (b) Assume further that $\Omega$ is the ball $B_{r}(0)$ of radius $r>0$ centered at $x=0$ . On the one hand, Theorem 1.3 extends [ 23 , Theorem 1.3] to ( 1.4 )-( 1.5 ) in an arbitrary domain $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}$ and, when $\Omega$ is a ball and the initial data are radially symmetric, applying the same approach to ( 1.1 ) is likely to provide an alternative proof of [ 23 , Theorem 1.3] . It however only gives the unboundedness of the solution, while [ 23 , Theorem 1.3] guarantees an exponential growth of the $L_{\infty}$ -norm of $u$ . On the other hand, Theorem 1.3 is also a consequence of the availability of a Liapunov functional and its properties, the latter being established in [ 10 , 11 , 13 ] . Roughly speaking, the proof of Theorem 1.3 involves three steps and proceeds along the lines of [ 13 ] . We first show that, given a solution $(u,v,w)$ to ( 1.4 )-( 1.5 ) such that $u\in L_{\infty}((0,\infty)\times\Omega)$ , then there are a sequence $(t_{k})_{k\geq 1}$ , $t_{k}\to\infty$ , of positive real numbers and a stationary solution $(u_{*},v_{*},w_{*})$ to ( 1.4 ) such that (1.17) $\mathcal{E}(u_{*},v_{*},w_{*})\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{E}(u(t_{k}),v(t_{k}),w(t_{k}))\ .$ It next follows from [ 13 , Lemma 3.5] that there is $\mu_{M}\geq 0$ depending only on the parameters in ( 1.4 ) and $M$ such that (1.18) $\mathcal{E}(u_{*},v_{*},w_{*})\geq-\mu_{M}\ .$ It now readily follows from ( 1.17 ), ( 1.18 ), and the fact that $\mathcal{E}$ is a Liapunov functional that, if $\mathcal{E}(u^{in},v^{in},w^{in})<-\mu_{M}$ , then $(u,v,w)$ cannot be bounded. ∎ 4. Unbounded solutions This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem 1.3 which is somewhat a direct consequence of the analysis performed in [ 10 , 11 , 13 ] . The main step is actually to recast the problem in a way suitable to apply results from [ 13 ] . Given $M>0$ , we define the set $\mathcal{S}_{M}$ as follows: $(u,v,w)\in\mathcal{S}_{M}$ if (4.1a) $\displaystyle(u,v,w)\in W_{2,\mathcal{B}}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})\ ,$ (4.1b) $\displaystyle u=v=M\frac{e^{w}}{\|e^{w}\|_{1}}\geq 0\;\text{ in }\;\Omega\ ,\qquad w\geq 0\;\text{ in }\;\Omega\ ,$ (4.1c) $\displaystyle-D\Delta w+\delta w=M\frac{e^{w}}{\|e^{w}\|_{1}}\;\text{in }\;\Omega\ ,\qquad\nabla w\cdot\mathbf{n}=0\;\text{ on }\;\partial\Omega\ .$ Equivalently, $\mathcal{S}_{M}$ is the set of non-negative stationary solutions $(u,v,w)$ to ( 1.4 ) which belong to $W_{2,\mathcal{B}}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})$ and for which $\|u\|_{1}=M$ . We begin with a lower bound of the Liapunov functional $\mathcal{E}$ (defined in ( 1.7 )) on $\mathcal{S}_{M}$ for suitable values of $M$ . Proposition 4.1 . (a) If $M\in(4\pi D,\infty)\setminus 4\pi D\mathbb{N}$ , then $\mu_{M}:=\inf_{(u,v,w)\in\mathcal{S}_{M}}\mathcal{E}(u,v,w)>-\infty\ .$ (b) If $\Omega=B_{r}(0)$ for some $r>0$ and $M\in(8\pi D,\infty)$ , then $\mu_{M}:=\inf_{(u,v,w)\in\mathcal{S}_{M,\text{rad}}}\mathcal{E}(u,v,w)>-\infty\ ,$ where $\mathcal{S}_{M,\text{rad}}:=\{(u,v,w)\in\mathcal{S}_{M}\ :\ u,v,w\;\text{ are radially symmetric}\}$ . Proof. – Case (a). Consider $(u,v,w)\in\mathcal{S}_{M}$ . Introducing $E(w):=\frac{D}{2}\|\nabla w\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}\|w\|_{2}^{2}-M\ln{\|e^{w}\|_{1}}+M\ln{M}-M+|\Omega|\ ,$ it follows from ( 3.9 ) that (4.2) $\mathcal{E}(u,v,w)\geq\mathcal{E}_{0}(u,w)\geq E(w)\ ,$ while the fact that $(u,v,w)\in\mathcal{S}_{M}$ implies that (4.3) $w\geq 0\;\text{ in }\;\Omega\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\|w\|_{1}=\frac{M}{\delta}\ .$ Setting $W:=w-\frac{M}{\delta|\Omega|}=w-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}w(x)\ \mathrm{d}x\ ,$ an alternative formula for $E(w)$ in terms of $W$ reads $\displaystyle E(w)$ $\displaystyle=\frac{D}{2}\|\nabla W\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}\|W\|_{2}^{2}-M\ln{\|e^{W}\|_{1}}-\frac{M^{2}}{2\delta|\Omega|}+M\ln{M}-M+|\Omega|$ (4.4) $\displaystyle=\frac{M}{|\Omega|}\mathcal{F}(W)-\frac{M^{2}}{2\delta|\Omega|}+M\ln{M}-M+|\Omega|-M\ln{|\Omega|}\ ,$ with (4.5) $\mathcal{F}(W):=\frac{|\Omega|}{2M}\left(D\|\nabla W\|_{2}^{2}+\delta\|W\|_{2}^{2}\right)-|\Omega|\ln{\left(\frac{\|e^{W}\|_{1}}{|\Omega|}\right)}\ .$ Furthermore, since $w$ solves ( 4.1c ), $W$ is a solution to $-D\Delta W+\delta W=\frac{M}{|\Omega|}\left(\frac{|\Omega|e^{W}}{\|e^{W}\|_{1}}-1\right)\;\text{in }\;\Omega\ ,\qquad\nabla W\cdot\mathbf{n}=0\;\text{ on }\;\partial\Omega\ .$ Since $M\in(4\pi D,\infty)\setminus 4\pi D\mathbb{N}$ , we infer from [ 13 , Lemma 3.5] that there is $\mu\geq 0$ which does not depend on $W$ such that (4.6) $\mathcal{F}(W)\geq-\mu\ .$ Combining ( 4.2 ), ( 4.4 ), and ( 4.6 ) completes the proof. – Case (b). The proof is the same as that for case (a), except that we use [ 13 , Corollary 3.7 & Remark 3.8] instead of [ 13 , Lemma 3.5] to obtain the lower bound ( 4.6 ) for all $M>8\pi D$ .
∎ The second step is to show that $\mathcal{E}$ is not bounded from below on the set $\mathcal{I}_{M,\theta}$ of initial data to which Theorem 1.1 applies. Proposition 4.2 . Let $\theta\in(5/6,1)$ . (a) If $M>4\pi D$ , then $\inf_{(u,v,w)\in\mathcal{I}_{M,\theta}}\mathcal{E}(u,v,w)=-\infty\ .$ (b) If $\Omega=B_{r}(0)$ for some $r>0$ and $M>8\pi D$ , then $\inf_{(u,v,w)\in\mathcal{I}_{M,\theta}^{\text{rad}}}\mathcal{E}(u,v,w)=-\infty\ ,$ where $\mathcal{I}_{M,\theta}^{\text{rad}}:=\{(u,v,w)\in\mathcal{I}_{M,\theta}\ :\ u,v,w\;\text{ are radially symmetric}\}$ . Proof. – Case (a). Fix $x_{0}\in\partial\Omega$ . For $\eta\in(0,\infty)$ and $x\in\Omega$ , define $\Theta_{\eta}(x):=2\ln{\left(\frac{\eta}{(\eta^{2}+\pi|x-x_{0}|^{2})}\right)}-\frac{2}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\ln{\left(\frac{\eta}{(\eta^{2}+\pi|y-x_{0}|^{2})}\right)}\ \mathrm{d}y\ .$ Then $\Theta_{\eta}\in W_{2}^{1}(\Omega)$ with $\langle\Theta_{\eta}\rangle=0$ and satisfies (4.7) $\lim_{\eta\to 0}\mathcal{F}(\Theta_{\eta})=-\infty\;\text{ and }\lim_{\eta\to 0}\|\nabla\Theta_{\eta}\|_{2}=\infty$ by [ 13 , Section 3] , since $M>4\pi D$ and the boundary of $\Omega$ is smooth. Let $\eta\in(0,\infty)$ . Since $W_{3}^{1}(\Omega)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot}}{{=}}[L_{3}(\Omega),W_{3,\mathcal{B}}^{2}(\Omega)]_{1/2}$ by [ 3 , Theorem 5.2] and $W_{3}^{1}(\Omega)$ is densely embedded in $W_{2}^{1}(\Omega)$ , there is a sequence $(W_{j,\eta})_{j\geq 1}$ in $W_{3,\mathcal{B}}^{2}(\Omega)$ such that (4.8) $\|W_{j,\eta}\|_{W_{2}^{1}}\leq 2\|\Theta_{\eta}\|_{W_{2}^{1}}\ ,\quad\langle W_{j,\eta}\rangle=0\ ,\quad j\geq 1\;\text{ and }\;\lim_{j\to\infty}\|W_{j,\eta}-\Theta_{\eta}\|_{W_{2}^{1}}=0\ .$ In addition, possibly after extracting a subsequence, we may assume that (4.9) $\lim_{j\to\infty}W_{j,\eta}(x)=\Theta_{\eta}(x)\;\text{ for a.e. }\;x\in\Omega\ .$ In addition, it follows from Hölder’s inequality and Proposition 3.3 that, for any measurable subset $\omega$ of $\Omega$ , $\displaystyle\int_{\omega}e^{W_{j,\eta}}\ \mathrm{d}x$ $\displaystyle\leq|\omega|^{1/2}\left(\int_{\omega}e^{2W_{j,\eta}}\ \mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/2}$ $\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\kappa_{0}}|\omega|^{1/2}\exp{\left(\frac{\|\nabla W_{j,\eta}\|_{2}^{2}}{4\pi}+\frac{\|W_{j,\eta}\|_{1}}{|\Omega|}\right)}$ $\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\kappa_{0}}|\omega|^{1/2}\exp{\left(\frac{\|\Theta_{\eta}\|_{W_{2}^{1}}^{2}}{\pi}+\frac{\sqrt{2}\|\Theta_{\eta}\|_{W_{2}^{1}}}{\sqrt{|\Omega|}}\right)}\ ,$ which allows us to deduce from Dunford-Pettis’ theorem that $\left(e^{W_{j,\eta}}\right)_{j\geq 1}\;\text{ is relatively sequentially weakly compact in }\;L_{1}(\Omega)\ .$ Since $\left(e^{W_{j,\eta}}\right)_{j\geq 1}$ converges to $e^{\Theta_{\eta}}$ a.e. in $\Omega$ by ( 4.9 ), we deduce from these two properties and Vitali’s theorem that (4.10) $\lim_{j\to\infty}\left\|e^{W_{j,\eta}}-e^{\Theta_{\eta}}\right\|_{1}=0\ .$ Recalling the definition ( 4.5 ) of $\mathcal{F}$ , we infer from ( 4.8 ) and ( 4.10 ) that (4.11) $\lim_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{F}(W_{\eta,j})=\mathcal{F}(\Theta_{\eta})\ .$ Owing to ( 4.8 ) and ( 4.11 ), there is $j_{\eta}\geq 1$ such that the function $W_{\eta}:=W_{j_{\eta},\eta}$ satisfies (4.12) $W_{\eta}\in W_{3,\mathcal{B}}^{2}(\Omega)\;\text{ and }\;\left\|W_{\eta}-\Theta_{\eta}\right\|_{W_{2}^{1}}+\left|\mathcal{F}(W_{\eta})-\mathcal{F}(\Theta_{\eta})\right|\leq\eta\ .$ After this preparation, for $\eta>0$ , we set (4.13) $w_{\eta}:=W_{\eta}+\frac{M}{\delta|\Omega|}\ ,\quad u_{\eta}:=M\frac{e^{w_{\eta}}}{\|e^{w_{\eta}}\|_{1}}\ ,$ and, since $-D\Delta w_{\eta}+\delta w_{\eta}\in L_{3}(\Omega)$ , we fix (4.14) $v_{\eta}\in W_{3}^{1}(\Omega)\;\;\text{ such that }\;\;\|v_{\eta}+D\Delta w_{\eta}-\delta w_{\eta}\|_{3}\leq\eta\ .$ Now, owing to ( 1.7a ), ( 4.4 ), and ( 4.13 ), $\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{0}(u_{\eta},w_{\eta})$ $\displaystyle=M\ln{M}-M\ln{\|e^{w_{\eta}}\|_{1}}-M+|\Omega|+\frac{D}{2}\|\nabla w_{\eta}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}\|w_{\eta}\|_{2}^{2}$ $\displaystyle=E(W_{\eta})=\frac{M}{|\Omega|}\mathcal{F}(W_{\eta})+\chi\ ,$ with $\chi:=-\frac{M^{2}}{2\delta|\Omega|}+M\ln{M}-M+|\Omega|-M\ln{|\Omega|}\ ,$ so that (4.15) $\mathcal{E}(u_{\eta},v_{\eta},w_{\eta})=\frac{M}{|\Omega|}\mathcal{F}(W_{\eta})+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\|-D\Delta w_{\eta}+\delta w_{\eta}-v_{\eta}\|_{2}^{2}+\chi\ .$ We then infer from ( 4.12 ), ( 4.14 ), ( 4.15 ), and Hölder’s inequality that $\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u_{\eta},v_{\eta},w_{\eta})$ $\displaystyle\leq\frac{M}{|\Omega|}\mathcal{F}(\Theta_{\eta})+\frac{\eta M}{|\Omega|}+\frac{\varepsilon|\Omega|^{1/3}}{2}\|-D\Delta w_{\eta}+\delta w_{\eta}-v_{\eta}\|_{3}^{2}+\chi$ $\displaystyle\leq\frac{M}{|\Omega|}\mathcal{F}(\Theta_{\eta})+\frac{\eta M}{|\Omega|}+\frac{\varepsilon\eta^{2}|\Omega|^{1/3}}{2}+\chi\ .$ Taking the limit $\eta\to 0$ in the previous inequality and using ( 4.7 ) lead us to $\lim_{\eta\to 0}\mathcal{E}(u_{\eta},v_{\eta},w_{\eta})=-\infty\ ,$ and completes the proof. – Case (b). The proof is the same as the previous one except that the starting point is [ 11 , Lemma 2] , the function $\Theta_{\eta}$ being defined as in Case (a) but with $x_{0}=0$ .
∎ Proof of Theorem 1.3 . – Case (a). Consider $M\in(4\pi D,\infty)\setminus 4\pi D\mathbb{N}$ and $\theta\in(5/6,1)$ . According to Proposition 4.2 , there is $(u^{in},v^{in},w^{in})\in\mathcal{I}_{M,\theta}$ such that (4.16) $\mathcal{E}(u^{in},v^{in},w^{in})<\mu_{M}\ ,$ the parameter $\mu_{M}$ being defined in Proposition 4.1 . Assume for contradiction that there is $\Lambda>0$ such that $\|u(t)\|_{\infty}\leq\Lambda$ for all $t\geq 0$ . We are then in a position to apply Proposition 3.3 (a) and deduce that there are a sequence $(t_{k})_{k\geq 1}$ , $t_{k}\to\infty$ , of positive real numbers and $(u_{*},v_{*},w_{*})\in\mathcal{S}_{M}$ such that $\mathcal{E}(u_{*},v_{*},w_{*})\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{E}(u(t_{k}),v(t_{k}),w(t_{k}))\ .$ Since $\mathcal{E}$ is a Liapunov function for ( 1.4 ) by Lemma 3.4 and $(u_{*},v_{*},w_{*})\in\mathcal{S}_{M}$ , we infer from Proposition 4.1 (a) and the previous identity that $\mu_{M}\leq\mathcal{E}(u_{*},v_{*},w_{*})\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{E}(u(t_{k}),v(t_{k}),w(t_{k}))\leq\mathcal{E}(u^{in},v^{in},w^{in})\ ,$ which contradicts ( 4.16 ) and completes the proof. – Case (b). The proof is the same as the previous one and relies on Proposition 4.1 (b) and Proposition 4.2 (b).
∎ 5. Related models To finish with, we just point out in an informal way that the related models ( 1.1 ) and ( 1.3 ) also have a Liapunov functional which is closely related to that of ( 1.4 ). Specifically, the functional $\mathcal{E}_{0}$ defined by ( 1.7a ) is a Liapunov functional for ( 1.1 ) and, if $(u,v,w)$ is a solution to ( 1.1 ), then $\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{E}_{0}(u,w)=-\int_{\Omega}u|\nabla(\ln{u}-w)|^{2}\ \mathrm{d}x-D\|\nabla\partial_{t}w\|_{2}^{2}\ .$ In the same vein, $\mathcal{E}_{0}$ is also a Liapunov functional for ( 1.3 ) and, if $(u,v,w)$ is a solution to ( 1.3 ), then $\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{E}_{0}(u,w)=-\int_{\Omega}u|\nabla(\ln{u}-w)|^{2}\ \mathrm{d}x-\nu\varepsilon\left(D\|\nabla\partial_{t}w\|_{2}^{2}+\delta\|\partial_{t}w\|_{2}^{2}\right)\ .$ It is then likely that the analysis performed for ( 1.4 ) adapts to ( 1.1 ) and ( 1.3 ), possibly with slightly different statements for ( 1.1 ). Indeed, Proposition 4.1 (b) is only valid for $M\in(8\pi D,\infty)\setminus 8\pi D\mathbb{N}$ in that case, see [ 11 , Lemma 3] .
