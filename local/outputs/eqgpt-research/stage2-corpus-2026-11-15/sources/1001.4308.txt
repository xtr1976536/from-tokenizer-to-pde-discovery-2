Schrödinger-Poisson system in the two-dimensional whole space Satoshi Masaki Address: Division of Mathematics Graduate School of Information Sciences Tohoku University Sendai 980-8579, Japan Email address: masaki@ims.is.tohoku.ac.jp Abstract. We consider the Cauchy problem of
the two-dimensional Schrödinger-Poisson system in the energy class.
Though the Newtonian potential diverges at the spatial infinity in the logarithmic order,
global well-posedness is proven in both defocusing and focusing cases.
The key is a decomposition of the nonlinearity into a sum of
the linear logarithmic potential and a good remainder,
which enables us to apply the perturbation method.
Our argument can be adapted to the one-dimensional problem. 1. Introduction This paper is devoted to the study of the Schödinger-Poisson system (1.1) $\left\{\begin{aligned} &i{\partial}_{t}u+\frac{1}{2}\Delta u=\lambda Pu,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{1+2},\\
&-\Delta P=|u|^{2},\\
&u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\end{aligned}\right.$ where $\lambda$ is a real constant.
We suppose $P$ is the Newtonian potential (1.2) $P=-\frac{1}{2\pi}(\log|x|*|u|^{2})$ where $*$ denotes the convolution.
For a suitable $u$ ,
this is the unique strong solution of $-\Delta P=|u|^{2}$ under the condition $|\nabla P|\to 0\text{ as }|x|\to\infty,\quad\nabla P\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\quad P(0)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\log|y|)|u(y)|^{2}dy$ (see [ 11 ] ).
When the dimensions are larger than two, the Schrödinger-Poisson
system is a special case of the Hartree equation and
one of the typical example of the nonlinear Schrödinger equation
with a nonlocal nonlinearity, and there is large amount of literature
(see [ 6 ] and references therein).
On the other hand, the two-dimensional case is less studied.
In [ 1 , 18 ] , ( 1.1 ) is considered
with some restrictive assumptions such as a neutrality condition
which confirms that the Newtonian potential ( 1.2 )
does not diverge at the spatial infinity and in particular belongs to $L^{2}$ space.
The Poisson equation is sometimes posed with a background (or doping profile): $-\Delta P=|u|^{2}-b,$ where $b$ is a given positive function.
Then, the neutrality condition is $\int|u|^{2}-bdx=0$ or equivalently $\mathcal{F}(|u|^{2}-b)(0)=0$ .
When we consider the problem in dimensions less than three, this condition is useful to control $P$ .
Notice that this condition excludes all nontrivial solutions when $b\equiv 0$ ,
and that we need to remove this condition for the study of ( 1.1 ).
In [ 11 ] , the above assumptions are removed and
the existence of a unique local solution is proven
for data in the usual Sobolev space $H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})$ $(s>2)$ despite the fact that the nonlinear potential diverges at the spatial infinity.
Since ( 1.2 ) is not necessarily defined for $u\in H^{s}$ ( $s>2$ )
we introduced a new formula $P=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\log\frac{|x-y|}{|y|}\right)|u(y)|^{2}dy$ which makes sense merely if $|u|^{2}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})$ ( $p\in(1,2)$ ).
We underline that the local solutions given there do not have finite energy
(the energy is given in ( 1.5 ) below).
Our aim in this paper is to prove that there exists a time-global solution
if initial data has finite energy. For our analysis, the following reduction is crucial:
We guess that the Newtonian potential ( 1.2 ) may behave like $-\frac{1}{2\pi}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\log|x|$ at the spatial infinity,
which will be the bad part of the nonlinearity,
and decompose the nonlinearity as $\lambda Pu=-\frac{\lambda}{2\pi}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}(\log\left\langle x\right\rangle)u-\frac{\lambda}{2\pi}u\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\log\frac{|x-y|}{\left\langle x\right\rangle}\right)|u(y)|^{2}dy,$ where $\left\langle x\right\rangle=(1+|x|^{2})^{1/2}$ .
We then
obtain $i{\partial}_{t}u+\frac{1}{2}\Delta u+\frac{\lambda}{2\pi}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}(\log\left\langle x\right\rangle)u=-\frac{\lambda}{2\pi}u\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\log\frac{|x-y|}{\left\langle x\right\rangle}\right)|u(y)|^{2}dy.$ It will turn out that the bad part of $P$ is correctly extracted from the original
nonlinearity and
therefore the behavior of the “new nonlinearity” becomes better.
Notice that one can also expect that $\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}$ is conserved because $\lambda$ is a real number.
Hence, putting $m:=-\frac{\lambda}{2\pi}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},$ we reach to the equation (1.3) $\left\{\begin{aligned} &i{\partial}_{t}u+\left(\frac{1}{2}\Delta-m\log\left\langle x\right\rangle\right)u=-\frac{\lambda}{2\pi}u\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\log\frac{|x-y|}{\left\langle x\right\rangle}\right)|u(y)|^{2}dy,\\
&u(0,x)=u_{0}(x).\end{aligned}\right.$ Notice that $-m\log\left\langle x\right\rangle$ is now completely independent of $u$ and that it therefore can be regarded as a linear potential.
In what follows, we work with this equation.
Observe that
if there exists a solution to ( 1.3 ) conserving $\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}$ ,
then it is also a solution of ( 1.1 ). Now, the linear part of the equation
is not $i{\partial}_{t}+(1/2)\Delta$ but $i{\partial}_{t}+(1/2)\Delta-m\log\left\langle x\right\rangle$ .
Thus, a natural choice of the function space on which we shall work is
not the Sobolev space $H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})$ any more, but the following one: (1.4) $\displaystyle\mathcal{H}:=\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2});\sqrt{\log\left\langle x\right\rangle}u\in L^{2}\},$ $\displaystyle\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}:=\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+\left\lVert\sqrt{\log\left\langle\cdot\right\rangle}u\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.$ If $m>0$ , that is, if $\lambda<0$ , then
the above space coincides with the form domain of
the positive operator $-\frac{1}{2}\Delta+m\log\left\langle x\right\rangle$ .
Our main result is the following: Theorem 1.1 . The problem ( 1.3 ) is globally well-posed in $\mathcal{H}$ .
Moreover, the solution conserves $\left\lVert u(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}$ and the energy (1.5) $E(t)=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\nabla u(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{\lambda}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\log|x-y|)|u(t,x)|^{2}|u(t,y)|^{2}dxdy.$ Corollary 1.2 . The Problem ( 1.1 ) is globally well-posed in $\mathcal{H}$ . Remark 1.3 . Let $u\in C(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{H})$ be a solution of ( 1.3 ) (and of ( 1.1 )) given in
Theorem 1.1 .
Then, $v:=u\exp(-i\frac{\lambda}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{t}\|\sqrt{\log|\cdot|}u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds)$ solves (1.6) $\left\{\begin{aligned} &i{\partial}_{t}v+\frac{1}{2}\Delta v=-\frac{\lambda}{2\pi}v\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\log\frac{|x-y|}{|y|}\right)|v(y)|^{2}dy,\\
&v(0,x)=u_{0}(x).\end{aligned}\right.$ Notice that the nonlinearity of ( 1.6 ) makes sense without
the momentum condition $\sqrt{\log|\cdot|}v\in L^{2}$ .
This observation explains why existence of a time-local solution can be proven
by assuming only $u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})$ ( $s>1$ ) in [ 11 ] . 1.1. Consequent results Our argument is also applicable to ( 1.1 )

Section 4 is devoted to the study of ( 1.7 ). 2. Preliminaries 2.1. Strichartz estimate We first summarize the properties on
the operator (2.1) $A:=\frac{1}{2}\Delta-m\log\left\langle x\right\rangle,$ where $m\neq 0$ is a real constant.
For any $m$ , $A$ is essentially self-adjoint on $C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})$ (see [ 13 ] ).
Since our potential is sub-quadratic, that is,
since $|{\partial}^{\alpha}\log\left\langle x\right\rangle|\to 0$ as $|x|\to\infty$ for $|\alpha|=2$ and ${\partial}^{\alpha}\log\left\langle x\right\rangle\in L^{\infty}$ for $|\alpha|\geqslant 3$ ,
the following estimate is established in [ 17 ] :
For any $T>0$ , $\left\lVert e^{itA}\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leqslant C|t|^{-1}\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{1}}$ for $t\in[-T,T]$ , where $C$ depends on $T$ (see also [ 8 ] ).
Once we know this type of estimate, the Strichartz estimate follows by interpolation.
We say that a pair $(q,r)$ is admissible if $2\leqslant r<\infty$ and $2/q=\delta(r):=1-2/r$ . Lemma 2.1 (Strichartz’s estimate) . For any $T>0$ ,
the following properties hold: • Suppose $\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})$ .
For any admissible pair $(q,r)$ , there exists a constant $C=C(T,q,r)$ such that $\left\lVert e^{itA}\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{q}((-T,T);L^{r})}\leqslant C\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}.$ • Let $I\subset(-T,T)$ be an interval and $t_{0}\in\overline{I}$ .
For any admissible pairs $(q,r)$ and $(\gamma,\rho)$ ,
there exists a constant $C=C(t,q,r,\gamma,\rho)$ such that $\left\lVert\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{i(t-s)A}F(s)ds\right\rVert_{L^{q}(I;L^{r})}\leqslant C\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(I;L^{\rho^{\prime}})}$ for every $F\in L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(I;L^{\rho^{\prime}})$ . 2.2. Some estiamtes Lemma 2.2 . Let $W$ be an arbitrary weight function such that $\nabla W$ , $\Delta W\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})$ .
It holds for all $T>0$ , admissible pair $(q,r)$ ,
and $\varphi\in\mathcal{H}$ that $\displaystyle\left\lVert[\nabla,e^{itA}]\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{q}((-T,T);L^{r})}\leqslant{}$ $\displaystyle C|T|\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{2},$ $\displaystyle\left\lVert[W,e^{itA}]\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{q}((-T,T);L^{r})}\leqslant{}$ $\displaystyle C|T|\left\lVert(1+\nabla)\varphi\right\rVert_{2}.$ Proof. Since $v=e^{itA}\varphi$ solves $i{\partial}_{t}v+Av=0$ ,
an explicit calculation shows $[\nabla,e^{itA}]\varphi=-i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)A}\frac{mx}{1+x^{2}}e^{isA}\varphi ds$ and $[W,e^{itA}]\varphi=i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)A}\left(\nabla W\cdot\nabla+\frac{1}{2}\Delta W\right)e^{isA}\varphi ds.$ The Strichartz estimate therefore gives the desired estimates.

For any $p\in[1,\infty)$ and $\varepsilon>0$ , there exist a function $W(x,y)\geqslant 0$ with $\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{y}L^{p}_{x}}\leqslant\varepsilon$ and a constant $C_{0}$ such that $|K(x,y)|\leqslant C_{0}+W(x,y)$ holds for all $(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2+2}$ . Proof. Take $\eta\in(0,1]$ and set $W(x,y)=|K(x,y)|{\bf 1}_{|x-y|\leqslant\eta}(x,y)$ .
If $\eta$ is sufficiently small then $\left\lVert W(\cdot,y)\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\leqslant\frac{\left\lVert\log|x|\right\rVert_{L^{p}(|x|\leqslant\eta)}+\log\left\langle|y|+\eta\right\rangle\left\lVert 1\right\rVert_{L^{p}(|x|\leqslant\eta)}}{1+\log\left\langle y\right\rangle}\leqslant\varepsilon$ since $\log|x|$ belongs to $L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})$ for all $p<\infty$ .
Moreover,
by (2.12) of [ 11 ] , $\sup_{|x-y|\geqslant\eta}K(x,y)\leqslant 1+\log\frac{\sqrt{3}}{\eta}$ for any $\eta\leqslant 1$ , which completes the proof.

We conclude from the Strichartz estimate, ( 3.2 ), and ( 3.5 ) that $\left\lVert Q[u]\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{T}}\leqslant C_{1}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}+C_{2}(T+T^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{r_{0}}})\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{T}}^{3}.$ A similar argument shows $\left\lVert Q[u_{1}]-Q[u_{2}]\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{T}}\leqslant C_{3}(T+T^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{r_{0}}})(\left\lVert u_{1}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{T}}+\left\lVert u_{2}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{T}})^{2}\left\lVert u_{1}-u_{2}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{T}}.$ Thus, if we take $M\geqslant 2C_{1}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}$ then there exists $T=T(M)$ such that $Q$ is a contraction map from $\mathcal{H}_{T,M}$ to itself. ∎ 3.2. Global existence We first give a useful blow-up criteria. Lemma 3.2 . Suppose $u_{0}\in\mathcal{H}$ .
Let $u\in C((-T_{\mathrm{min}},T_{\mathrm{max}});\mathcal{H})$ be a unique maximal solution given by Lemma 3.1 .
If $T_{\mathrm{max}}<\infty$ (resp. $T_{\mathrm{min}}<\infty$ ), then $\left\lVert\nabla u(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\to\infty$ as $t\uparrow T_{\mathrm{max}}$ (resp. $t\downarrow-T_{\mathrm{min}}$ ). Proof. We only consider positive time.
Suppose $T_{\mathrm{max}}<\infty$ .
Then, $\left\lVert u(t)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}$ has to diverge as $t\uparrow T_{\mathrm{max}}$ .
Otherwise, we can extend the solution beyond $T_{\mathrm{max}}$ by Lemma 3.1 .
Recall that $\left\lVert u(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}=\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}$ .
Since $\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\sqrt{\log\left\langle x\right\rangle}u(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}$ $\displaystyle{}=2\operatorname{Re}\int(\log\left\langle x\right\rangle){\partial}_{t}u(t)\overline{u(t)}dx$ $\displaystyle{}=-\operatorname{Im}\int(\log\left\langle x\right\rangle)\Delta u(t)\overline{u(t)}dx$ $\displaystyle{}=\operatorname{Im}\int\frac{x}{1+x^{2}}\cdot\nabla u(t)\overline{u(t)}dx,$ it holds that $\left\lVert\sqrt{\log\left\langle x\right\rangle}u(t_{2})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leqslant\left\lVert\sqrt{\log\left\langle x\right\rangle}u(t_{1})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+|t_{2}-t_{1}|\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}((t_{1},t_{2});L^{2})}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}$ for all $-T_{\mathrm{min}}<t_{1}<t_{2}<T_{\mathrm{max}}$ .
This implies that
if we assume $\limsup_{t\uparrow T_{\mathrm{max}}}\left\lVert\nabla u(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}<\infty$ then $\left\lVert u(t)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}$ never blows up.
We hence obtain the lemma.
∎ Remark 3.3 . As in [ 9 ] , the solution breaks down with concentration at a point
if $\|\sqrt{\log\left\langle x\right\rangle}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}=0$ .
However, this does not occur when $\|\nabla u(t)\|$ is bounded above.
Indeed, since $\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}(|x|<r)}\leqslant\left\lVert 1\right\rVert_{L^{4}(|x|<r)}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{4}}\leqslant Cr^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}$ for any $r>0$ and since $\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}(|x|<r)}=\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}-\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}(|x|\geqslant r)}\geqslant\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}-\frac{\|\sqrt{\log\left\langle x\right\rangle}u\|_{L^{2}}}{(\log\left\langle r\right\rangle)^{1/2}},$ by letting $r=\|\sqrt{\log\left\langle x\right\rangle}u\|_{L^{2}}$ , we obtain $\|\sqrt{\log\left\langle x\right\rangle}u\|_{L^{2}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leqslant C\left(\frac{\|\sqrt{\log\left\langle x\right\rangle}u\|_{L^{2}}}{\log\left\langle\|\sqrt{\log\left\langle x\right\rangle}u\|_{L^{2}}\right\rangle}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+C\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}},$ which implies $\|\sqrt{\log\left\langle x\right\rangle}u\|_{L^{2}}$ is strictly positive if $\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}<\infty$ . Proof of Theorem 1.1 . Let us establish a priori estimate of $\left\lVert\nabla u(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}$ . We first consider the case $\lambda<0$ .
Since $\log|x|\geqslant 0$ for $|x|\geqslant 1$ , $\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda}{4\pi}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2+2}}\log|x-y||u(x)|^{2}|u(y)|^{2}dxdy$ $\displaystyle{}\geqslant-\frac{|\lambda|}{4\pi}\iint_{|x-y|<1}\left\lvert\log|x-y|\right\rvert|u(x)|^{2}|u(y)|^{2}dxdy$ $\displaystyle{}\geqslant-\frac{|\lambda|}{4\pi}\left\lVert\log|x|\right\rVert_{L^{2}(|x|\leqslant 1)}^{2}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}$ By the $L^{2}$ -conservation and the Sobolev embedding,
we have (3.6) $\left\lVert\nabla u(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leqslant 2E_{0}+C\left\lVert\nabla u(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}.$ Therefore, there exists a constant $M$ independent of $t$ such that $\left\lVert\nabla u(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leqslant M$ . We now suppose $\lambda>0$ .
By Lemma 2.3 , for any $\varepsilon>0$ there exists a constant $C_{0}$ such that
the following estimate holds: $\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{4\pi}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2+2}}\log|x-y||u(x)|^{2}|u(y)|^{2}dxdy$ $\displaystyle{}\leqslant\frac{\lambda}{4\pi}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2+2}}\log\frac{|x-y|}{\left\langle x\right\rangle}|u(x)|^{2}|u(y)|^{2}dxdy+\frac{\lambda}{4\pi}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\lVert\sqrt{\log\left\langle x\right\rangle}u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}$ $\displaystyle{}\leqslant\frac{\lambda}{4\pi}(C_{0}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2})\left\lVert\sqrt{1+\log\left\langle x\right\rangle}u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{4\pi}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\lVert\sqrt{\log\left\langle x\right\rangle}u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}$ $\displaystyle{}\leqslant\frac{\lambda C_{0}}{4\pi}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{4}+\frac{\lambda(C_{0}+1)}{4\pi}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\lVert\sqrt{\log\left\langle x\right\rangle}u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\varepsilon\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{3}\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}$ $\displaystyle\quad+C\varepsilon\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\sqrt{\log\left\langle x\right\rangle}u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}$ $\displaystyle{}\leqslant C_{1}+C_{2}(\varepsilon+|t|)\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\left\lVert\nabla u(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+C_{3}\varepsilon|t|\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\left\lVert\nabla u(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},$ where $C_{i}$ ( $i=1,2,3$ ) depends only on $\lambda$ , $C_{0}$ , $\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}$ , and $\varepsilon$ .
Fix $T>0$ . Taking $\varepsilon<(8C_{3}T)^{-1}$ , we deduce from the conservation of $E(t)$ that (3.7) $\left(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\left\lVert\nabla u(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\right)^{2}\leqslant 4E(0)+4C_{1}+4C_{2}(\varepsilon+2T)\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\left\lVert\nabla u(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}$ for $0\leqslant t\leqslant 2T$ . This implies that $\sup_{t\in[0,2T]}\left\lVert\nabla u(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leqslant C(\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}},T)<\infty.$ Since $T$ is arbitrary, we obtain the global existence.
