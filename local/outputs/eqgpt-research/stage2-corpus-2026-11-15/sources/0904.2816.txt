Toronto, ON M5S 2E4, Canada Email address: oh@math.toronto.edu Abstract. We continue our study of the well-posedness theory of a one-parameter family of coupled KdV-type systems in the periodic setting.
When the value of a coupling parameter $\alpha\in(0,4)\setminus\{1\}$ , we show that
the Gibbs measure is invariant under the flow and
the system is globally well-posed almost surely on the statistical ensemble,
provided that certain Diophantine conditions are satisfied. Key words and phrases: KdV; well-posedness; Gibbs measure; Diophantine Conditions 2000 Mathematics Subject Classification 35Q53 1. Introduction In this paper, we consider coupled KdV systems of the form: (1) $\begin{cases}u_{t}+a_{11}u_{xxx}+a_{12}v_{xxx}+b_{1}uu_{x}+b_{2}uv_{x}+b_{3}u_{x}v+b_{4}vv_{x}=0\\
v_{t}+a_{21}u_{xxx}+a_{22}v_{xxx}+b_{5}uu_{x}+b_{6}uv_{x}+b_{7}u_{x}v+b_{8}vv_{x}=0\\
(u,v)\big|_{t=0}=(u_{0},v_{0})\end{cases}$ in the periodic setting, where $A=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a_{11}&a_{12}\\
a_{21}&a_{22}\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ is self-adjoint, and $u$ and $v$ are real-valued functions.
There are several systems of this type: the Gear-Grimshaw system [ 9 ] , the Hirota-Satsuma system [ 12 ] ,
the Majda-Biello system [ 16 ] , etc.
By applying the space-time scale changes along with the diagonalization of $A$ , one can reduce ( 1 ) to (2) $\begin{cases}u_{t}+u_{xxx}+\widetilde{b_{1}}uu_{x}+\widetilde{b_{2}}uv_{x}+\widetilde{b_{3}}u_{x}v+\widetilde{b_{4}}vv_{x}=0\\
v_{t}+\alpha v_{xxx}+\widetilde{b_{5}}uu_{x}+\widetilde{b_{6}}uv_{x}+\widetilde{b_{7}}u_{x}v+\widetilde{b_{8}}vv_{x}=0\\
(u,v)\big|_{t=0}=(u_{0},v_{0}),\end{cases}$ where $\alpha\neq 0$ , $(x,t)\in\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}$ with $\mathbb{T}=[0,2\pi)$ . In this paper, we assume that
( 2 ) has a Hamiltonian $H$ of the form
“ $H(u,v)=\frac{1}{2}\int u_{x}^{2}+\alpha v_{x}^{2}+$ nonlinear terms”
and that both $H$ and $N(u,v)=\frac{1}{2}\int u^{2}+bv^{2}$ , for some $b>0$ , are conserved under the flow of ( 2 ).
(Note that this is the case for the Gear-Grimshaw and the Majda-Biello systems among other coupled KdV systems.)
When $\alpha\neq 1$ , it is shown in [ 18 ] that
there is an interval $I_{0}$ around $\alpha=1$ such that particular resonances occur for $\alpha\in I_{0}\setminus\{1\}$ which are not present when $\alpha=1$ .
We show that, for $\alpha\in I_{0}\setminus\{1\}$ ,
the Gibbs measure $d\mu=Z^{-1}\exp(-\beta H(u,v))\prod_{x\in\mathbb{T}}du(x)\otimes dv(x)$ is invariant under the flow ( 2 )
and that ( 2 ) is globally well-posed almost surely on the statistical ensemble,
provided that certain Diophantine conditions are satisfied. As a model example, we consider the Majda-Biello system: (3) $\begin{cases}u_{t}+u_{xxx}+vv_{x}=0\\
v_{t}+\alpha v_{xxx}+(uv)_{x}=0\\
(u,v)\big|_{t=0}=(u_{0},v_{0}),\end{cases}\ (x,t)\in\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R},$ where $\mathbb{T}=[0,2\pi)$ , $0<\alpha\leq 4$ , and $u$ and $v$ are real-valued functions.
This system has been proposed by Majda and Biello [ 16 ] as a reduced asymptotic model to study the nonlinear resonant interactions of
long wavelength equatorial Rossby waves and barotropic Rossby waves with a significant mid-latitude projection,
in the presence of suitable horizontally and vertically sheared zonal mean flows.
In [ 16 ] , the values of $\alpha$ are numerically determined and they are $0.899$ , $0.960$ , and $0.980$ for different equatorial Rossby waves.
Of particular interest to us is the periodic case because of its challenging mathematical nature
as well as its physical relevance of the proposed model (the spatial period for the system before scaling is set as $40,000$ km in [ 16 ] .) Several conservation laws are known for the system: (4) $E_{1}=\int u\,dx,\ E_{2}=\int v\,dx,\ N(u,v)=\frac{1}{2}\int u^{2}+v^{2}dx,\ H(u,v)=\frac{1}{2}\int u_{x}^{2}+\alpha v_{x}^{2}-uv^{2}dx,$ where $H(u,v)$ is the Hamiltonian of the system.
There seems to be no other conservation law, suggesting that the Majda-Biello system may not be completely integrable.
The system has scaling which is similar to that of KdV and the critical Sobolev index $s_{c}$ is $-\frac{3}{2}$ just like KdV. First, we review the local and global well-posedness (LWP and GWP) results of ( 3 ) from [ 18 ] , [ 19 ] .
Note that all the results, except for the global well-posedness on $\mathbb{T}$ for $\alpha\in(0,1)\cup(1,4)$ , are sharp in the sense that
the smoothness/uniform continuity of the solution map fails below the specified regularities.
When $\alpha=1$ , we showed in [ 19 ] that ( 3 ) is globally well-posed
in $H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T})\times H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T})$ without the mean 0 condition on the initial data,
via the $I$ -method developed by Colliander-Keel-Staffilani-Takaoka-Tao [ 7 ] in the vector-valued variants $X^{s,b}_{p,q}$ of the Bourgain space $X^{s,b}$ [ 2 ] . Now, let’s turn to the case $\alpha\in(0,1)\cup(1,4]$ .
In this case, we have two distinct linear semigroups $S(t)=e^{-t\partial_{x}^{3}}$ and $S_{\alpha}(t)=e^{-\alpha t\partial_{x}^{3}}$ corresponding to the linear equations for $u$ and $v$ .
Thus, we need to define two distinct Bourgain spaces $X^{s,b}$ and $X_{\alpha}^{s,b}$ to encompass the situation.
For $s,b\in\mathbb{R}$ , let $X^{s,b}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})$ and $X_{\alpha}^{s,b}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})$ be the completion of
the Schwartz class $\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})$ with respect to the norms (5) $\displaystyle\|u\|_{X^{s,b}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})}=\big\|\langle n\rangle^{s}\langle\tau-n^{3}\rangle^{b}\widehat{u}(n,\tau)\big\|_{L^{2}_{n,\tau}(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R})}$ (6) $\displaystyle\|v\|_{X_{\alpha}^{s,b}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})}=\big\|\langle n\rangle^{s}\langle\tau-\alpha n^{3}\rangle^{b}\widehat{v}(n,\tau)\big\|_{L^{2}_{n,\tau}(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R})},$ where $\langle\,\cdot\,\rangle=1+|\cdot|$ .
Then, two of the crucial bilinear estimates in establishing the LWP of ( 3 ) are: (7) $\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}(v_{1}v_{2})\|_{X^{s,-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})}$ $\displaystyle\lesssim\|v_{1}\|_{X_{\alpha}^{s,\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})}\|v_{2}\|_{X_{\alpha}^{s,\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}).}$ (8) $\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}(uv)\|_{X_{\alpha}^{s,-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})}$ $\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{X^{s,\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})}\|v\|_{X_{\alpha}^{s,\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}).}$ First, consider the first bilinear estimate ( 7 ).
As in Kenig-Ponce-Vega [ 13 ] , we define the bilinear operator $\mathcal{B}_{s,b}(\cdot,\cdot)$ by $\mathcal{B}_{s,b}(f,g)(n,\tau)=\frac{n\langle n\rangle^{s}}{\langle\tau-n^{3}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{n_{1}+n_{2}=n}\intt_{\tau_{1}+\tau_{2}=\tau}\frac{f(n_{1},\tau_{1})g(n_{2},\tau_{2})}{\langle n_{1}\rangle^{s}\langle n_{2}\rangle^{s}\langle\tau_{1}-\alpha n_{1}^{3}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\tau_{2}-\alpha n_{2}^{3}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}}d\tau_{1}.$ Then, ( 7 ) holds if and only if $\left\|\mathcal{B}_{s,b}(f,g)\right\|_{L^{2}_{n,\tau}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}_{n,\tau}}\|g\|_{L^{2}_{n,\tau}}.$ As in the KdV case, $\partial_{x}$ appears on the left hand side of ( 7 ) and thus
we need to make up for this loss of derivative from $\langle\tau-n^{3}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\tau_{1}-\alpha n_{1}^{3}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\tau_{2}-\alpha n_{2}^{3}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}$ in the denominator.
Recall that we basically gain $\frac{3}{2}$ derivatives in the KdV case (with $n,n_{1},n_{2}\neq 0$ )
thanks to the algebraic identity (9) $n^{3}-n_{1}^{3}-n_{2}^{3}=3nn_{1}n_{2}$ for $n=n_{1}+n_{2}$ .
However, when $\alpha\neq 1$ , we no longer have such an identity and
we have $\displaystyle\max\big($ $\displaystyle\langle\tau-n^{3}\rangle,\langle\tau_{1}-\alpha n_{1}^{3}\rangle,\langle\tau_{2}-\alpha n_{2}^{3}\rangle\big)\sim\langle\tau-n^{3}\rangle+\langle\tau_{1}-\alpha n_{1}^{3}\rangle+\langle\tau_{2}-\alpha n_{2}^{3}\rangle$ (10) $\displaystyle\gtrsim\big|(\tau-n^{3})-(\tau_{1}-\alpha n_{1}^{3})-(\tau_{2}-\alpha n_{2}^{3})\big|=|n^{3}-\alpha n_{1}^{3}-\alpha n_{2}^{3}|,$ where $n=n_{1}+n_{2}$ and $\tau=\tau_{1}+\tau_{2}$ .
Note that the last expression in ( 10 ) can be 0 for infinitely many (nonzero) values of $n,\>n_{1},$ and $n_{2}$ ,
causing resonances.
By solving the resonance equation: (11) $n^{3}-\alpha n_{1}^{3}-\alpha n_{2}^{3}=0\text{ with }n=n_{1}+n_{2},$ we have $(n_{1},n_{2})=(c_{1}n,c_{2}n)$ or $(c_{2}n,c_{1}n)$ , where (12) $c_{1}=\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{\sqrt{-3+12\alpha^{-1}}}{6}\ \text{ and }\ c_{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{\sqrt{-3+12\alpha^{-1}}}{6}.$ Note that $c_{1}+c_{2}=1$ and that $c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{R}$ if and only if $0<\alpha\leq 4$ .
If $c_{1}\in\mathbb{Q}$ (and thus $c_{2}\in\mathbb{Q}$ ), then there are infinitely many values of $n\in\mathbb{Z}$ such that $c_{1}n,\>c_{2}n\in\mathbb{Z}$ . This causes resonances for infinitely many values of $n$ , and
thus we do not have any gain of derivative from $\langle\tau-n^{3}\rangle\langle\tau_{1}-\alpha n_{1}^{3}\rangle\langle\tau_{2}-\alpha n_{2}^{3}\rangle$ in this case. If $c_{1}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}$ , then $c_{1}n\notin\mathbb{Z}$ for any $n\in\mathbb{Z}$ .
i.e. $n-\alpha n_{1}^{3}-\alpha n_{2}^{3}\neq 0$ for any $n,n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}$ .
However, generally speaking, $n-\alpha n_{1}^{3}-\alpha n_{2}^{3}$ can be arbitrarily close to 0,
since $c_{1}n$ can be arbitrarily close to an integer.
Therefore, we need to measure how “close” $c_{1}$ is to rational numbers .
In [ 18 ] , we used the following definition regarding the Diophantine conditions commonly used in dynamical systems. Definition 1 (Arnold [ 1 ] ) . A real number $\rho$ is called of type $(K,\nu)$ ( or simply of type ${\nu}$ ) if there exist positive $K$ and $\nu$ such that for all pairs of integers $(m,n)$ , we have (13) $\left|\rho-\frac{m}{n}\right|\geq\frac{K}{|n|^{2+\nu}}.$ Also, for our purpose, we defined the minimal type index of a given real number $\rho$ . Definition 2 . Given a real number $\rho$ , define the minimal type index ${\nu_{\rho}}$ of ${\rho}$ by $\nu_{\rho}=\begin{cases}\infty\text{, if }\rho\in\mathbb{Q}\\
\inf\{\nu>0:\rho\text{ is of type }\nu\}\text{, if }\rho\notin\mathbb{Q}.\end{cases}$ Remark 1.1 . Then, by Dirichlet Theorem [ 1 , p.112] and [ 1 , p.116, lemma 3] ,
it follows that $\nu_{\rho}\geq 0$ for any $\rho\in\mathbb{R}$ and $\nu_{\rho}=0$ for almost every $\rho\in\mathbb{R}$ . Using the minimal type index $\nu_{c_{1}}$ of $c_{1}$ , for any $\varepsilon>0$ , we have (14) $|n^{3}-\alpha n_{1}^{3}-\alpha n_{2}^{3}|\gtrsim|n|^{1-\nu_{c_{1}}-\varepsilon}$ for all sufficiently large $n\in\mathbb{Z}$ , which provides a good lower bound on ( 10 ).
With ( 14 ), we proved that ( 7 ) holds for $s>\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\nu_{c_{1}}$ . The resonance equation of the second bilinear estimate ( 8 )
is given by (15) $\alpha n^{3}-n_{1}^{3}-\alpha n_{2}^{3}=0\text{ with }n=n_{1}+n_{2}.$ By solving ( 15 ), we obtain $(n_{1},n_{2})=\big(d_{1}n,(1-d_{1})n\big),\big(d_{2}n,(1-d_{2})n\big),(0,n)$ , where (16) $d_{1}=\tfrac{-3\alpha+\sqrt{3\alpha(4-\alpha)}}{2(1-\alpha)}\ \text{ and }\ d_{2}=\tfrac{-3\alpha-\sqrt{3\alpha(4-\alpha)}}{2(1-\alpha)}.$ Note that $d_{1},d_{2}\in\mathbb{R}$ if and only if $\alpha\in[0,1)\cup(1,4]$ .
Then,
for any $\varepsilon>0$ , we have (17) $|\alpha n^{3}-n_{1}^{3}-\alpha n_{2}^{3}|\gtrsim|n|^{1-\max(\nu_{d_{1}},\nu_{d_{2}})-\varepsilon}$ for all sufficiently large $n\in\mathbb{Z}$ .
With ( 17 ), we proved that
( 8 ) holds for $s>\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\max(\nu_{d_{1}},\nu_{d_{2}})$ with the mean 0 assumption on $u$ .
Note that the mean 0 assumption on $u$ is needed
since $n_{1}=0$ is a solution of ( 15 ) for any $n\in\mathbb{Z}$ .
Indeed, it is shown in [ 18 ] that ( 8 ) fails for any $s\in\mathbb{R}$ without the mean 0 assumption on $u$ . Remark 1.2 . We point out that the bilinear estimates ( 7 ) and ( 8 ) hold for $s\geq 0$ away from the resonance sets, i.e.
( 7 ) holds for $s\geq 0$ on $\{(n,n_{1}):|n|\gtrsim 1,|n_{1}-c_{1}n|\geq 1\text{ and }|n_{1}-c_{2}n|\geq 1\}$ ,
and
( 8 ) holds for $s\geq 0$ on $\{(n,n_{1}):|n|\gtrsim 1,|n_{1}-d_{1}n|\geq 1\text{ and }|n_{1}-d_{2}n|\geq 1\}$ . Now, let $s_{0}(\alpha)=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\max(\nu_{c_{1}},\nu_{d_{1}},\nu_{d_{2}})$ .
Note that $s_{0}=\frac{1}{2}$ for almost every $\alpha\in(0,4]$ in view of Remark 1.1 .
In [ 18 ] , we proved that, for $\alpha\in(0,4]\setminus\{1\}$ ,
the Majda-Biello system ( 3 ) is locally well-posed in $H^{s}(\mathbb{T})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{T})$ for $s\geq s^{\ast}(\alpha):=\min(1,s_{0}+)$ ,
assuming the mean 0 condition on $u_{0}$ . We’d like to point out the following.
On the one hand, we have $s^{\ast}(\alpha)=s_{0}(\alpha)=\frac{1}{2}+$ for almost every $\alpha\in(0,4]\setminus\{1\}$ .
On the other hand, for any interval $I\subset(0,4]$ , there exists $\alpha\in I$ such that $s^{\ast}(\alpha)=1$ .
This shows that the well-posedness (below $H^{1}$ ) of the periodic Majda-Biello system
is very unstable under a slight perturbation of the parameter $\alpha$ . Using the Hamiltonian $H(u,v)$ ,
one can easily obtain the GWP of ( 3 ) in $H^{1}(\mathbb{T})\times H^{1}(\mathbb{T})$ .
This result is sharp when $s^{\ast}=1$ ,
i.e. when $\max(\nu_{c_{1}},\nu_{d_{1}},\nu_{d_{2}})\geq 1$ .
In particular, it is sharp for $\alpha=4$ , since $c_{1}\in\mathbb{Q}$ for $\alpha=4$ . When $\max(\nu_{c_{1}},\nu_{d_{1}},\nu_{d_{2}})<1$ , we used the $I$ -method
to generate a sequence of modified energies (modified Hamiltonians) $H^{(j)}(u,v)(t)$ .
Using the second modified energy $H^{(2)}$ ,
it is shown in [ 19 ] that
( 3 ) is globally well-posed in $H^{s}(\mathbb{T})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{T})$ for $s\geq s^{**}:=\max\Big(\frac{6(s_{0}+)-2(s_{0}+)^{2}}{5-(s_{0}+)},\frac{2(s_{0}+)+9}{14}\Big)$ .
In particular, we have $s^{**}(\alpha)=\frac{5}{7}+$ for almost every $\alpha\in(0,4]\setminus\{1\}$ .
We also showed that the Fourier multiplier for the time derivative of the third modified energy $H^{(3)}$ is unbounded on a nontrivial set.
i.e. the $I$ -method fails before the GWP result matches the LWP one.
This shows that the GWP obtained via $H^{(2)}$ is the best possible result using the $I$ -method.
Note that there is a gap between $s^{*}(\alpha)$ and $s^{**}(\alpha)$ , unless $s^{*}=1$ .
In particular, $s^{*}=\frac{1}{2}+>s^{**}=\frac{5}{7}+$ for a.e. $\alpha\in(0,4]\setminus\{1\}$ . In an attempt to fill the gap between the LWP and the GWP results,
we consider the Gibbs measure of the form “ $d\mu=Z^{-1}\exp(-\beta H(u,v))\prod_{x\in\mathbb{T}}du(x)\otimes dv(x)$ ”.
First recall the following; Given a Hamiltonian flow $\begin{cases}\dot{p}_{i}=\frac{\partial H}{\partial q_{i}}\\
\dot{q}_{i}=-\frac{\partial H}{\partial p_{i}}\end{cases}$ on $\mathbb{R}^{2n}$ with Hamiltonian $H=H(p_{1},\cdots,p_{n},q_{1},\cdots,q_{n})$ ,
Liouville’s theorem states that the Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb{R}^{2n}$ is invariant under the flow.
From the conservation of the Hamiltonian $H$ , the Gibbs measures $e^{-\beta H}\prod_{i=1}^{n}dp_{i}dq_{i}$ are also invariant,
where $\beta$ is the reciprocal temperature.
In our context, the Hamiltonian $H(u,v)$ is conserved under the flow of ( 3 ).
Then, we’d like to use the invariance of the Gibbs measure $\mu$ (which holds true in finite dimensional case)
to prove a GWP result.
At this point, everything is merely formal, which needs to be made rigorous. In the context of NLS, Lebowitz-Rose-Speer [ 15 ] considered the Gibbs measure of the form $d\mu=\exp(-\beta H(u))\prod_{x\in\mathbb{T}}du(x)$ where $H(u)$ is the Hamiltonian given by $H(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int|u_{x}|^{2}\pm\frac{1}{p}\int|u|^{p}dx$ .
In the focusing case (with $-$ ), $H(u)$ is not bounded from below and this causes a problem.
Using the conservation of the $L^{2}$ norm,
they instead considered the Gibbs measure of the form $d\mu=\exp(-\beta H(u))\chi_{\{\|u\|_{L^{2}}\leq B\}}\prod_{x\in\mathbb{T}}du(x)$ , i.e. with an $L^{2}$ -cutoff.
This turned out to be a well-defined measure on $H^{\frac{1}{2}-}(\mathbb{T})=\bigcap_{s<\frac{1}{2}}H^{s}(\mathbb{T})$ (for $p<6$ with any $B>0$ , and $p=6$ with sufficiently small $B$ .)
Bourgain [ 4 ] continued this study and
proved the invariance of $\mu$ under the flow of NLS
and the global well-posedness almost surely on the statistical ensemble.
Note that [ 4 ] appeared before the so-called Bourgain’s method [ 3 ] or the $I$ -method [ 7 ] ,
i.e. there was virtually no method available to establish any GWP result
from a LWP result whose regularity was between two conservation laws.
This was the case for NLS for $4<p\leq 6$ .
We use this idea to obtain a.s. GWP of the Majda-Biello system ( 3 ).
Recently, Burq-Tzvetkov [ 6 ] independently and simultaneously used
similar ideas to prove a.s. GWP for
the nonlinear wave equation on the unit ball in $\mathbb{R}^{3}$ under the radial symmetry.
Also, see other work by Tzvetkov related to this subject [ 21 ] , [ 22 ] . For the Majda-Biello system ( 3 ), we have $H(u,v)=\frac{1}{2}\int u_{x}^{2}+\alpha v_{x}^{2}-uv^{2}dx$ .
It is known (c.f. Zhidokov [ 23 ] ) that the Wiener measure $d\rho=\widetilde{Z}^{-1}\exp(-\frac{1}{2}\int u_{x}^{2}+\alpha v_{x}^{2}dx)\prod_{x\in\mathbb{T}}du(x)\otimes dv(x)$ is a well-defined countably additive measure on $H^{\frac{1}{2}-}(\mathbb{T})\times H^{\frac{1}{2}-}(\mathbb{T})$ .
Since $-\frac{1}{2}\int uv^{2}dx$ is not sign-definite,
we need to add an $L^{2}$ cutoff in considering the Gibbs measure (weighted Wiener measure) as in [ 15 ] and [ 4 ] .
Then, $d\mu=Z^{-1}\exp(\frac{1}{2}\int uv^{2}dx)\chi_{\{\|(u,v)\|_{L^{2}}\leq B\}}d\rho$ is a well-defined countably additive measure on $H^{\frac{1}{2}-}(\mathbb{T})\times H^{\frac{1}{2}-}(\mathbb{T})$ .
When $\alpha=1$ , the invariance of the Gibbs measure $\mu$ directly follows
from Bourgain’s argument for KdV in [ 4 ] and the GWP of ( 3 ) in $H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T})\times H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T})$ obtained in [ 19 ] . Now, recall that for $\alpha\in(0,4)\setminus\{1\}$ ,
the Majda-Biello system ( 3 ) is LWP in $H^{s}(\mathbb{T})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{T})$ only for $s>\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\max(\nu_{c_{1}},\nu_{d_{1}},\nu_{d_{2}})\geq\frac{1}{2}$ and is $C^{3}$ ill-posed for $s<\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\max(\nu_{c_{1}},\nu_{d_{1}},\nu_{d_{2}})$ in the sense that the solution map is not $C^{3}$ (c.f. [ 18 ] .)
i.e. the flow of ( 3 ) is not well-defined on the support of the Gibbs measure $\mu$ in terms of the usual Sobolev spaces.
We instead consider the Cauchy problem for $(u_{0},v_{0})\in H^{s_{1},s_{2}}(\mathbb{T})\times H^{s_{1},s_{2}}(\mathbb{T})$ ,
where (18) $\|\phi\|_{H^{s_{1},s_{2}}}=\|\phi\|_{H^{s_{1}}}+\sup_{n}\langle n\rangle^{s_{2}}|\widehat{\phi}(n)|<\infty$ for some $s_{1},s_{2}$ with $0<s_{1}<\frac{1}{2}<s_{2}<1$ (to be determined later.)
First, recall that, as mentioned in Remark 1.2 ,
the bilinear estimates ( 7 ) and ( 8 )
fail for $s\leq\frac{1}{2}$ only at 4 frequencies near the resonances. $\sup_{n}\langle n\rangle^{s_{2}}|\widehat{\phi}(n)|$ in ( 18 ) exactly controls these particular resonances with the higher regularity $s_{2}>\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\max(\nu_{c_{1}},\nu_{d_{1}},\nu_{d_{2}})$ .
We have the following theorem. Theorem 1 . Let $\alpha\in(0,4)\setminus\{1\}$ and $\max(\nu_{c_{1}},\nu_{d_{1}},\nu_{d_{2}})<1$ . Assume the mean 0 condition on $u_{0}$ .
Let $\frac{1}{4}<s_{1}<\frac{1}{2}<s_{2}<1$ with $2s_{1}>s_{2}>\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\max(\nu_{c_{1}},\nu_{d_{1}},\nu_{d_{2}})$ .
Then, the Majda-Biello system ( 3 ) is locally well-posed in $H^{s_{1},s_{2}}(\mathbb{T})\times H^{s_{1},s_{2}}(\mathbb{T})$ . As seen in Bourgain’s work on mKdV and Zakharov system [ 4 ] , [ 5 ] ,
we have $\mu(H^{s_{1},s_{2}}\times H^{s_{1},s_{2}})=1$ for $0<s_{1}<\frac{1}{2}<s_{2}<1$ .
i.e. $H^{s_{1},s_{2}}(\mathbb{T})\times H^{s_{1},s_{2}}(\mathbb{T})$ contains the full support of $\mu$ .
In [ 4 ] , Bourgain proved the invariance of the Gibbs measure and a.s. GWP of mKdV
by establishing an improved local well-posedness in $H^{s_{1},s_{2}}$ with $s_{1}=\frac{1}{2}-$ and $s_{2}=1-$ .
Following his argument,
we obtain the a.s. global well-posedness of ( 3 ),
using the finite dimensional approximation to ( 3 )
along with the invariance of the finite dimensional Gibbs measure. Theorem 2 . Let $\alpha\in(0,4)\setminus\{1\}$ and $\max(\nu_{c_{1}},\nu_{d_{1}},\nu_{d_{2}})<1$ . Assume the mean 0 condition on $u_{0}$ .
Then, the Gibbs measure $\mu$ (with an $L^{2}$ -cutoff) is invariant under the flow of ( 3 ),
and ( 3 ) is globally well-posed a.s. on the statistical ensemble. We point out that Theorem 2 does not fill the gap between the LWP and GWP of ( 3 )
as we initially hoped.
However, it does establish a new GWP result
for almost every $\alpha\in(0,4)\setminus\{1\}$ which can not be obtained by the methods in [ 18 ] and [ 19 ] . This work is a part of the author’s Ph.D. thesis [ 17 ] .
This paper is organized as follows:
In Section 2, we introduce some standard notations.
In Section 3, we go over the basic theory of Gaussian Hilbert spaces and abstract Wiener spaces, and
we give the precise meaning to the Gibbs measure $\mu$ .
In Section 4, we introduce the function spaces and linear estimates.
Then, we prove Theorem 1 in Section 5,
and extend this local result to a.s. GWP in Section 6.
We also establish the invariance of the Gibbs measure $\mu$ .

and abstract Wiener spaces. 2. Notation On $\mathbb{T}$ , the spatial Fourier domain is $\mathbb{Z}$ .
Let $dn$ be the normalized counting measure on $\mathbb{Z}$ ,
and we say $f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{Z})$ , $1\leq p<\infty$ , if $\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}=\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{Z}}|f(n)|^{p}dn\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}:=\bigg(\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|f(n)|^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.$ If $p=\infty$ , we have the obvious definition involving the essential supremum.

Then, by Egoroff’s Theorem, given $\varepsilon>0$ there exists a set $E\subset\Omega$ with $\rho(E^{c})<\varepsilon$ such that $\langle n\rangle^{s_{2}}|\widehat{\phi}(n)|\to 0$ uniformly on $E$ .
Then, choose $N_{0}$ sufficiently large such that $\langle n\rangle^{s_{2}}|\widehat{\phi}(n)|<\varepsilon$ on $E$ for all $|n|\geq N_{0}$ .
