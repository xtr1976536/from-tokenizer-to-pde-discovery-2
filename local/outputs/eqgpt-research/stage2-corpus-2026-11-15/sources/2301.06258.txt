Email: hejingning@hznu.edu.cn August 24, 2026 Abstract We analyze the long-time behavior of solutions to a Navier–Stokes–Cahn–Hilliard system with chemotaxis effects and a solution-dependent mass source term. The fluid velocity $\bm{v}$ satisfies the Navier–Stokes system, the phase field variable $\varphi$ satisfies a convective Cahn–Hilliard equation with a singular potential (e.g., the Flory–Huggins type), the nutrient density $\sigma$ satisfies an advection-diffusion-reaction. For the initial boundary value problem in 2D, we prove the existence of the global attractor in a suitable phase space. Furthermore, we obtain the existence of an exponential
attractor, and it can be implied that the global attractor is of finite fractal dimension. Keywords: Navier–Stokes–Cahn–Hilliard system, Chemotaxis, Singular potential, Attractors. MSC 2010: 35A01, 35A02, 35K35, 35Q92, 76D05. 1 Introduction We investigate the following Navier–Stokes–Cahn–Hilliard type system $\displaystyle\partial_{t}\bm{v}+\bm{v}\cdot\nabla\bm{v}-\textrm{div}(2\eta(\varphi)D\bm{v})+\nabla p=(\mu+\chi\sigma)\nabla\varphi,$ (1.1a) $\displaystyle\textrm{div}\ \bm{v}=0,$ (1.1b) $\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varphi+\bm{v}\cdot\nabla\varphi=\Delta\mu-M(x,\varphi),$ (1.1c) $\displaystyle\mu=A\varPsi^{\prime}(\varphi)-B\Delta\varphi-\chi\sigma,$ (1.1d) $\displaystyle\partial_{t}\sigma+\bm{v}\cdot\nabla\sigma=\Delta(\sigma+\chi(1-\varphi))+S,$ (1.1e) in $\Omega\times(0,T)$ . Here, $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}$ is a bounded domain with smooth boundary $\partial\Omega$ and $T>0$ is a given final time of arbitrary magnitude. The system is subject to the following boundary conditions $\displaystyle\bm{v}=\mathbf{0},\quad{\partial}_{\bm{n}}\varphi={\partial}_{\bm{n}}\mu={\partial}_{\bm{n}}\sigma=0,\qquad\qquad$ on $\displaystyle\ \partial\Omega\times(0,T),$ (1.2) and the initial conditions $\displaystyle\bm{v}|_{t=0}=\bm{v}_{0}(x),\ \ \varphi|_{t=0}=\varphi_{0}(x),\ \ \sigma|_{t=0}=\sigma_{0}(x),\qquad$ in $\displaystyle\ \Omega.$ (1.3) Here, $\bm{n}=\bm{n}(x)$ denotes the unit outward normal vector on $\partial\Omega$ . System ( 1.1a )–( 1.1e ) can be regarded as a simplified version of the general thermodynamically consistent diffuse interface model in [ 31 ] where the authors study a two-component incompressible fluid mixture including a chemical species subject to diffusion and some significant transport mechanisms, like advection and chemotaxis (see also [ 39 ] and the references cited therein). In this system, the
state variables of the system are volume-averaged velocity $\bm{v}$ , the (modified) pressure $p$ , the difference in volume fractions of the binary mixture $\varphi$ , the concentration of the chemical species $\sigma$ , the chemical potential $\mu$ associated to $(\varphi,\sigma)$ . Here, $D\bm{v}=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\bm{v}+(\nabla\bm{v})^{\mathrm{T}})$ denotes the symmetrized velocity gradient. The potential $\varPsi$ denotes the
physically relevant free energy density given by the logarithmic type [ 6 ] : $\varPsi(r)=\frac{\theta}{2}\big[(1-r)\ln(1-r)+(1+r)\ln(1+r)\big]+\frac{\theta_{0}}{2}(1-r^{2}),\quad\forall\,r\in[-1,1],$ (1.4) with $0<\theta<\theta_{0}$ (see, e.g., [ 6 , 7 ] ), which is also known as Flory–Huggins potential. The source term $M(x,\varphi)$ in ( 1.1c ) (see (H4)
below) is related to biological mechanisms like proliferation, elimination of cells (see e.g., [ 30 ] ). Further discussions on biologically relevant mass source terms can be found in [ 33 ] and the
references cited therein. The source term $M(x,\varphi)$ can be viewed as a general form of Oono’s type $\alpha(\varphi-c_{0})$ with $\alpha\geq 0$ , $c_{0}\in(-1,1)$ , which represents nonlocal interactions (e.g. reversible chemical reaction) between the two components (cf. [ 21 , 32 ] ). Assuming that the components $\eta_{1}$ , $\eta_{2}>0$ are viscosities of the two homogeneous fluids, the mean viscosity is modeled by the concentration dependent term $\eta=\eta(\varphi)$ , for instance, a typical form is the linear combination: $\eta(\varphi)=\eta_{1}\frac{1+\varphi}{2}+\eta_{2}\frac{1-\varphi}{2}.$ (1.5) The positive constant $A$ is the surface tension coefficient, and the positive coefficient $B$ is related to thickness of the interfacial layer (i.e., the diffuse interface). The constant $\chi$ is related to certain specific transport mechanisms such as chemotaxis and active transport in the context of tumor growth modelling (see e.g., [ 18 , 16 ] ). As it has been shown in [ 27 , 31 ] , the following basic energy law holds for the problem ( 1.1a )–( 1.3 ) $\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\frac{1}{2}|\bm{v}|^{2}+A\varPsi(\varphi)+\frac{B}{2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\sigma|^{2}+\chi\sigma(1-\varphi)\Big]dx$ $\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\Omega}\Big[2\eta(\varphi)|D\bm{v}|^{2}+m(\varphi)|\nabla\mu|^{2}+|\nabla(\sigma+\chi(1-\varphi))|^{2}\Big]dx$ $\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\Omega}\left[-M(x,\varphi)\mu+(-\mathcal{C}h(\varphi)\sigma+S)(\sigma+\chi(1-\varphi))\right]dx,$ (1.6) which plays a significant role in the study of its global well-posedness. The system ( 1.1a )–( 1.3 ) with a regular potential was first investigated in [ 31 ] . The authors of [ 31 ] obtained the existence of global weak solutions in dimension two and three, the existence and uniqueness of global strong solutions in dimension two under an artificial assumption on the coefficients $A$ and $\chi$ . This extra assumption was removed in the recent work [ 27 ] , where the author considered a singular potential like ( 1.4 ) and proved the existence of global weak solutions in two and three dimensions. Then the authors in [ 28 ] proves the existence and regularity properties of the unique global strong solution for problem ( 1.1a )–( 1.3 ) in two dimensions. Neglecting the interaction due to nutrient and assuming $\alpha=0$ , our problem ( 1.1a )–( 1.1d ) reduces to the well-known “Model H” for two incompressible and
viscous Newtonian fluids [ 29 , 26 ] . This model has been extensively investigated in the literature under various hypotheses on mobility, fluid viscosity and potential function. For instance, for the Navier–Stokes–Cahn–Hilliard system, we refer to [ 5 , 13 , 43 ] for the regular potential case, and to [ 1 , 5 , 24 ] when the potential is singular (e.g., logarithmic). We refer to [ 4 , 35 ] for the system with nonlocal effect due to Oono’s interaction. Concerning the long-time behavior of the NSCH system
with regular potential, the existence of global and
exponential attractors was proved in [ 13 ] . The lower bound for the global attractor can be found in [ 14 ] , and the existence of a pullback exponential attractor was studied [ 3 ] . For the logarithmic potential case, we refer to [ 1 ] for the stability of local minima of the energy and the existence of the global attractor, and to the recent work [ 25 ] for the existence of the exponential attractor. Neglecting the fluid interaction in system ( 1.1a )–( 1.1d ), the well-posedness of a Cahn–Hilliard type system with chemotaxis was proved in [ 16 ] , the case with Dirichlet boundary conditions was investigated in [ 15 ] . Neglecting chemotaxis and active transport effects, the existence of global attractors of some related diffuse interface models under a choice of the regular potential was proved in [ 12 , 34 ] . Concerning the existence of exponential attractors for the Cahn–Hilliard equation, we refer to [ 36 , 37 ] and the references therein. Our aim is to continue the study of [ 27 , 28 ] and establish the long-time behavior of problem ( 1.1a )–( 1.3 ) with the choice of a singular potential like ( 1.4 ) in two dimensions from
the viewpoint of infinite dimensional dynamical systems. Based on the well-posedness results in [ 27 , 28 ] and the energy law ( 1.6 ) of the system, we define a semigroup map on a suitable phase-space. We prove the existence of a compact absorbing set in the phase-space and the strong continuity of the semigroup, which implies the existence of the global attractor (cf. [ 42 ] ). Then, we obtain the existence of exponential attractors by showing the so-called smoothing property (cf. [ 38 ] ). It is worth mentioning
that the separation property (that is, $\varphi$ stays away from the pure phases $\pm 1$ from a certain time on) makes the Cahn–Hilliard equation with singular potential can be regarded as a parabolic equation with a globally Lipschitz potential. Finally, we prove
some regularity properties of the global attractor and its finite fractal dimension. The remaining part of this paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 , we state the functional settings and introduce the main results. In Section 3 , we introduce the
dynamical system related to problem ( 1.1a )–( 1.1e ) and prove the existence of the global attractor. Section 4 is devoted to the existence of an exponential
attractor. 2 Main Results 2.1 Preliminaries Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}$ be a bounded domain with smooth boundary $\partial\Omega$ . Suppose that $T>0$ is a fixed final time. For the standard Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces, we use the notations $L^{p}:=L^{p}(\Omega)$ and $W^{k,p}:=W^{k,p}(\Omega)$ for any $p\in[1,+\infty]$ and $k>0$ , equipped with the norms $\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}}$ and $\|\cdot\|_{W^{k,p}}$ . We use $H^{k}:=W^{k,2}$ and the norm $\|\cdot\|_{H^{k}}$ when $p=2$ . We denote the norm and inner product on $L^{2}(\Omega)$ by $\|\cdot\|$ and $(\cdot,\cdot)$ , respectively.
We denote the dual space of a Banach space $X$ by $X^{\prime}$ , and denote the duality pairing between $X$ and $X^{\prime}$ by $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{X^{\prime},X}$ . For any given interval $J$ of $\mathbb{R}^{+}$ , we introduce
the function space $L^{p}(J;X)$ with $p\in[1,+\infty]$ , which
consists of Bochner measurable $p$ -integrable
functions by taking values in the Banach space $X$ .
We denote the vectorial space $X^{d}$ by the boldface letter $\bm{X}$ , which consists of the product structure. For every $f\in H^{1}(\Omega)^{\prime}$ , $\overline{f}$ denotes its generalized mean value over $\Omega$ satisfying $\overline{f}=|\Omega|^{-1}\langle f,1\rangle_{(H^{1})^{\prime},\,H^{1}}$ . If $f\in L^{1}(\Omega)$ , then its mean is simply given by $\overline{f}=|\Omega|^{-1}\int_{\Omega}f\,dx$ .
We recall the Poincaré–Wirtinger inequality $\|f-\overline{f}\|\leq C_{P}\|\nabla f\|,\quad\forall\,f\in H^{1}(\Omega),$ (2.1) where the constant $C_{P}$ depends only on $\Omega$ . Then we
notice that $f\to(\|\nabla f\|^{2}+|\overline{f}|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}$ is an equivalent norm on $H^{1}(\Omega)$ . We introduce the space $L^{2}_{0}(\Omega):=\{f\in L^{2}(\Omega):\overline{f}=0\}$ for $L^{2}$ functions with zero mean and denote $H^{2}_{N}(\Omega):=\{f\in H^{2}(\Omega):\,\partial_{\bm{n}}f=0\ \textrm{on}\ \partial\Omega\}$ for $H^{2}$ functions subject to homogeneous Neumann boundary condition. We recall the Ladyzhenskaya and Agmon’s inequalities in two dimensions, which will be used in the subsequent estimates $\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{4}}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|^{\frac{1}{2}},\qquad\forall\,f\in H^{1}(\Omega),$ $\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|^{\frac{1}{2}},\qquad\forall\,f\in H^{2}(\Omega).$ Then we recall some function spaces related to the Navier–Stokes equations (see e.g., [ 40 ] ). Suppose that $\bm{C}^{\infty}_{0,\mathrm{div}}(\Omega)$ is the space of divergence free vector fields in $(C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega))^{2}$ . Define $\bm{L}^{2}_{0,\mathrm{div}}(\Omega)$ and $\bm{H}^{1}_{0,\mathrm{div}}(\Omega)$ as the closure of $\bm{C}^{\infty}_{0,\mathrm{div}}(\Omega)$ with respect to the $\bm{L}^{2}$ and $\bm{H}^{1}_{0}$ norms, respectively. The space $\bm{H}^{1}_{0,\mathrm{div}}(\Omega)$ is equipped with the scalar product $(\bm{u},\bm{v})_{\bm{H}^{1}_{0,\mathrm{div}}}:=(\nabla\bm{u},\nabla\bm{v})$ for all $\bm{u},\,\bm{v}\in{\bm{H}^{1}_{0,\mathrm{div}}(\Omega)}$ and the norm $\|\bm{u}\|_{\bm{H}^{1}_{0,\mathrm{div}}}=\|\nabla\bm{u}\|$ . The classical Korn’s inequality
entails that $\|\nabla\bm{u}\|\leq\sqrt{2}\|D\bm{u}\|\leq\sqrt{2}\|\nabla\bm{u}\|$ for all $\bm{u}\in\bm{H}^{1}_{0,\mathrm{div}}(\Omega)$ .
It is well known that $\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)$ can be decomposed into $\bm{L}^{2}_{0,\mathrm{div}}(\Omega)\oplus\bm{G}(\Omega)$ , where $\bm{G}(\Omega):=\{\bm{f}\in\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega):\exists\,g\in H^{1}(\Omega),\ \bm{f}=\nabla g\}$ . For any function $\bm{f}\in\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)$ , the Helmholtz–Weyl decomposition holds: $\bm{f}=\bm{f}_{0}+\nabla g$ where $\bm{f}_{0}\in\bm{L}^{2}_{0,\mathrm{div}}(\Omega)$ and $\nabla g\in\bm{G}(\Omega)$ . Consequently, we can define the Helmholtz–Leray projection onto the space of divergence-free functions $\bm{P}:\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)\to\bm{L}^{2}_{0,\mathrm{div}}(\Omega)$ such that $\bm{P}(\bm{f})=\bm{f}_{0}$ . We recall the Stokes operator $\bm{S}:\bm{H}^{1}_{0,\mathrm{div}}(\Omega)\cap\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega)\to\bm{L}^{2}_{0,\mathrm{div}}(\Omega)$ satisfying $(\bm{S}\bm{u},\bm{\zeta})=(\nabla\bm{u},\nabla\bm{\zeta}),\quad\forall\,\bm{\zeta}\in\bm{H}^{1}_{0,\mathrm{div}}(\Omega),$ with domain $D(\bm{S})=\bm{H}^{1}_{0,\mathrm{div}}(\Omega)\cap\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega)$ (see e.g., [ 40 , Chapter III] ). Besides, there exists a constant $C>0$ such that $\|\bm{u}\|_{\bm{H}^{2}}\leq C\|\bm{S}\bm{u}\|$ for any $\bm{u}\in\bm{H}^{1}_{0,\mathrm{div}}(\Omega)\cap\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega)$ . The operator $\bm{S}$ is a canonical isomorphism from $\bm{H}^{1}_{0,\mathrm{div}}(\Omega)$ to $\bm{H}^{1}_{0,\mathrm{div}}(\Omega)^{\prime}$ .
We denote its inverse map by $\bm{S}^{-1}:\bm{H}^{1}_{0,\mathrm{div}}(\Omega)^{\prime}\to\bm{H}^{1}_{0,\mathrm{div}}(\Omega)$ . For any $\bm{f}\in\bm{H}^{1}_{0,\mathrm{div}}(\Omega)^{\prime}$ , there is a unique $\bm{u}=\bm{S}^{-1}\bm{f}\in\bm{H}^{1}_{0,\mathrm{div}}(\Omega)$ satisfying $(\nabla\bm{S}^{-1}\bm{f},\nabla\bm{\zeta})=\langle\bm{f},\bm{\zeta}\rangle_{(\bm{H}^{1}_{0,\mathrm{div}})^{\prime},\,\bm{H}^{1}_{0,\mathrm{div}}},\quad\forall\,\bm{\zeta}\in\bm{H}^{1}_{0,\mathrm{div}}(\Omega),$ and $\|\nabla\bm{S}^{-1}\bm{f}\|=\langle\bm{f},\bm{S}^{-1}\bm{f}\rangle_{(\bm{H}^{1}_{0,\mathrm{div}})^{\prime},\,\bm{H}^{1}_{0,\mathrm{div}}}^{\frac{1}{2}}$ is an equivalent norm on $\bm{H}^{1}_{0,\mathrm{div}}(\Omega)^{\prime}$ .
Finally, we recall the following regularity result (see e.g., [ 40 , Chapter III, Theorem 2.2.1] and [ 24 , Appendix B] ): Lemma 2.1 . Let $\Omega$ be a bounded smooth domain. For any $\bm{f}\in\bm{L}^{2}_{0,\mathrm{div}}(\Omega)$ ,
there exists a unique pair $\bm{u}\in\bm{H}^{1}_{0,\mathrm{div}}(\Omega)\cap\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega)$ and $p\in H^{1}(\Omega)\cap L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)$ satisfying $-\Delta\bm{u}+\nabla p=\bm{f}$ a.e. in $\Omega$ , that is, $\bm{u}=\bm{S}^{-1}\bm{f}$ . Theorem 2.2 (Strong well-posedness) . Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}$ be a bounded smooth domain and $T>0$ . Suppose that the hypotheses (H1)–(H4) are satisfied. As a consequence, for every bounded set $\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{X}_{m_{1},m_{2}}$ , it holds $\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}_{\bm{L}^{2}\times H^{1}\times L^{2}}(\mathcal{S}(t)\mathcal{B},\mathcal{M}_{m_{1},m_{2}})\leq\big(\max\{J_{0},C_{\mathcal{B}}\}e^{\omega_{0}t_{2}(\mathcal{B})}\big)e^{-\omega_{0}t},\quad\forall\,t\geq 0.$ This implies that $\mathcal{M}_{m_{1},m_{2}}$ attracts exponentially fast all bounded subsets $\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{X}_{m_{1},m_{2}}$ , that is,
its basin of exponential attraction is the whole phase space $\mathcal{X}_{m_{1},m_{2}}$ . The proof of Theorem 2.4 is complete. $\square$ Acknowledgments The author would like to thank Professor H. Wu for helpful discussions. The author is partially supported by Zhejiang Provincial Natural Science
