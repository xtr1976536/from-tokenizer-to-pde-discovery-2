This paper is concerned with the well-posedness and large-time behavior of a two dimensional PDE-ODE hybrid chemotaxis system describing the initiation of tumor angiogenesis. We first transform the system via a Cole-Hopf type transformation into a parabolic-hyperbolic system and show that the solution of the transformed system converges to a constant equilibrium state as time tends to infinity. Finally we reverse the Cole-Hopf transformation and obtain the relevant results for the pre-transformed PDE-ODE hybrid system. In contrast to the existing related results, where continuous initial datum is imposed, we are able to prove the asymptotic stability for discontinuous initial data with large oscillations. The key ingredient in our proof is the use of so-called “effective viscous flux”, which enables us to obtain the desired energy estimates and regularity. The technique of “effective viscous flux” turns out to be a very useful tool to study chemotaxis systems with initial data having low regularity and was rarely (if not) used in the literature for chemotaxis models. Key words and phrases: Chemotaxis, asymptotic stability,
discontinuous initial data, effective viscous flux 2000 Mathematics Subject Classification 35A01, 35B40, 35Q92, 92C17 1. Introduction In this paper we consider the following PDE-ODE hybrid chemotaxis model $\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}u_{t}=\Delta u-\nabla\cdot(\xi u\nabla\ln c),\\
c_{t}=-\mu uc\end{array}\right.$ which was proposed in [ 28 ] to describe the interaction between signaling molecules vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) and vascular endothelial cells during the initiation of tumor angiogenesis (see also in [ 5 , 6 ] ), where $u(x,t)$ and $c(x,t)$ denote the density of vascular endothelial cells and concentration of VEGF, respectively. The parameter $\xi>0$ is referred to as the chemotactic coefficient measuring the strength
of chemotaxis and $\mu$ denotes the degradation rate of the chemical VEGF. Here the chemical diffusion is neglected since it is far less important than its interaction with endothelial cells (cf. [ 28 ] ). On the other hand, the system ( 1 ) can also be regarded as a particular form of the well-known Keller-Segel system proposed in the seminal paper [ 25 ] describing the propagation of traveling wave band formed by bacterial chemotaxis observed in the experiment of Adler [ 2 ] . The distinguishing feature of the model ( 1 ) is twofold: (i) the first equation of ( 1 ) contains a logarithmic sensitivity function $\ln c$ which is singular at ${c=0}$ but is a very meaningful sensitivity representation (cf. [ 40 , 24 ] ; (ii) the second equation of ( 1 ) is an ODE and hence lacks a spatial structure. Either of these features is the source of challenges for mathematical analysis and numerical computations. To overcome these obstacles, a Cole-Hopf type transformation ${\bf v}=-\frac{1}{\mu}\nabla(\ln c)=-\frac{1}{\mu}\frac{\nabla c}{c}$ (1.4) has been introduced in [ 27 , 45 ] , which transforms the system ( 1 ) into a parabolic-hyperbolic system: $\begin{cases}u_{t}-\chi\nabla\cdot(u{\bf v})=\Delta u,\\
{\bf v}_{t}-\nabla u=0,\end{cases}$ (1.5) where $\chi=\mu\xi>0$ , ${\bf v}$ is a gradient vector and curl ${\bf v}=0$ . Apparently the transformed system ( 1.5 ) is much more manipulable mathematically than the original system ( 1 ) since the singularity vanishes and the quantity ${\bf v}$ possesses a spatial structure through $u$ . In this paper, we
shall consider the well-posedness and asymptotic behavior of solutions to ( 1.5 ) with initial data $(u,{\bf v})(x,0)=(u_{0},{\bf v}_{0})(x),\ \ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}$ (1.6) subject to the following asymptotic states $(u,{\bf v})(\pm\infty,t)=(\bar{u},{\bf 0}),$ (1.7) where $(\bar{u},{\bf 0})$ with $\bar{u}>0$ is a constant ground state of ( 1.5 )). Then we reverse the Cole-Hopf transformation ( 1.4 ) and get the well-posedness and asymptotic behavior of solutions to the original PDE-ODE chemotaxis system ( 1 ). There has been a considerable amount of interesting works carried out for the transformed system ( 1.5 ). The one-dimensional problem has been first studied extensively from various aspects such as the traveling wave solutions [ 22 , 34 , 35 , 32 , 39 , 41 , 44 ] , global dynamics of solutions in the whole space [ 12 , 30 , 49 , 37 ] or in the bounded interval [ 19 , 31 , 33 , 48 ] . For the multidimensional whole space $\mathbb{R}^{2}$ , the nonlinear stability of planar traveling wave was recently established in [ 3 ] . When the initial datum is close to the constant ground state $(\bar{u},{\bf 0})$ , numerous results have been obtained to the system ( 1.5 ). First the blowup criteria of classical solutions was established in [ 8 , 29 ] and the long-time behavior of solutions was obtained in [ 29 ] if $(u_{0}-\bar{u},{\bf v_{0}})\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})$ for $s>\frac{d}{2}+1$ and $\lVert(u_{0}-\bar{u},{\bf v_{0}})\rVert_{H^{s}\times H^{s}}$ is small. Later, Hao [ 13 ] established the global existence of mild solutions in the critical Besov space $\dot{B}_{2,1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\times(\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\frac{1}{2}})^{d}$ with minimal regularity in the Chemin-Lerner space framework. The global well-posedness of strong solutions of ( 1.5 ) in $\mathbb{R}^{3}$ was established in [ 7 ] if $\lVert(u_{0}-\bar{u},{\bf v_{0}})\rVert_{L^{2}\times H^{1}}$ is small. If the initial datum has a higher regularity such that $\lVert(u_{0}-\bar{u},{\bf v_{0}})\rVert_{H^{2}\times H^{1}}$ is small, the algebraic decay of solutions was further derived in [ 7 ] . Wang, Xiang and Yu [ 47 ] established the global existence and time decay rates of solutions of ( 1.5 ) in $\mathbb{R}^{d}$ for $d=2,3$ if $(u_{0}-\bar{u},{\bf v_{0}})\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})$ and $\lVert(u_{0}-\bar{u},{\bf v_{0}})\rVert_{H^{1}\times H^{1}}$ is small. Recently the small energy solution in $\mathbb{R}^{d}(d=2,3)$ was established in [ 46 ] . In the multidimensional bounded domain $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}(d=2,3)$ , the global existence and exponential decay rates of solutions under Neumann boundary conditions were obtained in [ 33 , 42 ] for small data, and local existence of classical solutions in two dimensions with Dirichlet boundary conditions was given in [ 20 ] . When the Laplacian (diffusion) in ( 1.5 ) was modified to a fractional Laplacian, the global existence of solutions of ( 1.5 ) in a torus with periodic boundary conditions in some dissipation regimes was established in [ 10 , 11 ] . In the above-mentioned results, all solutions obtained for ( 1.5 ) are classical thanks to the high regularity and smallness of the $H^{s}(s\geq 1)$ -norm on the initial data. The goal of this paper is to exploit the parabolic-hyperbolic system ( 1.5 ) with rougher initial data which are allowed to be discontinuous and possess large amplitude. Specifically we consider the initial data $(u_{0},v_{0})$ in some appropriate $L^{p}$ space and allow $\lVert u_{0}-\bar{u}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\lVert\mathbf{v}_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}$ to be arbitrarily large.
The low regularity of initial data and large amplitude bring us many new difficulties in analysis compared to the existing works (cf. [ 7 , 29 , 47 ] ). In particular, the integrability of $\nabla{\bf v}$ , which plays a crucial role in the analysis of [ 7 , 29 , 47 ] , can be easily attained therein but appears to be unattainable for our less regular initial data due to the hyperbolic effect of the second equation. Hence one can only expect to examine $L^{p}$ -integrability for ${\bf v}$ and hence obtain weak solutions instead of strong (classical) solutions. The key idea of gaining $L^{p}$ -integrability of ${\bf v}$ developed in this paper is to rewrite the first equation of ( 1.5 ) as $\tilde{u}_{t}=\nabla\cdot\mathbf{F}$ inspired by the works [ 15 , 36 ] on the Navier-Stokes equations, where $\mathbf{F}$ is the so called “effective viscous flux” defined by $\mathbf{F}=\nabla u+\chi u\bf{v}.$ (1.8) Then we rewrite the second equation of ( 1.5 ) as ${\bf v}_{t}=-\chi u{\bf v}+{\bf F}$ and try to attain the regularity of ${\bf v}$ through ${\bf F}$ whose regularity is easier than ${\bf v}$ to obtain. We remark that the dynamics of PDEs with discontinuous data has been an important topic arising from fluid mechanics and gas dynamics in order to understand how the discontinuity evolves in the fluid. For this, Hoff with his collaborators have developed some nice ideas and obtained a series of important results in this aspect (cf. [ 14 , 15 , 16 , 17 , 18 ] ). We refer to [ 4 , 21 , 26 , 38 ] and references therein for further development on the discontinuous data problem. However in our current work the “effective viscous flux” ${\bf F}$ exists in its divergence form (i.e. $\nabla\cdot{\bf F})$ , which is different from that used in the Navier-Stokes equations where the “effective viscous flux” exists in its gradient form and $L^{p}$ -norm of $\nabla{\bf F}$ can be achieved directly (cf. [ 15 , 16 , 17 , 18 , 36 ] ). This implies that besides the estimate of $\nabla\cdot{\bf F}$ , we have to estimate for the curl of ${\bf F}$ (denoted by $\nabla^{\bot}{\bf F}$ ) in order to get the estimates for $\nabla{\bf F}$ and hence the regularity for ${\bf F}$ , which is a key difference from the Navier-Stokes equation. The “effective viscous flux” technique appears to be a very powerful tool to study chemotaxis systems with low regular initial data and was rarely (if not) used in the literature. Since the dependence of solutions on $\chi$ and $\bar{u}$ is not the interest of this paper, we henceforth assume $\chi=\bar{u}=1$ throughout the paper for simplicity without further clarification. To state our results, we first introduce the definition of weak solutions of ( 1.5 )-( 1.7 ). Definition 1.1 (Weak solutions) . We say that $(u,{\bf v})$ is a weak solution of ( 1.5 )-( 1.7 ), if $(u,{\bf v})$ is suitably
integrable and satisfies for all test functions $\Psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[0,\infty))$ that $\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}u_{0}\Psi_{0}dx+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(u\Psi_{t}-\nabla u\cdot\nabla\Psi\right)dxdt=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}u{\bf v}\cdot\nabla\Psi dxdt\end{split}$ and $\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}{\bf v}_{0}^{j}\Psi_{0}dx+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left({\bf v}^{j}\Psi_{t}-u\Psi_{x_{j}}\right)dxdt=0,\end{split}$ where $j=1,2$ and $\Psi_{0}(x)=\Psi(x,0)$ . Then our main results in this paper are given as follows: Theorem 1.1 . Let $4<p_{0}<\infty$ and the initial
data satisfy $u_{0}-1\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\ \ {\bf v}_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap L^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\ \ u_{0}\geq 0,\ \ \nabla^{\bot}\cdot{\bf v}_{0}=0$ (1.9) where $\nabla^{\bot}=(\partial_{2},-\partial_{1})$ denotes the curl operator. Then for any constant $M>0$ with $\|{\bf v}_{0}\|_{L^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq M$ , there exists a constant $\varepsilon>0$ depending on $M$ , such that if $\lVert u_{0}-1\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}+\lVert\mathbf{v_{0}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}=\theta_{0}\leq\varepsilon,$ then the Cauchy problem ( 1.5 )-( 1.7 ) has a
global weak solution $(u,{\bf v})(x,t)$ satisfying $\begin{cases}\begin{split}&u-1\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty);L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))\cap C((0,\infty);C(\mathbb{R}^{2})),\ \nabla u\in L^{2}([0,\infty);L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})),\\
&{\bf v}\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty);L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap L^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))\cap C([0,\infty),H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})).\end{split}\end{cases}$ (1.10) and the following asymptotic convergence $\begin{split}\|u(x,t)-1\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\to 0,\ \ \|{\bf v}(x,t)\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\to 0~~as~~t\to\infty,\end{split}$ (1.11) where $2<p_{1}\leq\infty$ and $2<p_{2}<p_{0}<\infty$ . Remark 1.1 . The above results hold true regardless of the strength of initial perturbation from the constant ground state $(1,0)$ , namely the amplitude $\lVert u_{0}-1\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\lVert\bf{v}_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}$ can be arbitrarily large. The initial conditions ( 1.9 ) imply that the initial data of the Cauchy problem ( 1.5 )-( 1.7 ) could be discontinuous (such as piecewise constant with arbitrarily large jump discontinuities), which brings great challenges to the analysis. Moreover the condition $\nabla^{\bot}\cdot{\bf v}_{0}=0$ is a natural consequence the Cole-Hopf transformation ( 1.4 ). To prove Theorem 1.1 , we first mollify the initial data with a mollifying parameter $\delta$ and obtain the global smooth solution $(u^{\delta},\bf{v}^{\delta})$ depending on the mollifying parameter $\delta$ . Then we pass to the limit as $\delta\to 0$ and obtain a weak solution of ( 1.5 )-( 1.7 ). The core of the proof is to derive the global a priori estimates independent of the mollifying parameter $\delta$ . In this connection, the approaches and estimates developed in previous works [ 29 , 47 ] for small-amplitude and continuous initial data are not adequate for our current problem. In this paper, we shall introduce the so called “effective viscous flux” technique and make a full use of the structure
of ( 1.5 ) to obtain the desired uniform-in- $\delta$ estimates. Converting the results from $v$ to $c$ by reversing the Cole-Hopf transformation ( 1.4 ), we get the results for the original chemotaxis model ( 1 ). Theorem 1.2 . Let $4<p_{0}<\infty$ and the initial
data satisfy $u_{0}-1\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\ \ u_{0}(x)\geq 0,\ \ \nabla\ln c_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap L^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})$ Then for any constant $M>0$ with $\|\nabla\ln c_{0}\|_{L^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq M$ , there exists a constant $\varepsilon>0$ depending on $M$ , such that if $\lVert u_{0}-1\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}+\lVert\nabla\ln c_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\leq\varepsilon,$ the Cauchy
problem ( 1.5 )-( 1.7 ) has a
global weak solution $(u,c)(x,t)$ satisfying $\begin{cases}\begin{split}u-1&\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty);L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})),\ \ \ \ \nabla u\in L^{2}([0,\infty);L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})),\\
u-1&\in C((0,\infty);C(\mathbb{R}^{2})),\ \ \ {\nabla\ln c}\in C([0,\infty),H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})),\\
{\nabla\ln c}&\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty);L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap L^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))\cap L^{4}([0,\infty);L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))\end{split}\end{cases}$ and $\begin{split}\|u-1\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\to 0,\ \ \|\nabla\ln c\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\to 0~~as~~t\to\infty,\end{split}$ where $2<p_{1}\leq\infty$ and $2<p_{2}<p_{0}<\infty$ . Furthermore, if $c_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\ c_{0}>0$ , then there exists a positive constant $C$ independent of $t$ such that the solution has the following decay rates in time: $\begin{split}\lVert c\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq Ce^{-\frac{3}{4}t}.\end{split}$ (1.12) The rest of paper is organized as follows. Lemma 2.3 . Let $(\tilde{u},{\bf v})$ be a smooth solution of ( 2.4 ). Then there exists a
positive constant $C$ such that $\begin{split}\lVert\nabla{\bf F}\rVert_{L^{p}}\leq&C(\lVert\tilde{u}_{t}\rVert_{L^{p}}+\lVert\nabla^{\bot}\tilde{u}\cdot{\bf v}\rVert_{L^{p}}),\end{split}$ (2.7) where $p>1$ . Proof. First we recall the following inequality (cf. Lemma 2.4(1) in [ 23 ] ): $\begin{split}\lVert\nabla{\bf F}\rVert_{L^{p}}\leq&C\big(\lVert\nabla\cdot{\bf F}\rVert_{L^{p}}+\lVert\nabla^{\bot}\cdot{\bf F}\rVert_{L^{p}}\big).\end{split}$ (2.8) It then follows from ( 2.1 ) and ( 2.5 ) that $\begin{split}\nabla^{\bot}\cdot{\bf F}=&\nabla^{\bot}\cdot\nabla\tilde{u}+\nabla^{\bot}\cdot(\tilde{u}{\bf v})+\nabla^{\bot}\cdot{\bf v}\\
=&\nabla^{\bot}\cdot(\tilde{u}{\bf v})+\nabla^{\bot}\cdot{\bf v}\\
=&(\tilde{u}+1)\nabla^{\bot}\cdot{\bf v}+\nabla^{\bot}\tilde{u}\cdot{\bf v},\end{split}$ (2.9) where in the second equality we have used the fact that $\nabla^{\bot}\cdot\nabla=0$ . Operating $\nabla^{\bot}$ on the second equation of ( 2.4 ) and integrating the result over $(0,t)$ , we have from $\nabla^{\bot}\cdot{\bf{v}}_{0}=0$ that $\nabla^{\bot}\cdot\bf{v}=0,$ which updates ( 2.9 ) as $\nabla^{\bot}\cdot{\bf F}=\nabla^{\bot}\tilde{u}\cdot\bf{v}.$ This together with ( 2.6 ) and ( 2.8 ) gives $\begin{split}\lVert\nabla{\bf F}\rVert_{L^{p}}\leq&C\lVert\tilde{u}_{t}\rVert_{L^{p}}+C\lVert\nabla^{\bot}\tilde{u}\cdot{\bf v}\rVert_{L^{p}}.\end{split}$ Thus, the proof of Lemma 2.3 is completed. ∎ The following Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality will be frequently used in this paper. A Priori Estimates for Approximate Solutions In this section, we prove Theorem 1.1 , by
constructing approximate solutions based upon the following mollified initial data: $\begin{split}\tilde{u}^{\delta}_{0}=j^{\delta}*\tilde{u}_{0},\ \ \ {\bf v}_{0}^{\delta}=j^{\delta}*{\bf v}_{0},\end{split}$ where $\tilde{u}_{0}=u_{0}-1$ and $j^{\delta}$ is the standard mollifying kernel of width $\delta$ (e.g. see [ 1 ] ). Then we consider the following approximate system $\begin{cases}\tilde{u}^{\delta}_{t}-\Delta\tilde{u}^{\delta}=\nabla\cdot(\tilde{u}^{\delta}{\bf v}^{\delta})+\nabla\cdot{\bf v}^{\delta},\\
{\bf v}^{\delta}_{t}-\nabla\tilde{u}^{\delta}=0,\end{cases}$ (3.1) with smooth initial data $(\tilde{u}^{\delta}_{0},{\bf v}_{0}^{\delta})$ which satisfies $(\tilde{u}^{\delta}_{0},{\bf v}^{\delta}_{0})\in H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{2})$ (3.2) and $\lVert\tilde{u}^{\delta}_{0}\rVert^{2}+\lVert{\bf v}^{\delta}_{0}\rVert^{2}\leq\lVert\tilde{u}_{0}\rVert^{2}+\lVert{\bf v}_{0}\rVert^{2}=\theta_{0}.$ (3.3) By Lemma 2.1 , we can obtain the local existence of solutions to the approximate system ( 3.1 ) with initial data $(u^{\delta}_{0},{\bf v}_{0}^{\delta})$ satisfying ( 3.2 )-( 3.3 ).
Next, we shall show in a sequence of lemmas that these approximate solutions
satisfy some global a priori estimates, independent of the mollifying parameter $\delta$ .
These estimates will then be applied in Section 4 to obtain the solutions of
Theorem 1.1 as the limits of these approximate solutions. For the sake of simplicity, we still use $(\tilde{u},{\bf v})$ to
represent the approximate solution $(\tilde{u}^{\delta},{\bf v}^{\delta})$ in this section.
Let $T>0$ be a fixed time and $(\tilde{u},{\bf v})$ be the smooth
solution to ( 3.1 ) on $\mathbb{R}^{2}\times(0,T]$ . We set $\sigma=\sigma(t)=\min\{1,t\}$ and define $\begin{cases}\begin{split}&A_{1}(T)\triangleq\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\lVert\tilde{u}\rVert^{2}+\lVert{\bf v}\rVert^{2}\right)+\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\nabla\tilde{u}\rVert^{2}dt,\\
&A_{2}(T)\triangleq\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\sigma\lVert\nabla\tilde{u}\rVert^{2}+\sigma^{2}\lVert\tilde{u}_{t}\rVert^{2}+\sigma^{2}\lVert{\bf v}_{t}\rVert^{2}\right)+\int_{0}^{T}\left(\sigma\lVert\tilde{u}_{t}\rVert^{2}+\sigma^{2}\lVert\nabla\tilde{u}_{t}\rVert^{2}\right)dt,\\
&A_{3}(T)\triangleq\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\|{\bf v}\|_{L^{4}}^{4}+\int_{0}^{T}\|{\bf v}\|_{L^{4}}^{4}dt.\end{split}\end{cases}$ (3.4) Next, we shall employ the technique of a priori assumption to derive the a priori estimates for the smooth solutions of ( 3.1 )-( 3.3 ).
That is, we first assume that the smooth solution $(\tilde{u},{\bf v})$ satisfies for any $t\in[0,T]$ $\begin{split}A_{1}(T)\leq 2\theta_{0},\ \ A_{2}(T)\leq 2\theta_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}},\ \ A_{3}(T)\leq 2\theta_{0}^{\eta_{0}},\ \ \sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|{\bf v}\|_{L^{p_{0}}}\leq 6M,\end{split}$ (3.5) where $M$ and $\theta_{0}$ are from Theorem 1.1 , and $\eta_{0}$ is defined as $\begin{split}\eta_{0}\triangleq\frac{p_{0}-4}{2(p_{0}-2)}\in(0,\frac{1}{2}).\end{split}$ (3.6) Then we will derive the a priori estimates to obtain global solutions. Finally, we show the obtained global solutions satisfy the above a priori assumption and close our argument. We depart with the $L^{2}$ -estimate of $(\tilde{u},{\bf v})$ . Lemma 3.1 . Let the conditions of Theorem 1.1 hold and $(\tilde{u},{\bf v})$ be a smooth solution of ( 3.1 )-( 3.3 ) satisfying ( 3.5 ). Then it holds that $\lVert\tilde{u}\rVert^{2}+\lVert{\bf v}\rVert^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\nabla\tilde{u}\rVert^{2}dt\leq\frac{3\theta_{0}}{2}.$ (3.7) Proof. Multiplying the first equation of $(\ref{pw7-hptr})$ by $\tilde{u}$ and the second by $v$ , adding the results
and integrating by parts over $\mathbb{R}^{2}$ , we have $\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\lVert\tilde{u}\rVert^{2}+\lVert{\bf v}\rVert^{2}\right)+\lVert\nabla\tilde{u}\rVert^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla\cdot(\tilde{u}{\bf v})\tilde{u}dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\tilde{u}{\bf v}\nabla\tilde{u}dx.\end{split}$ (3.8) For the term on the right-hand side of ( 3.8 ), we use ( 2.10 ), Hölder and Cauchy-Schwarz inequalities to estimate it as $\begin{split}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\tilde{u}{\bf v}\nabla\tilde{u}dx\leq&\lVert\tilde{u}{\bf v}\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\lVert\nabla\tilde{u}\rVert^{2}\\
\leq&\lVert\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}\lVert{\bf v}\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\lVert\nabla\tilde{u}\rVert^{2}\\
\leq&C\lVert\tilde{u}\rVert\lVert\nabla\tilde{u}\rVert\lVert{\bf v}\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\lVert\nabla\tilde{u}\rVert^{2}\\
\leq&C\lVert\tilde{u}\rVert^{2}\lVert{\bf v}\rVert_{L^{4}}^{4}+\frac{1}{2}\lVert\nabla\tilde{u}\rVert^{2},\end{split}$ which therefore updates ( 3.8 ) as $\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\lVert\tilde{u}\rVert^{2}+\lVert{\bf v}\rVert^{2}\right)+\lVert\nabla\tilde{u}\rVert^{2}\leq C\lVert\tilde{u}\rVert^{2}\lVert{\bf v}\rVert_{L^{4}}^{4}.\end{split}$ Integrating the above result over $[0,t]$ and using ( 2.2 ), ( 3.4 ) and ( 3.5 ), we have $\begin{split}&\lVert\tilde{u}\rVert^{2}+\lVert{\bf v}\rVert^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\nabla\tilde{u}\rVert^{2}dt\\

{\bf v}^{\delta}\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty);L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap L^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))\cap L^{4}([0,\infty);L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{2})).\end{cases}$ (4.6) By ( 4.6 ) and the Aubin-Lions-Simon lemma (cf. [ 43 ] ), we can extract a subsequence, still denoted by $(u^{\delta},{\bf v}^{\delta})$ , such that the following convergence hold as $\delta\to 0$ $\begin{cases}{\bf v}^{\delta}(\cdot,t)\to{\bf v}\ \text{strongly in}\ C([0,\infty),H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})),\\
u^{\delta}(\cdot,t)\to u(\cdot,t)\ \text{strongly in}\ C((0,\infty),C(\mathbb{R}^{2})),\\
\nabla u^{\delta}(\cdot,t)\to\nabla u(\cdot,t)\ \text{weakly in}\ L^{2}([0,\infty),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})).\end{cases}$ Thus, the limit function $(u,{\bf v})$ is indeed a weak solution of the system ( 1.5 )-( 1.7 ) and inherits all the bounds of ( 4.4 ) which yield ( 1.10 ) and $\begin{cases}\begin{split}&\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\lVert u-1\rVert^{2}+\lVert{\bf v}\rVert^{2}\right)+\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\nabla u\rVert^{2}dt\leq\frac{3\theta_{0}}{2},\\
&\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\sigma\lVert\nabla u\rVert^{2}+\sigma^{2}\lVert u_{t}\rVert^{2}+\sigma^{2}\lVert{\bf v}_{t}\rVert^{2}\right)+\int_{0}^{T}\sigma\lVert u_{t}\rVert^{2}dt+\int_{0}^{T}\sigma^{2}\lVert\nabla u_{t}\rVert^{2}dt\leq\theta_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}},\\
&\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\|{\bf v}\|_{L^{4}}^{4}+\int_{0}^{t}\|{\bf v}\|_{L^{4}}^{4}dt\leq\theta_{0}^{\eta_{0}},\\
&\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|{\bf v}\|_{L^{p_{0}}}\leq 3M.\end{split}\end{cases}$ (4.7) To complete the proof of Theorem 1.1 ,
we only need to prove ( 1.11 ) . It follows from ( 4.7 ) and $\sigma=1$ for $t\geq 1$ that $\begin{split}\int_{1}^{\infty}\lVert\nabla\tilde{u}\rVert^{2}dt\leq C\end{split}$ and $\begin{split}\int_{1}^{\infty}\left|\left(\lVert\nabla\tilde{u}\rVert^{2}\right)_{t}\right|dt\leq&2\int_{1}^{\infty}\lVert\nabla\tilde{u}\rVert\lVert\nabla\tilde{u}_{t}\rVert dt\\
\leq&\left(\int_{1}^{\infty}\lVert\nabla\tilde{u}\rVert^{2}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{1}^{\infty}\sigma^{2}\lVert\nabla\tilde{u}_{t}\rVert^{2}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C.\end{split}$ Thus, $\lVert\nabla\tilde{u}\rVert\to 0~~\text{as}~~t\to\infty.$ (4.8) Using Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality ( 2.10 ) and $\sigma=1$ for $t\geq 1$ , we have $\begin{split}\sup\limits_{t\geq 1}\lVert\tilde{u}\rVert^{4}_{L^{\infty}}\leq&C\sup\limits_{t\geq 1}\sigma^{\frac{5}{2}}\lVert\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}\lVert\nabla\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}\leq C\sup\limits_{t\geq 1}\lVert\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{2}}\sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\nabla\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{2}}\sigma^{2}\lVert\nabla\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2},\end{split}$ which together with ( 3.36 ), ( 4.7 ) and ( 4.8 ) gives $\lVert u-1\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\to 0~~\text{as}~~t\to\infty.$ (4.9) By the interpolation inequality, ( 4.7 ) and ( 4.9 ), for any $2<p_{1}\leq\infty$ , we have $\lVert u-1\rVert_{L^{p_{1}}}\to 0~~\text{as}~~t\to\infty.$ On the other hand, by ( 3.39 ), we have from ( 3.46 ) and ( 3.47 ) that $\begin{split}\int_{1}^{\infty}\left|\left(\lVert{\bf v}\rVert_{L^{4}}^{4}\right)_{t}\right|dt\leq&C\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\tilde{u}+1|{\bf v}^{4}dxdt+C\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|{\bf F}||{\bf v}|^{3}dxdt\\
\leq&C\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}{\bf v}^{4}dxdt+C\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|{\bf F}||{\bf v}|^{3}dxdt\\
\leq&C\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}{\bf v}^{4}dxdt+C\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|{\bf F}|^{4}dxdt\\
\leq&C.\end{split}$ Combining the above inequality with ( 3.47 ), we have $\lVert{\bf v}\rVert_{L^{4}}\to 0~~\text{as}~~t\to\infty,$ which together with the interpolation inequality, ( 3.7 ) and ( 3.48 ) implies $\lVert{\bf v}\rVert_{L^{p_{2}}}\to 0~~\text{as}~~t\to\infty,\ \ 2<{p_{2}}<p_{0}.$ Hence ( 1.11 ) is proved and the proof of Theorem 1.1 is thus completed. 5. Proof of Theorem 1.2 In this section, we pass the results of the transformed chemotaxis model ( 1.5 ) to the original chemotaxis system ( 1 ). Noticing that the transformed and pre-transformed systems have the same quantity $u$ , we are left to prove the results for $c$ only.
We start with the proof of ( 1.12 ). From the second equation
of ( 1 ) and the Cole-Hopf transformation ( 1.4 ), we can derive that $(\ln c)_{t}=-u$ (5.1) which together with $c_{0}>0$ and $u\geq 0$ gives $0\leq c(x,t)\leq c(x,0)=c_{0}.$ (5.2) Integrating ( 5.1 ) over $[1,t)$ , we have from ( 5.2 ) that $\begin{split}c(x,t)=&c(x,1)\exp\left(-(t-1)-\int_{1}^{t}(u-1)d\tau\right)\\
\leq&c_{0}\exp\left(-(t-1)-\int_{1}^{t}(u-1)d\tau\right).\end{split}$ (5.3) From ( 3.37 ), we get $\begin{split}\int_{1}^{t}\lVert u-1\rVert_{L^{\infty}}d\tau\leq\frac{1}{4}(t-1),\end{split}$ which, along with ( 5.3 ) yields $\begin{split}\lVert c\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq Ce^{-\frac{3}{4}(t-1)}\leq Ce^{-\frac{3}{4}t}.\end{split}$ This completes the proof of Theorem 1.2 .
