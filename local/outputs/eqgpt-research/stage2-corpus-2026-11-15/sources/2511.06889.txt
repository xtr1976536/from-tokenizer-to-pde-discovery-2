Keywords: chemotaxis; periodicity; asymptotics; convergence; cross-diffusion 1 Introduction This work is centered around the asymptotic behavior of the solution to the following system of two coupled nonlinear parabolic partial differential equations $\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=D\Delta u+\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)+ru(1-u)-uv,\quad&x\in\Omega,\penalty\ t>0,\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}=\Delta v+au-v+f(x,t),\quad&x\in\Omega,\penalty\ t>0,\end{cases}$ (1) subject to nonnegative initial values and Neumann homogeneous boundary conditions $\begin{cases}u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\geq 0,\quad v(x,0)=v_{0}(x)\geq 0,&x\in\Omega,\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,\penalty\ t>0,\end{cases}$ (2) posed over a bounded and convex domain $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}$ with smooth boundary, with parameters $D,\penalty\ \chi,\penalty\ r>0,\penalty\ a\geq 0$ and a nonnegative known source function $f\geq 0$ . System ( 1 ) has recently been proposed in [ 5 ] as a model for the interaction between a bacterial population and a lethal chemical agent, such as a toxin or an antibiotic. In the model, $u(x,t)$ denotes the bacterial density and $v(x,t)$ the concentration of the chemical at position $x\in\Omega$ and time $t>0$ , accounting for the following mechanisms: 1. Cell motility and chemical diffusion , incorporated by the Laplacian terms $\Delta u$ and $\Delta v$ . 2. Negative chemotaxis , this is, bacterial repulsion from regions of high chemical concentration, described by $\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)$ . 3. Logistic bacterial growth and chemical lethality , modeled respectively by the terms $ru(1-u)$ and $-uv$ . 4. Self-production and degradation of the chemical , captured by $au-v$ . 5. An external supply of the substance , represented by the source function $f$ . These interactions considered for the model are common in microbiological systems where organisms react to toxins, antibiotics, or self-produced metabolic byproducts. This is the case for instance for E. coli bacteria and $H_{2}O_{2}$ . It is known that E. coli cells naturally produce $H_{2}O_{2}$ molecules [ 17 , 16 ] , which act as a lethal chemorepellent [ 3 , 21 , 8 ] , leading to bacterial migration towards regions of lower concentrations in order to survive. Although for most bacteria such as E. coli , the self production of a chemo-repellent is in general not a means of self regulation or signaling —but instead a consequence of their metabolism [ 9 ] —, its combination with the negative chemotaxis and the intrinsic dynamics of self-production, degradation, logistic growth and lethality creates an intricate feedback mechanism between bacterial motion and the interactions with the chemical, whose asymptotics are analyzed in this work. We note as well that by allowing $a=0$ , the model also accounts for the interactions with an exogenous antibiotic, this is, when the chemical is not produced by the bacteria themselves. From a mathematical point of view, the first models for chemotaxis were introduced by Keller and Segel [ 10 , 11 ] , considering the nonlinear term $\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)$ . Over the years, the Keller-Segel model has been extensively studied, showing the rich behavior of its solutions, ranging from aggregation phenomena to finite-time blow-up (see for instance the surveys by Horstmann [ 7 ] or Hillen and Painter [ 6 ] ). In terms of global existence, it is well known that adding a logistic growth term to the model, resulting in a system of the form $\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=D_{1}\Delta u-\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)+ru-\mu u^{2},\quad&x\in\Omega,\penalty\ t>0,\\
\\
\displaystyle\tau\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}=D_{2}\Delta v+u-\alpha v,&x\in\Omega,\penalty\ t>0,\end{cases}$ (3) under Neumann homogeneous boundary conditions and nonnegative initial values, can suppress the finite-time blow-up of solutions that certain solutions to the minimal Keller-Segel system experiment. In particular, in Tello and Winkler [ 19 ] , the parabolic-elliptic case (corresponding to $\tau=0$ ) is analyzed for positive chemotaxis (i.e. attractant signaling, with $\chi>0$ ). The authors prove that system ( 3 ) admits a unique solution that remains globally bounded for any parameter set, if the problem is posed over a bounded domain in dimension $n\leq 2$ . Moreover, in the higher dimensional case, global boundedness still holds provided that the damping effect of the logistic term is sufficiently strong, specifically when $\mu>\displaystyle\frac{n-2}{2}\chi$ , so that the quadratic death term $-\mu u^{2}$ dominates the aggregation induced by chemotaxis. For the fully parabolic version (with $\tau>0$ ), system ( 3 ) was studied by Winkler in [ 22 ] for a general $\chi\in\mathbb{R}$ , thus accounting for positive and negative chemotaxis, obtaining global boundedness of solutions in arbitrary dimensions for convex domains if $\mu$ is large enough. For the positive chemotaxis case there exists $M>0$ such that if $\displaystyle\frac{\mu}{\chi}>M$ , the unique non-trivial spatially homogeneous steady state of the system becomes globally asymptotically stable, and any solution $(u,v)$ converges to it, as proven in [ 23 ] . Our system ( 1 ) mainly differs from ( 3 ) not only in considering the negative chemotaxis scenario, but also in the asymmetrical death term $-uv$ , —which does not have a counterpart on the $v$ equation, as cell death does not result in an increase of the chemical concentration—, and in the external supply $f$ , which greatly affects the dynamics of the system. Several other works have addressed similar competitive terms, mainly in the form of Lotka-Volterra interactions between two species that are attracted by the same chemical. The elliptic case for the equation of the chemical was studied in [ 20 ] in a weakly competitive scenario, obtaining as well the convergence of solutions to the non-trivial spatially homogeneous steady state, under appropriate parameter restrictions. The parabolic case is considered as well in [ 2 , 12 , 24 ] with similar results for the asymptotic behavior of solutions. A system closely related to ours is given by $\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\Delta u-\chi\nabla(u\nabla v)+\mu u(1+f(x,t)-u),\quad&x\in\Omega,\penalty\ t>0,\\
\\
\displaystyle\tau\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}=\Delta v+u-v,&x\in\Omega,\penalty\ t>0,\end{cases}$ (4) where the logistic term satisfies $1+f(x,t)>0$ , which can be interpreted as a spatio-temporal dependent carrying capacity for the biological species. In particular, the system is analyzed by Negreanu, Tello and Vargas in [ 14 ] , with the assumption that $f(x,t)$ converges to a spatially homogeneous and time periodic function $\tilde{f}$ , modeling seasonal fluctuations in the maximum population load that the environment can support. A generalization of system ( 4 ) with two species in competitive interaction and periodic logistic dynamics was introduced and studied in [ 15 ] In particular, for system ( 4 ) in [ 14 ] , under the assumptions that $\begin{split}f\in L^{\infty}\big(\Omega\times(0,\infty)\big),\\
\\
\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla f|^{2}dx\hskip 2.84544ptdt<\infty,\\
\\
\int_{0}^{\infty}||f-\tilde{f}||_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\hskip 2.84544ptdt<\infty,\end{split}$ (5) as well as $\mu>\frac{\chi^{2}}{16}\max\left\{\int_{\Omega}u_{0}\hskip 2.84544ptdx,\penalty\ \frac{1}{\mu}\left(1+||f||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,\infty))}\right)\right\},$ (6) it is proven that any solution $(u,v)$ to ( 4 ) verifies $||u-\tilde{u}||_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+||v-\tilde{v}||_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\to 0,\quad\text{as }t\to\infty,$ where $(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})$ is the solution to the ODE system associated to system ( 4 ), this is $\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{d\tilde{u}}{dt\penalty\ }=\mu\tilde{u}\big(1+\tilde{f}(t)-\tilde{u}\big),\quad&t>0,\\
\\
\displaystyle\tau\frac{d\tilde{v}}{dt\penalty\ }=\tilde{u}-\tilde{v},&t>0,\end{cases}$ (7) with initial values $\tilde{u}(0)=\int_{\Omega}u_{0}(x)\hskip 2.84544ptdx,\quad\tilde{v}(0)=\int_{\Omega}v_{0}(x)\hskip 2.84544ptdx.$ Main results and structure of the work We recall our system, given by $\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=D\Delta u+\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)+ru(1-u)-uv,\quad&x\in\Omega,\penalty\ t>0,\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}=\Delta v+au-v+f(x,t),\quad&x\in\Omega,\penalty\ t>0,\end{cases}$ under Neumann homogeneous boundary conditions and nonnegative initial values. Our aim is to develop a similar analysis as done for system ( 4 ), considering that $f(x,t)$ converges in time (in a certain sense specified below) to a spatially homogeneous function $\tilde{f}(t)$ . In this way, the main result of the work is Theorem 4.1 , that ensures that if certain parameter conditions are met, any solution $(u,v)$ converges in $L^{2}(\Omega)$ to $(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})$ , the corresponding solution to the ODE system associated to our system ( 1 ), given by $\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{d\tilde{u}}{dt\penalty\ }=r\tilde{u}(1-\tilde{u})-\tilde{u}\tilde{v},\quad&t>0,\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{d\tilde{v}}{dt\penalty\ }=a\tilde{u}-\tilde{v}+\tilde{f},\quad&t>0,\end{cases}$ (8) with initial values $\tilde{u}(0)=\int_{\Omega}u_{0}(x)\penalty\ dx,\quad\tilde{v}(0)=\int_{\Omega}v_{0}(x)\penalty\ dx.$ (9) This extends the existing information about solutions to system ( 1 ), which, to the best of our knowledge, only includes the qualitative analysis performed in [ 5 ] for constant choices of $f$ , as well as periodicity properties for the associated ODE system ( 8 ) in the case $a=0$ and a time-periodic $\tilde{f}(t)$ . In particular, [ 5 ] proves that the balance between the logistic growth rate $r$ and the external supply of the chemical $f$ governs the linearized dynamics of the system. More specifically, for a constant source, $f(x,t)\equiv f>0$ , if $f>r$ , it is proven that the only nonnegative spatially homogeneous steady state $(0,f)$ is locally asymptotically stable, while if $f<r$ , there exists a secondary nonnegative equilibrium $(u_{*},v_{*})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left(\displaystyle\frac{r-f}{r+a},\frac{r(f+a)}{r+a}\right)$ . In this case, $(0,f)$ is unstable, while $(u_{*},v_{*})$ is locally asymptotically stable. The local dynamics were at times preserved globally, while certain parameter gave rise to aggregation phenomena in the form of spike patterns. For the case in which $a=0$ and $\tilde{f}(t)$ is a time-periodic function of period $T>0$ , it is shown that if $r>\bar{\tilde{f}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\tilde{f}(t)\hskip 2.84544ptdt$ , then system ( 8 ) admits a periodic solution of period $T$ , thus inheriting the periodicity of the source $\tilde{f}$ . The convergence of solutions $(u,v)$ to $(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})$ was only tested numerically with positive results. In this way, the present work provides an analytical proof of the convergence of solutions to the full PDE system ( 1 ) to its associated ODE system ( 8 ), that holds as well for the more interesting case $a>0$ , when the second equation is not decoupled. In particular, for the proof it is assumed that $f=f(x,t)$ and $\tilde{f}=\tilde{f}(t)$ are nonnegative functions satisfying the following properties $\begin{split}f\in L^{\infty}\big(\Omega\times(0,\infty)\big),\\
\\
\int_{0}^{\infty}||f-\tilde{f}||_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\hskip 2.84544ptdt<\infty,\end{split}$ (10) which are also considered in ( 5 ) for system ( 4 ). However, for our proof, we need to also assume a stronger convergence of $f$ to $\tilde{f}$ in the sense that $\int_{0}^{\infty}||f-\tilde{f}||^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\hskip 2.84544ptdt+\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big|\Big|\int_{\Omega}f-\tilde{f}\Big|\Big|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\hskip 2.84544ptdt<\infty.$ (11) In contrast, the second hypothesis for $f$ in ( 5 ) regarding the integrability in time of $||\nabla f||_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}$ is not needed for our study. In the most natural case in which $\tilde{f}$ is periodic, $f$ represents a source term that is asymptotically periodic in time, globally bounded, and whose spatial heterogeneities decay in time, in the sense that the second part of ( 10 ) and ( 11 ) hold. This models a persistent global periodic source with certain localized spatial perturbations that are however controlled in the long term. For instance, $f$ can be taken as $f(x,t)=\tilde{f}(t)+p(t)q(x),$ where $\tilde{f}\in L^{\infty}\big((0,\infty)\big)$ is time periodic, $q\in L^{1}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)$ encapsulates the spatial heterogeneities, and $p\in L^{1}\big((0,\infty)\big)\cap L^{\infty}\big((0,\infty)\big)$ represents the time decay of such heterogeneities. In nature, there are various examples of chemotactic processes exhibiting such periodic dynamics. For instance, in the presence of the chemoattractant cyclic AMP, in [ 18 ] the movement of Dictyostelium discoideum amoebae was analyzed, concluding that the average velocity towards their center of aggregation is periodic in time. An example more closely related to our scenario was reported in [ 25 ] , in which E. coli cells were exposed to spatially and temporally varying attractant sources. It was shown that at low source frequencies, the bacterial population density oscillates in synchrony with the periodically supplied attractant. These observations provide a biological motivation for analyzing the long-time behavior of Keller-Segel-type systems subject to periodic external inputs, and for identifying the conditions under which the macroscopic variables inherit the periodicity of the source. The article is structured as follows. After this introduction, Section 2 covers the global existence of bounded solutions to the system, while Section 3 includes further estimates that allow us to prove that the solution $u$ converges to $\displaystyle\bar{u}(t)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}u(x,t)\hskip 2.84544ptdx$ . The main result of the article, Theorem 4.1 is included in Section 4 , proving that indeed $\bar{u}(t)$ converges to $\tilde{u}(t)$ , and proceeding similarly for $v$ . A final discussion concerning the case in which $\tilde{f}$ is time periodic is included in Section 5 , with some comments regarding the conditions under which the solutions to the ODE system ( 8 ) inherit the periodicity of $\tilde{f}$ . 2 Existence and uniqueness of solutions In this section we present Theorem 2.1 , the main result concerning the global existence of bounded solutions to system ( 1 ). For brevity reasons, as the proof is similar to others and mainly follows the steps in [ 22 ] , we only provide an outline of it. Theorem 2.1 . Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}$ , $n\geq 1$ be a convex, bounded domain with smooth boundary and assume that $f\in L^{\infty}\big(\Omega\times(0,\infty)\big)$ , $u_{0}\in C^{0}(\bar{\Omega})$ and $v_{0}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)$ . Then, for all $\chi\in\mathbb{R}$ , $D>0$ and $a\geq 0$ there exists $r_{0}>0$ such that if $r\geq r_{0}$ , problem ( 1 ) with boundary and initial conditions ( 2 ) admits a uniquely determined nonnegative global classical solution $(u,v)$ with $||u||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+||v||_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}<c,\quad\text{for all }t>0,$ for a certain $c>0$ independent of $t$ . Sketch of the proof As above mentioned, the proof can be carried out similarly as in [ 22 ] . Firstly, the local existence of solutions in an interval $(0,T_{\max})$ is standard following the results by Amann [ 1 ] . Then, after appropriate estimates for the quantity $\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m}b_{k}\int_{\Omega}u^{k}|\nabla v|^{2m-2k}$ , for conveniently selected weights $b_{k}$ , time-independent bounds for $u$ in $L^{m}(\Omega)$ and for $\nabla v$ in $L^{2m}(\Omega)$ can be obtained for arbitrarily large $m\in\mathbb{N}$ . The main difference with respect to [ 22 ] lies on the conditions for $r_{0}$ based on previois estimates that depend on $||f||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,\infty))}$ . Lastly, standard semigroup theory allows to obtain the desired bounds for $u$ and $v$ . 3 Further estimates Next, we derive some estimates that play a crucial role when analyzing the asymptotic behavior of the solutions. The first result entails a lower bound for the total mass of $u$ . Lemma 3.1 . Let $f$ be such that $||f||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,\infty))}<\infty$ . Then, if $r>0$ is large enough, there exists $c>0$ satisfying $\int_{\Omega}u\geq c,\quad\text{for all }t>0.$ Proof. Firstly, the assumptions on $f$ and $r$ ensure that by Theorem 2.1 there exists a unique global solution $(u,v)$ to system ( 1 ), satisfying $u\in L^{\infty}\big(\Omega\times(0,\infty)\big)$ and $v\in L^{\infty}\big((0,\infty);\penalty\ W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)\big)$ . The main part of the proof follows the method introduced by Mizukami and Yokota in [ 13 ] , Lemma 4.2. To this end, we take an arbitrary $\beta\in(0,1)$ satisfying $0<\beta<\min\left\{1,\frac{4\left(r-||v||_{L^{\infty}\left(\Omega\times(0,\infty)\right)}\right)}{D\chi^{2}||\nabla v||^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,\infty))}}-1\right\},$ (12) and consider $\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u^{-\beta}$ . In particular, the choice of $r>0$ must be large enough to satisfy $r>\frac{D\cdot\chi^{2}\cdot||\nabla v||^{2}_{L^{\infty}\left(\Omega\times(0,\infty)\right)}}{4}+||v||_{L^{\infty}\left(\Omega\times(0,\infty)\right)},$ (13) ensuring that indeed $\beta>0$ . Integrating by parts we obtain $\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}u^{-\beta}=&-\beta\int_{\Omega}u^{-\beta-1}\Bigg(D\Delta u-\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)+ru\left(1-u-\frac{v}{r}\right)\Bigg)\\
=&-D\beta(\beta+1)\int_{\Omega}u^{-\beta-2}|\nabla u|^{2}-\chi\beta(\beta+1)\int_{\Omega}u^{-\beta-1}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\\
&-r\beta\int_{\Omega}u^{-\beta}\left(1-u-\frac{v}{r}\right),\quad\text{ for all t>0}.\end{split}$ (14) The term involving the product of the gradients can be bounded using Young’s inequality as follows $\begin{split}-\chi\beta(\beta+1)\int_{\Omega}u^{-\beta-1}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\leq\chi\beta(\beta+1)\int_{\Omega}u^{-\beta-1}|\nabla u||\nabla v|\\
\leq D\beta(\beta+1)\int_{\Omega}u^{-\beta-2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{D\beta(\beta+1)\chi^{2}}{4}\int_{\Omega}u^{-\beta}|\nabla v|^{2},\end{split}$ (15) for all $t>0$ . Notice that the first term appearing on the right hand side here cancels the corresponding one in ( 14 ). We can moreover estimate the final term in ( 15 ) by making use of the $L^{\infty}\big(\Omega\times(0,\infty)\big)$ bound for $\nabla v$ . In particular, for all $t>0$ we have $\frac{D\beta(\beta+1)\chi^{2}}{4}\int_{\Omega}u^{-\beta}|\nabla v|^{2}\leq||\nabla v||^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,\infty)}\cdot\frac{D\beta(\beta+1)\chi^{2}}{4}\int_{\Omega}u^{-\beta}.$ (16) Next, concerning the last term appearing in ( 14 ), we have $-r\beta\int_{\Omega}u^{-\beta}\left(1-u-\frac{v}{r}\right)=-r\beta\int_{\Omega}u^{-\beta}+r\beta\int_{\Omega}u^{1-\beta}+\beta\int_{\Omega}u^{-\beta}v.$ Relying this time on the global boundedness of $u$ , the second term on the right hand side of the above expression is such that $r\beta\int_{\Omega}u^{1-\beta}\leq r\beta|\Omega|||u||_{L^{\infty}\left(\Omega\times(0,\infty)\right)}^{1-\beta},\quad\text{for all }t>0,$ (17) and for the last one we have $\beta\int_{\Omega}u^{-\beta}v\leq\beta||v||_{L^{\infty}\left(\Omega\times(0,\infty)\right)}\int_{\Omega}u^{-\beta}\quad\text{for all }t>0.$ (18) In this way, by combining estimates ( 15 )-( 18 ), we can rewrite ( 14 ) directly as $\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}u^{-\beta}+c_{1}\int_{\Omega}u^{-\beta}\leq c_{2},\quad\text{for all }t>0,$ (19) where $\begin{split}c_{1}&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\beta\left(r-||v||_{L^{\infty}\left(\Omega\times(0,\infty)\right)}-||\nabla v||^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,\infty)}\cdot\frac{D(\beta+1)\chi^{2}}{4}\right),\\
\\
c_{2}&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=r\beta|\Omega|||u||_{L^{\infty}\left(\Omega\times(0,\infty)\right)}^{1-\beta}>0.\end{split}$ The choice of $\beta$ in ( 12 ), combined with assumption ( 13 ) ensures that indeed $c_{1}>0$ as well. Thus, the differential inequality ( 19 ) implies that there exists $c_{\beta}>0$ such that $\int_{\Omega}u^{-\beta}\leq c_{\beta},\quad\text{for all }t>0.$ (20) Next, combining the the Cauchy Schwarz inequality with the obtained bound ( 20 ), we have for all $t>0$ $|\Omega|=\int_{\Omega}\frac{u^{\beta/2}}{u^{\beta/2}}\leq\left[\int_{\Omega}u^{\beta}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\left[\int_{\Omega}u^{-\beta}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq c_{\beta}^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\left[\int_{\Omega}u^{\beta}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}.$ Lastly, as $\beta\in(0,1)$ , using Hölder’s inequality with exponents $p=\beta^{-1}>1$ , $q=(1-\beta)^{-1}>1$ , it follows that $|\Omega|^{2}\cdot c_{\beta}^{-1}\leq\int_{\Omega}u^{\beta}=\int_{\Omega}\left(u^{\beta}\cdot 1\right)\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}u\right)^{\beta}|\Omega|^{1-\beta},\quad\text{for all }t>0,$ which finishes the proof upon taking $c\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left(|\Omega|^{1+\beta}\cdot c_{\beta}^{-1}\right)^{1/\beta}$ .
∎ Next, we prove a similar result concerning a lower bound for the solution $\tilde{u}$ to the associated ODE system. In this case it is again a threshold for the logistic growth rate $r$ which drives the dynamics of the system. Lemma 3.2 . Let $\tilde{u}$ be the solution to system ( 8 ) with initial values ( 9 ) and assume that $r>\max\big\{\tilde{v}(0),\penalty\ a\cdot\max\{\tilde{u}(0),1\}+||\tilde{f}||_{L^{\infty}(0,\infty)}\big\}.$ (21) Then, there exists $c>0$ such that $\tilde{u}(t)>c,\quad\text{for all }t>0.$ Proof. From the first equation in system ( 8 ) we have $\frac{1}{r\tilde{u}}\cdot\frac{\penalty\ d\tilde{u}}{dt}+\tilde{u}=1-\frac{\tilde{v}}{r},\quad\text{for all }t>0.$ (22) The main element in the proof is based on the fact that under hypothesis ( 21 ), the solution $\tilde{v}$ is such that $\displaystyle 1-\frac{\tilde{v}}{r}>0$ . To prove this, we start by noting that $\tilde{u}$ satisfies $\frac{\penalty\ d\tilde{u}}{dt}=r\tilde{u}(1-\tilde{u})-\tilde{u}\tilde{v}\leq r\tilde{u}(1-\tilde{u}),$ for all $t>0$ , which directly implies that $\tilde{u}$ is bounded from above by the solution of the logistic equation, and in particular $\tilde{u}(t)\leq\max\{\tilde{u}(0),1\},\quad\text{for all }t>0.$ (23) Substituting the bound into the second equation in ( 8 ) yields $\frac{\penalty\ d\tilde{v}}{dt}=a\tilde{u}-\tilde{v}+\tilde{f}\leq\left[a\cdot\max\{\tilde{u}(0),1\}+||\tilde{f}||_{L^{\infty}(0,\infty)}\right]-\tilde{v},$ for all $t>0$ . Solving the differential inequality, one obtains $\tilde{v}(t)\leq\max\big\{\tilde{v}(0),a\cdot\max\{\tilde{u}(0),1\}+||\tilde{f}||_{L^{\infty}(0,\infty)}\big\},\quad\text{for all }t>0.$ (24) Thus, if $\displaystyle r>\max\big\{\tilde{v}(0),a\cdot\max\{\tilde{u}(0),1\}+||\tilde{f}||_{L^{\infty}(0,\infty)}\big\}$ , ( 22 ) implies that there exists $\varepsilon>0$ such that $\frac{1}{r\tilde{u}}\cdot\frac{\penalty\ d\tilde{u}}{dt}+\tilde{u}>\varepsilon,\quad\text{for all }t>0.$ (25) The remaining of the proof is standard. By defining $\displaystyle\tilde{\varepsilon}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{2}\min\{\tilde{u}(0),\varepsilon\}>0$ , we prove that $\tilde{u}(t)>c$ for all $t>0$ . We proceed by contradiction by supposing there exists $t_{0}>0$ such that $\tilde{u}(t)>\tilde{\varepsilon}$ for all $t\in(0,t_{0})$ , while also satisfying $\tilde{u}(t_{0})=\tilde{\varepsilon}$ and $\displaystyle\frac{\penalty\ d\tilde{u}}{dt}(t_{0})\leq 0$ . In this way, at $t=t_{0}$ ( 25 ) turns into $\frac{1}{r\tilde{\varepsilon}}\cdot\frac{\penalty\ d\tilde{u}}{dt}(t_{0})+\tilde{\varepsilon}>\varepsilon.$ Thus, as $\varepsilon>\tilde{\varepsilon}$ we have $\frac{\penalty\ d\tilde{u}}{dt}(t_{0})>r\tilde{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon-\tilde{\varepsilon})>0,$ which contradicts our initial assumption that $\displaystyle\frac{\penalty\ d\tilde{u}}{dt}(t_{0})\leq 0$ . Thus, the proof is complete by taking $c\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\tilde{\varepsilon}$ .
∎ Next, we introduce the nonnegative function $k_{1}(t)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\int_{\Omega}\left(u(x,t)-\int_{\Omega}u(x,t)\penalty\ dx\right)^{2}dx,$ (26) which verifies the integrability property proved in the following lemma. Lemma 3.3 . Let $f$ be such that $||f||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,\infty))}<\infty$ , and that $\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{\infty}||f-\tilde{f}||_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\hskip 2.84544ptdt<\infty\\
&\int_{0}^{\infty}||f-\tilde{f}||^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\hskip 2.84544ptdt+\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big|\Big|\int_{\Omega}f-\tilde{f}\Big|\Big|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\hskip 2.84544ptdt<\infty.\end{split}$ (27) for some spatially homogeneous function $\tilde{f}$ . Then, if $r>0$ is large enough, the function $k_{1}$ defined in ( 26 ) verifies $\int_{0}^{\infty}k_{1}(t)\penalty\ dt\leq c<\infty,$ for a certain $c>0$ . Proof. First, the assumption on $r$ being large enough grants the applicability of Theorem 2.1 and Lemmas 3.1 and 3.2 . To prove the result, we first decompose $v$ into two components, $w$ and $z$ , each one accounting for one of the terms in $\displaystyle\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}$ . More precisely, we let $w$ and $z$ be the solutions of $\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\partial w}{\partial t}=\Delta w+au-w,\quad&x\in\Omega,\penalty\ t>0,\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial z}{\partial t}=\Delta z+f-z,\quad&x\in\Omega,\penalty\ t>0,\end{cases}$ (28) with initial values $w(x,0)=z(x,0)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}v(x,0)$ , and Neumann homogeneous boundary conditions, clearly having $v=w+z.$ In this way, the dynamics of $w$ capture the production term $+au$ , while the source term $+f$ is handled solely by the equation for $z$ . Naturally, the existence theory developed for $v$ in Theorem ( 2.1 ) extends to both $w$ and $z$ , resulting in their global boundedness. The equation for $u$ is now given by $\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=D\Delta u+\chi\nabla\cdot\Big(u\nabla(w+z)\Big)+ru\left(1-u-\frac{w}{r}-\frac{z}{r}\right),$ (29) for $x\in\Omega,\penalty\ t>0$ . Moreover, the associate ODE system ( 8 ) now becomes $\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{d\tilde{u}}{dt\penalty\ }=r\tilde{u}\left(1-\tilde{u}-\frac{\tilde{w}}{r}-\frac{\tilde{z}}{r}\right),\quad&t>0,\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{d\tilde{w}}{dt\penalty\ }=a\tilde{u}-\tilde{w},\quad&t>0,\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{d\tilde{z}}{dt\penalty\ }=\tilde{f}-\tilde{z},\quad&t>0,\end{cases}$ (30) with initial values $\displaystyle\tilde{u}(0)=\int_{\Omega}u_{0}(x)\hskip 2.84544ptdx$ , $\displaystyle\tilde{w}(0)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}v_{0}(x)\hskip 2.84544ptdx$ , and $\displaystyle\tilde{z}(0)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}v_{0}(x)\hskip 2.84544ptdx$ . We start by defining the following functionals $\begin{split}&F_{1}(t)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\int_{\Omega}\frac{u(x,t)}{\tilde{u}(t)}\penalty\ dx-1+\ln\tilde{u}(t)-\int_{\Omega}\ln u(x,t)\penalty\ dx,\\
\\
&F_{2}(t)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\ln\left(\int_{\Omega}u(x,t)\penalty\ dx\right)-\ln\tilde{u}(t),\end{split}$ (31) with the same structure as others used in the study of chemotaxis systems, such as in [ 2 , 14 ] . Given that $|\Omega|=1$ , $F_{1}$ verifies $F_{1}(t)=\int_{\Omega}h\left(\frac{u}{\penalty\ \tilde{u}}\right),\quad\text{for }h(s)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=s-1-\ln s,$ with $h(s)\geq 0$ for all $s>0$ , having $\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}h(s)=+\infty$ . Thus, $F_{1}(t)\geq 0$ . With respect to $F_{2}$ , Lemma 3.1 and the fact that $\tilde{u}(t)\leq\max\{\tilde{u}(0),1\}$ for all $t>0$ grant the existence of a $c_{3}>0$ such that $F_{2}(t)\geq-c_{3}>-\infty$ for all $t>0$ . We first compute the time derivative of $F_{2}$ . -\int_{\Omega}\left[\left(f-\int_{\Omega}f\right)\Delta v\right]&\leq\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta v|^{2}+\int_{\Omega}\left(f-\int_{\Omega}f\right)^{2}.\end{split}$ (51) The first two are a direct consequence of having $\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Delta v=0$ due to the Neumann homogeneous boundary conditions and a standard integration by parts, while the third and fourth one follow directly from the pointwise identity $\displaystyle-AB\leq\frac{1}{4}A^{2}+B^{2}$ for any $A,B\in\mathbb{R}$ . &\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta v|^{2}+a^{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(u-\int_{\Omega}u\right)^{2}+\int_{\Omega}\left(f-\int_{\Omega}f\right)^{2},\end{split}$ (52) for all $t>0$ . Considering that $\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(u-\int_{\Omega}u\hskip 2.84544ptdx\right)^{2}dx$ is precisely the function $k_{1}$ defined in ( 26 ), whose integrability was proven in Lemma 3.3 , and the fact that $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\left(f-\int_{\Omega}f\hskip 2.84544ptdx\right)^{2}dx\hskip 2.84544ptdt<\infty$ due to ( 11 ), as seen in Lemma 3.3 , integrating ( 52 ) in time over $(0,\infty)$ yields the desired result.
∎ The next lemma provides another integrability result, this time for $\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\hskip 2.84544ptdx$ . Lemma 3.5 . Assume that $f$ satisfies ( 10 ) and ( 11 ) for a certain $\tilde{f}$ . Then, if $r>0$ is large enough in the sense of the previous lemmas, there exists $c>0$ such that $\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\hskip 2.84544ptdt\leq c<\infty.$ Proof. Considering functionals $F_{1}$ and $F_{2}$ as in ( 31 ), but this time directly with $v$ instead of decomposing it into $w$ and $z$ , we obtain after an integration by parts $\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}(F_{1}+F_{2})&=\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{u}{\tilde{u}}\right)-\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{\Omega}\ln u\right)+\frac{d}{dt}\left(\ln\int_{\Omega}u\right)\\
&\leq\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{u}{\tilde{u}}\right)+\int_{\Omega}\left[-\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{2}}+\chi\frac{\nabla u\nabla v}{u}\right]+F_{3}\\
&\leq\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{u}{\tilde{u}}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{2}}+\frac{\chi^{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2}+F_{3},\end{split}$ (53) for all $t>0$ , where $F_{3}(t)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=-\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}uv}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u}+\int_{\Omega}v+\frac{\displaystyle r\int_{\Omega}u(1-u)}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u}-r\int_{\Omega}(1-u),$ having used Young’s inequality for the second step in ( 53 ). Firstly, for all $t>0$ we have $\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}v-\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}uv}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u}&=\frac{\displaystyle\left(a\int_{\Omega}u+\int_{\Omega}f-\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}v\right)\cdot\left(\int_{\Omega}u\right)-\int_{\Omega}uv}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u}\\
&=-\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}v+\frac{\displaystyle a\left(\int_{\Omega}u^{2}-k_{1}\right)+\int_{\Omega}u\cdot\int_{\Omega}f-\int_{\Omega}uv}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u}\\
&\leq-\frac{a}{||u||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,\infty))}}\cdot k_{1}+\left(\frac{||u||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,\infty))}}{c_{1}}-1\right)\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}v,\end{split}$ where $c_{1}>0$ is such that $c_{1}\leq\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u(x,t)\hskip 2.84544ptdx$ for all $t>0$ , as provided by Lemma 3.1 . Moreover, for the second part of $F_{3}$ , we have $\begin{split}\frac{\displaystyle r\int_{\Omega}u(1-u)}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u}-r\int_{\Omega}(1-u)=r\int_{\Omega}u-\frac{\displaystyle r\int_{\Omega}u^{2}}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u}=-\frac{rk_{1}}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u}\leq 0,\quad\text{for all }t>0.\end{split}$ Thus, it follows that $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}F_{3}(t)\hskip 2.84544ptdt<\infty$ . Next, a time integration of ( 53 ) provides $\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{2}}dx\hskip 2.84544ptdt<\infty,$ as $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2}dx\hskip 2.84544ptdt$ is finite by Lemma 3.4 . The global boundedness of $u$ established in Theorem 2.1 allows us to bound our desired integral in the following way $\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\hskip 2.84544ptdt\leq||u||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,\infty))}^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2}dx\hskip 2.84544ptdt<\infty,$ (54) which finishes the proof.
∎ Once the integrability of $\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}$ has been achieved, by continue by proving a pointwise bound for it. Lemma 3.6 . Let $f$ be such that hypotheses ( 10 ) and ( 11 ) hold for a certain $\tilde{f}$ . Then, if $r>0$ is large enough in the sense of the previous lemmas, there exists $c>0$ independent of $t$ such that $\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\leq c<\infty,\quad\text{for all }t>0.$ Proof. We begin by multiplying the first equation in ( 1 ) by $-\Delta u$ , taking into account that $\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)=\nabla u\cdot\nabla v+u\Delta v$ . Thus, substituting bounds ( 56 ) and ( 57 ) into ( 55 ), we obtain $\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{D}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta u|^{2}\leq(c_{1}+c_{3})\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}+c_{2}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta v|^{2}+c_{4}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2},$ for all $t>0$ , after which a direct integration provides the result, taking into account that the all the terms appearing on the right hand side are integrable by means of Lemmas 3.4 and 3.5 . Notice that as a by-product, we also obtain that $\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta u|^{2}dx\hskip 2.84544ptdt<\infty.$ ∎ Next, after the integrability properties obtained in Lemmas 3.4 - 3.6 , we can prove a key result concerning the boundedness of the time derivative of the function $k_{1}$ introduced in ( 26 ). Lemma 3.7 . Let $k_{1}$ be the function defined in ( 26 ) and assume that $f$ satisfies ( 10 ) and ( 11 ) for a certain $\tilde{f}$ . In view of the regularity properties of the classical solution $(u,v)$ assessed in Theorem 2.1 , we have that $k_{1}\in C^{1}(0,\infty)$ , and computing its derivative, we obtain $\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}k_{1}(t)&=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\left(u-\int_{\Omega}u\right)^{2}=\int_{\Omega}\left(u-\int_{\Omega}u\right)u_{t}\\

Similarly, we have $\begin{split}\left|\hskip 2.84544ptr\int_{\Omega}\left[\left(u-\int_{\Omega}u\right)u\left(1-u-\frac{v}{r}\right)\right]\right|\leq r\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\left|1-u-\frac{v}{r}\right|+r\int_{\Omega}u\cdot\int_{\Omega}u\left|1-u-\frac{v}{r}\right|\\

In this way, a combination of the three estimates of the right hand side of ( 58 ), and fact that by Lemma 3.6 , $\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}$ is bounded for all $t>0$ , leads to the boundedness of $\displaystyle\left|\frac{d}{dt}k_{1}(t)\right|$ for all $t>0$ .

The natural question that arises is whether the solution $(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})$ does inherit the periodicity of $\tilde{f}$ or not. As mentioned in the Introduction, the ODE system ( 8 ) was considered in [ 5 ] for $a=0$ , and a periodic function $\tilde{f}$ of period $T>0$ , having $\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{d\tilde{u}}{dt\penalty\ }=r\tilde{u}(1-\tilde{u})-\tilde{u}\tilde{v},\quad&t>0,\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{d\tilde{v}}{dt\penalty\ }=-\tilde{v}+\tilde{f},\quad&t>0,\end{cases}$ (71) with initial values $\tilde{u}(0)=\int_{\Omega}u_{0}(x)\penalty\ dx,\quad\tilde{v}(0)=\int_{\Omega}v_{0}(x)\penalty\ dx.$ As the second equation is uncoupled, the system can be explicitly solved, determining the threshold value $r_{\min}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\tilde{f}(s)\hskip 2.84544ptds,$ (72) the average value of $\tilde{f}$ over its period. It is proven in the paper that if $r>r_{\min}$ , then system ( 71 ) admits a unique positive periodic solution, that can be constructed by adequately selecting $\tilde{u}(0)$ and $\tilde{v}(0)$ . In particular, the unique periodic solution corresponds to the pair of initial values $(\tilde{u}(0),\tilde{v}(0))=(u^{per}_{0},v^{per}_{0})$ , where $\begin{split}u^{per}_{0}(r)&=\frac{e^{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}(r-\tilde{f}(s))\penalty\ ds}-1}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left(r\penalty\ e^{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}(r-\tilde{f}(\tau))\penalty\ d\tau}\right)\penalty\ ds},\\
\\
v^{per}_{0}(r)&=\frac{1}{e^{T}-1}\int_{0}^{T}\tilde{f}(s)e^{s}\penalty\ ds.\end{split}$ (73) For $a>0$ , the question remains open due to the nonlinear coupling of the system. The result obtained in Lemma 3.2 provides a partial answer to this: if $r>r_{\min,a}$ , where $r_{\min,a}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\max\big\{\tilde{v}(0),\penalty\ a\cdot\max\{\tilde{u}(0),1\}+||\tilde{f}||_{L^{\infty}(0,\infty)}\big\},$ (74) then there exists a constant $c>0$ such that $\tilde{u}(t)>c$ for all $t>0$ . While this rules out a decay of $\tilde{u}$ to zero, it does not directly imply that $(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})$ is $T$ –periodic. Moreover, $r_{\min,a}$ is not a sharp threshold in the sense of the decoupled case, since substituting $a=0$ in $r_{\min,a}$ produces a larger value than the exact threshold $r_{\min}$ obtained for $a=0$ .
