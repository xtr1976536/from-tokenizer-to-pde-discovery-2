We prove the existence of a large family of two-dimensional standing waves, that are triple periodic solutions, for a Boussinesq system which describes two-way propagation of water waves in a channel. Our proof uses the Lyapunov-Schmidt method to find the bifurcation standing waves. Key words and phrases: standing wave; Boussinesq system; 1. Introduction In this article, we study the standing wave patterns of a two-dimensional system (1.1) $\begin{cases}\eta_{t}+\nabla\cdot\textbf{v}+\nabla\cdot(\eta\textbf{v})-\frac{1}{6}\Delta\eta_{t}=0,\\
\textbf{v}_{t}+\nabla\eta+\frac{1}{2}\nabla|\textbf{v}|^{2}-\frac{1}{6}\Delta\textbf{v}_{t}=0,\end{cases}$ which was put forward by Bona, Colin and Lannes [ 3 ] . The horizontal location $\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2})$ and time $t$ are scaled by $h_{0}$ and $\sqrt{h_{0}/g}$ with $h_{0}$ being the average
depth of water in the undisturbed state and $g$ being the acceleration of gravity.
The unknown function $\eta(\mathbf{x},t)$ and $\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x},t)=(v_{1}(\mathbf{x},t),v_{2}(\mathbf{x},t))$ ,
represent the dimensionless deviation of the water surface from its undisturbed state and the horizontal velocity at the level of $\sqrt{2/3}h_{0}$ of the depth of the undisturbed fluid,
scaled by $h_{0}$ and $\sqrt{gh_{0}}$ ,
respectively. The system ( 1.1 ) and its one-dimensional version (1.2) $\begin{cases}&\eta_{t}+u_{x}+(u\eta)_{x}+au_{xxx}-bu_{xxt}=0,\\
&u_{t}+\eta_{x}+uu_{x}+c\eta_{xxx}-du_{xxt}=0,\end{cases}$ named as Boussineq-type systems or $abcd-$ systems, was introduced by Bona, Chen and Saut [ 1 , 2 ] describe the small-amplitude and long wavelength gravity waves of an ideal, incompressible liquid. The systems ( 1.1 ) and ( 1.2 ) are the first order approximations to the full Euler equations in the small parameters $\epsilon_{1}=A/h_{0}$ and $\epsilon_{2}=h_{0}^{2}/\lambda^{2}$ , where $A$ is the typical wave amplitude and $\lambda$ is the typical wavelength
(see Bona, Colin and Lannes [ 3 ] for the rigorous proof). For the one-dimensional systems, they are at the same order of accuracy with
the well-known KdV (Korteweg-de Vries), BBM (Benjamin-Bona-Mahoney) and Boussinesq equations, but works for a wider range of problems, meaning the wave do not have to be traveling in one direction. The water wave patterns such as traveling waves and standing waves have been studied in different modelings in the past (c.f [ 4 , 5 , 6 , 8 , 9 , 10 , 11 , 12 ] ).
In this paper, we aim to continue the study of Chen and Iooss on the 1-D standing wave [ 4 ] of the $abcd$ -system ( 1.2 ) and the 2-D traveling wave patterns
of ( 1.1 ) [ 5 , 6 ] . Here, we look for periodic solutions in $(\mathbf{x},t)$ , and introduce the scaled variables $\tilde{\mathbf{x}}=\frac{\sqrt{6}}{\lambda}\mathbf{x}$ and $\tilde{t}=\frac{\sqrt{6}}{T}t$ with $\lambda/\sqrt{6}$ and $T/\sqrt{6}$ being the wave length and the time period. By dropping the tilde for the re-scaled Boussinesq system of ( 1.1 ), one obtains (1.3) $\begin{cases}\eta_{t}+\beta\nabla\cdot\textbf{v}+\beta\nabla\cdot(\eta\textbf{v})-\omega\Delta\eta_{t}=0,\\
\textbf{v}_{t}+\beta\nabla\eta+\frac{1}{2}\beta\nabla|\textbf{v}|^{2}-\omega\Delta\textbf{v}_{t}=0,\end{cases}$ where $\omega=(1/\lambda)^{2}$ and $\beta$ is a positive parameter defined as $\beta=T/\lambda.$ We are looking for solution of system ( 1.3 ) under the form of 2-dimensional standing waves, i.e. $\eta$ and v are periodic functions in both time $t$ and space x , where $\textbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}$ . This paper is organized as follows: In section 2, we set the linearized problem and study its solution near zero. In section 3, we apply the Lyapunov-Schmidt method to obtain the bifurcation standing waves with bifurcating parameter $\beta$ . 2. Linearized operator For fixed $\omega>0$ , we start by studying the linearized system (2.1) $\begin{cases}\eta_{t}+\beta\nabla\cdot\textbf{v}-\omega\Delta\eta_{t}=\nabla\cdot\textbf{f}\\
\textbf{v}_{t}+\beta\nabla\eta-\omega\Delta\textbf{v}_{t}=\nabla g,\end{cases}$ and we denote it as (2.2) $\mathcal{L}U=\mathcal{G}F$ where $\displaystyle U=(\eta,\textbf{v})^{T},\ \ \ \ \ F=(g,\textbf{f})^{T},$ $\displaystyle\mathcal{L}U=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\eta_{t}+\beta\nabla\cdot\textbf{v}-\omega\Delta\eta_{t}\\
\textbf{v}_{t}+\beta\nabla\eta-\omega\Delta\textbf{v}_{t}\\
\end{array}\right),\ \ \ \ \mathcal{G}F=(\nabla\cdot\textbf{f},\nabla g)^{T}.$ To simplify the problem, we look for solutions $\eta$ being odd and $\mathbf{v}$ being even in $t$ , it then follows from ( 2.1 ) that $g$ is even and $\mathbf{f}$ is odd in time.
We now write the Fourier series, (2.3) $\eta=\sum_{q\geq 0,\textbf{p}\in\Gamma}\eta_{\textbf{p}q}e^{i\textbf{p}\cdot\textbf{x}}\cos{qt},$ (2.4) $\textbf{v}=\sum_{q>0,\textbf{p}\in\Gamma}\textbf{v}_{\textbf{p}q}e^{i\textbf{p}\cdot\textbf{x}}\sin{qt},$ (2.5) $g=\sum_{q\geq 0,\textbf{p}\in\Gamma}g_{\textbf{p}q}e^{i\textbf{p}\cdot\textbf{x}}\cos{qt},$ (2.6) $\textbf{f}=\sum_{q>0,\textbf{p}\in\Gamma}\textbf{f}_{\textbf{p}q}e^{i\textbf{p}\cdot\textbf{x}}\sin{qt},$ where $q\in\mathbb{Z}$ and $\Gamma$ is a hexagonal lattice of the $x_{1}x_{2}$ -plane defined by two vectors $\mathbf{P}_{1}$ and $\mathbf{P}_{2}$ such that, for $\mathbf{p}\in\Gamma$ , one can write (2.7) $\mathbf{p}=(p_{1},p_{2})=n_{1}\mathbf{P}_{1}+n_{2}\mathbf{P}_{2},n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}.$ We denote $\theta_{1}$ and $-\theta_{2}$ to be angle of the two vectors $\mathbf{P}_{1}$ and $\mathbf{P}_{2}$ with $x_{1}$ axis for $0<\theta_{1},\theta_{2}<\pi$ . Thus, we can write $\mathbf{P}_{1}:=k_{1}(1,\tau_{1}),\ \ \mathbf{P}_{2}:=k_{2}(1,-\tau_{2}),$ where $k_{j}>0$ and $\tau_{j}=\tan{\theta_{j}}>0$ with $j=1,2$ . According to ( 2.7 ), one has (2.8) $p_{1}:=p_{1}(n_{1},n_{2})=k_{1}n_{1}+k_{2}n_{2},\ \ p_{2}:=p_{2}(n_{1},n_{2})=\tau_{1}k_{1}n_{1}-\tau_{2}k_{2}n_{2}.$ In addition, it is no harm to assume that $\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{p}0}=\mathbf{f}_{\mathbf{p}0}=\mathbf{0}:=(0,0)$ for $\mathbf{p}\in\Gamma$ if they are needed in the paper. We now substitute ( 2.3 )-( 2.6 ) into ( 2.1 ) to solve the linear system, and obtain: • for $q>0$ and $\mathbf{p}\in\Gamma$ (2.9) $\begin{cases}-q(1+\omega|\textbf{p}|^{2})\eta_{\textbf{p}q}+i\beta\textbf{p}\cdot\textbf{v}_{\textbf{p}q}=i\textbf{p}\cdot\textbf{f}_{\textbf{p}q},\\
i\beta\textbf{p}\eta_{\textbf{p}q}+q(1+\omega|\textbf{p}|^{2})\textbf{v}_{\textbf{p}q}=i\textbf{p}g_{\textbf{p}q};\end{cases}$ • for $q=0$ and $\mathbf{p}\neq(0,0)$ , $\eta_{\mathbf{p}q}=\frac{1}{\beta}g_{\mathbf{p}q}$ and $\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{p}q}=(0,0)$ ; • for $q=0$ and $\mathbf{p}=(0,0)$ , $\eta_{\mathbf{p}q}$ is arbitrary and $\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{p}q}=(0,0)$ . Set $\Delta(\textbf{p},q)=q^{2}(1+\omega|\textbf{p}|^{2})^{2}-\beta^{2}|\textbf{p}|^{2}.$ For $\Delta(\textbf{p},q)\neq 0$ , according to ( 2.9 ), we have (2.10) $\eta_{\textbf{p}q}=-\Delta^{-1}[iq(1+\omega|\textbf{p}|^{2})(\textbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{f}_{\textbf{p}q})+\beta|\textbf{p}|^{2}g_{\textbf{p}q}],$ (2.11) $\textbf{v}_{\textbf{p}q}=-\Delta^{-1}[-iq(1+\omega|\textbf{p}|^{2})\textbf{p}g_{\textbf{p}q}+\beta\textbf{p}(\textbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{f}_{\textbf{p}q})].$ For $\Delta(\textbf{p},q)=0$ , according to the second equation of ( 2.9 ) we have $\textbf{v}_{\textbf{p}q}=\frac{-i\beta\textbf{p}\eta_{\textbf{p}q}+i\textbf{p}g_{\textbf{p}q}}{q(1+\omega|\textbf{p}|^{2})},$ then it follows from the first equation of ( 2.9 ) that $-\eta_{\textbf{p}q}\left(q^{2}(1+\omega|\textbf{p}|^{2})^{2}-\beta^{2}|\textbf{p}|^{2}\right)=iq(1+\omega|\textbf{p}|^{2})\textbf{p}\cdot\textbf{f}_{\textbf{p}q}+\beta|\textbf{p}|^{2}g_{\textbf{p}q}.$ Hence, based on $q^{2}(1+\omega|\textbf{p}|^{2})^{2}-\beta^{2}|\textbf{p}|^{2}=0$ , i.e. $q(1+\omega|\textbf{p}|^{2})-\beta|\textbf{p}|=0$ , we have the compatibility condition for the system ( 2.9 ) in the form $iq(1+\omega|\textbf{p}|^{2})\textbf{p}\cdot\textbf{f}_{\textbf{p}q}+\beta|\textbf{p}|^{2}g_{\textbf{p}q}=0,$ or (2.12) $|\mathbf{p}|g_{\textbf{p}q}+i\textbf{p}\cdot\textbf{f}_{\textbf{p}q}=0.$ If such condition is satisfied, one has the solution of ( 2.9 ) as, (2.13) $\displaystyle\eta_{\textbf{p}q}=c|\mathbf{p}|+\frac{1}{\beta}g_{\textbf{p}q}=c\sqrt{p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}}+\frac{1}{\beta}g_{\textbf{p}q},$ (2.14) $\displaystyle\textbf{v}_{\textbf{p}q}=-ic\mathbf{p}=-ic(p_{1},p_{2})$ where $c$ is arbitrary in $\mathbb{C}$ . For $q>0$ , the linearized operator $\mathcal{L}$ defined in ( 2.2 ) has a non-trivial kernel if there exists a pair of $(\textbf{p},q)$ satisfying $\Delta(\textbf{p},q)=0$ .
The following lemmas provide the detail information on $(\mathbf{p},q)$ when a kernel exists. Lemma 2.1 . Given $\beta>0$ , let $p_{1}:=k_{1}n_{1}+k_{2}n_{2},\quad p_{2}:=\tau_{1}k_{1}n_{1}-\tau_{2}k_{2}n_{2},$ then the set $\Omega_{\beta}:=\{(n_{1},n_{2},q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}^{+},q\left(1+\omega(p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2})\right)-\beta\sqrt{p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}}=0\}$ is either empty or finite. Proof. For $\beta$ positive, $(n_{1},n_{2},q)\in\Omega_{\beta}$ implies $q=\frac{\beta\sqrt{p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}}}{1+\omega(p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2})}\leq\frac{\beta}{\frac{1}{\sqrt{p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}}}+\omega\sqrt{p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}}}\leq\frac{\beta}{2\sqrt{\omega}}.$ Hence, the only possible values are $q=1,2,...,[\frac{\beta}{2\sqrt{\omega}}]$ . For each $q$ such that $(n_{1},n_{2},q)\in\Omega_{\beta}$ , it implies (2.15) $\omega q\left(\sqrt{p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}}\right)^{2}-\beta\left(\sqrt{p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}}\right)+q=0.$ Thus, there are at most two possible values of $\sqrt{p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}}$ . Now, for given $K>0$ , we consider the number of pairs for $(n_{1},n_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}$ satisfying: (2.16) $K=p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}=(k_{1}n_{1}+k_{2}n_{2})^{2}+(\tau_{1}k_{1}n_{1}-\tau_{2}k_{2}n_{2})^{2}.$ • If $n_{1}\cdot n_{2}\geq 0$ , according to ( 2.16 ) and $(n_{1},n_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}$ , one has $(k_{1}|n_{1}|+k_{2}|n_{2}|)^{2}\leq K,$ which follows that the number of pairs of $(n_{1},n_{2})$ is finite. • If $n_{1}\cdot n_{2}<0$ , according to ( 2.16 ) and $(n_{1},n_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}$ , one has $(\tau_{1}k_{1}|n_{1}|+\tau_{2}k_{2}|n_{2}|)^{2}\leq K,$ which follows that the number of pairs of $(n_{1},n_{2})$ is finite. Therefore, for each value of $\sqrt{p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}}$ (which is finite), we have finite choices of $(n_{1},n_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}$ . The proof is now complete.
∎ We now study the norm of the operator $\mathcal{L}$ . Lemma 2.2 . Given $\beta>0$ ,
there exists a constant $M>0$ , depending only on $\beta$ , such that for any $(n_{1},n_{2},q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}^{+}$ , $(n_{1},n_{2},q)\notin\Omega_{\beta}$ with $(p_{1},p_{2})$ defined in ( 2.8 ), we have $\frac{\sqrt{p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}}}{\left|q(1+\omega(p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}))-\beta\sqrt{p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}}\right|}\leq M.$ Proof. We first consider the pairs $(n_{1},n_{2},q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}^{+}$ with $(n_{1},n_{2},q)\notin\Omega_{\beta}$ satisfying $q\sqrt{p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}}>2\beta/\omega$ , then $q(1+\omega(p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}))-\beta\sqrt{p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}}\geq q+\beta\sqrt{p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}}>0,$ therefore, $\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}}}{\left|q(1+\omega(p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}))-\beta\sqrt{p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}}\right|}\leq\frac{\sqrt{p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}}}{q+\beta\sqrt{p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}}}\leq\frac{1}{\beta}.$ Next, we consider the case for $(n_{1},n_{2},q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}^{+}$ , $(n_{1},n_{2},q)\notin\Omega_{\beta}$ and $(n_{1},n_{2})\neq(0,0)$ satisfies (2.17) $q\sqrt{p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}}\leq 2\beta/\omega.$ We claim that, in such a case, there are finite pairs of $(n_{1},n_{2},q)$ . (A): We notice that $\sqrt{p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}}=0$ implies that $(n_{1},n_{2})=(0,0)$ . According to ( 2.8 ), $p_{1}=k_{1}n_{1}+k_{2}n_{2}=0,\quad p_{2}=\tau_{1}k_{1}n_{1}-\tau_{2}k_{2}n_{2}=0$ and $\begin{bmatrix}k_{1}&k_{2}\\
\tau_{1}k_{1}&\tau_{2}k_{2}\end{bmatrix}=-(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2})k_{1}k_{2}\neq 0,$ it follows that the only solution is the trivial solution. Therefore, $\sqrt{p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}}>0$ for any $(n_{1},n_{2})\neq(0,0)$ . (B): There are finite $(n_{1},n_{2})$ satisfying ( 2.17 ) for any $q\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}$ . This can be seen by noticing $q\geq 1$ and $\sqrt{p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}}\leq\frac{\beta}{2\omega}\cdot\frac{1}{q}\leq\frac{\beta}{2\omega},$ along with the process in ( 2.16 ). The proof of the claim now follows from combining $q\leq\frac{\beta}{2\omega}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}}},\quad q\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}$ since $\sqrt{p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}}>0$ for $(n_{1},n_{2})\neq(0,0)$ (see (A)) and the conclusion in (B).
Because the number of pairs of $(n_{1},n_{2},q)$ is finite and the denominator $\left|q(1+\omega(p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}))-\beta\sqrt{p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}}\right|\neq 0,$ due to $(n_{1},n_{2},q)\notin\Omega_{\beta}$ , one can obtain the upper bound, $M$ , depending on $\beta$ . Finally, for $(n_{1},n_{2},q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}^{+}$ , $(n_{1},n_{2})=(0,0)$ and $(n_{1},n_{2},q)\notin\Omega_{\beta}$ , we can check the bound is $0$ . The proof is now complete.

Lemma 2.3 . Given positive numbers $\beta$ , if $\Omega_{\beta}$ has finite elements and $\mathcal{T}$ is defined as in ( 2.19 )-( 2.23 ), then the operator $\mathcal{T}$ is bounded from $H^{s}_{\natural,e}\times H^{s}_{\natural,o}\times H^{s}_{\natural,o}$ to $H^{s}_{\natural,e}\times H^{s}_{\natural,o}\times H^{s}_{\natural,o}$ for any $s\geq 0$ . Proof. According to ( 2.20 ) and ( 2.21 ) for $\Delta(\mathbf{n},q)\neq 0$ and $q\neq 0$ , we have $\displaystyle|\eta_{\textbf{n}q}|+|\textbf{v}_{\textbf{n}q}|\leq$ $\displaystyle\frac{C\left(\left|q(p_{1},p_{2})\right|(1+\omega(p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}))+\beta(p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2})\right)}{q^{2}\left(1+\omega(p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2})\right)^{2}-\beta^{2}(p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2})}(|g_{\textbf{n}q}|+|\textbf{f}_{\textbf{n}q}|)$ $\displaystyle\leq$ $\displaystyle\frac{C\sqrt{p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}}}{\left|q(1+\omega(p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}))-\beta\sqrt{p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}}\right|}(|g_{\textbf{n}q}|+|\textbf{f}_{\textbf{n}q}|).$ Then, it follows from Lemma 2.2 $|\eta_{\textbf{n}q}|+|\textbf{v}_{\textbf{n}q}|\leq CM(|g_{\textbf{n}q}|+|\textbf{f}_{\textbf{n}q}|).$ The proof of case in ( 2.22 ) and ( 2.23 ) can also be readily checked.

Theorem 2.4 . Assume that $\beta_{0}$ is such that $\Omega_{\beta_{0}}$ have finite elements $(n_{1}^{(j)},n_{2}^{(j)},q^{(l)})$ in $\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}^{+}$ with $j=1,2,...,N_{1}$ and $l=1,2..,N_{2}$ .
