Toronto, ON M5S 2E4, Canada Email address: oh@math.toronto.edu Abstract. We consider the local well-posedness problem of a one-parameter family of coupled KdV-type systems
both in the periodic and non-periodic setting.
In particular, we show that certain resonances occur, closely depending on the value of a coupling parameter $\alpha$ when $\alpha\neq 1$ .
In the periodic setting, we use the Diophantine conditions to characterize the resonances,
and establish sharp local well-posedness of the system in $H^{s}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}),s\geq s^{\ast}$ ,
where $s^{\ast}=s^{\ast}(\alpha)\in(\frac{1}{2},1]$ is determined by the Diophantine characterization of certain constants derived from the coupling parameter $\alpha$ .
We also present a sharp local (and global) result in $L^{2}(\mathbb{R})$ .
In the appendix, we briefly discuss the local well-posedness result in $H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda})$ for $\alpha=1$ without the mean 0 assumption,
by introducing the vector-valued $X^{s,b}$ spaces. Key words and phrases: KdV; well-posedness; ill-posedness; bilinear estimate; Diophantine condition 2000 Mathematics Subject Classification 35Q53 1. Introduction In this paper, we consider the local well-posedness (LWP) in both periodic and non-periodic settings
of coupled KdV systems of the form: (1) $\begin{cases}u_{t}+a_{11}u_{xxx}+a_{12}v_{xxx}+b_{1}uu_{x}+b_{2}uv_{x}+b_{3}u_{x}v+b_{4}vv_{x}=0\\
v_{t}+a_{21}u_{xxx}+a_{22}v_{xxx}+b_{5}uu_{x}+b_{6}uv_{x}+b_{7}u_{x}v+b_{8}vv_{x}=0\\
(u,v)\big|_{t=0}=(u_{0},v_{0}),\end{cases}$ where $A=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a_{11}&a_{12}\\
a_{21}&a_{22}\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ is self-adjoint and non-singular, and $u$ and $v$ are real-valued functions.
There are several systems of this type: the Gear-Grimshaw system [ 13 ] , the Hirota-Satsuma system [ 14 ] ,
the Majda-Biello system [ 20 ] , etc.
Now, write $A$ as $A=M^{-1}DM$ where $D=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}d_{1}&0\\
0&d_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\big)$ with $d_{j}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}$ and $M$ is orthogonal.
Then, by letting $M\big(\begin{smallmatrix}u\\
v\end{smallmatrix}\big)(x,d_{1}^{-1}t)\mapsto\big(\begin{smallmatrix}u\\
v\end{smallmatrix}\big)(x,t)$ ,
one can reduce ( 1 ) to (2) $\begin{cases}u_{t}+u_{xxx}+\widetilde{b_{1}}uu_{x}+\widetilde{b_{2}}uv_{x}+\widetilde{b_{3}}u_{x}v+\widetilde{b_{4}}vv_{x}=0\\
v_{t}+\alpha v_{xxx}+\widetilde{b_{5}}uu_{x}+\widetilde{b_{6}}uv_{x}+\widetilde{b_{7}}u_{x}v+\widetilde{b_{8}}vv_{x}=0\\
(u,v)\big|_{t=0}=(u_{0},v_{0}),\end{cases}$ where $\alpha=\frac{d_{2}}{d_{1}}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}$ , $(x,t)\in\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}\times\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}$ ,
with $\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}=[0,2\pi\lambda)$ for some $\lambda>0$ .
Note that we do not consider the case $\alpha=0$ (i.e. $d_{2}=0$ ) or $\alpha=\pm\infty$ (i.e. $d_{1}=0$ ),
since ( 2 ) is not dispersive in those cases. When $\alpha=1$ , the basic techniques developed for the LWP of KdV by Kenig-Ponce-Vega [ 16 ] can be applied to ( 2 )
as discussed below, thus yielding the LWP of ( 2 ) in $H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda})\times H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda})$ and $H^{-\frac{3}{4}+}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{-\frac{3}{4}+}(\mathbb{R})$ .
However, when $\alpha\neq 1$ , we show that there is an interval $I_{0}$ such that particular resonances occur for $\alpha\in I_{0}\setminus\{1\}$ and that the regularities for the LWP in both periodic and non-periodic settings
need to be much higher than those for $\alpha=1$ .
In particular,
we use the Diophantine conditions
to quantify this regularity in the periodic setting. As a model example, we consider the local well-posedness (LWP) problem of the following system: (3) $\begin{cases}u_{t}+u_{xxx}+vv_{x}=0\\
v_{t}+\alpha v_{xxx}+(uv)_{x}=0\\
(u,v)\big|_{t=0}=(u_{0},v_{0}),\end{cases}\ (x,t)\in\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}\times\mathbb{R}\text{ or }\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R},$ where $\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}=[0,2\pi\lambda)$ with $\lambda\geq 1$ , $0<\alpha\leq 1$ , and $u$ and $v$ are real-valued functions.
We consider the Cauchy problem ( 3 ) with $(u_{0},v_{0})\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda})$ or $H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R})$ .
While we state and prove our results only for $0<\alpha\leq 1$ for the physical reason mentioned below,
we discuss the corresponding results for other values of $\alpha\neq 0$ .
In particular, we have $I_{0}$ = $(0,4]$ for ( 3 ).
See Remark 4.6 . The system ( 3 ) has been proposed by Majda and Biello [ 20 ] as a reduced asymptotic model to study the nonlinear resonant interactions of
long wavelength equatorial Rossby waves and barotropic Rossby waves with a significant mid-latitude projection,
in the presence of suitable horizontally and vertically sheared zonal mean flows.
We henceforth refer to ( 3 ) as the Majda-Biello System .
In [ 20 ] , the values of $\alpha$ are numerically determined and they are $0.899$ , $0.960$ , and $0.980$ for different equatorial Rossby waves.
Of particular interest to us is the periodic case because of more challenging mathematical nature of the periodic problem
as well as the physical relevance of the proposed model (the spatial period for the system before scaling is set as $40,000$ km in [ 20 ] .) First, we review the recent well-posedness results for the Korteweg-de Vries (KdV) equation: (4) $\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{t}+u_{xxx}+uu_{x}=0\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\in H^{s}{(\mathbb{T})}\text{ or }H^{s}{(\mathbb{R})}.\end{array}\right.$ Bourgain [ 8 ] proved the LWP in $L^{2}(\mathbb{T})$ and $L^{2}(\mathbb{R})$ via the contraction mapping principle.
Kenig-Ponce-Vega [ 16 ] improved Bourgain’s result and established the LWP in $H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T})$ and $H^{-\frac{3}{4}+}(\mathbb{R})$ .
Colliander-Keel-Staffilani-Takaoka-Tao [ 11 ] proved the corresponding global well-posedness results via the $I$ -method.
More recently, Christ-Colliander-Tao [ 10 ] proved the LWP in $H^{-\frac{3}{4}}(\mathbb{R})$ via the modified Miura transform and the LWP of the modified KdV,
and
Kappeler-Topalov [ 15 ] proved the global well-posedness of the KdV in $H^{-1}(\mathbb{T})$ ,
using the complete integrability of the equation. (See [ 11 ] and the references therein.) In this paper, we use the contraction mapping principle to show the LWP of ( 3 ), following the basic arguments in [ 8 ] , [ 16 ] , [ 11 ] .
Before discussing how the KdV theory immediately yields the LWP of ( 3 ) (and ( 2 )) when $\alpha=1$ ,
we list some basic properties of the Majda-Biello system ( 3 ).
Several conservation laws are known for the system: $E_{1}=\int u\,dx,\quad E_{2}=\int v\,dx,\quad E_{3}=\int u^{2}+v^{2}dx,\quad E_{4}=\frac{1}{2}\int u_{x}^{2}+\alpha v_{x}^{2}-uv^{2}dx,$ where $E_{4}$ is the Hamiltonian of the system. No other conservation laws
seem apparent; whether the Majda-Biello system ( 3 ) is completely integrable is unknown.
Also, the system has scaling which is similar to that of KdV and the critical Sobolev index $s_{c}$ is $-\frac{3}{2}$ just like KdV. In [ 8 ] , Bourgain introduced a new weighted space-time Sobolev space $X^{s,b}$ whose norm is given by (5) $\|u\|_{X^{s,b}(Z\times\mathbb{R})}=\big\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\langle\tau-\xi^{3}\rangle^{b}\widehat{u}(\xi,\tau)\big\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\tau}(Z^{\ast}\times\mathbb{R})},$ where $\langle\>\cdot\>\rangle=1+|\cdot|$ , and the spatial Fourier domain $Z^{\ast}=\mathbb{R}$ if the spatial domain $Z=\mathbb{R}$ and $Z^{\ast}=\mathbb{Z}/\lambda$ if $Z=\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}$ .
Kenig-Ponce-Vega [ 16 ] proved that the sharp bilinear estimate (6) $\|\partial_{x}(uv)\|_{X^{s,b-1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|u\|_{X^{s,b}}\|v\|_{X^{s,b}}$ holds for $s>-\frac{3}{4}$ and $b=\frac{1}{2}+$ .
Let $S(t)=e^{-t\partial_{x}^{3}}$ and $\eta(t)$ be a smooth cutoff function supported on $[-2,2]$ whose value is 1 on $[-1,1]$ .
Then, along with the linear estimates [ 18 ] : $\begin{cases}\vphantom{\bigg|}\|\eta(t)S(t)u_{0}\|_{X^{s,b}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{x}(\mathbb{R})}\\
\Big\|\eta(t)\int_{0}^{t}S(t-t^{\prime})N\big(u(t^{\prime})\big)dt^{\prime}\Big\|_{X^{s,b}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\left\|N(u)\right\|_{X^{s,b-1},}\end{cases}$ the bilinear estimate ( 6 ) and scaling establish the LWP of KdV ( 4 ) in $H^{s}(\mathbb{R})$ for $s>-\frac{3}{4}$ .
Clearly, the same argument applies to the non-periodic Majda-Biello system ( 3 ) when $\alpha=1$ .
Hence, when $\alpha=1$ , ( 3 ) is locally well-posed in $H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R})$ for $s>-\frac{3}{4}$ . In the periodic case, we have a weaker linear estimate on the Duhamel term [ 8 ] , [ 11 ] : $\left\|\eta(t)\int_{0}^{t}S(t-t^{\prime})N\big(u(t^{\prime})\big)dt^{\prime}\right\|_{X^{s,\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}\times\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\left\|N(u)\right\|_{Z^{s}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}\times\mathbb{R}),}$ where $\|u\|_{Z^{s}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}\times\mathbb{R})}=\|u\|_{X^{s,-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}\times\mathbb{R})}+\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\langle\tau-\xi^{3}\rangle^{-1}\widehat{u}(\xi,\tau)\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}L^{1}_{\tau}(\mathbb{Z}/\lambda\times\mathbb{R})}.$ This is due to the fact that $b=\frac{1}{2}$ .
Strictly speaking, we need to use $\|u\|_{Y^{s}}=\|u\|_{X^{s,\frac{1}{2}}}+\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\widehat{u}(\xi,\tau)\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}L^{1}_{\tau}}$ ,
instead of $X^{s,\frac{1}{2}}$ norm,
in order to control the $C([-T,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}))$ norm of solutions.
However, this modification does not cause a serious problem,
and
we use $X^{s,\frac{1}{2}}$ norm for simplicity in the current discussion.
The crucial bilinear estimate for the LWP of KdV is then: (7) $\|\partial_{x}(uv)\|_{Z^{s}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}\times\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\lambda^{0+}\|u\|_{X^{s,\frac{1}{2}}}\|v\|_{X^{s,\frac{1}{2}}.}$ Assuming the mean 0 condition on $u$ and $v$ , it is shown in [ 16 ] and [ 11 ] that the bilinear estimate
( 7 )
holds for $s\geq-\frac{1}{2}$ with $b=\frac{1}{2}$ and fails for any $b\in\mathbb{R}$ if $s<-\frac{1}{2}$ .
A key ingredient is the algebraic identity (8) $\xi^{3}-\xi_{1}^{3}-\xi_{2}^{3}=3\xi\xi_{1}\xi_{2},\qquad\rm{for}\qquad\xi=\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}.$ When $\alpha=1$ , the same estimates can be applied to the periodic Majda-Biello system ( 3 ) with the mean 0 assumption.
Hence, along with the conservation of the means $E_{1}$ and $E_{2}$ , it follows that, when $\alpha=1$ , ( 3 ) is locally well-posed in $H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda})\times H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda})$ for the mean 0 initial conditions.
We address the LWP theory without the mean 0 assumption in the appendix. Now, let’s turn to the case $0<\alpha<1$ .
In this case, we have two linear semigroups $S(t)=e^{-t\partial_{x}^{3}}$ and $S_{\alpha}(t)=e^{-\alpha t\partial_{x}^{3}}$ corresponding to the linear equations for $u$ and $v$ ,
respectively.
Thus, the Fourier transform of the solutions to the linear equation for $u$ is supported on $\{\tau=\xi^{3}\}$ and
that to the linear equation for $v$ is supported on $\{\tau=\alpha\xi^{3}\}$ .
Since $\alpha\neq 1$ , they are supported on distinct curves, and this causes nontrivial resonance interactions
which are not present when $\alpha=1$ .
It turns out that the Cauchy problem ( 3 ) is locally well-posed in $H^{s}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda})$ for $s\geq s^{\ast}=s^{\ast}(\alpha)$ , where $s^{\ast}(\alpha)$ ranges over $(1/2,1]$ depending on the value of the coupling parameter $\alpha$ .
In order to precisely quantify $s^{\ast}$ in terms of $\alpha$ , we need to go through some preliminaries. For $\alpha\neq 1$ , we need to define two distinct $X^{s,b}$ spaces.
For $s,b\in\mathbb{R}$ , let $X^{s,b}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}\times\mathbb{R})$ and $X_{\alpha}^{s,b}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}\times\mathbb{R})$ be the completion of
the Schwartz class $\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})$ with respect to the norms (9) $\displaystyle\|u\|_{X^{s,b}(\mathbb{T_{\lambda}}\times\mathbb{R})}$ $\displaystyle=\big\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\langle\tau-\xi^{3}\rangle^{b}\widehat{u}(\xi,\tau)\big\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\tau}(\mathbb{Z/\lambda}\times\mathbb{R})}$ (10) $\displaystyle\|v\|_{X_{\alpha}^{s,b}(\mathbb{T_{\lambda}}\times\mathbb{R})}$ $\displaystyle=\big\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\langle\tau-\alpha\xi^{3}\rangle^{b}\widehat{v}(\xi,\tau)\big\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\tau}(\mathbb{Z/\lambda}\times\mathbb{R})}.$ The study of the periodic Majda-Biello system ( 3 ) will be based on the iteration in the spaces $X^{s,\frac{1}{2}}\times X_{\alpha}^{s,\frac{1}{2}}$ . For the following argument, assume $\lambda=1$ for simplicity.
(For general $\lambda\geq 1$ , the implicit constants in ( 11 ) and ( 12 ) below depend on $\lambda$ .
See Propositions 3.7 and 3.8 .)
From the standard estimates on the Duhamel terms, the LWP of ( 3 ) follows once we establish the following bilinear estimates: (11) $\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}(v_{1}v_{2})\|_{X^{s,b-1}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})}$ $\displaystyle\lesssim\|v_{1}\|_{X_{\alpha}^{s,b}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})}\|v_{2}\|_{X_{\alpha}^{s,b}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})}$ (12) $\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}(uv)\|_{X_{\alpha}^{s,b-1}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})}$ $\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{X^{s,b}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})}\|v\|_{X_{\alpha}^{s,b}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}).}$ First, consider the first bilinear estimate ( 11 ).
As in the KdV case [ 8 ] , [ 16 ] , [ 11 ] , we define the bilinear operator $\mathcal{B}_{s,b}(\cdot,\cdot)$ by $\mathcal{B}_{s,b}(f,g)(\xi,\tau)=\frac{\xi\langle\xi\rangle^{s}}{\langle\tau-\xi^{3}\rangle^{1-b}}\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}=\xi}\intt_{\tau_{1}+\tau_{2}=\tau}\frac{f(\xi_{1},\tau_{1})g(\xi_{2},\tau_{2})}{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{s}\langle\xi_{2}\rangle^{s}\langle\tau_{1}-\alpha\xi_{1}^{3}\rangle^{b}\langle\tau_{2}-\alpha\xi_{2}^{3}\rangle^{b}}d\tau_{1}.$ Then, ( 11 ) holds if and only if (13) $\left\|\mathcal{B}_{s,b}(f,g)\right\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\tau}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\tau}}\|g\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\tau}}.$ As in the KdV case, $\partial_{x}$ appears on the left hand side of ( 11 ) and thus
we need to make up for this loss of derivative from $\langle\tau-\xi^{3}\rangle^{1-b}\langle\tau_{1}-\alpha\xi_{1}^{3}\rangle^{b}\langle\tau_{2}-\alpha\xi_{2}^{3}\rangle^{b}$ in the denominator.
Recall that we basically gained $\frac{3}{2}$ derivatives in the KdV case (at least for $b=\frac{1}{2}$ with $\xi,\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\neq 0$ )
thanks to the algebraic identity ( 8 ).
However, when $\alpha\neq 1$ , we no longer have such an identity and
we have $\displaystyle\max\big(\langle\tau$ $\displaystyle-\xi^{3}\rangle,\langle\tau_{1}-\alpha\xi_{1}^{3}\rangle,\langle\tau_{2}-\alpha\xi_{2}^{3}\rangle\big)\sim\langle\tau-\xi^{3}\rangle+\langle\tau_{1}-\alpha\xi_{1}^{3}\rangle+\langle\tau_{2}-\alpha\xi_{2}^{3}\rangle$ (14) $\displaystyle\gtrsim\big|(\tau-\xi^{3})-(\tau_{1}-\alpha\xi_{1}^{3})-(\tau_{2}-\alpha\xi_{2}^{3})\big|=|\xi^{3}-\alpha\xi_{1}^{3}-\alpha\xi_{2}^{3}|,$ where $\xi=\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}$ and $\tau=\tau_{1}+\tau_{2}$ .
Note that the last expression in ( 14 ) can be 0 for infinitely many (nonzero) values of $\xi,\>\xi_{1},$ and $\xi_{2}$ ,
causing resonances .
By solving the resonance equation : (15) $\xi^{3}-\alpha\xi_{1}^{3}-\alpha\xi_{2}^{3}=0\text{ with }\xi=\xi_{1}+\xi_{2},$ we have $(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=(c_{1}\xi,c_{2}\xi)$ or $(c_{2}\xi,c_{1}\xi)$ , where (16) $c_{1}=\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{\sqrt{-3+12\alpha^{-1}}}{6}\ \text{ and }\ c_{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{\sqrt{-3+12\alpha^{-1}}}{6}.$ Note that $c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{R}$ (with $\ c_{1}+c_{2}=1$ )
if and only if $0<\alpha\leq 4.$ If $c_{1}\in\mathbb{Q}$ (and thus $c_{2}\in\mathbb{Q}$ ), then there are infinitely many values of $\xi\in\mathbb{Z}$ such that $c_{1}\xi,\>c_{2}\xi\in\mathbb{Z}$ . This causes resonances for infinitely many values of $\xi$ , and
thus we do not expect any gain of derivative from $\langle\tau-\xi^{3}\rangle\langle\tau_{1}-\alpha\xi_{1}^{3}\rangle\langle\tau_{2}-\alpha\xi_{2}^{3}\rangle$ in this case. If $c_{1}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}$ , then $c_{1}\xi$ is not an integer for any $\xi\in\mathbb{Z}$ .
Thus, $\xi-\alpha\xi_{1}^{3}-\alpha\xi_{2}^{3}\neq 0$ for any $\xi,\>\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}$ .
However, generally speaking, $\xi-\alpha\xi_{1}^{3}-\alpha\xi_{2}^{3}$ can be arbitrarily close to 0,
since $c_{1}\xi$ can be arbitrarily close to an integer.
Therefore, we need to measure how “close” $c_{1}$ is to rational numbers .
We’ll use the following definition regarding the Diophantine conditions commonly used in dynamical systems. Definition 1 (Arnold [ 2 ] ) . A real number $\rho$ is called of type $(K,\nu)$ ( or simply of type ${\nu}$ ) if there exist positive $K$ and $\nu$ such that
for all pairs of integers $(m,n)$ , we have (17) $\left|\rho-\frac{m}{n}\right|\geq\frac{K}{|n|^{2+\nu}}.$ Also, for our purpose, we define the minimal type index of a given real number $\rho$ . Definition 2 . Given a real number $\rho$ , define the minimal type index ${\nu_{\rho}}$ of ${\rho}$ by $\nu_{\rho}=\begin{cases}\infty\text{, if }\rho\in\mathbb{Q}\\
\inf\{\nu>0:\rho\text{ is of type }\nu\}\text{, if }\rho\notin\mathbb{Q}\end{cases}$ Remark 1.1 . Then, by Dirichlet Theorem [ 2 , p.112] and [ 2 , p.116, lemma 3] ,
it follows that $\nu_{\rho}\geq 0$ for any $\rho\in\mathbb{R}$ and $\nu_{\rho}=0$ for almost every $\rho\in\mathbb{R}$ . Using the minimal type index of $c_{1}$ , we prove, in Propositions 3.7 and 3.9 ,
the bilinear estimate ( 11 )
holds for $s\geq\min(1,\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\nu_{c_{1}}+)$ with $b=\frac{1}{2}$ ,
and fails for $b\in\mathbb{R}$ if $s<\min(1,\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\nu_{c_{1}})$ . Let us now turn to the second bilinear estimate ( 12 ).
In this case, the resonance equation is given by (18) $\alpha\xi^{3}-\xi_{1}^{3}-\alpha\xi_{2}^{3}=0\text{ with }\xi=\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}.$ By solving ( 18 ), we obtain $(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=\big(d_{1}\xi,(1-d_{1})\xi\big),\big(d_{2}\xi,(1-d_{2})\xi\big),(0,\xi)$ , where (19) $d_{1}=\tfrac{-3\alpha+\sqrt{3\alpha(4-\alpha)}}{2(1-\alpha)}\ \text{ and }\ d_{2}=\tfrac{-3\alpha-\sqrt{3\alpha(4-\alpha)}}{2(1-\alpha)}.$ Note that $d_{1},d_{2}\in\mathbb{R}$ if and only if $\alpha\in[0,1)\cup(1,4]$ . Using the minimal type indices $\nu_{d_{1}}$ and $\nu_{d_{2}}$ , we prove, in Propositions 3.8 and 3.10 ,
the bilinear estimate ( 12 )
holds for $s\geq\min(1,\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\max(\nu_{d_{1}},\nu_{d_{2}})+)$ with $b=\frac{1}{2}$ under the mean 0 assumption on $u$ ,
and fails for any $b\in\mathbb{R}$ if $s<\min(1,\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\max(\nu_{d_{1}},\nu_{d_{2}}))$ .
Note that we need to take the maximum of $\nu_{d_{1}}$ and $\nu_{d_{2}}$ since we have $d_{1}+d_{2}\notin\mathbb{Q}$ in general, unlike $c_{1}+c_{2}=1$ . Finally, we state the main local well-posedness result.
Let $s_{0}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\max(\nu_{c_{1}},\nu_{d_{1}},\nu_{d_{2}})$ .
Note that $s_{0}=\frac{1}{2}$ for almost every $\alpha\in(0,1)$ in view of Remarks 1.1 and 3.12 . Theorem 1 . Let $0<\alpha<1$ and $\lambda\geq 1$ . Assume the mean 0 condition on $u_{0}$ .
Then, the Majda-Biello system ( 3 ) is locally well-posed in $H^{s}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda})$ for $s\geq s^{\ast}(\alpha):=\min(1,s_{0}+).$ In particular, for almost every $\alpha\in(0,1)$ , the Majda-Biello system is locally well-posed in $H^{s}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda})$ for $s>\frac{1}{2}$ .
Moreover, the (real-valued) Majda-Biello system is globally well-posed
in $H^{1}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda})\times H^{1}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda})$ due to the conservation of the Hamiltonian. This result is sharp in the following sense.
Consider the solution map $\Phi_{t}:(u_{0},v_{0})\in H^{s}\times H^{s}\longmapsto\big(u(t),v(t)\big)\in H^{s}\times H^{s}$ for $|t|\ll 1$ .
Then, we have the following necessary conditions on the Sobolev index $s$ (This kind of result is often referred to as ill-posedness results. c.f. [ 17 ] , [ 26 ] .) Theorem 2 . (a) If $\max(\nu_{c_{1}},\nu_{d_{1}},\nu_{d_{2}})>1$ and the solution map is $C^{2}$ , then $s\geq 1.$ In particular, this result applies to the case when any of $c_{1},d_{1},$ or $d_{2}$ is a rational number. (b) If $\max(\nu_{c_{1}},\nu_{d_{1}},\nu_{d_{2}})\leq 1$ and the solution map is $C^{3}$ , then $s\geq s_{0}$ . Therefore, if we require that the solution map is smooth (at least $C^{3}$ ), then the LWP theory on $\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}$ is complete
(except for the endpoint case $s=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\max(\nu_{c_{1}},\nu_{d_{1}},\nu_{d_{2}})$ when $\max(\nu_{c_{1}},\nu_{d_{1}},\nu_{d_{2}})<1$ which leads to a number-theoretic question. See Remark 4.4 .) We’d like to point out the following.
On the one hand the regularity index $s^{\ast}(\alpha)$ for the local well-posedness is $\frac{1}{2}+$ for almost every $\alpha\in(0,1)$ .
On the other hand, for any interval $I\subset(0,1)$ , there exists $\alpha\in I$ such that $s^{\ast}(\alpha)=1$ .
This shows that the well-posedness (below $H^{1}$ ) of the periodic Majda-Biello system ( 3 )
is very unstable under a slightest perturbation of the parameter $\alpha$ .
Also, note that $\alpha=1$ is a special value since ( 3 ) is LWP for $s\geq-\frac{1}{2}$ when $\alpha=1$ . Lastly, note that we have stated Theorem 1 on $\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}$ for $\lambda\geq 1$ since in proving the global well-posedness on $\mathbb{T}$ (i.e. $\lambda=1$ ) via the I-method,
we need to have LWP for $\lambda\geq 1$ due to the scaling argument. See [ 22 ] .
However, Theorem 1 itself holds on $\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}$ for any $\lambda>0$ as long as $\lambda$ is fixed. Now, let’s discuss the LWP in the non-periodic setting for $0<\alpha<1$ .
In this case, the LWP of ( 3 ) follows once we prove the bilinear estimates: (20) $\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}(v_{1}v_{2})\|_{X^{s,b-1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}$ $\displaystyle\lesssim\|v_{1}\|_{X_{\alpha}^{s,b}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|v_{2}\|_{X_{\alpha}^{s,b}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}$ (21) $\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}(uv)\|_{X_{\alpha}^{s,b-1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}$ $\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{X^{s,b}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|v\|_{X_{\alpha}^{s,b}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.$ As in the periodic case, we obtain two resonance equations $\xi^{3}-\alpha\xi_{1}^{3}-\alpha\xi_{2}^{3}=0$ and $\alpha\xi^{3}-\xi_{1}^{3}-\alpha\xi_{2}^{3}=0$ with $\xi=\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}$ ,
giving rise to $c_{1}$ , $d_{1}$ , and $d_{2}$ .
Since the spatial Fourier variable $\xi$ is not discrete in this case,
the rational/irrational character of $c_{1},d_{1},$ and $d_{2}$ is irrelevant.
In Propositions 5.3 and 5.4 ,
we prove the sharp bilinear estimates ( 20 ) and ( 21 ) for $s\geq 0$ with some $b=\frac{1}{2}+$ .

More importantly, while the resonance for $\alpha=1$ makes the bilinear estimate ( 25 )

∎ 6. On the Ill-posedness Results There are also -so called- “ill-posedness” results for dispersive equations such as the KdV equation ( 4 ).
However, this term often refers to the necessary conditions for uniform continuity or smoothness of the solution map $\Phi_{t}:u_{0}\in H^{s}\longmapsto u(t)\in H^{s}$ .
The Cauchy problem is not necessarily ill-posed in the sense of the usual definition, even when these results hold.
However, since the contraction argument provides smoothness of the solution map
it is often natural to consider a strengthened notion of
well-posedness requiring the solution map to be uniformly continuous/smooth.
In this latter sense, the following results may be regarded as “ill-posedness” results. For KdV, Bourgain [ 9 ] proved that if the solution map is $C^{3}$ , then $s\geq-\frac{3}{4}$ on $\mathbb{R}$ and $s\geq-\frac{1}{2}$ on $\mathbb{T}$ .

then $s\geq-\frac{3}{4}$ on $\mathbb{R}$ and $s\geq-\frac{1}{2}$ on $\mathbb{T}$ .
These results are obtained from the corresponding ill-posedness results of mKdV
and the (modified) Miura transform, which is not available for the Majda-Biello system ( 3 ).
