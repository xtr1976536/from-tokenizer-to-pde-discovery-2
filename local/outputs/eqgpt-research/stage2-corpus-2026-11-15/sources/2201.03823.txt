We are concerned with the barotropic compressible Navier–Stokes system
in a bounded domain of $\mathbb{R}^{d}$ (with $d\geq 2$ ). In a critical regularity setting ,
we establish local well-posedness
for large data with no vacuum and global well-posedness for small
perturbations of a stable constant equilibrium state. Our results rely on new maximal regularity estimates - of independent interest - for the
semigroup of the Lamé operator, and of
the linearized compressible Navier–Stokes equations. 1. Introduction We are concerned with the following barotropic compressible Navier–Stokes system in a $\mathcal{C}^{\infty}$ bounded domain $\Omega$ of $\mathbb{R}^{d}$ , $d\geq 2$ : (1.1) $\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho+\divergence(\rho u)=0\quad&\hbox{in }\ \mathbb{R}_{+}\times\Omega,\\
&\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\divergence(\rho u\otimes u)-2\divergence(\mu D(u))-\nabla(\lambda\divergence u)+\nabla P=0&\quad\hbox{in }\ \mathbb{R}_{+}\times\Omega,\\
&u=0&\quad\hbox{on }\ \mathbb{R}_{+}\times\partial\Omega,\\
&(\rho,u)|_{t=0}=(\rho_{0},u_{0})&\quad\hbox{in }\ \Omega.\end{aligned}\right.$ The unknowns are the (scalar nonnegative) density $\rho=\rho(t,x)$ and the vector-field $u=u(t,x).$ The notation $D(u)$ stands for the symmetric part of the Jacobian matrix of $u.$ The viscosity coefficients $\lambda$ and $\mu$ are smooth functions
of $\rho$ satisfying $\mu>0$ and $\lambda+2\mu>0.$ We shall often assume (with no loss of generality)
that the average value of the density on $\Omega,$ a conserved quantity, is equal to $1.$ The mathematical study of the Cauchy problem (or initial boundary value problem)
for the compressible Navier–Stokes system has been initiated sixty years
ago with the pioneering works by J. Serrin [ 32 ] and J. Nash [ 31 ] who established the local-in-time existence and uniqueness of classical solutions.
In the case $\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{3},$ the global existence of strong solutions
with Sobolev regularity has been first proved by A. Matsumura and T. Nishida [ 25 ] , for small perturbations
of a constant state $(\rho,u)=(\bar{\rho},0)$ under the stability condition $P^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})>0.$ The proof was based on subtle energy estimates that enabled the authors
to pinpoint some $\mathrm{L}^{2}$ -in-time integrability for both the density and the velocity,
as well as algebraic time decay estimates. With completely different methods based on parabolic maximal regularity in the framework of Lebesgue spaces, local existence has been established by V. Solonnikov [ 33 ] for general data with no vacuum (see also the more recent work by
the first author [ 8 ] where critical regularity is almost achieved)
as well as global existence for small perturbations of $(\bar{\rho},0)$ (see [ 29 ] , and [ 21 ] ). In the present paper, we want to recover the classical results of strong solutions
for ( 1.1 ) in the bounded domain case within a critical regularity setting , that is,
in functional spaces that are invariant by the following rescaling for all $\ell>0$ : (1.2) $\bigl(\rho_{0}(x),u_{0}(x)\bigr)\leadsto\bigl(\rho_{0}(\ell x),\ell u_{0}(\ell x)\bigr)\quad\hbox{and}\quad\bigl(\rho(t,x),u(t,x)\bigr)\leadsto\bigl(\rho(\ell^{2}t,\ell x),\ell u(\ell^{2}t,\ell x)\bigr)\cdotp$ Observe that the above rescaling leaves the whole system
invariant, up to a change of the pressure term (provided the fluid domain is dilated accordingly,
of course). As first noticed by H. Fujita and T. Kato in [ 17 ] for the incompressible
Navier–Stokes equations, working in scaling invariant spaces is the key to getting
optimal well-posedness results. Our main goal here is to prove the following type of statements: • local well-posedness for general data having critical regularity
and such that $\rho_{0}>0$ ; • if, in addition, $P^{\prime}(1)>0,$ global well-posedness
for data $(\rho_{0},u_{0})$ that are small perturbations of $(1,0)$ (for some norm
having the invariance of the first part of ( 1.2 )). When the fluid domain is the whole space,
a number of results in that spirit have been
established, and the critical norms are always
built upon homogeneous Besov spaces with last index equal to $1$ . More precisely, it has been first observed in [ 7 ] that one can take any data such that $\rho_{0}-1$ is small in $\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{d/2-1}_{2,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{d/2}_{2,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),$ and $u_{0}$ is small in $\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{d/2-1}_{2,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).$ Later works (see, e.g., [ 5 ] , [ 6 ] )
pointed out that it is actually enough to assume the high frequencies of
the data to be in the larger space $\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{d/p}_{p,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{d/p-1}_{p,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})$ for some $p$ in the range $\bigl(2,\min(4,\frac{2d}{d-2})\bigr)\cdotp$ Here we aim at extending those results to the
physically relevant case where the fluid domain is bounded and the velocity
vanishes at the boundary. Compared to works in the whole space,
the expected difficulty is that one can no longer
use techniques based on the Fourier transform to investigate ( 1.1 )
(in particular, global results of [ 7 ] were based on a decomposition
into low and high frequencies of the solution).
Whether one can adapt those techniques to the domain case
is unclear.
In the present paper, we focus on the bounded domain case which is expected
to be easier than the unbounded domain case since, somehow,
low frequencies do not exist
(therefore, prescribing different regularity for low and high frequencies is irrelevant). Since the linearized compressible Navier–Stokes system may be associated to
an analytic semigroup in suitable functional spaces, using maximal $\mathrm{L}^{q}$ -regularity
seems to be an acceptable substitute to Fourier analysis. However, as already pointed out
in previous works (see, e.g., [ 8 ] ), reaching critical regularity
within the classical theory would require maximal $\mathrm{L}^{1}$ -regularity ,
which is false in the setting of Lebesgue or
Sobolev spaces for instance. For the reader’s convenience, let us briefly recall what maximal regularity is.
Let $X$ be a Banach space and $-A:\mathcal{D}(A)\subset X\to X,$ the generator of a bounded analytic semigroup $(T(t))_{t\geq 0}$ on $X.$ Consider for $f\in\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X)$ , $1\leq q\leq\infty$ , the abstract Cauchy problem $\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} u^{\prime}(t)+Au(t)&=f(t)\qquad(t>0),\\
u(0)&=0.\end{aligned}\right.$ By virtue of [ 4 , Prop. 3.1.16] the unique mild solution to this problem is given by the variation of constants formula $\displaystyle u(t)=\int_{0}^{t}T(t-\tau)f(\tau)\;\mathrm{d}\tau\qquad(t>0).$ We say that $A$ has maximal $\mathrm{L}^{q}$ -regularity if, for every $f\in\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X),$ it holds for almost every $t>0$ that $u(t)\in\mathcal{D}(A),$ and $Au\in\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X)$ . Notice that in this case also $u^{\prime}\in\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X)$ and that the closed graph theorem implies the existence of a constant $C>0$ such that for all $f\in\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X)$ it holds $\displaystyle\|u^{\prime},Au\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X)}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X)}.$ See the monographs of Denk, Hieber, and Prüss [ 13 ] and of Kunstmann and Weis [ 23 ] for further information. Our aim here is to adapt an argument of real interpolation that originates from
Da Prato-Grisvard’s work in [ 12 ] so as to
reach the endpoint $q=1$ that turns out to be the key to proving global-in-time
results in critical regularity framework (in this respect, see also our recent paper [ 11 ] ). We perform the analysis first for the semigroup associated to the Lamé operator
(namely the linearization of the velocity equation if neglecting the pressure term),
so as to get a local well-posedness result for general data
with critical regularity, then for the linearization of the whole system ( 1.1 ) about $(\rho,u)=(1,0)$ to obtain a global result. Back to the nonlinear system, one cannot just push all nonlinear terms
to the right-hand side and bound them according to Duhamel’s formula, though.
The troublemaker is the convection
term in the density equation, namely $u\cdot\nabla\rho,$ that causes
a loss of one derivative (this reflects the fact that the system under
consideration is partly hyperbolic). The way to overcome the difficulty is well-known:
it is called Lagrangian coordinates. Indeed, if rewriting
( 1.1 ) in Lagrangian coordinates, then one just has to consider
the evolution equation for the velocity which is of parabolic type.
Therefore, not only the loss of derivative may be avoided,
but also the solution may be obtained (either locally for large data, or globally for small data)
by means of the contraction mapping argument in Banach spaces. Let us now come to the main results of the paper. Theorem 1.1 . Assume that $\Omega$ is a smooth bounded domain of $\mathbb{R}^{d}$ ( $d\geq 2$ ) and let $p$ be in $(d-1,2d).$ Then, for all initial densities $\rho_{0}\in\mathrm{B}^{d/p}_{p,1}(\Omega),$ positive and bounded away from zero, and all $u_{0}\in\mathrm{B}^{d/p-1}_{p,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}),$ System ( 1.1 ) admits a unique solution $(\rho,u)$ on some nontrivial time interval $[0,T],$ such that $(\rho,u)\in\mathcal{C}_{b}([0,T];\mathrm{B}^{d/p}_{p,1}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{B}^{d/p-1}_{p,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}))$ and $(\rho,u)\in\mathrm{W}^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathrm{B}^{{d}/{p}}_{p,1}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{B}^{{d}/{p}-1}_{p,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}))\cap\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathrm{B}^{{d}/{p}}_{p,1}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{B}^{{d}/{p}+1}_{p,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}))\cdotp$ Furthermore, $\inf_{(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\Omega}\>\rho(t,x)>0$ and the average of $\rho$ is time independent. Proving a global result for small perturbations of a stable constant state
is based on maximal regularity estimates for the linearized compressible Navier–Stokes system
(where $\mu^{\prime}=\lambda+\mu$ ): (1.3) $\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}a+\divergence u=f\quad&\hbox{in }\ \mathbb{R}_{+}\times\Omega,\\
&\partial_{t}u-\mu\Delta u-\mu^{\prime}\nabla\divergence u+\nabla a=g&\quad\hbox{in }\ \mathbb{R}_{+}\times\Omega,\\
&u=0&\quad\hbox{on }\ \mathbb{R}_{+}\times\partial\Omega,\\
&(a,u)|_{t=0}=(a_{0},u_{0})&\quad\hbox{in }\ \Omega.\end{aligned}\right.$ The following statement extends the work by
P.B. Mucha and W. Zaja̧czkowski [ 29 ] to the endpoint case where
the time Lebesgue exponent is equal to $1,$ and also provides exponential decay for the solutions of the system. Theorem 1.2 . Take initial data $(a_{0},u_{0})$ in $\mathrm{B}^{s+1}_{p,1}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})$ and source terms $(f,g)$ in $\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathrm{B}^{s+1}_{p,1}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}))$ with $(s,p)$ satisfying $1<p<\infty\quad\hbox{and}\quad\max\biggl(\frac{1}{p},\frac{d}{p}-\frac{d}{2}\biggr)-1<s<\frac{1}{p}\cdotp$ Assume also that the average of $a_{0}$ and of $f(t)$ (for a.e. $t>0$ ) is zero.
Then, System ( 1.3 ) has a unique global
solution $(a,u)$ in the space (1.4) $\displaystyle\mathrm{E}_{p}^{s}:=\mathrm{W}^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathrm{B}^{s+1}_{p,1}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}))\cap\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathrm{B}^{s+1}_{p,1}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{B}^{s+2}_{p,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}))\cdotp$ Additionally, there exist two positive constants $c$ and $C$ depending only on $p,$ $\Omega,$ $\mu$ , and $\mu^{\prime}$ such that if $\mathrm{e}^{ct}(f,g)\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathrm{B}^{s+1}_{p,1}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}))$ , then (1.5) $\|\mathrm{e}^{ct}(a,u)\|_{\mathrm{E}^{s}_{p}}\leq C\biggl(\|(a_{0},u_{0})\|_{\mathrm{B}^{s+1}_{p,1}\times\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,1}}+\|\mathrm{e}^{ct}(f,g)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathrm{B}^{s+1}_{p,1}\times\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,1})}\biggr)\cdotp$ After recasting System ( 1.1 ) in Lagrangian coordinates, combining the above
result with nonlinear estimates allows to get the following global well-posedness result
for critical regularity: Theorem 1.3 . Let $\Omega,$ $p$ , and $d$ be as in Theorem 1.1 and assume in addition that $P^{\prime}(1)>0.$ Let $\rho_{0}\in\mathrm{B}^{d/p}_{p,1}(\Omega)$ with average $1$ and $u_{0}\in\mathrm{B}^{d/p-1}_{p,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}).$ There exists a constant $\alpha=\alpha(\lambda,\mu,p,d,P,\Omega)>0$ such that if $a_{0}:=\rho_{0}-1$ and $u_{0}$ satisfy (1.6) $\|a_{0}\|_{\mathrm{B}^{d/p}_{p,1}}+\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{B}^{d/p-1}_{p,1}}\leq\alpha,$ then System ( 1.1 ) admits a unique global solution $(\rho,u)$ with $(a,u):=(\rho-1,u)$ in the maximal regularity space $\mathrm{E}_{p}:=\mathrm{E}_{p}^{d/p-1}.$ In addition, there exists $c>0$ depending only on the parameters of the system,
on $p$ , and on $\Omega$ such that $(a,u)$ fulfills: $\|\mathrm{e}^{ct}(a,u)\|_{\mathrm{E}_{p}}\leq C\bigl(\|a_{0}\|_{\mathrm{B}^{d/p}_{p,1}}+\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{B}^{d/p-1}_{p,1}}\bigr)\cdotp$ The rest of the paper unfolds as follows.
The next two sections are dedicated to the “linear study” namely
proving maximal regularity results first for the Lamé operator,
and next for the linearized compressible Navier–Stokes system.
In Section 5 , we prove our main global existence result.
In Section 6 , we establish
local existence results with no smallness condition on the velocity,
first in the easy case where the initial density is close to a constant
and, next, assuming only that the density is bounded away from zero.
Some technical results are recalled/proved in Appendix. Acknowledgement The authors have been partially supported by ANR-15-CE40-0011. 2. Some background from semigroup theory We use this section to introduce the basic functional analytic notions and arguments that are crucial for the theory that is developed afterwards. Let $X$ denote a Banach space over the complex field. For $\theta\in(0,\pi)$ define the sector $\mathrm{S}_{\theta}$ in the complex plane $\displaystyle\mathrm{S}_{\theta}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}:\lvert\arg(z)\rvert<\theta\},$ and set $\mathrm{S}_{0}:=(0,\infty)$ . The standard definition of (bounded) analytic semigroups reads: Definition 2.1 . A family $(T(z))_{z\in\mathrm{S}_{\theta}\cup\{0\}}\subset{\mathcal{L}}(X)$ , $\theta\in(0,\pi/2]$ , is called an analytic semigroup of angle $\theta$ if (1) $T(0)=\Id$ and $T(z+w)=T(z)T(w)$ for all $z,w\in\mathrm{S}_{\theta}$ ; (2) the map $z\mapsto T(z)$ is analytic in $\mathrm{S}_{\theta}$ ; (3) $\lim_{\mathrm{S}_{\vartheta}\ni z\to 0}T(z)x=x$ for all $x\in X$ and all $0<\vartheta<\theta$ . If in addition (4) $\|T(z)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(X)}$ is bounded in $\mathrm{S}_{\vartheta}$ for all $0<\vartheta<\theta$ , the family $(T(z))_{z\in\mathrm{S}_{\theta}\cup\{0\}}$ is called a bounded analytic semigroup . To any analytic semigroup of some angle $\theta\in(0,\pi/2],$ one can attach a unique operator $A:\mathcal{D}(A)\subset X\to X$ defined by $\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(A):=\Big\{x\in X:\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{t}(T(t)x-x)\text{ exists}\Big\}$ and, for $x\in\mathcal{D}(A),$ $\displaystyle Ax:=-\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{t}(T(t)x-x).$ The operator $-A$ is called the generator of $(T(z))_{z\in\mathrm{S}_{\theta}\cup\{0\}}.$ Combining ( 1 ) and ( 2 ) one readily sees that the range of $T(z)$ is contained in $\mathcal{D}(A)$ for any $z\in\mathrm{S}_{\theta}$ and that the function $u:[0,\infty)\to X$ given by $u(t):=T(t)x$ solves the abstract Cauchy problem (2.1) $\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} u^{\prime}(t)+Au(t)&=0&&t>0,\\
u(0)&=x.\end{aligned}\right.$ From the PDE perspective, one can wonder if, whenever $A:\mathcal{D}(A)\subset X\to X$ is
a given linear operator, $-A$ is the generator of an analytic semigroup.
At this point, we need to recall the notion of a sectorial operator. Definition 2.2 . A linear operator $B:\mathcal{D}(B)\subset X\to X$ is called sectorial of angle $\omega$ for some $\omega\in[0,\pi)$ if its spectrum satisfies $\sigma(B)\subset\overline{\mathrm{S}_{\omega}}$ and if for all $\omega<\omega^{\prime}<\pi$ there exists $C>0$ such that $\displaystyle\|\lambda(\lambda-B)^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(X)}\leq C\qquad(\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathrm{S}_{\omega^{\prime}}}).$ The following characterization theorem for analytic semigroups is classical [ 15 , Thm. II.4.6] . Theorem 2.3 . Let $A:\mathcal{D}(A)\subset X\to X$ be a linear operator.
Then $-A$ is the generator of an analytic semigroup if and only if $A$ is densely defined and there exists $z\in\mathbb{C}$ such that $z+A$ is sectorial of some angle $\omega\in[0,\pi/2)$ . Moreover, $-A$ generates a bounded analytic semigroup if and only if additionally one can choose $z=0$ , i.e., $A$ itself is sectorial of angle $\omega\in[0,\pi/2)$ . Remark 2.4 . The condition that $z+A$ is sectorial of angle $\omega\in[0,\pi/2)$ is equivalent to the fact that there exists $R>0$ such that $\sigma(-A)\subset\overline{\mathrm{S}_{\omega}}\cup B(0,R)$ and such that $\displaystyle\|\lambda(\lambda+A)^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(X)}\leq C\qquad(\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[\overline{\mathrm{S}_{\omega^{\prime}}}\cup B(0,R)]).$ Remark 2.5 . If $-A$ generates a bounded analytic semigroup and if $0\in\rho(A)$ , then the corresponding semigroup is exponentially decaying. Indeed, as $A$ is sectorial of angle $\omega\in[0,\pi/2)$ and as the resolvent set is open, one finds that $\displaystyle\inf_{\lambda\in\sigma(A)}\operatorname{Re}(\lambda)>0.$ Thus, there exists $\varepsilon>0$ and $\omega^{\prime}\in[0,\pi/2)$ such that $A-\varepsilon$ is sectorial of angle $\omega^{\prime}$ which implies that the semigroup generated by $\varepsilon-A$ is bounded. This in turn implies the exponential decay of the semigroup generated by $-A$ . To solve nonlinear equations, it is helpful to consider ( 2.1 ) for a homogeneous initial value but for an inhomogeneous right-hand side of the first equation, i.e., (2.2) $\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} u^{\prime}(t)+Au(t)&=f(t)&&t\in(0,T),\\
u(0)&=0,\end{aligned}\right.$ where $0<T\leq\infty$ and $f\in\mathrm{L}^{q}(0,T;X)$ , $1\leq q\leq\infty$ . As recalled in the introduction, a densely defined operator $A:\mathcal{D}(A)\subset X\to X$ is said to have maximal $\mathrm{L}^{q}$ -regularity if there exists a constant $C>0$ such that for all $f\in\mathrm{L}^{q}(0,T;X),$ System ( 2.2 ) has a unique solution $u$ that satisfies $u(t)\in\mathcal{D}(A)$ for almost all $t\in(0,T),$ is almost everywhere differentiable and such that $\displaystyle\|u^{\prime},Au\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(0,T;X)}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(0,T;X)}.$ It is classical, see, e.g., Dore [ 14 , Cor. 4.4] , that the maximal $\mathrm{L}^{q}$ -regularity of $A$ implies that $-A$ generates an analytic semigroup.
Characterizing when a given operator admits maximal $\mathrm{L}^{q}$ -regularity is often a difficult issue, which involves questions on the geometry of Banach spaces and operator-valued multiplier theorems, see [ 13 , 23 ] . However, if one is willing to change the underlying Banach space into a real interpolation space between $X$ and $\mathcal{D}(A)$ , then the question of maximal $\mathrm{L}^{q}$ -regularity simplifies tremendously. It is a classical result of Da Prato and Grisvard [ 12 ] , that is described below. To state the result, we need to introduce the definition of a part of an operator onto another space. Definition 2.6 . Let $X$ and $Y$ be Banach spaces and $C:\mathcal{D}(C)\subset X\to X$ be a linear operator. The part of $C$ in $Y$ is the operator given by $\displaystyle\mathcal{D}({\bf C}):=\{y\in\mathcal{D}(C)\cap Y:Cy\in Y\},\quad{\bf C}y:=Cy.$ Let in the following $B$ denote the time derivative operator on $(0,T)$ , with $0<T\leq\infty$ , i.e., $\displaystyle B:\{u\in\mathrm{W}^{1,q}((0,T);X):u(0)=0\}\subset\mathrm{L}^{q}(0,T;X)\to\mathrm{L}^{q}(0,T;X),\quad u\mapsto u^{\prime}.$ It is well-known, see, e.g., [ 19 , Sec. 8.4-8.6] , that $B$ is sectorial of angle $\pi/2$ . Furthermore, let $A$ be a densely defined and sectorial operator of angle $\omega\in[0,\pi/2)$ , i.e., $-A$ is the generator of a bounded analytic semigroup. We lift the operator $A$ to the time-dependent space by defining $\displaystyle A^{\uparrow}:\mathcal{D}(A^{\uparrow}):=\mathrm{L}^{q}(0,T;\mathcal{D}(A))\subset\mathrm{L}^{q}(0,T;X)\to\mathrm{L}^{q}(0,T;X),\quad[A^{\uparrow}u](t):=Au(t).$ As the operator $A$ does not explicitly depend on time, the resolvents of $A^{\uparrow}$ and $B$ commute, i.e., it holds $\displaystyle(\lambda-A^{\uparrow})^{-1}(\mu-B)^{-1}=(\mu-B)^{-1}(\lambda-A^{\uparrow})^{-1}\qquad(\lambda\in\rho(A),\,\mu\in\rho(B)).$ In this situation, the theorem of Da Prato and Grisvard may be formulated as follows, see [ 12 , Thm. 3.11, Lem. 3.5] : Theorem 2.7 . Let $\theta\in(0,1)$ and $1\leq q\leq\infty$ . With the notation above, the part of the operator $\displaystyle C:=A^{\uparrow}+B\quad\textit{with domain}\quad\mathcal{D}(C):=\mathcal{D}(A^{\uparrow})\cap\mathcal{D}(B)$ in the real interpolation space $\displaystyle\big(\mathrm{L}^{q}(0,T;X),\mathcal{D}(A^{\uparrow})\big)_{\theta,q}=\mathrm{L}^{q}(0,T;(X,\mathcal{D}(A))_{\theta,q})$ is sectorial of angle $\pi/2$ . Furthermore, there exists $M>0$ such that for all $\lambda>0$ and $y\in\mathrm{L}^{q}(0,T;(X,\mathcal{D}(A))_{\theta,q})$ it holds that $A^{\uparrow}(\lambda+A^{\uparrow}+B)^{-1}y\in\mathrm{L}^{q}(0,T;(X,\mathcal{D}(A))_{\theta,q})$ and $B(\lambda+A^{\uparrow}+B)^{-1}y\in\mathrm{L}^{q}(0,T;(X,\mathcal{D}(A))_{\theta,q})$ and that $\hfil\displaystyle\quad\|A^{\uparrow}(\lambda+A^{\uparrow}+B)^{-1}y\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(0,T;(X,\mathcal{D}(A))_{\theta,q})}+\|B(\lambda+A^{\uparrow}+B)^{-1}y\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(0,T;(X,\mathcal{D}(A))_{\theta,q})}\hfill$ $\hfil\displaystyle\hfill\leq M\|y\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(0,T;(X,\mathcal{D}(A))_{\theta,q})}.\quad$ The application of this theorem to the situation of maximal regularity is as follows. By construction, the solution operator to ( 2.2 ) is given by $\displaystyle(A^{\uparrow}+B)^{-1},$ so that the question of whether $A$ has maximal $\mathrm{L}^{q}$ -regularity is about whether $A^{\uparrow}+B$ is invertible and whether $\displaystyle A^{\uparrow}(A^{\uparrow}+B)^{-1}\quad\text{and}\quad B(A^{\uparrow}+B)^{-1}$ are bounded. We present how to derive these properties by means of the theorem of Da Prato and Grisvard, for operators $A$ that are additionally invertible. Notice that the invertibility of $A$ implies the invertibility of $A^{\uparrow}$ . By the argument in Remark 2.5 , there exists $\varepsilon>0$ such that $A^{\uparrow}-\varepsilon$ is sectorial of angle less than $\pi/2$ as well. Applying Theorem 2.7 to $C:=(A^{\uparrow}-\varepsilon)+B$ shows that there exists $K>0$ such that $\displaystyle\|B(A^{\uparrow}+B)^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(\mathrm{L}^{q}(0,T;(X,\mathcal{D}(A))_{\theta,q}))}=\|B(\varepsilon+C)^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(\mathrm{L}^{q}(0,T;(X,\mathcal{D}(A))_{\theta,q}))}\leq K$ and $\displaystyle\|A^{\uparrow}(A^{\uparrow}$ $\displaystyle+B)^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(\mathrm{L}^{q}(0,T;(X,\mathcal{D}(A))_{\theta,q}))}+\|(A^{\uparrow}+B)^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(\mathrm{L}^{q}(0,T;(X,\mathcal{D}(A))_{\theta,q}))}$ $\displaystyle\leq\|(A^{\uparrow}-\varepsilon)(\varepsilon+C)^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(\mathrm{L}^{q}(0,T;(X,\mathcal{D}(A))_{\theta,q}))}+(1+\varepsilon)\|(\varepsilon+C)^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(\mathrm{L}^{q}(0,T;(X,\mathcal{D}(A))_{\theta,q}))}$ $\displaystyle\leq K.$ This shows that there exists a constant $K>0$ such that whenever $f\in\mathrm{L}^{q}(0,T;(X,\mathcal{D}(A))_{\theta,q})$ , the equation ( 2.2 ) has a unique solution $u$ satisfying (2.3) $\displaystyle\|u,u^{\prime},Au\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(0,T;(X,\mathcal{D}(A))_{\theta,q})}\leq K\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(0,T;(X,\mathcal{D}(A))_{\theta,q})}.$ In later sections, we will in particular be interested in the case $q=1$ . We conclude this section, by shortly discussing how to extend this theory to include inhomogeneous initial values in ( 2.2 ) if $q=1$ .
We have to investigate under which conditions on $x$ the function $t\mapsto AT(t)x$ lies in $\mathrm{L}^{1}(0,T;(X,\mathcal{D}(A))_{\theta,1})$ . Now, we use that the real interpolation space $(X,\mathcal{D}(A))_{\theta,q}$ can be characterized by means of the semigroup $(T(t))_{t\geq 0}$ . Study of the Lamé operator This section is dedicated to the study of the linearization of the velocity equation
of System ( 1.1 ), when neglecting the pressure.
We shall first establish various
regularity results for the Lamé operator $L$ given by (3.1) $L=-\mu\Delta-z\nabla\divergence,$ then look at the properties of the associated semigroup, with particular attention to the maximal $\mathrm{L}^{q}$ -regularity

Throughout the section, $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}$ , $d\geq 1$ , is a smooth bounded domain.
The Lebesgue exponent $p$ is supposed to satisfy $1<p<\infty$ , the microlocal parameter $q$ satisfies $1\leq q\leq\infty$ , and we assume that the real number $s$ is such that (3.2) $\displaystyle-1+\frac{1}{p}<s<\frac{1}{p}\cdotp$ Recall that ( 3.2 ) ensures that elements of $\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,q}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})$ have no trace at the boundary. As a start, let us record the standard $\mathrm{L}^{2}$ -theory of the Lamé operator, following the exposition in [ 27 ] . Let $Du$ denote the Jacobian matrix of a vector field $u,$ and let $\nabla u$ denote its transpose. Define the $\curl$ of $u$ by $\displaystyle\curl u:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\nabla u-Du).$ Let $\mu>0$ and $z\in\mathbb{C}$ and define the sesquilinear form (3.3) $\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}:\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathrm{W}^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})\times\mathrm{W}^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})&\longrightarrow\mathbb{C},\\

Thus, since $T:X_{p}^{0}\to X^{-1}_{p}$ is an isomorphism, it holds $\displaystyle\|\lambda(\lambda+\mathcal{L}_{p})^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(X^{-1}_{p})}$ $\displaystyle=\|\lambda T\Phi^{-1}(\Phi(\lambda+L_{p})^{-1}\Phi^{-1})\Phi T^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(X^{-1}_{p})}$ $\displaystyle\leq C\|\lambda\Phi(\lambda+L_{p})^{-1}\Phi^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(X^{0}_{p})}\leq C.$ Then, by real interpolation we derive that for all $1<p<\infty$ , $1\leq q\leq\infty$ , and $-1<s<0$ there exists $C>0$ such that for all $\lambda\in\mathrm{S}_{\theta}$ it holds (3.12) $\displaystyle\|\lambda(\lambda+\mathcal{L}_{p})^{-1}|_{X^{s}_{p,q}}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(X^{s}_{p,q})}\leq C.$ Finally, we prove that $\rho(\mathcal{L}_{p})\subset\rho({\bf L}_{p,q,s})$ and that $(\lambda+\mathcal{L}_{p})^{-1}|_{X^{s}_{p,q}}=(\lambda+{\bf L}_{p,q,s})^{-1}$ holds for $\lambda\in\rho(-\mathcal{L}_{p})$ . Let $\lambda\in\rho(-\mathcal{L}_{p})$ . Clearly $\lambda+{\bf L}_{p,q,s}$ inherits the injectivity of $\lambda+\mathcal{L}_{p}$ . For the surjectivity, let $\mathfrak{f}\in X^{s}_{p,q}$ . Since $\lambda\in\rho(-\mathcal{L}_{p})$ , there exists $\mathfrak{u}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L}_{p})=X^{0}_{p}$ such that $(\lambda+\mathcal{L}_{p})\mathfrak{u}=\mathfrak{f}$ . Since $X^{0}_{p}\hookrightarrow X^{s}_{p,q}$ , the definition of the part of an operator now implies that $\mathfrak{u}\in\mathcal{D}({\bf L}_{p,q,s})$ and that $(\lambda+{\bf L}_{p,q,s})\mathfrak{u}=\mathfrak{f}$ . Consequently, this together with ( 3.12 ) implies that $-{\bf L}_{p,q,s}$ generates a bounded analytic semigroup on $X^{s}_{p,q}$ . In the case $0<s<1$ this follows immediately by the characterization in ( 2.4 ) and the fact that $-L_{p}$ generates a bounded analytic semigroup on $\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})$ , see Proposition 3.4 . The final case $s=0$ follows by interpolation.

Let $\eta\in\mathbb{C}^{d}$ be a unit eigenvector to $\mathcal{S}(\xi)$ with corresponding eigenvalue $\alpha\in\mathbb{C}$ . Then, $\displaystyle\alpha\eta=\mathcal{S}(\xi)\eta=\mu\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}\eta+zM\eta,\quad\hbox{hence}\quad z^{-1}(\alpha-\mu\lvert\xi\rvert^{2})\eta=M\eta.$ Hence, $\eta$ is an eigenvector to $M$ with corresponding eigenvalue $z^{-1}(\alpha-\mu\lvert\xi\rvert^{2})$ . we get ( A.1 ). For $d=2,$ one needs to verify the following supplementary condition. Lemma A.2 . Let $d=2$ and let $\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}$ be linearly independent. Let $f$ be in $\mathrm{B}^{d/p}_{p,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})$ for some $1\leq p\leq\infty.$ Let $\psi$ be a smooth function, supported in the unit ball of $\mathbb{R}^{d}.$ Denote $\psi_{\delta,x_{0}}:=\psi(\delta^{-1}(\cdot-x_{0}))$ for $\delta>0$ and $x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.$ Then, $\lim_{\delta\to 0}\|\psi_{\delta,x_{0}}\>(f-f(x_{0}))\|_{\mathrm{B}^{d/p}_{p,1}}=0\quad\hbox{uniformly with respect to }x_{0}.$ Proof. Let us first establish the result for $g$ a smooth function with bounded derivatives at all order. Let without loss of generality $\delta\in(0,1)$ .
We first notice, owing to the mean value theorem and the fact that $\psi_{\delta,x_{0}}$ is supported in a ball of radius $\delta$ that $\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi_{\delta,x_{0}}(g-g(x_{0}))\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\leq C\|\nabla g\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}}\delta^{1+\frac{d}{p}-\alpha}\quad\hbox{for all }\ \alpha\in\mathbb{N}.$ Next, we see that for any couple $(\beta,\gamma)$ of integers with $\gamma\geq 1,$ $\|\nabla^{\beta}\psi_{\delta,x_{0}}\>\nabla^{\gamma}(g-g(x_{0}))\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\leq C\|\nabla^{\gamma}g\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}}\delta^{\frac{d}{p}-\beta}.$ Consequently, in light of Leibniz formula, for all integer $\alpha$ there exists a constant $C_{\alpha}>0$ depending only on $g$ and such that for all $x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ and $\delta\in(0,1),$ $\|\nabla^{\alpha}(\psi_{\delta,x_{0}}\,(g-g(x_{0})))\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\leq C_{\alpha}\delta^{\frac{d}{p}+1-\alpha}.$ If $\alpha\in\mathbb{N}$ is such that $d/p<\alpha\leq d/p+1$ , the exponent of $\delta$ in the previous inequality is non-negative. Moreover, combining the derived estimates with the following interpolation inequality $\|h\|_{\mathrm{B}^{d/p}_{p,1}}\leq C\|h\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{1-\frac{d}{p\alpha}}\|h\|_{\mathrm{W}^{\alpha,p}}^{\frac{d}{p\alpha}}$ and the assumption that $\delta<1$ yields that there exists a constant $C_{g}>0$ depending only on $g,$ $p$ and $d$ such that (B.1) $\|\psi_{\delta,x_{0}}\,(g-g(x_{0}))\|_{\mathrm{B}^{d/p}_{p,1}}\leq C_{g}\delta^{(1+\frac{d}{p})(1-\frac{d}{p\alpha})}\quad\hbox{for all }\ \delta\in(0,1)\quad\hbox{and}\quad x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.$ Let us now prove the proposition for a general function $f$ in $\mathrm{B}^{d/p}_{p,1}.$ Fix some $\varepsilon>0$ and
take $g$ smooth with bounded derivatives at all order such that $\|f-g\|_{\mathrm{B}^{d/p}_{p,1}}\leq\varepsilon.$ We have $\|\psi_{\delta,x_{0}}\,(f-f(x_{0}))\|_{\mathrm{B}^{d/p}_{p,1}}\leq\|\psi_{\delta,x_{0}}\,(g-g(x_{0}))\|_{\mathrm{B}^{d/p}_{p,1}}+\|\psi_{\delta,x_{0}}\,(f-g)\|_{\mathrm{B}^{d/p}_{p,1}}+|f(x_{0})-g(x_{0})|\|\psi_{\delta,x_{0}}\|_{\mathrm{B}^{d/p}_{p,1}}.$ Using Proposition 5.2 , Inequality ( B.1 ) and the embedding $\mathrm{B}^{d/p}_{p,1}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{\infty},$ we thus have $\|\psi_{\delta,x_{0}}\,(f-f(x_{0}))\|_{\mathrm{B}^{d/p}_{p,1}}\leq C_{g}\delta^{(1+\frac{d}{p})(1-\frac{d}{p\alpha})}+C\|\psi_{\delta,x_{0}}\|_{\mathrm{B}^{d/p}_{p,1}}\|f-g\|_{\mathrm{B}^{d/p}_{p,1}}.$ Using the invariance (up to an harmless constant) of the norm in $\mathrm{B}^{d/p}_{p,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})$ by translation and dilation,
and the definition of $g,$ we end up with $\|\psi_{\delta,x_{0}}\,(f-f(x_{0}))\|_{\mathrm{B}^{d/p}_{p,1}}\leq C_{g}\delta^{(1+\frac{d}{p})(1-\frac{d}{p\alpha})}+C\varepsilon,$ which ensures $\|\psi_{\delta,x_{0}}\,(f-f(x_{0}))\|_{\mathrm{B}^{d/p}_{p,1}}\leq 2C\varepsilon$ provided $\delta$ is small enough.
