Abstract This paper deals with the classical solution of the following chemotaxis system with generalized logistic growth and indirect signal production $\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}u_{t}=\epsilon\Delta u-\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)+ru-\mu u^{\theta},&\\
0=d_{1}\Delta v-\beta v+\alpha w,&\\
0=d_{2}\Delta w-\delta w+\gamma u,&\end{array}\right.$ and the so-called strong $W^{1,q}(\Omega)$ -solution of hyperbolic-elliptic-elliptic model $\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}u_{t}=-\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)+ru-\mu u^{\theta},&\\
0=d_{1}\Delta v-\beta v+\alpha w,&\\
0=d_{2}\Delta w-\delta w+\gamma u,&\end{array}\right.$ in arbitrary bounded domain $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}$ , $n\geq 1$ , where $r,\mu,d_{1},d_{2},\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta>0$ and $\theta>1$ . Via applying the viscosity vanishing method, we first prove that the classical solution of ( Abstract ) will converge to the strong $W^{1,q}(\Omega)$ -solution of ( Abstract ) as $\epsilon\rightarrow 0$ . After structuring the local well-pose of ( Abstract ), we find that the strong $W^{1,q}(\Omega)$ -solution will blow up in finite time with non-radial symmetry setting if $\Omega$ is a bounded convex domain, $\theta\in(1,2]$ , and the initial data is suitable large. Moreover, for any positive constant $M$ and the classical solution of ( Abstract ), if we add another hypothesis that there exists positive constant $\epsilon_{0}(M)$ with $\epsilon\in(0,\ \epsilon_{0}(M))$ , then the classical solution of ( Abstract ) can exceed arbitrarily large finite value in the sense: one can find some points $\left(\tilde{x},\tilde{t}\right)$ such that $u(\tilde{x},\tilde{t})>M$ . Keywords : local well-pose, viscosity vanishing method, chemotaxis, blow-up, damping source, indirect signal production. AMS(2010) Subject Classification : 35B44; 35K55; 35K40; 92C17. 1 Introduction In this paper, we consider following initial boundary problem to chemotaxis system with indirect signal production $\displaystyle{}\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}u_{t}=\epsilon\Delta u-\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)+ru-\mu u^{\theta},&x\in\Omega,\ t>0,\\
0=d_{1}\Delta v-\beta v+\alpha w,&x\in\Omega,\ t>0,\\
0=d_{2}\Delta w-\delta w+\gamma u,&x\in\Omega,\ t>0,\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}=\frac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,\ t>0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0},&x\in\Omega,\end{array}\right.$ where $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}$ is an bounded domain with smooth boundary $\partial\Omega,n\geq 1,\ \frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}$ denotes the derivative with respect to the outer normal on $\partial\Omega$ , a no-flux condition is implied on boundary so that the ecosystem is closed to the exterior environment. The parameters satisfy $\epsilon,r,\mu,d_{1},d_{2},\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta>0,\theta>1.$ (1.7) Initial data $u_{0}$ is positive and satisfy $u_{0}\in W^{1,q}(\Omega),q>n.$ (1.8) Chemotaxis model with indirect signal production mechanism has been proposed in [ 49 ] to describe the spread and aggregative behaviors of the mountain pine beetle (MPB) in a forest habitat considered negligibly thin in its vertical dimension. The model describes the space and time evolutions of the densities of flying MPB, nesting MPB and the concentration of the beetle pheromone, they are represented by $u=u(x,t),w=w(x,t)$ and $v=v(x,t)$ , respectively. Problem ( 1 ) can be viewed as a generalization of the classical Keller-Segel system, and the main feature of the corresponding system is that the signal productions occur in an indirect process, with first $u$ producing the third quantity $w$ and the latter being exclusively responsible for the release of $v$ . 1.1 Background Chemotaxis is a biological phenomenon describing the movement of individuals in the direction of increasing chemical concentration spread in the environment where they reside. The classical Keller-Segel model was proposed in [ 22 ] to describe the aggregation of cellular slime molds Dictyostelium discoideum and in [ 23 ] to describe the wave propagation of bacterial chemotaxis, where the chemical signals inducing a bias in the motion of the species is produced directly by the species itself. However, in a large number of realistic situations the mechanisms of signal production may be substantially more complicated, see for example [ 3 , 4 , 45 , 55 , 58 ] . For following chemotaxis model with indirect signal production mechanism, $\displaystyle{}\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}u_{t}=\Delta u-\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)+f(u),\\
\tau v_{t}=\Delta v-v+w,\\
\tau w_{t}=d\Delta w-\delta w+u,\end{array}\right.$ when $f(u)=0$ , Tao and Winkler [ 52 ] considered the model in the unit disk and radially symmetric setting with $d=0$ and the second equation replaced by $0=\Delta v-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}wdx+w$ , for the solution to this parabolic-elliptic-ODE system they found the mass threshold property refer to blow-up in infinite time rather than in finite time, which is surprisingly different from the case of classical Keller-Segel model. Later on, Laurençot [ 43 ] considered model ( 1.1 ) with $d=0$ in arbitrary two-dimensional bounded domains. The author actually showed that the system possess a Liapunov functional and that the properties of this Liapunov functional provide insight on the boundedness or unboundedness of the solutions. For $d>0$ , Fujie and Senba [ 8 ] studied system ( 1.1 ) with a fully parabolic type indirect signal production mechanism. Unlike the observations obtained in [ 11 , 12 , 14 , 38 , 39 ] for the classical Keller-Segel system and in [ 43 , 52 ] for the corresponding ODE-type indirect signal production system, therein the critical mass phenomenon occurs in the two-dimensional situation, the authors in [ 8 ] found that for the corresponding PDE-type indirect signal production model ( 1.1 ) the four-dimensional setting is the critical case and the critical mass is $m_{c}:=\frac{(8\pi)^{2}}{\chi}$ . Specifically speaking, when $n\leq 3$ one can invoke smoothing effects of diffusion terms of each equation of ( 1.1 ) and derive sufficiently high regularity estimates for solutions guaranteeing global existence from the mass conservation law. However in the four-dimensional setting the smoothing effect does not work enough and solutions remain bounded whenever the domain and initial data are radially symmetric such that $\int_{\Omega}u_{0}<m_{c}$ . Bai and Liu [ 1 ] obtained an extension as well as a simplified proof for this boundedness result. Whereas the system under mixed boundary conditions in a smooth bounded convex domain possesses some solutions which blows up in finite or infinite time provided that $\int_{\Omega}u_{0}>m_{c}$ [ 9 ] . From the mentioned papers we know such indirect signal production mechanisms may delay or prevent the spontaneous singularity formation. It is widely known that the repulsion effect in chemotaxis model actually also benefits the global boundedness of solutions. The following attraction-repulsion chemotaxis system was proposed in [ 36 ] to describe the aggregation of microglia observed in Alzheimer’s disease and in [ 44 ] to describe the quorum effect in the chemotactic process, $\displaystyle{}\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}u_{t}=\Delta u-\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)+\xi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla w)+f(u),&x\in\Omega,\quad t>0,\\
\tau v_{t}=\Delta v+\tilde{\alpha}u-\tilde{\beta}v,&x\in\Omega,\quad t>0,\\
\tau w_{t}=\Delta w+\tilde{\gamma}u-\tilde{\delta}w,&x\in\Omega,\quad t>0.\end{array}\right.$ For the global boundedness results with $n\leq 3$ , one can see [ 19 , 18 , 17 , 34 , 32 , 33 , 53 ] , see also [ 20 ] for the critical mass phenomenon with $n=2$ in an attraction-repulsion chemotaxis system with the attraction dominating case. Tao and Wang [ 53 ] considered problem ( 1.1 ) with multi-dimensional case and $\tilde{\beta}=\tilde{\delta}$ , they studied the global solvability, boundedness, blow-up, existence of non-trivial stationary solutions and asymptotic behavior of the system for various ranges of parameter values. For problem ( 1.1 ) with $\tau=0,\chi\tilde{\alpha}=\xi\tilde{\gamma},\tilde{\beta}\not=\tilde{\delta}$ and $f$ satisfying $f(s)\leq a-bs^{r}\ \ \text{for all}\ \ s\geq 0\ \ \text{with some}\ \ a\geq 0,\ b>0\ \text{and}\ r\geq 1,$ Li and Xiang [ 28 ] obtained that the boundedness of solutions was determined specially under the assumption that $n\geq 2,r>\frac{1}{2}\left(\sqrt{n^{2}+4n}-n+2\right)$ . Thus, owing to the effect of repulsion, the exponent $r$ is allowed to take values less than 2 such that the solution remains uniformly bounded in time. Wang et al. [ 60 ] proved the solution to the corresponding fully parabolic system is globally bounded when $n\leq 3\ \ \text{or}\ \ r>r_{n}:=\left\{\frac{n+2}{4},\ \frac{n\sqrt{n^{2}+6n+17}-n^{2}-3n+4}{4}\right\}\ \ \text{with}\ \ n\geq 2.$ It is clearly $r_{4}=\frac{3}{2}$ . More recently, Li and Wang [ 30 ] found that problem ( 1.1 ) with $\tau=0,\chi\tilde{\alpha}=\xi\tilde{\gamma}$ and $\tilde{\beta}\not=\tilde{\delta}$ possesses a globally bounded and classical solution if $r=\frac{3}{2}$ . There are also many works about problem ( 1.1 ) with $f(u)$ being a damping source term in order to improve the system’s consistency with biological reality or adapt it to complex biological situations. It is well known that an appropriate logistic damping can prevent blow-up of solutions to the classical Keller-Segel system $\displaystyle{}\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}U_{t}=\epsilon\Delta U-\chi\nabla\cdot(U\nabla V)+\tilde{r}U-\tilde{\mu}U^{2},&x\in\Omega,\quad t>0,\\
\kappa V_{t}=\Delta V-V+U,&x\in\Omega,\quad t>0.\end{array}\right.$ When $\kappa=0$ , if either $n\leq 2$ and $\tilde{\mu}>0$ is arbitrary, or if $n\geq 3$ and $\tilde{\mu}$ suitable lager, then for all reasonably regular initial data model ( 1.1 ) possesses a globally bounded classical solution [ 54 ] . Moreover, the effect of logistic damping is stronger than that of chemotactic aggregation when $\tilde{\mu}\geq\frac{n-2}{n}\chi$ [ 21 , 70 , 54 ] . For the case of $\kappa=1$ , Winkler [ 62 ] showed that if $\mu$ is sufficiently large then problem ( 1.1 ) possesses a unique bounded solution, and there are also further progresses in this direction, one can see [ 31 , 69 ] . Especially, for any $\tilde{\mu}>0$ when $n=3$ and the domain $\Omega$ is convex, it is known from [ 26 ] that blow-up is not possible in the weak solution sense. In drastic contrast to ( 1.1 ), Hu and Tao [ 15 ] proved an arbitrarily small quadratic degradation term is sufficient to suppress any blow-up phenomenon in problem ( 1.1 ) when $n=3$ and $d=0$ , they further showed the solution exponentially stabilizes to the constant stationary solution if $\mu$ suitable lager. The boundedness result got in [ 15 ] had been improved in [ 27 ] to a general case, wherein $f(u)=u-u^{\theta}$ is considered and the solution exists bounded when $n\geq 2$ and $\theta>\frac{n}{2}$ . Ren and Liu [ 48 ] proved that the solution is bounded if $n\geq 3$ and $\theta=\frac{n}{2}$ . Lv and Wang [ 37 ] studied a chemotaxis system with signal-dependent motility, indirect signal production and generalized logistic source, and they established the global existence of the solution under some assumptions on the parameters of the logistic source. One can refer to [ 7 , 35 , 41 , 40 , 46 , 47 , 50 , 51 , 75 ] for further extensions of related chemotaxis models involving ODE-type indirect signal production mechanism and damping source. For the PDE-type indirect signal production systems with damping source, Zhang el at. [ 74 ] claimed that if $f(u)=\mu(u-u^{\theta})$ and $\theta>\frac{n}{4}+\frac{1}{2}$ , then the solution is globally bounded. This boundedness conclusion still holds for a chemotaxis system with indirect signal production and rotational sensitivity [ 5 ] . The authors in [ 59 , 73 ] considered the corresponding quasilinear fully parabolic chemotaxis system with indirect signal production and logistic source, and they obtained the global boundedness of solutions under some assumptions on parameters of self-diffusion, cross-diffusion and logistic source. 1.2 Main results Let us return the attention to problem ( 1 ) and give the main results of this paper. From the existing papers as mentioned above, we know the indirect production mechanism is helpful for the global existence of solutions, and it is well known that the damping source is also a main method to avoid the chemotactic collapse. Problem ( 1 ) is provided with these two characteristics at the same time, as far as we know, thus there is no any blow-up result or unbound phenomenon for it. In fact, even for the classical chemotaxis-growth system ( 1.1 ), the analysis of this aspect is incomplete. When $\kappa=0,\tilde{\mu}\in(0,1)$ , Winkler [ 64 ] considered dynamical behaviors of model ( 1.1 ) with $n=\chi=1$ , and he claimed that if $\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}$ is sufficiently large, where $p>\frac{1}{1-\tilde{\mu}}$ , then there exists $T>0$ such that to each $M>0$ there corresponds some $\epsilon_{0}(M)>0$ with the property that for any $\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0}(M))$ one can find $t_{\epsilon}\in(0,T)$ and $x_{\epsilon}\in\Omega$ such that the classically solution satisfies $U(x_{\epsilon},t_{\epsilon})>M.$ (1.20) The authors in [ 25 ] extended this result to higher dimensional and radially symmetric case. In addition, under the radially symmetric assumptions on physical region and initial data we [ 71 ] investigated the chemotaxis models with Lotka-Volterra type competitive species and gained the property ( 1.20 ) for the corresponding solutions recently. For more results on the property like ( 1.20 ) with related fully parabolic chemotaxis models under the radially symmetric assumptions, one can see [ 29 , 57 , 65 , 61 , 72 ] . However, to the best of our knowledge, there is no any result about such transient growth phenomena for problem ( 1 ). In this paper, we thus aim to study this dynamic behavior of solution to problem ( 1 ) with non-radial symmetry assumption. Unlike the outcomes for the classical Keller-Segel system ( 1.1 ) with $\tau=0$ obtained in [ 25 , 64 ] , we shall establish the corresponding conclusions for problem ( 1 ) in a general bound domain, so it is an improvement on the basis of [ 25 , 64 ] . Say concretely, we shall consider problem ( 1 ) with applying the viscosity vanishing method. In this end, we need further study the corresponding hyperbolic-elliptic-elliptic model which may be seen as an auxiliary problem and can be formally obtained by letting $\epsilon\rightarrow 0$ , $\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}=-\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)+ru-\mu u^{\theta},&x\in\Omega,\quad t>0,\\
0=d_{1}\Delta v-\beta v+\alpha w,&x\in\Omega,\quad t>0,\\
0=d_{2}\Delta w-\delta w+\gamma u,&x\in\Omega,\quad t>0,\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}=\frac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,\quad t>0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}&x\in\Omega.\end{array}\right.$ (1.21) Then, we consider the so-called strong $W^{1,q}$ -solution $(u,v,w)$ (see [ 64 ] ) to problem ( 1.21 ), which can be defined as following sense: Definition 1.1 . Let $r,\mu,d_{1},\alpha,\beta,d_{2},\gamma,\delta>0$ and $\theta>1,T^{*}\in(0,+\infty],q>n$ . A strong $W^{1,q}$ -solution of problem ( 1.21 ) in $\Omega\times(0,T^{*})$ is the nonnegative function $u\in C(\bar{\Omega}\times[0,T^{*}))\cap L_{loc}^{\infty}([0,T^{*});W^{1,q}(\Omega))$ and $v,w\in C^{2}(\bar{\Omega}\times[0,T^{*}))$ such that $u$ satisfies $\begin{split}-\int_{0}^{T^{*}}\int_{\Omega}u\varphi_{t}-\int_{\Omega}u_{0}\varphi(\cdot,0)=\int_{0}^{T^{*}}\int_{\Omega}u\nabla v\nabla\varphi+\int_{0}^{T^{*}}\int_{\Omega}(ru-\mu u^{\theta})\varphi\end{split}$ (1.22) for any $\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}([0,T^{*});\bar{\Omega})$ , and $v,w$ classically solves $\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0=d_{1}\Delta v-\beta v+\alpha w,&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T^{*})\\
0=d_{2}\Delta w-\delta w+\gamma u,&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T^{*})\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}=\frac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}=0,&(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,T^{*}).\end{array}\right.$ In fact, with means of a compactness argument we shall prove that the solution of problem ( 1 ) converges to the strong $W^{1,q}$ -solution of ( 1.21 ) as $\epsilon\rightarrow 0$ . More importantly, by means of a logarithmic Sobolev inequality we can built the result in a general physical domain. Specifically, we can ensure that problem ( 1.21 ) is locally well posed in the following sense. Theorem 1.1 . Let $\Omega$ be a bounded region and $u_{0}\in W^{1,q}(\Omega),q>n$ . Then there exists a constant $T^{*}\in(0,+\infty]$ and problem ( 1.21 ) admits an unique strong $W^{1,q}$ -solution $(u,v,w)$ with $u\in C(\bar{\Omega}\times[0,T^{*}))\cap L_{loc}^{\infty}([0,T^{*});W^{1,q}(\Omega))$ and $v,w\in C^{2,0}(\bar{\Omega}\times[0,T^{*}))$ . Moreover, $\mbox{either}\ \ T^{*}=+\infty\ \ \mbox{or}\ \ \limsup_{t\rightarrow T^{*}}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=+\infty,$ and for any $t\in(0,T^{*})$ we have $\begin{split}\beta\int_{\Omega}v(\cdot,t)=\alpha\int_{\Omega}w(\cdot,t)&=\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\delta}\int_{\Omega}u(\cdot,t)\leq\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\delta}m_{1},\end{split}$ (1.24) where $m_{1}:=\max\left\{\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}u_{0},\ \ \left(\frac{r}{\mu}\right)^{\frac{1}{\theta-1}}\right\}.$ (1.25) Although the strong $W^{1,q}$ -solution exists boundedness with $L^{1}(\Omega)$ -norm for all $(0,T^{*})$ and problem ( 1.21 ) possesses the indirect signal production mechanism along with the logistic source, we will prove that there exists still a finite time blow-up phenomenon with $n\geq 1$ . Due to the lack of free diffusion of $u$ , it should be pointed out that the blow-up result for problem ( 1.21 ) holds with non-radial symmetry setting and for all $n\geq 1$ . Concretely, we have following result. Theorem 1.2 . Let $n\geq 1$ , $\Omega$ be a bounded convex region, the parameters satisfy ( 1.7 ) with $\theta\in(1,2]$ . Then one can find suitably large constants $c_{1},c_{2}>0$ with the following property: If the positive initial data $u_{0}\in W^{1,q}(\Omega),q>n$ is suitably large such that $\|u_{0}\|^{\theta}_{L^{\theta}(\Omega)}>c_{1}\cdot\max\left\{1,\ m_{1}^{\theta}\right\}+c_{2},$ (1.26) where $m_{1}$ is the positive constant given by ( 1.25 ), then the strong $W^{1,q}$ -solution of problem ( 1.21 ) blows up in finite time. Remark 1 . To the best of our knowledge, for the chemotaxis systems with both of the ODE- and PDE-types indirect signal production as mentioned above, the existence of finite time blow-up solutions has been still open. For all $n\geq 1$ , Theorem 1.2 provides a finite time blow-up result for problem ( 1.21 ) with non-radial symmetry setting although there exist the logistic source, to a certain degree, this phenomenon reflects how important the free diffusion of $u$ is for the dynamic behavior of the solution to the corresponding chemotaxis model. We then obtain the transient growth phenomena to problem ( 1 ) by means of a continuous dependence argument. Theorem 1.3 . Let $n\geq 1$ , $\Omega$ be a bounded convex region, $M$ be an arbitrary positive constant, the parameters satisfy ( 1.7 ) with $\theta\in(1,2]$ . If there exists a positive constant $\epsilon_{0}(M)$ such that $\epsilon\in(0,\ \varepsilon_{0}(M))$ and the initial data satisfy ( 1.8 ) and ( 1.26 ), then for the classical solution of problem ( 1 ) one can find some points $\left(\tilde{x},\tilde{t}\right)$ such that $u\left(\tilde{x},\tilde{t}\right)>M.$ (1.27) Remark 2 . We mainly focus on the effects of $\epsilon$ and the size of $u_{0}$ on the behaviors of the classical solution of problem ( 1 ), and the main contribution of Theorem 1.3 is that it provide the property ( 1.27 ) to the chemotaxis system with indirect signal production mechanism: In a general convex domain, if the initial data is sufficiently lager with the fixed coefficient, if further the species do not diffuse too fast, i.e. $\epsilon$ is suitable small, then we can make sure the existence of some time up to which any threshold of the species density will be surpassed. Remark 3 . Let us explain the assumption on $\epsilon$ in Theorem 1.3 . In order to give a convergence between the classical solution of problem ( 1 ) and the $W^{1,q}$ -solution of problem ( 1.21 ) (see Lemma 3.2 ), we need a boundedness (independent of $\epsilon$ ) of $u$ in $L^{\infty}([0,T];W^{1,q}(\Omega))$ (see Lemma 2.9 ), in this process we have to give a upper bound on $\epsilon$ to handle the boundary integration. Moreover, as the same requirements in [ 64 , Lemma 3.4] and [ 25 , Lemma 3.10] , an upper bound of $\epsilon$ is also required for problem ( 1 ) to get an uniformly bounded (independent of $\epsilon$ ) estimate for the time derivative. Furthermore, from a biological point of view, this smallness assumption on the free diffusion rate $\epsilon$ obviously will be of benefit to the aggregation behavior of the system. On the other hand, although $\theta\in(1,2]$ , due to the smallness assumption on $\epsilon$ , we can see that the property ( 1.27 ) does not conflicts any boundedness results about problem ( 1 ) mentioned above, since the free diffusion rate considered therein is fixed for a positive constant. Remark 4 . Theorem 1.3 extends the classical Keller-Segel growth-type model considered in [ 25 , 64 ] to problem ( 1 ), and removes the radially symmetric assumptions on $\Omega$ and initial data which were needed in [ 25 , 64 ] . Assume $v(x,t):=\chi z(x,t)-\xi w(x,t),\xi\gamma=\chi\alpha$ , then the following attraction-repulsion chemotaxis system $\displaystyle{}\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}u_{t}=\epsilon\Delta u-\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla z)+\xi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla w)+ru-\mu u^{\theta},&x\in\Omega,\ t>0,\\
0=d_{1}\Delta z+\alpha z-\beta z,&x\in\Omega,\ t>0,\\
0=d_{2}\Delta w+\gamma u-\delta w,&x\in\Omega,\ t>0,\end{array}\right.$ becomes to $\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}u_{t}=\epsilon\Delta u-\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)+ru-\mu u^{\theta},&x\in\Omega,\ t>0,\\
0=d_{1}\Delta v-\beta v+\bar{\alpha}w,&x\in\Omega,\ t>0,\\
0=d_{2}\Delta w-\delta w+\gamma u,&x\in\Omega,\ t>0,\end{array}\right.$ where $\bar{\alpha}=\xi(\frac{d_{1}\delta}{d_{2}}-\beta)$ , so as a by-product, we can see from Theorem 1.3 that the transient growth phenomena is also true for the attraction-repulsion chemotaxis model. Corollary 1.1 . For the classical solution of the corresponding initial-boundary value problem ( 1.2 ), let $n\geq 1$ , $\Omega$ be a bounded convex region, $\theta\in(1,2],\chi,\xi,r,\mu,d_{1},d_{2},\beta,\delta,\gamma>0$ with $\xi\gamma=\chi\alpha,\ \ d_{1}\delta\not=d_{2}\beta,$ if for any positive constant $M$ , there exists a positive constant $\epsilon_{0}(M)$ such that $\epsilon\in(0,\ \varepsilon_{0}(M))$ and the initial data satisfy ( 1.8 ) and ( 1.26 ), in this setting the positive constants $c_{1},c_{2}$ depends on $\chi,\xi,d_{1},d_{2},\beta,\gamma,\delta,\mu,n$ and $\Omega$ , then the property ( 1.27 ) is valid. Remark 5 . Also, like in Remark 3 , in view of the smallness assumption on $\epsilon$ , we point out that property ( 1.27 ) for the attraction-repulsion chemotaxis model does not contradicts any boundedness results about the corresponding problem ( 1.2 ). We would like to mention the results got in [ 63 , 66 ] which convey to us that chemotactic collapses are still possible even in the presence of superlinear growth restrictions. How this singularity property can be constructed for problem ( 1 )? We leave this open problem as a future investigation. The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we give some known results concerning local existence of solution to problem ( 1 ) as well as some useful preliminary Lemmata. In Section 3, we consider the local well-pose for the corresponding hyperbolic-elliptic-elliptic model ( 1.21 ), and then give the proofs of our main theorems. 2 Preliminaries As a preliminary, we state firstly the following local well-posedness of classical solution to problem ( 1 ). As to problem ( 1 ), the local existence and uniqueness of solutions are established by Banach’s fixed point theorem and well-known parabolic regularity theory in the same way as in for example [ 2 , 13 , 62 , 67 ] . Lemma 2.1 . For any parameters satisfy ( 1.7 ) and positive initial value satisfying ( 1.8 ), there exists a $T_{\max}\in(0,+\infty]$ and unique positive classical solution to problem ( 1 ) in $\Omega\times(0,T_{\max})$ . We further have the extensibility criterion: either $T_{\max}=+\infty$ , or $\lim_{t\rightarrow T_{\max}}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=+\infty.$ Throughout the sequel, it will not be mentioned any further that $C>0$ is a generical constant although they can change line by line. We recall following maximal regularity of elliptic problem, which can be found in [ 25 , Lemma 4] , or see [ 6 , 10 ] for its’ detailed proof. Lemma 2.2 . For any $f\in C(\bar{\Omega})$ , the solution $\phi$ of following problem $\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta\phi+\phi=f,&x\in\Omega,\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\nu}=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.$ satisfies $\|\phi\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}$ for all $p\in[1,\infty]$ . Moreover, for any $q\geq 1,\iota>0$ , there is a constant $C_{*}>0$ such that $\|v\|_{W^{2,q}(\Omega)}\leq C_{*}\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}$ and $\|v\|_{C^{2+\iota}(\Omega)}\leq C_{*}\|f\|_{C^{\iota}(\Omega)}$ . Lemma 2.3 . Let $p>1$ and $\eta_{1},\eta_{2}>0$ be three constants, then for any classical solution $(v,w)$ of the problem $\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0=d_{1}\Delta v-\beta v+\alpha w,&x\in\Omega,\\
0=d_{2}\Delta w-\delta w+\gamma u,&x\in\Omega,\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}=\frac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.$ there are two positive constants $C_{1}=C(\eta_{1},p,d_{1},\alpha,\beta),C_{2}=C(\eta_{2},p,d_{2},\delta,\gamma)>0$ such that $\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}v^{p+1}\leq\eta_{1}\int_{\Omega}w^{p+1}+C_{1}\left(\int_{\Omega}w\right)^{p+1},\\
\int_{\Omega}w^{p+1}\leq\eta_{2}\int_{\Omega}u^{p+1}+C_{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}u\right)^{p+1}.\end{split}$ Proof. The conclusion can be proved by the same arguments in [ 64 , Lemma 2.2] or [ 25 , Lemma 2.2] , so we omit the details here.
∎ In order to manage the boundary integration, the following three lemmata are necessary for us, they can be found in [ 16 ] . Lemma 2.4 . Let $\Omega$ be a bounded domain in $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ with smooth boundary. If $\psi\in C^{2}(\bar{\Omega})$ satisfies $\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nu}=0$ , then $\frac{\partial|\nabla\psi|^{2}}{\partial\nu}\leq C_{\Omega}|\nabla\psi|^{2}$ , where $C_{\Omega}>0$ is a constant depending only on the curvatures of $\partial\Omega$ . Lemma 2.5 . Let $\Omega$ be a bounded domain in $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ with smooth boundary and $\tilde{r}>0$ be a constant. Then $W^{\tilde{r},2}(\partial\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\partial\Omega)$ is a compact embedding and there exists a linear and bounded map from $W^{\tilde{r}+\frac{1}{2},2}(\Omega)$ onto $W^{\tilde{r},2}(\partial\Omega)$ . Lemma 2.6 . Let $n\in\mathbb{N}$ and $s,s_{1}\geq 1$ . ∎ 3 Proofs of Theorems 1.1 and 1.3 3.1 Local well-posed for problem ( 1.21 ) Without any restrictions on the space dimension, this section is devoted to find out that whether the transient growth phenomena will hold to problem ( 1 ). Especial speaking, we consider problem ( 1 ) in non-radial symmetry case, and then obtain clear conditions such that the phenomena shall occur in the sense that the solution surpasses any given threshold. As mentioned above, the main tool in this section are the viscosity vanishing method and some important estimates, then an auxiliary problem with the corresponding hyperbolic-elliptic-elliptic model, i.e., problem ( 1.21 ), is needed for us. So, in order to avoid any confusions we shall denote the solution of problem ( 1 ) with any $\epsilon>0$ by $(u_{\epsilon},v_{\epsilon},w_{\epsilon})$ in this section and rewrite problem ( 1 ) as $\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{\epsilon t}=\epsilon\Delta u_{\epsilon}-\nabla\cdot(u_{\epsilon}\nabla v_{\epsilon})+ru_{\epsilon}-\mu u_{\epsilon}^{\theta},&x\in\Omega,\quad t>0,\\

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u_{\epsilon}}{\partial\nu}=\frac{\partial v_{\epsilon}}{\partial\nu}=\frac{\partial w_{\epsilon}}{\partial\nu}=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,\quad t>0,\\

The main strategies come from [ 25 , 21 , 64 ] , wherein the corresponding hyperbolic-elliptic type system related to the classical Keller-Segel growth model was studied. Kang and Stevens [ 21 ] extended the radially symmetric setting as in [ 25 , 64 ] to a bounded but convex domain via a logarithmic Sobolev inequality in terms of BMO norm and Sobolev norm [ 24 ] . The novelty of this part is that we extend the hyperbolic-elliptic model to problem ( 1.21 ) which including indirect signal production mechanism, moreover, we remove the convexity hypothesis in [ 21 ] . We first claim that the $W^{1,q}$ -solution of problem ( 1.21 ) is uniquely determined. Lemma 3.1 . Problem ( 1.21 ) possesses at most one strong $W^{1,q}$ -solution. Proof. &u_{\epsilon_{j}}\rightharpoonup^{*}u\ \mbox{ in }\ L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{1,q}(\Omega))\\
&v_{\epsilon_{j}}\rightarrow v\ \mbox{ in }\ C^{2,0}(\bar{\Omega}\times[0,T]),\\
&w_{\epsilon_{j}}\rightarrow w\ \mbox{ in }\ C^{2,0}(\bar{\Omega}\times[0,T]),\end{split}$ (3.6) as $\epsilon_{j}\rightarrow 0$ , i.e., $j\rightarrow\infty$ . Proof. By Lemma 2.9 we know there is a $T>0$ such that for all $q>n$ , $u_{\epsilon}(x,t)\in L^{\infty}([0,T];W^{1,q}(\Omega)).$ (3.7) Then, we can utilize elliptic estimates to the two elliptic equations in ( 3.1 ) to further get $w_{\epsilon}\in L^{\infty}([0,T];W^{3,q}(\Omega))$ and $v_{\epsilon}\in L^{\infty}([0,T];W^{5,q}(\Omega))$ . Since the positive coefficient $C$ ( 2.5 ) is independent of $\epsilon$ and $t$ , the boundedness we obtained is uniform in time and $\epsilon$ . Furthermore, due to the boundedness of $\epsilon$ , as noted in Remark 3 already, we can get a suitable strong regularity of the time derivative of solution to problem ( 3.1 ) by the same ways as in [ 64 , Lemma 3.4] and [ 25 , Lemma 3.10] , that is, for $p>1$ , $u_{\epsilon t}(x,t)\in L^{p}\left([0,T];(W^{1,\frac{q}{q-1}}(\Omega))^{*}\right).$ (3.8) Hence, the rest of the proof can be done by a standard compactness argument, cf. [ 25 , Lemma 4.4] and [ 64 , Lemma 4.3] . We include a short proof here for the sake of completeness and readers’ convenience. Make use of ( 3.7 ), ( 3.8 ) and a variant of the classical Aubin-Lions lemma (see [ 64 , Lemma 4.4] ), we know for any subsequence of $(\epsilon_{j})_{j}$ one can pick a further subsequence thereof such that $\begin{split}&u_{\epsilon_{j_{i}}}\rightarrow u\ \ \mbox{ in }\ C([0,T];C^{\iota}(\Omega)),\\
&u_{\epsilon_{j_{i}}}\rightharpoonup^{*}u\ \mbox{ in }\ L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{1,q}(\Omega))\end{split}$ (3.9) as $i\rightarrow\infty$ . Moreover, from the propagation of the Cauchy-property and Lemma 2.2 we know that there exists positive constant $C$ such that $\begin{split}\left\|w_{\epsilon_{j_{i}}}-w_{\epsilon_{j_{k}}}\right\|_{C^{2,0}(\Omega\times[0,T])}&\leq\left\|w_{\epsilon_{j_{i}}}-w_{\epsilon_{j_{k}}}\right\|_{C^{2+\iota,0}(\Omega\times[0,T])}\\
&\leq C\left\|u_{\epsilon_{j_{i}}}-u_{\epsilon_{j_{k}}}\right\|_{C^{\iota,0}(\Omega\times[0,T])}\end{split}$ for any $i,k\in\mathbb{N}$ . Thus, ( 3.9 ) gives $w_{\epsilon_{j_{i}}}\rightarrow w$ in $C^{2,0}(\Omega\times[0,T])$ . Similarly, we can also get the convergence for element $v$ in $C^{2,0}(\Omega\times[0,T])$ , thus ( 3.6 ) holds. Finally, testing the first equation of problem ( 3.1 ) by an arbitrary $\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}([0,T);\bar{\Omega})$ , $\begin{split}&-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}u_{\epsilon}\varphi_{t}-\int_{\Omega}u_{0\epsilon}\varphi(\cdot,0)\\
&=-\epsilon\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{\epsilon}\nabla\varphi+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}u_{\epsilon}\nabla v_{\epsilon}\nabla\varphi+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(ru_{\epsilon}-\mu u_{\epsilon}^{\theta})\varphi.\end{split}$ Taking $\epsilon=\epsilon_{j_{i}}\rightarrow 0$ in above equation, ( 3.6 ) implies ( 1.22 ) immediately. Moreover, due to the uniqueness of solution as obtained in Lemma 3.1 , we know the whole sequence converges to the solution $(u,v,w)$ of problem ( 1.21 ).
∎ Now we show the local existence of the strong $W^{1,p}$ -solution of problem ( 1.21 ) under small initial data. Lemma 3.3 . Let $D>0$ be any constant, $(\epsilon_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}$ be the zero sequence given by Lemma 3.2 as well as the initial value of problem ( 1.21 ) satisfies $\|u_{0}\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega)}\leq D$ , then there is number $T(D)>0$ such that problem ( 1.21 ) admits a strong $W^{1,p}$ -solution $(u,v,w)$ in $\Omega\times(0,T(D))$ . &u_{\epsilon_{j}}\rightharpoonup^{*}u\ \ \mbox{ in }\ L^{\infty}((0,T(D));W^{1,q}(\Omega))\\
&v_{\epsilon_{j}}\rightarrow v\ \mbox{ in }\ C^{2,0}(\bar{\Omega}\times[0,T(D)]),\\
&w_{\epsilon_{j}}\rightarrow w\ \mbox{ in }\ C^{2,0}(\bar{\Omega}\times[0,T(D)])\end{split}$ as $\epsilon_{j}\rightarrow 0$ . Moreover, element $u$ of problem ( 1.21 ) satisfies the estimate ( 2.5 ). Proof. We can infer from Lemma 2.9 that solutions $u_{\epsilon_{j}}$ to problem ( 3.1 ) exists bounded on $(0,T(D))$ , then Lemma 3.2 tells us there is a strong $W^{1,q}$ -solution of problem ( 1.21 ) with the claimed approximation properties, so we only need to prove estimate ( 2.5 ) for solution of problem ( 1.21 ). According to ( 2.5 ) we know the solution component $u_{\epsilon_{j}}$ of problem ( 3.1 ) satisfies $\|u_{\epsilon_{j}}\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega)}^{q}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|\left(\|u_{0\epsilon_{j}}\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega)}^{-2q}+1\right)e^{-2Ct}-1\right|}}.$ It follows from ( 3.10 ) that the convergence of the right hand side is obvious as $\epsilon_{j}\rightarrow 0$ , moreover, Lemma 2.9 implies the boundedness of $(u_{\epsilon_{j}})_{j}$ and $(\nabla u_{\epsilon_{j}})_{j}$ in $L^{q}(\Omega)$ , so by $L^{q}$ -weak convergence along a subsequence and the weak lower semicontinuity of the norm we can obtain $\begin{split}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega)}^{q}&\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\left(\|u_{\epsilon_{j_{k}}}(\cdot,t)\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega)}^{q}\right)\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|\left(\|u_{0}\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega)}^{-2q}+1\right)e^{-2Ct}-1\right|}},\end{split}$ then our proof is complete.
∎ Solutions of problem ( 1.21 ) constructed up to now may only exist with small initial data on time interval $(0,T(D))$ . We next give the proof of Theorem 1.1 which suggests that the solution problem ( 1.21 ) can exist on a maximal time interval and is locally well-posed (cf. [ 64 , Theorem 1.2] ). Proof of Theorem 1.1 . With the help of Lemma 3.1 , we can apply Lemma 3.3 with $D:=\|u_{0}\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega)}$ to gain $T>0$ and a unique strong $W^{1,q}$ -solution of problem ( 1.21 ) in $\Omega\times(0,T)$ fulfilling $\begin{split}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega)}^{q}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|\left(\|u_{0}\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega)}^{-2q}+1\right)e^{-2Ct}-1\right|}},\end{split}$ (3.11) for almost every $t\in(0,T)$ . Accordingly, the set $\begin{split}S:&=\left\{\tilde{T}>0\ |\ \exists\ \mbox{strong}\ W^{1,q}\mbox{-solution of problem \eqref{2} in}\ \Omega\times(0,T)\right.\\
&\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\ \left.\mbox{that satisfies \eqref{4.47} for a.e.}\ t\in(0,\tilde{T})\right\}\end{split}$ is not empty and $T^{*}:=\sup S\leq\infty$ is well-defined. The $L^{1}$ -norm boundedness of $u$ to problem ( 1.21 ) as given by ( 1.24 ) can be got by the ODE comparison and a suitable test function as in [ 64 , Lemma 4.1] , [ 25 , Lemma 4.7] and [ 71 , Lemma 4.1] , we omit the detail here. We only have to show the extensibility criterion $\mbox{either}\ \ T^{*}=+\infty\ \ \mbox{or}\ \ \limsup_{t\rightarrow T^{*}}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=+\infty.$ Arguing with contradiction, we assume $T^{*}<+\infty\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \limsup_{t\rightarrow T^{*}}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<+\infty,$ then for all $(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T^{*})$ there exists $M>0$ such that $u(x,t)\leq M.$ (3.12) Let $N\subset(0,T_{\max})$ be a set of measure zero, as provided by the definition of $S$ , such that ( 3.11 ) holds for all $t\in(0,T_{\max})\backslash N$ , then ( 3.11 ) gives us $\|u(\cdot,t_{0})\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega)}\leq D_{1},\quad\forall\ t\in(0,T_{\max})\backslash N$ with some $D_{1}>0$ . Thus, choosing any $t_{0}\in(0,T_{\max})\backslash N$ such that $t_{0}>T_{\max}-\frac{T(D_{1})}{2}$ here, we would infer that $\displaystyle\tilde{u}(\cdot,t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u(\cdot,t),&t\in(0,t_{0}],\\

For $t>t_{0}$ , ( 3.13 ) gives us $\begin{split}\|\tilde{u}(\cdot,t)\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega)}^{q}&\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left(\|u(t_{0})\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega)}^{-2q}+1\right)e^{-2C(t-t_{0})}-1}}.\end{split}$ (3.15) It is easy to see from ( 3.11 ) that $\begin{split}\left(\|u(t_{0})\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega)}^{-2q}+1\right)e^{-2C(t-t_{0})}&\geq\left(\|u_{0}\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega)}^{-2q}+1\right)e^{-2Ct_{0}}e^{-2C(t-t_{0})}\\

∎ 3.2 Blow-up of problem ( 1.21 ) The following technical tool is important for us, which comes from [ 64 , Lemma 4.9] . Lemma 3.4 . Let $a>0,b\geq 0,d>0,\kappa>1$ be such that $a>(\frac{2b}{d})^{\frac{1}{\kappa}},$ Then if for some $T>0$ the function $y\in C([0,T))$ is nonnegative and satisfies $y(t)\geq a-bt+d\int_{0}^{t}y^{\kappa}(s)$ for all $t\in(0,T)$ , we necessarily have $T\leq\frac{2}{(\kappa-1)a^{\kappa-1}d}.$ Proof of Theorem 1.2 . &=-\frac{\arctan\left(\|u_{0}\|_{W^{1,\theta+1}(\Omega)}^{-(\theta+1)}\right)}{C}\\
&\geq-\frac{\pi}{4C},\end{split}$ Let $y(t)=\int_{\Omega}u^{\theta}$ for $t\in(0,T_{0})$ , then $y(t)\in C^{0}([0,T_{0}))$ is nonnegative and it follows from the arbitrariness of $t_{0}$ and Hölder’s inequality that $\begin{split}y(t)&\geq y(0)-\frac{(\theta-1)C_{3}\pi}{4C}-(\theta-1)C_{3}m_{1}^{\theta+1}t+\frac{\alpha\gamma(\theta-1)}{6d_{1}\delta}|\Omega|^{-\frac{1}{\theta}}\int_{0}^{t}y^{\frac{\theta+1}{\theta}}(\tau),\end{split}$ for all $t\in(0,T_{0})$ , then we can infer from ( 3.17 ) that the condition of Lemma 3.4 is satisfied and then $T_{0}\leq\frac{12d_{1}\theta\delta|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{\theta}}}{\alpha\gamma(\theta-1)\left[\|u_{0}\|^{\theta}_{L^{\theta}(\Omega)}-\frac{(\theta-1)C_{3}\pi}{4C}\right]^{\frac{1}{\theta}}},$ which combines with the definition of $T_{0}$ in ( 3.18 ) imply that $\begin{split}\left[\|u_{0}\|^{\theta}_{L^{\theta}(\Omega)}-\frac{(\theta-1)C_{3}\pi}{4C}\right]^{\frac{1}{\theta}}&<\ln\left(\|u_{0}\|_{W^{1,\theta+1}(\Omega)}^{-2(\theta+1)}+1\right)\left[\|u_{0}\|^{\theta}_{L^{\theta}(\Omega)}-\frac{(\theta-1)C_{3}\pi}{4C}\right]^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\\
&\leq\frac{24d_{1}\theta\delta|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{\theta}}C}{\alpha\gamma(\theta-1)},\end{split}$ then $\|u_{0}\|^{\theta}_{L^{\theta}(\Omega)}<\left(\frac{24d_{1}\theta\delta|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{\theta}}C}{\alpha\gamma(\theta-1)}\right)^{\theta}+\frac{(\theta-1)\pi}{4C},$ this contradicts ( 3.16 ), so we get ( 3.18 ), i.e., $T^{*}\leq T_{0}$ , so the conclusion that $u$ blows up in finite time is then an immediate consequence of Theorem 1.1 .
∎ 3.3 Proof of Theorem 1.3 Now we are at the position to show the proof of Theorem 1.3 . Proof of Theorem 1.3 . Let $(u_{\epsilon},v_{\epsilon},w_{\epsilon})$ be the classical solution of problem ( 3.1 ) and $(u,v,w)$ be the strong $W^{1,p}$ -solution of problem ( 1.21 ). Assume that the conclusion of Theorem 1.3 were not true, then for the solution of problem ( 1.21 ) there would be $M>0$ and any $\epsilon>0$ such that $u_{\epsilon}(x,t)\leq M,$ (3.25) for all $(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,+\infty)$ . Let $(\epsilon_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,1)$ be a zero sequence such that $\epsilon_{j}\rightarrow 0$ as $j\rightarrow\infty$ , so we can infer from ( 3.25 ) and Lemma 3.2 that $\begin{split}&u_{\epsilon_{j}}\rightarrow\bar{u}\quad\mbox{ in}\ \ C(\bar{\Omega}\times[0,T]),\\
&v_{\epsilon_{j}}\rightarrow\bar{v}\quad\mbox{ in}\ \ C^{2,0}(\bar{\Omega}\times[0,T]),\\
&w_{\epsilon_{j}}\rightarrow\bar{w}\quad\mbox{ in}\ \ C^{2,0}(\bar{\Omega}\times[0,T]),\end{split}$ as $j\rightarrow\infty$ , where $(\bar{u},\bar{v},\bar{w})$ is the strong $W^{1,p}$ -solution of problem ( 1.21 ). Due to Lemma 3.1 we know such solution is unique and then $(\bar{u},\bar{v},\bar{w})=(u,v,w)$ and $u(x,t)=\bar{u}(x,t)\leq M\quad\mbox{ in}\ \ \Omega\times(0,T).$ However, since the initial data satisfies ( 1.26 ), we can see from Theorem 1.2 that the strong $W^{1,p}$ -solution $(u,v,w)$ of problem ( 1.21 ) blows up in finite, so there is a contradiction.
