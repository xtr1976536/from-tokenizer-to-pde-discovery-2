v_{t}+u_{x}+(vu)_{x}+u_{xxx}=0,\end{cases}$ (1.1) is a special case of a broad class of Boussinesq systems or the so-called abcd systems derived by Bona, Chen and Saut in [ BCS02 , BCS04 ] from the two-dimensional Euler equations. Compared to those uni-directional models, such as the KdV equation, the Boussinesq equation and the BBM equation, the bi-directional systems ( 1.1 ) provide wider range of applications in reality. The Cauchy problem of the system ( 1.1 ) posed either on $\mathbb{R}$ or on the periodic domain $\mathbb{T}$ has been well studied. In particular, it is known to be analytically well-posed in the space $H^{s}(\mathbb{R})$ for any $s>-\frac{3}{4}$ , but related bilinear estimates fail in $H^{s}(\mathbb{R})$ for any $s<-\frac{3}{4}$ . (See e.g. [ AC08 , BGK10 , YZ22a ] ). In this paper, we are concerned with the well-posedness of the initial-boundary-value problem (IBVP) for the coupled KdV-KdV system posed on the half line $\mathbb{R}^{+}$ : $\begin{cases}u_{t}+v_{x}+uu_{x}+v_{xxx}=0,\\
v_{t}+u_{x}+(vu)_{x}+u_{xxx}=0,\\
u(x,0)=\phi(x),\quad v(x,0)=\psi(x),\\
u(0,t)=h_{1}(t),\quad v(0,t)=h_{2}(t),\quad v_{x}(0,t)=h_{3}(t).\end{cases}\qquad x,\,t>0.$ (1.2) Let’s first briefly review some highly related literatures. The IBVP for the single KdV equation $\begin{cases}w_{t}+w_{x}+w_{xxx}=0,\\
w(x,0)=\phi(x),\quad w(0,t)=h(t).\end{cases}\qquad x,\,t>0.$ (1.3) posed on the half line has been investigated extensively. Bona, Sun and Zhang [ BSZ02 ] developed a method by using the Laplace transform to establish the well-posedness of ( 1.3 ) in the space $H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{+})$ for $s>\frac{3}{4}$ . Later, they improved the range of $s$ to be $s>-\frac{3}{4}$ in [ BSZ06 ] by taking advantage of the Fourier restriction spaces introduced by Bourgain [ Bou93a ] . During the same period, Colliander and Kenig [ CK02 ] introduced the Duhamel boundary forcing operator to establish the well-posedness of ( 1.3 ) in the space $H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{+})$ for $s\geq 0$ . After that, Holmer [ Hol06 ] generalized this method to improve the range of the index $s$ to be $s>-\frac{3}{4}$ by constructing an analytic family of boundary forcing operators. These two methods automatically establish locally analytical well-posedness since both of them took advantage of a certain fixed point argument. As a result, the threshold $-\frac{3}{4}$ is optimal concerning the analytical well-posedness, see [ CCT03 ] . These two methods were also applied to study the well-posedness of the IBVP for more general equations, such as the Boussinesq equations [ Xue08 , CT17 , LCZ18 ] , the BBM equation [ BCH14 ] , the nonlinear Schodinger equations [ Hol05 , ET16 , Cav17 , BSZ18 , GW20 ] , the Klein-Gordon-Schodinger system [ CT19 ] , the Schodinger-KdV systems [ CC19 ] and many more. But few works have been devoted to investigate the low regularity well-posedness of the IBVP for KdV-KdV systems such as ( 1.2 ). Part of the reason is due to the complicated structure of the systems. Actually, it was discovered in [ Oh09 ] and [ YZ22a ] that the threshold index for the well-posedness of the Cauchy problem for KdV-KdV systems on $\mathbb{R}$ may be as large as $0$ or $\frac{3}{4}$ instead of $-\frac{3}{4}$ , depending on the dispersion coefficients and how $u$ and $v$ are interacted in the systems. The goal of this paper is to study the IBVP for the particular KdV-KdV system ( 1.2 ) on the half line $\mathbb{R}^{+}$ and establish the well-posedness beyond the threshold $-\frac{3}{4}$ . In order to adopt results and techniques obtained in the study of KdV-type equations, the new variables $U:=\frac{1}{4}(u+v)$ and $V:=\frac{1}{4}(u-v)$ are introduced so that the linear parts of ( 1.2 ) on $U$ and $V$ are decoupled with the KdV type. More precisely, the new system reads as $\begin{cases}U_{t}+U_{x}+U_{xxx}+3UU_{x}+(UV)_{x}-VV_{x}=0,\vskip 2.168pt\\
V_{t}-V_{x}-V_{xxx}-UU_{x}+(UV)_{x}+3VV_{x}=0,\vskip 2.168pt\\
U(x,0)=\frac{1}{4}(\phi+\psi),\quad V(x,0)=\frac{1}{4}(\phi-\psi),\vskip 2.168pt\\
U(0,t)=\frac{1}{4}(h_{1}+h_{2}),\quad V(0,t)=\frac{1}{4}(h_{1}-h_{2}),\quad V_{x}(0,t)-U_{x}(0,t)=-\frac{1}{2}h_{3}.\end{cases}\qquad x,\,t>0.$ For ease of notations, we still denote $(U,V)$ as $(u,v)$ , and write $\left(\frac{1}{4}(\phi+\psi),\frac{1}{4}(\phi-\psi)\right)$ as $(\phi,\psi)$ and $\left(\frac{1}{4}(h_{1}+h_{2}),\frac{1}{4}(h_{1}-h_{2}),-\frac{1}{2}h_{3}\right)$ as $(h_{1},h_{2},h_{3})$ . Then it reduces to study the following IBVP: $\begin{cases}u_{t}+u_{xxx}+u_{x}+3uu_{x}+(uv)_{x}-vv_{x}=0,\\
v_{t}-v_{xxx}-v_{x}-uu_{x}+(uv)_{x}+3vv_{x}=0,\\
u(x,0)=\phi(x),\quad v(x,0)=\psi(x),\\
u(0,t)=h_{1}(t),\quad v(0,t)=h_{2}(t),\quad v_{x}(0,t)-u_{x}(0,t)=h_{3}(t),\end{cases}\qquad x,t>0.$ (1.4) which is equivalent to the IBVP ( 1.2 ) for the well-posedness in the space $H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{+})$ . For any $s\in\mathbb{R}$ , we define ${\cal H}_{x}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{+}):=H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{+}),\quad{\cal H}^{s}_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{+}):=H^{\frac{s+1}{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\times H^{\frac{s+1}{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\times H^{\frac{s}{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{+}).$ When $s\geq 3$ , the well-posedness of ( 1.4 ) in the space $H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{+})$ can be established concerning the classical solutions $(u,v)\in C([0,T];{\cal H}^{s}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{+}))$ . So in the remaining of this paper, we will focus on the case $s\leq 3$ . In addition, for lower regularity solutions, there will be a serious uniqueness issue. For more details, the readers can refer to Remark 2.4 in section 2. In this situation, the type of solutions of ( 1.4 ) that we are interested in is the so-called mild solutions which can be viewed as limits of strong solutions. The precise definition is given below. Definition 1.1 (Mild solutions) . Let $s\leq 3$ and $T>0$ be given. (i) A function pair $(u,v)\in C([0,T];{\cal H}^{3}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{+}))\cap C^{1}([0,T];{\cal H}^{0}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{+}))$ is said to be a strong solution of the IBVP ( 1.4 ) in the space $C([0,T];{\cal H}^{s}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{+}))$ if the equations in ( 1.4 ) hold for a.e. $(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times(0,T)$ and $(u,v,v_{x})|_{x=0}\in{\cal H}^{s}_{t}(0,T)$ . (ii) A function pair $(u,v)\in C([0,T];{\cal H}^{s}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{+}))$ is said to be a mild solution of the IBVP ( 1.4 ) if there exists a sequence of strong solutions $(u_{n},v_{n})\in C([0,T];{\cal H}^{3}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{+}))$ such that $\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}(u_{n},v_{n})\to(u,v)$ in $C([0,T];{\cal H}^{s}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{+}))$ and $\lim\limits_{n\to 0}(h_{1,n},h_{2,n},h_{3,n})\to(h_{1},h_{2},h_{3})$ in ${\cal H}^{s}_{t}(0,T)$ , where $(h_{1,n},h_{2,n},h_{3,n}):=\left.(u_{n},v_{n},\partial_{x}v_{n})\right|_{x=0}$ . After the concept of solutions is fixed, we can discuss the (unconditional) local well-posedness of ( 1.4 ) which is understood in the following sense. Definition 1.2 (Local well-posedness) . For any $s\leq 3$ , the IBVP ( 1.4 ) is said to be locally well-posed in the space ${\cal H}_{x}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{+})$ if for any $r>0$ , there exists some time $T>0$ , depending only on $r$ and $s$ , such that for any naturally compatible 1 1 1 For the concept of compatibility, the reader can refer to Definition 2.7 . $(\phi,\psi)\in{\cal H}^{s}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{+})$ and $\vec{h}=(h_{1},h_{2},h_{3})\in{\cal H}^{s}_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{+})$ with $\|\phi,\psi)\|_{{\cal H}^{s}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}+\|\vec{h}\|_{{\cal H}^{s}_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}\leq r$ ,
the IBVP ( 1.4 ) admits a unique mild solution $(u,v)\in C([0,T];{\cal H}^{s}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{+}))$ and, moreover, the solution map is continuous in the corresponding spaces. If the solution map is real analytic instead, then the IBVP ( 1.4 ) is said to be locally analytically well-posed. We intend to find those values of $s$ for which the IBVP ( 1.4 ) is locally well-posed in the space ${\cal H}^{s}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{+})$ . The following theorem is the main result of this paper. Theorem 1.3 . The IBVP ( 1.4 ) is locally analytically well-posed in the space ${\cal H}^{s}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{+})$ , with any naturally compatible data $(\phi,\psi)\in{\cal H}^{s}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{+})$ and $\vec{h}=(h_{1},h_{2},h_{3})\in{\cal H}^{s}_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{+})$ ,
for any $s>-\frac{3}{4}$ . To prove Theorem 1.3 which is a result on local well-posedness, we use scaling argument to introduce $u^{\beta}(x,t)=\beta u(\beta^{\frac{1}{2}}x,\beta^{\frac{3}{2}}t)\quad\text{and}\quad v^{\beta}(x,t)=\beta v(\beta^{\frac{1}{2}}x,\beta^{\frac{3}{2}}t),$ where $\beta\in(0,1]$ is a parameter.
Then ( 1.4 ) becomes $\begin{cases}u^{\beta}_{t}+u^{\beta}_{xxx}+\beta u^{\beta}_{x}+3u^{\beta}u^{\beta}_{x}+(u^{\beta}v^{\beta})_{x}-v^{\beta}v^{\beta}_{x}=0,\\
v^{\beta}_{t}-v^{\beta}_{xxx}-\beta v^{\beta}_{x}-u^{\beta}u^{\beta}_{x}+(u^{\beta}v^{\beta})_{x}+3v^{\beta}v^{\beta}_{x}=0,\\
u^{\beta}(x,0)=\phi^{\beta}(x),\quad v^{\beta}(x,0)=\psi^{\beta}(x),\\
u^{\beta}(0,t)=h_{1}^{\beta}(t),\quad v^{\beta}(0,t)=h_{2}^{\beta}(t),\quad v^{\beta}_{x}(0,t)-u^{\beta}_{x}(0,t)=h_{3}^{\beta}(t),\end{cases}\qquad x,t>0,$ where $\begin{cases}\phi^{\beta}(x):=\beta\phi(\beta^{\frac{1}{2}}x),\quad\psi^{\beta}(x):=\beta\psi(\beta^{\frac{1}{2}}x),\\
h_{1}^{\beta}(t):=\beta h_{1}(\beta^{\frac{3}{2}}t),\quad h_{2}^{\beta}(t):=\beta h_{2}(\beta^{\frac{3}{2}}t),\quad h_{3}^{\beta}(t):=\beta^{\frac{3}{2}}h_{3}(\beta^{\frac{3}{2}}t).\end{cases}$ When $s>-\frac{3}{4}$ , $\|(\phi^{\beta},\psi^{\beta})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}\lesssim\beta^{\frac{3}{8}}\|(\phi,\psi)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+})},\quad\|(h_{1}^{\beta},h_{2}^{\beta},h_{3}^{\beta})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}\lesssim\beta^{\frac{3}{8}}\|(h_{1},h_{2},h_{3})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{+})},$ which implies $\lim_{\beta\to 0^{+}}\|(\phi^{\beta},\psi^{\beta})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}+\|(h_{1}^{\beta},h_{2}^{\beta},h_{3}^{\beta})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}=0.$ Thus, in order to prove Theorem 1.3 , it is sufficient to establish Theorem 1.4 below for the following IBVP: $\begin{cases}u_{t}+u_{xxx}+\beta u_{x}+3uu_{x}+(uv)_{x}-vv_{x}=0,\\
v_{t}-v_{xxx}-\beta v_{x}-uu_{x}+(uv)_{x}+3vv_{x}=0,\\
u(x,0)=\phi(x),\quad v(x,0)=\psi(x),\\
u(0,t)=h_{1}(t),\quad v(0,t)=h_{2}(t),\quad v_{x}(0,t)-u_{x}(0,t)=h_{3}(t).\end{cases}\qquad x,t>0.$ (1.5) Theorem 1.4 . For any $s>-\frac{3}{4}$ and $T>0$ , there exists an $\epsilon>0$ , depending only on $T$ and $s$ , such that for any $\beta\in(0,1]$ and for any naturally compatible data $(\phi,\psi)\in{\cal H}^{s}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{+})$ and $\vec{h}=(h_{1},h_{2},h_{3})\in{\cal H}^{s}_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{+})$ with $\|\phi,\psi)\|_{{\cal H}^{s}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}+\|\vec{h}\|_{{\cal H}^{s}_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}\leq\epsilon,$ the IBVP ( 1.5 ) admits a unique mild solution $(u,v)\in C\big([0,T];{\cal H}^{s}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\big)$ , and moreover, the solution map is real analytic in the corresponding spaces. We remark that the well-posedness obtained in Theorem 1.4 (or equivalently Theorem 1.3 ) is unconditional (see Remark 2.4 ) since the uniqueness is secured in the space $C\big([0,T];{\cal H}^{s}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\big)$ itself rather than in a smaller subspace. The reason that the unconditional uniqueness can be justified is because we are concerning with the mild solutions. If the distributional solutions, instead of the mild solutions, are adopted in Definition 1.2 , then the unconditional uniqueness is still a challenging open problem. 2 Outline of the method and applications 2.1 Key ingredients in the proof of Theorem 1.4 Theorem 1.4 will be proved using the approach similar to that developed in [ BSZ02 , BSZ06 , CK02 , Hol06 ] for the KdV equation but with some modifications to handle the bi-directional system ( 1.5 ). Consider the Cauchy problem for the linear KdV-type equation posed on $\mathbb{R}$ , $w_{t}+\alpha w_{xxx}+\beta w_{x}=0,\qquad w(x,0)=w_{0}(x),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},\,t\in\mathbb{R},$ (2.1) where $\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}$ and $\beta\in\mathbb{R}$ . Let $W_{R}^{\alpha,\beta}$ be the semigroup operator associated to ( 2.1 ). The solution of ( 2.1 ) is then given explicitly by $W_{R}^{\alpha,\beta}(t)w_{0}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\xi x}e^{i\phi^{\alpha,\beta}(\xi)t}\widehat{w_{0}}(\xi)d\xi,$ (2.2) where $\widehat{w_{0}}(\xi)$ is the Fourier transform of $w_{0}(x)$ , and $\phi^{\alpha,\beta}$ is the cubic polynomial defined as below: $\phi^{\alpha,\beta}(\xi)=\alpha\xi^{3}-\beta\xi.$ (2.3) Definition 2.1 . For $\alpha,\beta,s,b\in\mathbb{R}$ , the Fourier restriction space $X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,b}$ is defined to be the completion of the Schwarz class ${\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})$ under the norm $\|w\|_{X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,b}}=\left\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\langle\tau-\phi^{\alpha,\beta}(\xi)\rangle^{b}\widehat{w}(\xi,\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\tau}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},$ (2.4) where $\phi^{\alpha,\beta}$ is as defined in ( 2.3 ) and $\widehat{w}$ represents the space-time Fourier transform of $w$ . When $b>\frac{1}{2}$ , it follows directly from the Sobolev embedding that $X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,b}\subset C_{t}(\mathbb{R};H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))$ . But the estimate on the boundary integral operator (see e.g. Lemma 4.3 and 4.10 ) forces $b\leq\frac{1}{2}$ , and then the space $X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,b}$ may not be in $C_{t}(\mathbb{R};H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))$ . In addition, the bilinear estimates used in the Cauchy problems will fail. In order to overcome these difficulties, we introduce some modified Fourier restriction spaces $X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,b,\sigma}$ and $Y_{s,b,\sigma}^{\alpha,\beta}$ . The spaces $X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,b,\sigma}$ will be used to justify the bilinear estimates and $Y_{s,b,\sigma}^{\alpha,\beta}$ will serve as suitable solution spaces. Definition 2.2 . For $\alpha,\beta,s,b\in\mathbb{R}$ and $\sigma>\frac{1}{2}$ , let $\Lambda^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,\sigma}$ be the completion of the Schwarz class ${\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})$ under the norm $\|w\|_{\Lambda^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,\sigma}}=\left\|\mathbbm{1}_{\{e^{|\xi|}\leq 3+|\tau|\}}\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\langle\tau-\phi^{\alpha,\beta}(\xi)\rangle^{\sigma}\widehat{w}(\xi,\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\tau}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},$ (2.5) where $\mathbbm{1}$ means the characteristic function. In addition, we denote $X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,b,\sigma}=X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,b}\cap\Lambda^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,\sigma}\quad\text{and}\quad Y_{s,b,\sigma}^{\alpha,\beta}=X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,b,\sigma}\cap C_{t}(\mathbb{R},H^{s}(\mathbb{R})).$ The norm of the space $X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,b,\sigma}$ is defined to be $\begin{split}\|w\|_{X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,b,\sigma}}&=\|w\|_{X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,b}}+\|w\|_{\Lambda^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,\sigma}}\\
&\sim\left\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\big[\langle L\rangle^{b}+\mathbbm{1}_{\{e^{|\xi|}\leq 3+|\tau|\}}\langle L\rangle^{\sigma}\big]\widehat{w}(\xi,\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\tau}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},\end{split}$ (2.6) where $L=\tau-\phi^{\alpha,\beta}(\xi)$ , and the norm of the space $Y_{s,b,\sigma}^{\alpha,\beta}$ is defined to be $\|w\|_{Y^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,b,\sigma}}=\|w\|_{X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,b,\sigma}}+\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|w(x,t)\|_{H_{x}^{s}(\mathbb{R})}.$ (2.7) From the above definition, we see that when $b\leq\frac{1}{2}$ , the space $X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,b,\sigma}$ provides more regularity in time than the space $X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,b}$ in the low frequency region. This improvement is essential to establish the bilinear estimates in Proposition 2.10 . Note that for any $b\leq\sigma$ , $\|w\|_{X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,b,\sigma}}\leq 2\|w\|_{X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,\sigma}}.$ (2.8) Besides, $e^{|\xi|}\leq 3+|\tau|$ when $|\xi|\leq 1$ . Thus, $\|w\|_{X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,b,\sigma}}\gtrsim\left\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\big[\langle L\rangle^{b}+\mathbbm{1}_{\{|\xi|\leq 1\}}\langle L\rangle^{\sigma}\big]\widehat{w}(\xi,\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\tau}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},\quad\mbox{where $L=\tau-\phi^{\alpha,\beta}(\xi)$}.$ (2.9) For technical reasons, when $\sigma>\frac{1}{2}$ , we define the $Z_{s,\sigma-1}^{\alpha,\beta}$ space to be the completion of the Schwarz class $\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2})$ with respect to the following norm. $\|w\|_{Z^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,\sigma-1}}:=\left\|\langle\tau\rangle^{\frac{s}{3}+\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}\langle\tau-\phi^{\alpha,\beta}(\xi)\rangle^{\sigma-1}\widehat{w}(\xi,\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\tau}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.$ (2.10) This space is an intermediate space which will only be used to estimate the inhomogeneous term in the Duhamel formula, see Lemma 3.6 . The definition of this space is a modification of the $Y_{s,\sigma-1}$ space defined in Section 5.3 in [ Hol06 ] . Let $\Omega_{T}:=\mathbb{R}^{+}\times(0,T)$ for given $T>0$ . We define a restricted

We will first establish the following conditional well-posedness. Theorem 2.3 (Conditional Well-posedness) . Let $-\frac{3}{4}<s\leq 3$ , $T>0$ and $0\leq\beta\leq 1$ be given. There exist $\sigma=\sigma(s)>\frac{1}{2}$ and $r=r(s,T)>0$ such that for any naturally compatible $(\phi,\psi)\in{\cal H}^{s}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{+})$ , $\vec{h}=(h_{1},h_{2},h_{3})\in{\cal H}^{s}_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{+})$ related to the IBVP ( 1.5 ) with $\|\phi,\psi)\|_{{\cal H}^{s}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}+\|\vec{h}\|_{{\cal H}^{s}_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}\leq r,$ the system of the integral equations (SIE) ( 2.11 ) admits a unique solution $(u,v)\in\mathcal{Y}_{\sigma}(\Omega_{T}):=Y^{1,\beta}_{s,\frac{1}{2},\sigma}(\Omega_{T})\times Y^{-1,-\beta}_{s,\frac{1}{2},\sigma}(\Omega_{T})$ Moreover, the solution map is real analytic in the corresponding spaces. Remark 2.4 . The well-posedness presented in Theorem 2.3 is conditional in the sense that
the solution $(u,v)\in C([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{s}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{+}))$ of ( 2.11 ) is in fact the restriction for a function defined on $\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}$ to the region $(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{+}$ ,
and the uniqueness of the solution holds in a subspace $\mathcal{Y}_{\sigma}(\Omega_{T})$ rather than in the full space $C([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{s}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{+}))$ ,
which leads to a serious issue:
If a solution of the IBVP ( 1.5 ) is found in a different approach, will it be the same as that presented by Theorem 2.3 ? According to the definition of mild solutions, one can find two sequences of classic solutions of the IBVP ( 1.4 ), $(u^{(1)}_{n},v^{(1)}_{n}),(u^{(2)}_{n},v^{(2)}_{n})\in C(0,T;H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{+}))\times C(0,T;H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{+})),\quad n\geq 1,$ with $\begin{cases}u^{(1)}_{n}(x,0)=p^{(1)}_{n}(x),\quad v^{(1)}_{n}(x,0)=q^{(1)}_{n}(x),\\
u^{(1)}_{n}(0,t)=a^{(1)}_{n}(t),\quad v^{(1)}_{n}(0,t)=b^{(1)}_{n}(t),\quad\partial_{x}(v^{(1)}_{n}-u^{(1)}_{n})(0,t)=c^{(1)}_{n}(t),\end{cases}$ and $\begin{cases}u^{(2)}_{n}(x,0)=p^{(2)}_{n}(x),\quad v^{(2)}_{n}(x,0)=q^{(2)}_{n}(x),\\
u^{(2)}_{n}(0,t)=a^{(2)}_{n}(t),\quad v^{(2)}_{n}(0,t)=b^{(2)}_{n}(t),\quad\partial_{x}(v^{(2)}_{n}-u^{(2)}_{n})(0,t)=c^{(2)}_{n}(t),\end{cases}$ such that $\lim_{n\to\infty}(u^{(1)}_{n},v^{(1)}_{n})=(u^{(1)},v^{(1)}),\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}(u^{(2)}_{n},v^{(2)}_{n})=(u^{(2)},v^{(2)}),\quad\mbox{in}\quad C(0,T;H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{+})),$ $\lim_{n\to\infty}(p^{(1)}_{n},q^{(1)}_{n})=(p,q),\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}(p^{(2)}_{n},q^{(2)}_{n})=(p,q)\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathcal{H}^{s}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{+}),$ and $\lim_{n\to\infty}(a^{(1)}_{n},b^{(1)}_{n},c^{(1)}_{n})=(a,b,c),\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}(a^{(2)}_{n},b^{(2)}_{n},c^{(2)}_{n})=(a,b,c)\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathcal{H}^{s}_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{+}).$ We then denote $(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})\in\left[C(0,T_{3};H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{+}))\right]^{2},$ $(\widetilde{u}_{n}^{(1)},\widetilde{v}_{n}^{(1)})\in\left[C(0,T^{1}_{n,max};H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{+}))\right]^{2},$ $(\widetilde{u}_{n}^{(2)},\widetilde{v}_{n}^{(2)})\in\left[C(0,T^{2}_{n,max};H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{+}))\right]^{2},$ to be the solutions provided by Corollary 6.1 with conditions corresponding to $(p,q,a,b,c),\quad(p^{(1)}_{n},q^{(1)}_{n},a^{(1)}_{n},b^{(1)}_{n},c^{(1)}_{n}),\quad(p^{(2)}_{n},q^{(2)}_{n},a^{(2)}_{n},b^{(2)}_{n},c^{(2)}_{n}),$ respectively. We define $T^{*}:=\min\{T,T_{3}\}$ . According to Lemma 6.2 , we can infer that $T^{1}_{n,max}\geq T^{*}$ , $T^{2}_{n,max}\geq T^{*}$ and $(u^{(1)}_{n},v^{(1)}_{n})=(\widetilde{u}_{n}^{(1)},\widetilde{v}_{n}^{(1)}),\quad(u^{(2)}_{n},v^{(2)}_{n})=(\widetilde{u}_{n}^{(2)},\widetilde{v}_{n}^{(2)}),\quad\mbox{in }[C(0,T^{*};H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{+}))]^{2},$ due to the uniqueness of the classic solution of the IBVP ( 1.4 ). Moreover, according to the continuity of the solution map in Corollary 6.1 , one has $\lim_{n\to\infty}(\widetilde{u}_{n}^{(1)},\widetilde{v}_{n}^{(1)})=(\tilde{u},\tilde{v}),\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}(\widetilde{u}_{n}^{(2)},\widetilde{v}_{n}^{(2)})=(\tilde{u},\tilde{v}),\quad\mbox{in }[C(0,T^{*};H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{+}))]^{2}.$ Therefore, we deduce that $(u^{(1)},v^{(1)})=(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})=(u^{(2)},v^{(2)})$ in $[C(0,T^{*};H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{+}))]^{2}$ .
Finally, we can use continuity property of the solutions $(u^{(1)},v^{(1)})$ , $(u^{(2)},v^{(2)})$ and $(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})$ to show $T_{1}=T_{2}=T_{3}$ .
∎ Appendix A Proofs of Lemma 6.5 –Lemma 6.7 The proofs for Lemma 6.5 , Lemma 6.6 and Lemma 6.7 are similar, so we will only prove Lemma 6.7 . In order to make the argument more clearly, we first present several auxiliary results. We start with the following Lemma A.1 which is a refinement of Proposition 5.4 on the temporal regularity of the term $(uv)_{x}$ . Lemma A.1 . Let $-\frac{3}{4}<s\leq 3$ , $\alpha\neq 0$ and $|\beta|\leq 1$ . Then there exists $\epsilon_{0}=\epsilon_{0}(s,\alpha)>0$ such that for any $\sigma\in(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon_{0}]$ , $\|(uv)_{x}\|_{X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,\sigma-1+\epsilon_{0}}}\leq C\|u\|_{X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,\frac{1}{2},\sigma}}\|v\|_{X^{-\alpha,-\beta}_{s,\frac{1}{2},\sigma}},$ (A.1) where $C=C(s,\alpha,\sigma)$ . Proof. By duality, it suffices to prove $\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\xi\,\widehat{uv}\widehat{w}\,d\xi\,d\tau\leq C\|u\|_{X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,\frac{1}{2},\sigma}}\|v\|_{X^{-\alpha,-\beta}_{s,\frac{1}{2},\sigma}}\|w\|_{X^{\alpha,\beta}_{-s,1-\sigma-\epsilon_{0}}},$ for any $u\in X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,\frac{1}{2},\sigma}$ , $v\in X^{-\alpha,-\beta}_{s,\frac{1}{2},\sigma}$ and $w\in X^{\alpha,\beta}_{-s,1-\sigma-\epsilon_{0}}$ . Similar to the proof of Proposition 5.4 , we denote $L_{1}=\tau_{1}-\phi^{\alpha,\beta}(\xi_{1}),\quad L_{2}=\tau_{2}-\phi^{-\alpha,-\beta}(\xi_{2}),\quad L_{3}=\tau_{3}-\phi^{\alpha,\beta}(\xi_{3}).$ and $M_{1}=\langle L_{1}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathbbm{1}_{\{e^{|\xi_{1}|}\leq 3+|\tau_{1}|\}}\langle L_{1}\rangle^{\sigma},\quad M_{2}=\langle L_{2}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathbbm{1}_{\{e^{|\xi_{2}|}\leq 3+|\tau_{2}|\}}\langle L_{2}\rangle^{\sigma}.$ In addition, we define $\begin{array}[]{c}f_{1}(\xi_{1},\tau_{1})=\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{s}M_{1}\widehat{u}(\xi_{1},\tau_{1}),\quad f_{2}(\xi_{2},\tau_{2})=\langle\xi_{2}\rangle^{s}M_{2}\widehat{v}(\xi_{2},\tau_{2}),\\
f_{3}(\xi_{3},\tau_{3})=\langle\xi_{3}\rangle^{-s}\langle L_{3}\rangle^{1-\sigma-\epsilon_{0}}\widehat{w}(\xi_{3},\tau_{3}).\end{array}$ Then it reduces to justify $\int\limits_{A}\frac{|\xi_{3}|\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{\rho}\langle\xi_{2}\rangle^{\rho}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{3}|f_{i}(\xi_{i},\tau_{i})|}{\langle\xi_{3}\rangle^{\rho}M_{1}M_{2}\langle L_{3}\rangle^{1-\sigma-\epsilon_{0}}}\leq C\,\prod_{i=1}^{3}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{\xi\tau}},$ (A.2) where $\rho=-s$ and the set $A$ is as defined in ( 5.11 ). In the remaining proof, instead of introducing $\sigma_{0}$ as in ( 5.24 ), we define $\epsilon_{0}=\frac{1}{16}-\frac{1}{12}\,\rho$ .
Then for any $\sigma\in(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon_{0}]$ , ( A.2 ) can be proved in an analogous way as the proof of ( 5.6 ) in Proposition 5.4 . ∎ Next, we will take advantage of Proposition A.1 to deduce a slightly stronger estimate since the spatial regularity requirement on the right-hand side of ( A.3 ) is weaker than that of ( A.1 ). Lemma A.2 . Let $-\frac{3}{4}<s_{1}\leq s_{2}\leq 3$ , $\alpha\neq 0$ , $|\beta|\leq 1$ . Then there exists $\epsilon_{0}=\epsilon_{0}(s_{1},s_{2},\alpha)>0$ such that for any $\sigma\in(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon_{0}]$ , $\|(uv)_{x}\|_{X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s_{2},\sigma-1+\epsilon_{0}}}\leq C\Big(\|u\|_{X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s_{1},\frac{1}{2},\sigma}}\|v\|_{X^{-\alpha,-\beta}_{s_{2},\frac{1}{2},\sigma}}+\|u\|_{X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s_{2},\frac{1}{2},\sigma}}\|v\|_{X^{-\alpha,-\beta}_{s_{1},\frac{1}{2},\sigma}}\Big),$ (A.3) where $C=C(s_{1},s_{2},\alpha,\sigma)$ . Proof. By duality, it suffices to prove $\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\xi\,\widehat{uv}\widehat{w}\,d\xi\,d\tau\leq C\Big(\|u\|_{X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s_{1},\frac{1}{2},\sigma}}\|v\|_{X^{-\alpha,-\beta}_{s_{2},\frac{1}{2},\sigma}}+\|u\|_{X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s_{2},\frac{1}{2},\sigma}}\|v\|_{X^{-\alpha,-\beta}_{s_{1},\frac{1}{2},\sigma}}\Big)\|w\|_{X^{\alpha,\beta}_{-s_{2},1-\sigma-\epsilon_{0}}},$ for any $u\in X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s_{2},\frac{1}{2},\sigma}$ , $v\in X^{-\alpha,-\beta}_{s_{2},\frac{1}{2},\sigma}$ and $w\in X^{\alpha,\beta}_{-s_{2},1-\sigma-\epsilon_{0}}$ . Denote $L_{1},L_{2},L_{3},M_{1},M_{2}$ in the same way as the above proof for Lemma A.1 , and define $\begin{array}[]{c}f_{1}(\xi_{1},\tau_{1})=\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{s_{1}}M_{1}\widehat{u}(\xi_{1},\tau_{1}),\ \,f_{2}(\xi_{2},\tau_{2})=\langle\xi_{2}\rangle^{s_{1}}M_{2}\widehat{v}(\xi_{2},\tau_{2}),\ \,f_{3}(\xi_{3},\tau_{3})=\langle\xi_{3}\rangle^{-s_{2}}\langle L_{3}\rangle^{1-\sigma-\epsilon_{0}}\widehat{w}(\xi_{3},\tau_{3}).\end{array}$ Denote $\rho=-s_{1}$ and $r=s_{2}-s_{1}\geq 0$ . Then similar to ( A.2 ), it reduces to show $\begin{split}&\quad\,\int\limits_{A}\langle\xi_{3}\rangle^{r}\frac{|\xi_{3}|\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{\rho}\langle\xi_{2}\rangle^{\rho}|f_{1}(\xi_{1},\tau_{1})f_{2}(\xi_{2},\tau_{2})f_{3}(\xi_{3},\tau_{3})|}{\langle\xi_{3}\rangle^{\rho}M_{1}M_{2}\langle L_{3}\rangle^{1-\sigma-\epsilon_{0}}}\\
&\leq C\,\Big(\|f_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{\xi\tau}}\|\langle\xi\rangle^{r}f_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{\xi\tau}}+\|\langle\xi\rangle^{r}f_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{\xi\tau}}\|f_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{\xi\tau}}\Big)\|f_{3}\|_{L^{2}_{\xi\tau}}.\end{split}$ (A.4) Since $r\geq 0$ and $\sum_{i=1}^{3}\xi_{i}=0$ , then $\langle\xi_{3}\rangle^{r}=\langle\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}\rangle^{r}\leq 2^{r}\big(\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{r}+\langle\xi_{2}\rangle^{r}\big)$ .
Consequently, $\text{LHS of (\ref{bilin, high-low reg})}\leq 2^{r}(I+II)$ ,
where $\displaystyle I=\int\limits_{A}\frac{|\xi_{3}|\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{\rho}\langle\xi_{2}\rangle^{\rho}|\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{r}f_{1}(\xi_{1},\tau_{1})|\,|f_{2}(\xi_{2},\tau_{2})f_{3}(\xi_{3},\tau_{3})|}{\langle\xi_{3}\rangle^{\rho}M_{1}M_{2}\langle L_{3}\rangle^{1-\sigma-\epsilon_{0}}},$ $\displaystyle II=\int\limits_{A}\frac{|\xi_{3}|\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{\rho}\langle\xi_{2}\rangle^{\rho}|f_{1}(\xi_{1},\tau_{1})|\,|\langle\xi_{2}\rangle^{r}f_{2}(\xi_{2},\tau_{2})f_{3}(\xi_{3},\tau_{3})|}{\langle\xi_{3}\rangle^{\rho}M_{1}M_{2}\langle L_{3}\rangle^{1-\sigma-\epsilon_{0}}}.$ According to Lemma A.1 , $I\leq C\,\|\langle\xi\rangle^{r}f_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{\xi\tau}}\|f_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{\xi\tau}}\|f_{3}\|_{L^{2}_{\xi\tau}},\quad II\leq C\,\|f_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{\xi\tau}}\|\langle\xi\rangle^{r}f_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{\xi\tau}}\|f_{3}\|_{L^{2}_{\xi\tau}}.$ Thus, ( A.4 ) is verified.
∎ Recalling the cut-off function $\eta\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R})$ which satisfies $\eta(t)=1$ on $(-1,1)$ and $\text{supp}\,\eta\subset(-2,2)$ , the following is a classical estimate on the Fourier restriction norms when the time is localized. Lemma A.3 . For any $s\in\mathbb{R}$ , $-\frac{1}{2}<b_{1}\leq b_{2}<\frac{1}{2}$ , $\alpha\neq 0$ , $|\beta|\leq 1$ and $0<T\leq 1$ , there exists a constant $C=C(s,b_{1},b_{2})$ such that $\Big\|\eta\Big(\frac{t}{T}\Big)w\Big\|_{X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,b_{1}}}\leq C\,T^{b_{2}-b_{1}}\|w\|_{X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,b_{2}}},\quad\forall\,w\in X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,b_{2}}.$ (A.5) The proof of this estimate is well-known, see e.g. Lemma 2.11 in [ Tao06 ] . Combining Lemma A.2 and Lemma A.3 yields the outcome below. Lemma A.4 . Let $-\frac{3}{4}<s_{1}\leq s_{2}\leq 3$ , $\alpha\neq 0$ , $|\beta|\leq 1$ and $0<T\leq 1$ . Then there exists $\epsilon_{0}=\epsilon_{0}(s_{1},s_{2},\alpha)>0$ such that for any $\sigma\in(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon_{0}]$ , $\Big\|\eta\Big(\frac{t}{T}\Big)(uv)_{x}\Big\|_{X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s_{2},\sigma-1}}\leq CT^{\epsilon_{0}}\Big(\|u\|_{X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s_{1},\frac{1}{2},\sigma}}\|v\|_{X^{-\alpha,-\beta}_{s_{2},\frac{1}{2},\sigma}}+\|u\|_{X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s_{2},\frac{1}{2},\sigma}}\|v\|_{X^{-\alpha,-\beta}_{s_{1},\frac{1}{2},\sigma}}\Big),$ (A.6) where $C=C(s_{1},s_{2},\alpha,\sigma)$ . Proof. Firstly, we choose $\epsilon_{0}$ as that in Lemma A.2 . Then for any $\sigma\in(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon_{0}]$ , we choose $w=(uv)_{x}$ , $s=s_{2}$ , $b_{1}=\sigma-1$ and $b_{2}=\sigma-1+\epsilon_{0}$ in ( A.5 ) to obtain $\Big\|\eta\Big(\frac{t}{T}\Big)(uv)_{x}\Big\|_{X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s_{2},\sigma-1}}\leq CT^{\epsilon_{0}}\|(uv)_{x}\|_{X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s_{2},\sigma-1}}.$ Then we can apply Lemma A.2 to verify ( A.6 ).
∎ Similar to Lemma A.3 , the estimate for modified Fourier restriction norms of the localized (in time) Duhamel term associated with the semigroup operator $W^{\alpha,\beta}_{R}$ can also be established. Lemma A.5 . Let $s\in\mathbb{R}$ , $\alpha\neq 0$ , $|\beta|\leq 1$ , $\frac{1}{2}<\sigma<1$ and $0<T\leq 1$ . Then there exists a constant $C=C(s,\alpha,\sigma)$ such that $\left\|\eta\Big(\frac{t}{T}\Big)\int^{t}_{0}W_{R}^{\alpha,\beta}(t-\tau)F\,d\tau\right\|_{X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,\frac{1}{2},\sigma}}\leq C\Big\|\eta\Big(\frac{t}{2T}\Big)F\Big\|_{X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,\sigma-1}}.$ (A.7) Proof. Define $g(x,t)=\eta\big(\frac{t}{2T}\big)F(x,t)$ . Then it is easily seen that LHS of ( A.7 ) $\displaystyle=\left\|\eta\Big(\frac{t}{T}\Big)\int^{t}_{0}W_{R}^{\alpha,\beta}(t-\tau)g\,d\tau\right\|_{X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,\frac{1}{2},\sigma}}.$ Then similar to Lemma 3.4 (also see Lemma 2.1 in [ GTV97 ] ), we have $\displaystyle\left\|\eta\Big(\frac{t}{T}\Big)\int^{t}_{0}W_{R}^{\alpha,\beta}(t-\tau)g\,d\tau\right\|_{X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,\frac{1}{2},\sigma}}$ $\displaystyle\leq C\|g\|_{X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,\sigma-1}}=C\Big\|\eta\Big(\frac{t}{2T}\Big)F\Big\|_{X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,\sigma-1}}.$ ∎ Now we have developed all the tools needed to justify Lemma 6.7 . Proof of Lemma 6.7 . Firstly, we choose $\epsilon_{0}=\epsilon_{0}(s_{1},s_{2},\alpha)>0$ as that in Lemma A.4 . Then for any $\sigma\in(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon_{0}]$ , we apply Lemma A.5 , with $s=s_{2}$ and $F=(w_{1}w_{2})_{x}$ , to conclude that $\left\|\eta\Big(\frac{t}{T}\Big)\int^{t}_{0}W_{R}^{\alpha,\beta}(t-\tau)(w_{1}w_{2})_{x}\right\|_{X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s_{2},\frac{1}{2},\sigma}}\leq C\Big\|\eta\Big(\frac{t}{2T}\Big)(w_{1}w_{2})_{x}\Big\|_{X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s_{2},\sigma-1}}.$ Then it follows from Lemma A.4 that $\Big\|\eta\Big(\frac{t}{2T}\Big)(w_{1}w_{2})_{x}\Big\|_{X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s_{2},\sigma-1}}\leq CT^{\epsilon_{0}}\Big(\|w_{1}\|_{X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s_{1},\frac{1}{2},\sigma}}\|w_{2}\|_{X^{-\alpha,-\beta}_{s_{2},\frac{1}{2},\sigma}}+\|w_{1}\|_{X^{\alpha,\beta}_{s_{2},\frac{1}{2},\sigma}}\|w_{2}\|_{X^{-\alpha,-\beta}_{s_{1},\frac{1}{2},\sigma}}\Big),$ where $C=C(s_{1},s_{2},\alpha,\sigma)$ . Combining the above two estimates leads to ( 6.9 ) in Lemma 6.7 .
