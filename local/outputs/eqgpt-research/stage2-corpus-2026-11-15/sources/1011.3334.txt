The equations arise as steady state equations of an age-structured predator-prey system with spatial dispersion. Based
on unilateral global bifurcation methods for Fredholm operators and on maximal regularity for parabolic equations, global bifurcation of positive solutions is derived. Keywords: cross-diffusion, age structure, global bifurcation, Fredholm operator, maximal regularity 1 Introduction Consider the situation that an age-structured prey and an age-structured predator population inhabit the same spatial region $\Omega$ , that the individuals of both populations undergo spatial fluctuation and that the predator population exerts a repulsive pressure on the prey population. If $u=u(t,a,x)\geq 0$ and $v=v(t,a,x)\geq 0$ denote the density of the prey and the predator population, respectively, at time $t\geq 0$ , age $a\in[0,a_{m})$ for a maximal age $a_{m}>0$ , and spatial position $x\in\Omega$ , a simple model reads $\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+\partial_{a}u-\Delta_{x}\left(\left(\delta_{1}+\gamma v\right)u\right)$ $\displaystyle=-\alpha_{1}u^{2}-\alpha_{2}uv\ ,\quad t>0\ ,\quad a\in(0,a_{m})\ ,\quad x\in\Omega\ ,$ (1.1) $\displaystyle\partial_{t}v+\partial_{a}v-\delta_{2}\Delta_{x}v$ $\displaystyle=-\beta_{1}v^{2}+\beta_{2}vu\ ,\quad t>0\ ,\quad a\in(0,a_{m})\ ,\quad x\in\Omega\ .$ (1.2) These equations are subject to the nonlocal age boundary conditions $\displaystyle u(t,0,x)$ $\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}B_{1}(a)\,u(t,a,x)\,\mathrm{d}a\ ,\quad t>0\ ,\quad x\in\Omega\ ,$ (1.3) $\displaystyle v(t,0,x)$ $\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}B_{2}(a)\,v(t,a,x)\,\mathrm{d}a\ ,\quad t>0\ ,\quad x\in\Omega\ ,$ (1.4) the spatial boundary conditions $\displaystyle u(t,a,x)$ $\displaystyle=0\ ,\quad t>0\ ,\quad a\in(0,a_{m})\ ,\quad x\in\partial\Omega\ ,$ (1.5) $\displaystyle v(t,a,x)$ $\displaystyle=0\ ,\quad t>0\ ,\quad a\in(0,a_{m})\ ,\quad x\in\partial\Omega\ ,$ (1.6) and are supplemented with time initial conditions.
The $\Delta_{x}$ -term in ( 1.1 ) describes spatial movement of prey individuals. Besides intrinsic dispersion with coefficient $\delta_{1}>0$ , it reflects an increase of the dispersive force on the prey by repulsive interference with an increase of the predator population. Here, $\gamma\geq 0$ is the predator population pressure coefficient. We refer to [ 24 ] for a derivation of such kind of models (without age-structure). The right hand sides of ( 1.1 ) and ( 1.2 ) take into account intra- and inter-specific interactions of the two populations with positive coefficients $\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta_{1},\beta_{2}>0$ . Equations ( 1.5 ) and ( 1.6 ) describe creation of new individuals
with nonnegative birth rates $B_{1}$ and $B_{2}$ .
The reader is referred to [ 32 ] and the references therein for further information about linear and nonlinear age-structured population equations with (and without) spatial dispersal. Although such models have a long history, there does not seem to be much literature about equations with nonlinear diffusion. The well-posedness of equations ( 1.1 )-( 1.6 ) might be derived within the general framework presented in [ 28 ] though the results therein do not directly apply due to the cross-diffusion term. We shall remark that the model considered herein is a rather simple biological model and there may be more accurate models, e.g. with different maximal ages, nonlocal dependences in the reaction terms etc. The aim of the present paper is thus rather to provide a mathematical framework to treat parabolic equations with cross-diffusion and nonlocal initial conditions. Namely, the present paper is dedicated to positive equilibrium (i.e. time-independent) solutions to ( 1.1 )-( 1.6 ) which shall be established based on global bifurcation methods. We write the birth rates in the form $B_{1}(a)=\eta b_{1}(a)$ and $B_{2}(a)=\xi b_{2}(a)$ , where $b_{1}$ , $b_{2}$ are some fixed birth profiles and the parameters $\eta$ , $\xi$ measuring the intensities of the fertility shall serve as bifurcation parameters.
Aiming at a simple and compact notation, we let $b:=b_{1}=b_{2}$ and $\delta_{1}=\delta_{2}=1$ .
We emphasize that these simplifications are made merely for the sake of readability and do not impact in any way on the mathematical analysis to follow. To shorten notation further, we shall agree upon the following convention: given a function defined on $J:=[0,a_{m}]$ and denoted by a lower case letter, say $u$ or $v$ , we use the corresponding capital letter $U$ or $V$ to denote its age-integral with weight $b$ , i.e., $U:=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a)u(a)\,\mathrm{d}a\ ,\quad V:=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a)v(a)\,\mathrm{d}a\ .$ (1.7) Depending on the values of the parameters $\eta$ and $\xi$ , we are looking for nonnegative and nontrivial functions $u=u(a,x)$ and $v=v(a,x)$ satisfying the nonlinear parabolic system with cross-diffusion $\displaystyle\partial_{a}u-\Delta_{x}\big((1+\gamma v)u\big)$ $\displaystyle=-\alpha_{1}u^{2}-\alpha_{2}uv\ ,\quad a\in(0,a_{m})\ ,\quad x\in\Omega\ ,$ (1.8) $\displaystyle\partial_{a}v-\Delta_{x}v$ $\displaystyle=-\beta_{1}v^{2}+\beta_{2}vu\ ,\,\quad a\in(0,a_{m})\ ,\quad x\in\Omega\ ,$ (1.9) subject to the nonlocal initial conditions $\displaystyle u(0,x)$ $\displaystyle=\eta U\ ,\quad x\in\Omega\ ,$ (1.10) $\displaystyle v(0,x)$ $\displaystyle=\xi V\ ,\quad x\in\Omega\ ,$ (1.11) and spatial Dirichlet boundary conditions $\displaystyle u(a,x)$ $\displaystyle=0\ ,\quad a\in(0,a_{m})\ ,\quad x\in\partial\Omega\ ,$ (1.12) $\displaystyle v(a,x)$ $\displaystyle=0\ ,\quad a\in(0,a_{m})\ ,\quad x\in\partial\Omega\ .$ (1.13) Clearly, of particular interest are coexistence states , that is, solutions $(u,v)$ with both components $u$ and $v$ nontrivial and nonnegative. Based on bifurcation techniques, existence of equilibrium solutions was established in [ 30 , 31 ] for similar systems with linear diffusion and in [ 26 , 27 , 29 ] for a single equation with nonlinear diffusion. Except for [ 29 ] , the bifurcation parameter was also chosen to be a measure for the intensity of the fertility as above. Prior to the just cited papers, bifurcation methods were used in [ 9 ] to derive positive equilibrium solutions for a single equations with linear diffusion. We also refer to [ 15 ] where the large time behavior of population dynamics with age-dependence and linear spatial diffusion was analyzed. More attention attracted than the age-structured parabolic equations so far have related elliptic systems of the form $-\Delta_{x}[(1+\gamma v)u]=u(\mu-u\pm cv)\ ,\quad-\Delta_{x}v=v(\lambda-v\pm bu)$ in $\Omega$ subject to Dirichlet conditions on $\partial\Omega$ with positive constants $\mu,\lambda,b,c$ ;
both for the case of linear diffusion $\gamma=0$ (e.g., see [ 3 , 4 , 8 , 17 , 18 ] and the references therein) and the case with cross-diffusion $\gamma>0$ (e.g., see [ 10 , 11 , 13 , 14 , 23 ] and the references therein). It is worthwhile to remark that for such elliptic equations with cross-diffusion, the transformation $z:=(1+\gamma v)u$ leads to a semilinear elliptic system for $z$ and $v$ , for which, when written in the form $(z,v)=S(z,v)$ , the corresponding solution operator $S$ enjoys suitable compactness properties. Moreover, for such a system, positivity and a-priori bounds of solutions may be derived using the maximum principle, e.g. [ 10 , 13 , 23 ] . For the nonlocal parabolic equations ( 1.7 )-( 1.13 ) under consideration herein, however, a corresponding transformation still yields nonlinear second order terms with respect to the spatial variable (or first order time derivatives). Consequently, the underlying solution operator does not enjoy similar compactness properties. As we are interested in global bifurcation of positive solutions, it is thus not clear how to apply directly the unilateral global bifurcation methods of [ 16 , 21 ] as in [ 30 ] , which require compactness of the underlying solution operators. To overcome this deficiency, we shall invoke recent results of Shi $\&$ Wang [ 23 ] on unilateral global bifurcation for Fredholm operators, which are based on López-Gómez’s interpretation [ 16 ] of the global alternative of Rabinowitz [ 21 ] and on the global bifurcation results of Pejsachowicz $\&$ Rabier [ 19 ] . These results yield a (global) continuum of positive solutions.
We shall also point out that, due to the cross-diffusion term, no comparison principle in the spirit of [ 30 , Lem.3.2] is available. In some cases, the lack of such a comparison principle prevents us from determining which of the possible alternatives the constructed continuum of coexistence states satisfies (see Theorem 2.3 and Theorem 2.4 below). 2 Main Results We suppose throughout this paper that the birth profile $b\in L_{\infty}^{+}((0,a_{m}))\ \text{with}\ b(a)>0\ \text{for $a$ near $a_{m}$}$ (2.1) is normalized such that $\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a)e^{-\lambda_{1}a}\,\mathrm{d}a=1\ ,$ (2.2) where $\lambda_{1}>0$ denotes the principal eigenvalue of $-\Delta_{x}$ on $\Omega$ subject to Dirichlet boundary conditions on $\partial\Omega$ . Before stating our results on coexistence states in more detail, we first recall some auxiliary results from [ 30 ] about semi-trivial states, that is, about solutions $(u,v)$ with one vanishing component. Taking for instance $v\equiv 0$ in ( 1.8 )-( 1.9 ) and using convention ( 1.7 ), we obtain the reduced problem $\partial_{a}u-\Delta_{x}u=-\alpha_{1}u^{2}\ ,\quad u(0,\cdot)=\eta U\ ,$ (2.3) subject to Dirichlet boundary conditions.
Introducing, for $q\in(n+2,\infty)$ fixed, the solution space $\mathbb{W}_{q}:=L_{q}((0,a_{m}),W_{q,D}^{2}(\Omega))\cap W_{q}^{1}((0,a_{m}),L_{q}(\Omega))\ ,$ where $W_{q,D}^{2}(\Omega):=\{u\in W_{q}^{2}(\Omega)\,;\,u=0\,\text{on}\,\partial\Omega\}\ ,$ and letting $\mathbb{W}_{q}^{+}$ denote its positive cone, the following result was proved in [ 30 , Thm.2.1, Cor.3.3, Lem.3.6, Cor.3.7] : Theorem 2.1 . Suppose ( 2.1 ) and ( 2.2 ).
For each $\eta>1$ there is a unique solution $u_{\eta}\in\mathbb{W}_{q}^{+}\setminus\{0\}$ to equation ( 2.3 ). The mapping $(\eta\mapsto u_{\eta})$ belongs to $C^{\infty}((1,\infty),\mathbb{W}_{q})$ and satisfies $\|u_{\eta}\|_{\mathbb{W}_{q}}\rightarrow 0$ as $\eta\rightarrow 1$ and $\|u_{\eta}\|_{\mathbb{W}_{q}}\rightarrow\infty$ as $\eta\rightarrow\infty$ . For $\eta>1$ and $a\in J$ , the derivative $\partial_{\eta}u_{\eta}(a)$ is a (strictly) positive function on $\Omega$ .
If $\eta_{1}>\eta_{2}$ , then $u_{\eta_{1}}\geq u_{\eta_{2}}$ . Finally, if $\eta\leq 1$ , then ( 2.3 ) has no solution in $\mathbb{W}_{q}^{+}\setminus\{0\}$ . Given $\xi>1$ , we let $v_{\xi}\in\mathbb{W}_{q}^{+}\setminus\{0\}$ denote the unique solution to the corresponding equation for $v$ when taking $u\equiv 0$ in ( 1.8 )-( 1.9 ). Though we shall work in the solution space $\mathbb{W}_{q}$ , we remark that solutions to ( 1.7 )-( 1.13 ), so in particular $u_{\eta}$ and $v_{\xi}$ , are smooth with respect to both $a\in J$ and $x\in\Omega$ (see Lemma 3.7 below). 2.1 Bifurcation with Respect to the Parameter $\eta$ We first shall keep $\xi$ fixed and regard $\eta$ as bifurcation parameter. We thus write $(\eta,u,v)$ for solutions to ( 1.7 )-( 1.13 ) with $u$ , $v$ belonging to $\mathbb{W}_{q}^{+}$ . Theorem 2.1 entails, for any $\xi\geq 0$ , the semi-trivial branch $\mathfrak{B}_{1}:=\{(\eta,u_{\eta},0)\,;\,\eta>1\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}\times(\mathbb{W}_{q}^{+}\setminus\{0\})\times\mathbb{W}_{q}^{+}$ (2.4) of solutions. For $\xi>1$ , an additional semi-trivial branch $\mathfrak{B}_{2}:=\{(\eta,0,v_{\xi})\,;\,\eta\geq 0\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{W}_{q}^{+}\times(\mathbb{W}_{q}^{+}\setminus\{0\})\ $ (2.5) exists. In the latter case, a continuum of coexistence states bifurcates from $\mathfrak{B}_{2}$ : Theorem 2.2 . Suppose ( 2.1 )-( 2.2 ) and let $\xi>1$ . There exists $\eta_{0}:=\eta_{0}(\xi)>0$ such that an unbounded continuum $\mathfrak{C}$ emanates from $(\eta_{0},0,v_{\xi})\in\mathfrak{B}_{2}$ , and $\mathfrak{C}\setminus\{(\eta_{0},0,v_{\xi})\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}\times(\mathbb{W}_{q}^{+}\setminus\{0\})\times(\mathbb{W}_{q}^{+}\setminus\{0\})$ consists of coexistence states $(\eta,u,v)$ to ( 1.7 )-( 1.13 ).
Near the bifurcation point $(\eta_{0},0,v_{\xi})$ , $\mathfrak{C}$ is a continuous curve. There is no other bifurcation point on $\mathfrak{B}_{2}$ or on $\mathfrak{B}_{1}$ to positive coexistence states. The value of $\eta_{0}(\xi)$ corresponding to the bifurcation point is determined in ( 3.30 ). We turn to the case $\xi<1$ , which is more involved. Recall that $\mathfrak{B}_{1}$ is the only semi-trivial branch of solutions in this case. Theorem 2.3 . Suppose ( 2.1 )-( 2.2 ). There is $\delta\in[0,1)$ with the property that, given $\xi\in(\delta,1)$ , there exists $\eta_{1}:=\eta_{1}(\xi)>1$ such that a continuum $\mathfrak{S}$ emanates from $(\eta_{1},u_{\eta_{1}},0)\in\mathfrak{B}_{1}$ , and $\mathfrak{S}\setminus\{(\eta_{1},u_{\eta_{1}},0)\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}\times(\mathbb{W}_{q}^{+}\setminus\{0\})\times(\mathbb{W}_{q}^{+}\setminus\{0\})$ consists of coexistence states $(\eta,u,v)$ of ( 1.7 )-( 1.13 ). The continuum $\mathfrak{S}$ (i) is unbounded, or (ii) connects $(\eta_{1},u_{\eta_{1}},0)$ to a solution $(\eta_{*},u,v)$ of ( 1.7 )-( 1.13 ) with $\eta_{*}\in(0,1)$ and $u,v\in\mathbb{W}_{q}^{+}\setminus\{0\}$ . Near the bifurcation point $(\eta_{1},u_{\eta_{1}},0)$ , $\mathfrak{S}$ is a continuous curve. There is no other bifurcation point on $\mathfrak{B}_{1}$ to positive coexistence states. The values of $\delta$ and $\eta_{1}(\xi)$ are given in ( 4.5 ) and ( 4.6 ), respectively. Note that alternative (i) above always occurs if cross-diffusion is not taken into account, i.e. if $\gamma=0$ , see [ 31 , Thm.1.5] . Alternative (ii) stems from a technical condition, and we conjecture that alternative (i) is the generic case also for the present situation where $\gamma>0$ . However, as occurrence of alternative (ii) is not ruled out by our analysis, Theorem 2.3 is rather a local bifurcation result in this regard. 2.2 Bifurcation with Respect to the Parameter $\xi$ Since the cross-diffusion term involves the predator density $v$ , bifurcation from semi-trivial solution branches is more intricate when regarding $\xi$ (the measure of the predator fertility) as parameter and keeping $\eta$ fixed. We shall only consider the situation $\eta>1$ , the reason being explained in Remark 5.21 . We now write $(\xi,u,v)$ for solutions to ( 1.7 )-( 1.13 ). Then, there are two semi-trivial branches of solutions $\mathfrak{T}_{1}:=\{(\xi,0,v_{\xi})\,;\,\xi>1\}\ ,\qquad\mathfrak{T}_{2}:=\{(\xi,u_{\eta},0)\,;\,\xi\geq 0\}\ .$ Also in this case, bifurcation from the semi-trivial branch $\mathfrak{T}_{2}$ occurs: Theorem 2.4 . Suppose ( 2.1 )-( 2.2 ) and let $\eta>1$ . There exists $\xi_{0}:=\xi_{0}(\eta)\in(0,1)$ such that a continuum $\mathfrak{R}$ of solutions to ( 1.7 )-( 1.13 ) emanates from $(\xi_{0},u_{\eta},0)\in\mathfrak{T}_{2}$ satisfying the alternatives (i) $\mathfrak{R}\setminus\{(\xi_{0},u_{\eta},0)\}$ is unbounded in $\mathbb{R}^{+}\times(\mathbb{W}_{q}^{+}\setminus\{0\})\times(\mathbb{W}_{q}^{+}\setminus\{0\})$ , or (ii) $\mathfrak{R}$ connects $\mathfrak{T}_{2}$ with $\mathfrak{T}_{1}$ . Except for the bifurcation point(s), $\mathfrak{R}$ consists exclusively of coexistence states.
Near the bifurcation point $(\xi_{0},u_{\eta},0)$ , $\mathfrak{R}$ is a continuous curve. There is no other bifurcation point on $\mathfrak{T}_{2}$ or on $\mathfrak{T}_{1}$ to positive coexistence states. The values of $\xi_{0}(\eta)$ and $\xi_{1}=\xi_{1}(\eta)>1$ , corresponding to the connection point $(\xi_{1},0,v_{\xi_{1}})\in\mathfrak{T}_{1}$ if alternative (ii) occurs, are determined in ( 5.1 ) and ( 5.2 ), respectively. Given the assumptions of this theorem, if $\gamma=0$ , one can show that alternative (ii) occurs if $\eta$ is less than a certain value or if additional assumptions on the birth rates and on $\alpha_{j}$ , $\beta_{j}$ are imposed, see [ 30 , Thm.2.2] . As these proofs are along the lines of the proof of Theorem 2.2 , these sections are kept rather short. 3 Proof of Theorem 2.2 3.1 Notations and Preliminaries Given two Banach spaces $F_{1}$ and $F_{0}$ , we shall use the notation $\mathcal{L}(F_{1},F_{0})$ for the set of bounded linear operators and $\mathcal{K}(F_{1},F_{0})$ for the set of compact linear operators between $F_{1}$ and $F_{0}$ . We set $\mathcal{L}(F_{0}):=\mathcal{L}(F_{0},F_{0})$ and similarly $\mathcal{K}(F_{0}):=\mathcal{K}(F_{0},F_{0})$ . The set of toplinear isomorphisms between $F_{1}$ and $F_{0}$ is denoted by $\mathcal{L}is(F_{1},F_{0})$ . Let $-\Delta_{D}$ denote the negative Laplacian on $L_{q}:=L_{q}(\Omega)$ subject to Dirichlet boundary conditions, that is, $-\Delta_{D}:=-\Delta_{x}$ with domain $W_{q,D}^{2}$ , where $W_{q,D}^{\kappa}:=W_{q,D}^{\kappa}(\Omega):=\{u\in W_{q}^{\kappa}\,;\,u=0\ \text{on}\ \partial\Omega\}$ for $\kappa>1/q$ and $W_{q,D}^{\kappa}:=W_{q}^{\kappa}(\Omega)$ for $0\leq\kappa<1/q$ . As $q>n+2$ , we have $W_{q,D}^{2-2/q}\hookrightarrow C^{1}(\bar{\Omega})$ by the Sobolev embedding theorem. In particular, $\mathrm{int}(W_{q,D}^{2-2/q,+})\not=\emptyset$ , that is, the interior of the positive cone of $W_{q,D}^{2-2/q}$ is not empty.
Let $\gamma_{0}$ denote the trace operator defined by $\gamma_{0}u:=u(0)$ for $u\in\mathbb{W}_{q}$ , which is well-defined owing to the embedding [ 2 , III.Thm.4.10.2] $\mathbb{W}_{q}\hookrightarrow C\big([0,a_{m}],W_{q,D}^{2-2/q}\big)\ .$ In fact, we have, due to the interpolation inequality [ 2 , I.Thm.2.11.1] , $\mathbb{W}_{q}\hookrightarrow C^{1-1/q-\vartheta}([0,a_{m}],W_{q,D}^{2\vartheta})\ ,\quad 0\leq\vartheta\leq 1-1/q\ .$ (3.1) In the following, we let $\dot{\mathbb{W}}_{q}^{+}:=\mathbb{W}_{q}^{+}\setminus\{0\}$ .
Recall that an operator $A\in\mathcal{L}(W_{q,D}^{2},L_{q})$ is said to have maximal $L_{q}$ -regularity provided $(\partial_{a}+A,\gamma_{0})\in\mathcal{L}is(\mathbb{W}_{q},\mathbb{L}_{q}\times W_{q,D}^{2-2/q})\ ,$ where $\mathbb{L}_{q}:=L_{q}(J,L_{q})$ . Recall that $-\Delta_{D}$ has maximal $L_{q}$ -regularity. If $A:J\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(W_{q,D}^{\kappa},L_{q})$ for some $\kappa\geq 0$ , we write $(Au)(a):=A(a)u(a)$ for $a\in J$ and $u:J\rightarrow W_{q,D}^{\kappa}$ . The following perturbation result is proved in [ 20 , Cor.3.4] , which is also valid for $\mathbb{R}^{k}$ -valued functions, i.e., if $L_{q}$ is replaced by $L_{q}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})$ etc.: Lemma 3.5 . Let $A\in C(J,\mathcal{L}(W_{q,D}^{2},L_{q}))$ be such that $A(a)$ has maximal $L_{q}$ -regularity for each $a\in J$ and let $B\in L_{q}(J,\mathcal{L}(W_{q,D}^{2-2/q},L_{q}))$ . Then $(\partial_{a}+A+B,\gamma_{0})\in\mathcal{L}is(\mathbb{W}_{q},\mathbb{L}_{q}\times W_{q,D}^{2-2/q})$ . denote the unique parabolic evolution operator corresponding to $-\Delta_{D}+h\in C^{\varrho}\big(J,\mathcal{L}(W_{q,D}^{2},L_{q})\big)$ ,

Note that the evolution operator is positive, i.e. $\Pi_{[h]}(a,\sigma)\Phi\in L_{q}^{+}\ ,\quad 0\leq\sigma\leq a\leq a_{m}\ ,\quad\Phi\in L_{q}^{+}\ .$ Since $-\Delta_{D}$ has maximal $L_{q}$ -regularity, it follows from Lemma 3.5 that $(\partial_{a}-\Delta_{D}+h,\gamma_{0})\in\mathcal{L}is(\mathbb{W}_{q},\mathbb{L}_{q}\times W_{q,D}^{2-2/q})$ (3.2) and, in particular, $\Pi_{[h]}(\cdot,0)\Phi\in\mathbb{W}_{q}$ for $\Phi\in W_{q,D}^{2-2/q}$ .
We set $H_{[h]}:=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a)\,\Pi_{[h]}(a,0)\,\mathrm{d}a\ .$ Then $H_{[h]}\in\mathcal{K}(W_{q,D}^{2-2/q})$ owing to standard regularizing effects of the parabolic evolution operator $\Pi_{[h]}$ and the compact embedding $W_{q,D}^{2}\hookrightarrow W_{q,D}^{2-2/q}$ . Moreover, as $\Pi_{[h]}(a,0)$ for $a\in(0,a_{m})$ is strongly positive on $W_{q,D}^{2-2/q}$ by [ 6 , Cor.13.6] , the same holds true for $H_{[h]}$ due to ( 2.1 ), that is, $H_{[h]}\Phi\in\mathrm{int}(W_{q,D}^{2-2/q,+})\ ,\quad\Phi\in W_{q,D}^{2-2/q,+}\setminus\{0\}\ .$ (3.3) The corresponding spectral radius $r(H_{[h]})$ can thus be characterized according to the Krein-Rutman theorem [ 1 , Thm.3.2] (see [ 30 , Lem.3.1] ): Lemma 3.6 . For $h\in C^{\varrho}(J,C(\bar{\Omega}))$ with $\varrho>0$ , the spectral radius $r(H_{[h]})>0$ is a simple eigenvalue of $H_{[h]}$ with a corresponding eigenfunction belonging to $\mathrm{int}(W_{q,D}^{2-2/q,+})$ . It is the only eigenvalue of $H_{[h]}$ with a positive eigenfunction. Moreover, if $h$ and $g$ both belong to $C^{\varrho}(J,C(\bar{\Omega}))$ with $g\geq h$ but $g\not\equiv h$ , then $r(H_{[g]})<r(H_{[h]})$ . If both $u\equiv 0$ and $v\equiv 0$ , then the limit $\psi\in\dot{\mathbb{W}}_{q}^{+}$ of $\psi_{j}:=v/\|v_{j}\|_{\mathbb{W}_{q}}$ satisfies $\partial_{a}\psi-\Delta_{D}\psi=0$ with $\psi(0)=\xi\Psi$ leading to the contradiction $1=\xi r(H_{[0]})=\xi$ due to ( 3.4 ). If $v\equiv 0$ but $u\not\equiv 0$ , then $u\in\dot{\mathbb{W}}_{q}^{+}$ satisfies $\partial_{a}u-\Delta_{D}u=-\alpha_{1}u^{2}$ and $u(0)=\eta U$ and thus $u=u_{\eta}$ with necessarily $\eta>1$ by the uniqueness statement of Theorem 2.1 . Hence $\psi\in\dot{\mathbb{W}}_{q}^{+}$ satisfies $\partial_{a}\psi-\Delta_{D}\psi=\beta_{2}u_{\eta}\psi$ with $\psi(0)=\xi\Psi$ giving the contradiction $\eta=\eta_{1}$ by ( 4.6 ). If $u\equiv 0$ but $v\not\equiv 0$ , then $v\in\dot{\mathbb{W}}_{q}^{+}$ satisfies $\partial_{a}v-\Delta_{D}v=-\beta_{1}v^{2}$ and $v(0)=\xi V$ what is impossible according to Theorem 2.1 since $\xi<1$ . Therefore, neither $u\equiv 0$ nor $v\equiv 0$ and we conclude $\eta=\eta_{*}$ . This proves the claim.
∎ Lemma 4.20 . There is no other bifurcation point to positive coexistence states on $\mathfrak{B}_{1}$ . Proof. The assumption $(\eta,u_{\eta},0)\in\mathfrak{B}_{1}$ being a bifurcation point to positive coexistence states corresponds to the case $\eta>1$ , $u\not\equiv 0$ , and $v\equiv 0$ in the proof of Lemma 4.19 and analogously implies $\eta=\eta_{1}$ .
∎ This completes the proof of Theorem 2.3 . 5 Proof of Theorem 2.4 Again, the main part of the proof of Theorem 2.4 is a straightforward modification of Section 3 , and we thus omit details. Let $\eta>1$ be fixed. Linearization around $(\xi,u_{\eta},0)\in\mathfrak{T}_{2}$ entails the existence of a continuum $\mathfrak{R}^{+}$ in $\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{W}_{q}\times\mathbb{W}_{q}$ of solutions to ( 1.7 )-( 1.13 ) bifurcating from $(\xi_{0},u_{\eta},0)$ , where $\xi_{0}:=\xi_{0}(\eta)\in(0,1)$ is given by $\xi_{0}:=\frac{1}{r(H_{[-\beta_{2}u_{\eta}]})}\ .$ (5.1) Near the bifurcation point $(\xi_{0},u_{\eta},0)$ , $\mathfrak{R}^{+}$ is a continuous curve in $\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\dot{\mathbb{W}}_{q}^{+}\times\dot{\mathbb{W}}_{q}^{+}$ and it can be shown exactly as in Lemma 3.15 or Lemma 4.19 that $\mathfrak{R}:=\mathfrak{R}^{+}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{W}_{q}^{+}\times\mathbb{W}_{q}^{+})$ satisfies the alternatives: (a) $\mathfrak{R}\setminus\{(\xi_{0},u_{\eta},0)\}$ is unbounded in $\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\dot{\mathbb{W}}_{q}^{+}\times\dot{\mathbb{W}}_{q}^{+}$ , or (b) $\mathfrak{R}$ reaches the boundary of $\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\dot{\mathbb{W}}_{q}^{+}\times\dot{\mathbb{W}}_{q}^{+}$ at some point $(\xi,u,v)\not=(\xi_{0},u_{\eta},0)$ with $u\equiv 0$ or $v\equiv 0$ . If (b) occurs, choose a sequence $(\xi_{j},u_{j},v_{j})$ in $\mathfrak{R}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\dot{\mathbb{W}}_{q}^{+}\times\dot{\mathbb{W}}_{q}^{+})$ converging toward $(\xi,u,v)\not=(\xi_{0},u_{\eta},0)$ . Putting $\phi_{j}:=u_{j}/\|u_{j}\|_{\mathbb{W}_{q}}$ and $\psi_{j}:=v_{j}/\|v_{j}\|_{\mathbb{W}_{q}}$ , we may assume that $\phi_{j}\rightarrow\phi$ and $\psi_{j}\rightarrow\psi$ in $\mathbb{W}_{q}$ . If both $u\equiv 0$ and $v\equiv 0$ , then $\phi\in\dot{\mathbb{W}}_{q}^{+}$ satisfies $\partial_{a}\phi-\Delta_{D}\phi=0$ with $\phi(0)=\eta\Phi$ and so $1=\eta r(H_{[0]})$ contradicting ( 3.4 ) and $\eta>1$ . If $u\not\equiv 0$ but $v\equiv 0$ , then $u=u_{\eta}$ according to Theorem 2.1 , and $\psi\in\dot{\mathbb{W}}_{q}^{+}$ satisfies $\partial_{a}\psi-\Delta_{D}\psi=\beta_{2}u_{\eta}\psi$ with $\psi(0)=\xi\Psi$ . Hence $\xi=\xi_{0}$ by ( 5.1 ) what is impossible since $(\xi,u,v)\not=(\xi_{0},u_{\eta},0)$ . Therefore, the only possibility is $u\equiv 0$ but $v\not\equiv 0$ . In this case necessarily $\xi>1$ and $v=v_{\xi}$ in view of Theorem 2.1 . So $\mathfrak{R}$ joins up with $\mathfrak{T}_{1}$ at $(\xi,0,v_{\xi})$ . We remark that then the relation $\eta r(G_{\xi})=1$ (5.2) with $G_{\xi}$ given in ( 3.29 ) must hold, since $\phi\in\dot{\mathbb{W}}_{q}^{+}$ satisfies ( 3.33 ). That no other bifurcation point(s) on $\mathfrak{T}_{2}$ or $\mathfrak{T}_{1}$ exist(s) is immediate by the previous observations. This yields Theorem 2.4 . Remark 5.21 . Since the operator $A_{\xi}$ in ( 3.28 ) does not yield a suitable maximum principle, there is no analogue of Lemma 3.6 for the spectral radius of $G_{\xi}$ and the only information we have on $r(G_{\xi})$ is that it is positive for each $\xi>1$ as observed in Section 3.2 . Consequently, given $\eta>0$ , we cannot decide a priori whether ( 5.2 ) holds for some $\xi>1$ . However, for $\eta>1$ , if $\mathfrak{R}$ joins up with $\mathfrak{T}_{1}$ , then ( 5.2 ) must occur and the connection point $(\xi,0,v_{\xi})$ on $\mathfrak{T}_{1}$ is determined by this relation. Then again, the relation ( 5.2 ) is also a sufficient condition for the existence of a continuous curve of positive coexistence solutions bifurcating from $\mathfrak{T}_{1}$ . The same difficulty arises when considering bifurcation from $\mathfrak{T}_{1}$ with respect to $\xi$ when $\eta<1$ is fixed. In this case, ( 5.2 ) is again a necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of a bifurcation point on $\mathfrak{T}_{1}$ to a curve of positive coexistence states, which then extends to an unbounded continuum.
