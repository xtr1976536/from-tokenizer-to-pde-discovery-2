This model is a three-component reaction-diffusion system that delineates the geographical expansion of an initially localized population of farmers into a region occupied by hunter-gatherers. By considering the scenario where farmers and hunter-gatherers possess identical motility, Aoki et al. previously concluded, through numerical simulations and some formal linearization arguments, that there are four different types of spreading behaviors depending on the parameter values.
In this paper, we concentrate on the general case for which farmers and hunter-gatherers possess different motility. By providing more sophisticated estimates, we not only theoretically justify for the spreading speed of the Aoki-Shida-Shigesada model, but also establish sharp estimates for the long-time behaviors of solutions. These estimates enable us to validate all four types of spreading behaviors observed by Aoki et al. . Key words: Farmer and hunter-gatherer model; long time behavior; spreading speed;  logarithmic correction 1 Introduction In 1996, Aoki, Shida and Shigesada [ 2 ] proposed the following three-component reaction-diffusion system to study the process of Neolithic transition from hunter-gatherers to farmers (actually, in [ 2 ] , they only considered the with case with same diffusion rate $D_{f}=D_{c}=D_{h}$ , but in the present paper we formulate the problem with different diffusion speeds): $\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}F=D_{f}\partial_{xx}F+r_{f}F(1-(F+C)/K),\\
&\partial_{t}C=D_{c}\partial_{xx}C+r_{c}C(1-(F+C)/K)+e(F+C)H,\ \ \ \ \ \ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}.\\
&\partial_{t}H=D_{h}\partial_{xx}H+r_{h}H(1-H/L)-e(F+C)H,\end{aligned}\right.$ The population densities of initial farmers, converted farmers and hunter-gatherers are represented by $F$ , $C$ and $H$ , respectively.
This model contains nine positive parameters: $D_{f}$ , $D_{c}$ and $D_{h}$ are the diffusion coefficients; $r_{f}$ , $r_{c}$ and $r_{h}$ are the intrinsic growth rates; $K$ and $L$ are the carrying capacities of farmers and hunter-gatherers; $e$ is the conversion rate of hunter-gatherers to farmers. Note that, in [ 2 ] , $D_{h}$ is always assumed to be equal to $D_{c}$ and $D_{f}$ . However, it is more natural to imagine that hunter-gatherers possess a different motility with farmers. As shown in [ 2 ] , by a suitable change of variables, the above system is converted to: $\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}F=\partial_{xx}F+aF(1-F-C),\\
&\partial_{t}C=d_{c}\partial_{xx}C+C(1-F-C)+sH(F+C),\\
&\partial_{t}H=d_{h}\partial_{xx}H+bH(1-H-g(F+C)),\end{aligned}\right.\quad\quad\mbox{in}\ \ \mathbb{R},$ (1.1) where $d_{c}=D_{c}/D_{f}$ , $d_{h}=D_{h}/D_{f}$ , $a=r_{f}/r_{c}$ , $b=r_{h}/r_{c}$ , $s=eL/r_{c}$ and $g=eK/r_{h}$ .
We consider the following initial condition $H(0,x)\equiv 1,\ \ C(0,x)\equiv 0,\ \ F(0,x)=F_{0}(x)\geq 0\ \ \ \ (F_{0}\not\equiv 0),$ (1.2) where $F_{0}(x)$ is a compactly supported continuous function. Such initial data indicates a localized population of farmers in a region that was originally occupied by hunter-gatherers. The ODE system related to the system ( 1.1 ) possesses the following four different types of steady states: $(F,C,H)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &(0,0,0),\ (0,0,1),\\
&(\widehat{F},\widehat{C},0),\ \ \mbox{where}\ \ \widehat{F}+\widehat{C}=1,\ \widehat{F}\geq 0,\ \widehat{C}\geq 0,\\
&(0,C^{*},H^{*}),\ \ \mbox{where}\ \ C^{*}=(1+s)/(1+sg),\ H^{*}=(1-g)/(1+sg).\end{aligned}\right.$ The first two steady states always exist and unstable. The third one is a line of neutral equilibria, which always exists and stable if $g\geq 1$ . The fourth one exists and is stable if and only if $g<1$ . As noted in [ 17 ] , we call the case $g\geq 1$ by high conversion rate case and the case $g<1$ by low conversion rate case throughout this paper. 1.1 Previous works In the previous paper [ 2 ] , Aoki et al. observed four different types of spreading behaviors depending on the parameter values, namely those of $a$ , $s$ and $g$ in ( 1.1 ) under the assumption that $d_{c}=d_{h}=1$ . Furthermore, by mainly on numerical simulations, but also some formal analysis of the minimal speed of the traveling wave, they found the speed of the spreading fronts is determined by $\max\{2\sqrt{a},2\sqrt{1+s}\}$ . In our previous work [ 17 ] , for the first time, we made mathematically rigorous studies to justify their claims with the same assumption on diffusion speeds. Moreover, we will conclude how do our results justify the four different spreading behavior. Front propagation for scalar reaction-diffusion equations has been studied extensively and there exist vast literature on this theme. Early in 1937, Fisher [ 11 ] and Kolmogorov, Petrovsky and Piskunov [ 16 ] introduced a scalar reaction-diffusion equation with monostable nonlinearity as a model equation in population genetics, which studies the propagation of dominant gene in a homogeneous environment. In particular, [ 16 ] made an important early analysis of the structure of the set of traveling waves for a special class of monostable reaction-diffusion equation, the so-called KPP equation. Application of reaction-diffusion equations to ecology was pioneered by Skellam [ 18 ] in 1951. As regards the propagation of fronts for solutions with compactly supported initial data (that is, the so-called “spreading front”) is concerned, the first rigorous mathematical results in multi-dimensional homogeneous environment were provided by Aronson and Weinberger [ 3 ] , for the case of monostable nonlinearity and also for bistable nonlinearity. As regards the propagation of fronts for solutions of the reaction-diffusion system, there exists a rich literature on the cooperate system and the competition system, for which the comparison principle still hold.
However, apart from some recent works such as [ 9 ] , much less is known about the spreading properties for systems for which the comparison principle does not hold. We first recall the classical spreading
result for the monostable scalar equation from Aronson and Weinberger [ 3 ] : Proposition 1.3 (Spreading for the scalar KPP equation ( [ 3 ] )) Consider the so-called KPP equation $\partial_{t}u(t,x)=d\partial_{xx}u(t,x)+f(u(t,x)),\ t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R},$ (1.4) wherein $f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})$ satisfies $f(0)=f(1)=0,\ f(u)>0\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ f^{\prime}(u)\leq f^{\prime}(0)\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ u\in(0,1).$ Define $c^{*}(d,f)=2\sqrt{df^{\prime}(0)}$ . Then, for any nontrivial compactly supported and continuous initial data $u_{0}(x)$ , the solution $u\equiv u(t,x;u_{0}(x))$ of ( 1.4 ): $\underset{t\to+\infty}{\lim}\underset{|x|\leq ct}{\sup}|1-u(t,x)|=0,\ 0<c<c^{*}(d,f),$ and $\underset{t\to+\infty}{\lim}\underset{|x|\geq ct}{\sup}u(t,x)=0,\ c>c^{*}(d,f).$ Moreover, the so-called spreading speed $c^{*}(d,f)$ coincides with the minimal speed of the traveling wave solution of admitted by ( 1.4 ). Next, we present our main results on the spreading properties of solutions of the system ( 1.1 ).
Throughout this paper, we define $c_{f}:=2\sqrt{a}$ , $c_{c}:=2\sqrt{d_{c}(1+s)}$ , and $c^{*}:=\max\{c_{f},c_{c}\}$ .
The following Theorem 1.4 and Theorem 1.5 can be regarded as an analogue of the well-known "hair-trigger effect" for scalar monostable equation [ 3 ] . Moreover, since $H_{0}(x)\equiv 1$ , one may find that the spreading speed of ( 1.1 ) is always determined by the larger value of $c_{f}$ and $c_{c}$ , which is only determined by the parameters in the $F$ -equation and $C$ -equation. Our first result is concerned with the analysis of the leading edge, which provides an upper estimate on the spreading speed. As we observed from Figure $1$ - $4$ , for all four cases, the behaviors of solutions are almost same on the leading edge. Theorem 1.4 Let $(F,C,H)$ be the solution of ( 1.1 ) with the initial data satisfying ( 1.2 ). Then for any $2\sqrt{a}<c_{1}$ and $c^{*}<c_{2}$ , it holds: $\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{c_{1}t\leq|x|}F(t,x)=0,$ (1.5) $\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{c_{2}t\leq|x|}\Big(C(t,x)+|1-H(t,x)|\Big)=0.$ (1.6) The most difficult part of the analysis is the behaviors of the solutions in the final zone, where hunter-gatherers, initial farmers and converted farmers heavily interact with each other. A large part of this paper is devoted to the analysis in this region.
Our second result deals with the propagation of farmers in the final zone, which provides a lower estimate on the spreading speed. Theorem 1.5 Let $(F,C,H)$ be the solution of ( 1.1 ) with the initial data satisfying ( 1.2 ). Then for any $c_{1}<c^{*}$ , it holds: $\liminf_{t\rightarrow\infty}\inf_{|x|\leq c_{1}t}\Big(F(t,x)+C(t,x)\Big)\geq 1,$ (1.7) $\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{|x|\leq c_{1}t}H(t,x)\leq\max\{0,1-g\}.$ (1.8) Remark 1.6 The values $2\sqrt{a}$ and $2\sqrt{d_{c}(1+s)}$ indicate the spreading speed of initial farmers and converted farmers respectively. More precisely, if we let $C\equiv 0$ in ( 1.1 ), then the spreading speed of initial farmers directly comes from the single equation $\partial_{t}F=\partial_{xx}F+aF(1-F-C).$ On the other hand, if we let $F\equiv 0$ in ( 1.1 ), then we get $\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}C=d_{c}\partial_{xx}C+C(1-F-C)+sH(F+C),\\
&\partial_{t}H=d_{h}\partial_{xx}H+bH(1-H-g(F+C)),\end{aligned}\right.\quad\quad\mbox{in}\ \ \mathbb{R}.$ The main results in [ 7 ] indicates the spreading speed of converted farmers is equal to $2\sqrt{d_{c}(1+s)}$ . At last, we show our main results about the asymptotic profiles of solutions in the final zone. The precise results will be presented in two cases: the case $a>d_{c}(1+s)$ and the case $a<d_{c}(1+s)$ , respectively. The critical case $a=d_{c}(1+s)$ will not be discussed in the present paper. Moreover, for the each case, the profiles of solutions are also depending on the value of $g$ . For the high conversion rate case, we not only show that all of the original hunter-gatherers convert to farmers at last, but also investigate the explicit profiles ( the bump phenomena ) of the $F$ -component and $C$ -component in the final zone. For the low conversion rate case, we find that the $F$ converges to $0$ global uniformly. Then,
we can investigate how the $C$ and $H$ behave in the finial zone by considering the dynamics of the underlying ODE system: $\left\{\begin{aligned} &C_{t}=C(1-C)+sCH,\\
&H_{t}=bH(1-H-gC).\end{aligned}\right.$ (1.9) We expect the solution of the PDE system ( 1.1 ) to uniformly converge to the equilibrium $(C^{*},H^{*})$ in the final zone as $t\to+\infty$ . We prove the conjecture by the established fact that a strict Lyapunov function exists. Theorem 1.7 Assume $d_{c}(1+s)>a$ . Let $(F,C,H)$ be the solution of ( 1.1 ) with the initial data satisfying ( 1.2 ). For any $g>0$ , one has $\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}}F(t,x)=0.$ (1.10) Moreover, it holds: (1) (high conversion rate case) if $g\geq 1$ , then for any $c_{1}<c^{*}$ , one has $\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{|x|\leq c_{1}t}\Big(|C(t,x)-1|+H(t,x)\Big)=0.$ (1.11) (2) (low conversion rate case) if $g<1$ , then for any $c_{1}<c^{*}$ , one has $\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{|x|\leq c_{1}t}\Big(|C(t,x)-C^{*}|+|H(t,x)-H^{*}|\Big)=0.$ (1.12) Remark 1.8 Different with Theorem 1.10 in [ 17 ] , here we do not need any hypothesis on values of $g$ and $b$ , due to a more involved analysis on the limit system. Roughly speaking, the profile of the solution in the region $|x|\leq\varepsilon_{*}t$ is more similar like the Heat equation type. Theorem 1.9 (Bump phenomenon) Assume $d_{c}(1+s)>a$ and $g>1$ . Denote $k^{*}:=\min\left(\frac{1}{2d_{c}},\frac{d_{c}}{2}\right),\quad d^{*}:=\max(1,d_{c}).$ Then for $\varepsilon_{*}>0$ small enough and $0<\theta<\frac{1}{2}$ , there exist $C_{2}>C_{1}>0$ , $C_{3}>0$ , and $T>0$ such that both $C_{1}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{4t}}\leq F(t,x)\leq C_{2}t^{-k^{*}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{4d^{*}t}},\ $ (1.13) $1-C_{2}t^{-k^{*}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{4d^{*}t}}\leq C(t,x)\leq 1-C_{1}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{4t}}+C_{3}t^{-(1+\theta)},$ (1.14) hold for $t\geq T$ , $|x|\leq\varepsilon_{*}t$ . Since there is little interaction between farmers and hunter-gatherers on the leading edge, the analysis of this zone is rather straightforward. We first deal with the analysis of $F$ and $C$ on the leading edge and begin the proof with some simple upper estimates on the spreading speed. From $C\geq 0$ for all $(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}$ , by a well-known super-solution $\bar{F}:=\min\{A_{1}e^{-\sqrt{a}(|x|-2\sqrt{a}t)},1\},$ (2.1) one has $\underset{t\to+\infty}{\lim}\underset{|x|\geq ct}{\sup}F(t,x)\leq\underset{t\to+\infty}{\lim}\underset{|x|\geq ct}{\sup}\bar{F}(t,x)=0\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ c>c^{*}\geq 2\sqrt{a},$ (2.2) provided that $A_{1}$ is large enough such that $\bar{F}(0,x)\geq F(0,x)$ . If not, then $\psi(0,x;\eta_{0})\leq p_{\eta_{1}}(x)$ for $x\in\mathbb{R}$ , which implies $p_{\eta_{1}}\geq p_{\eta_{0}}$ , and obtains a contradiction. Then, we consider $\eta^{*}=\sup\{\eta\in[\eta_{1},\eta_{0}]\ ;\ \psi(0,x;\eta)\leq p_{\eta_{1}}(x)\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}\}.$ One can deduce from the comparison principle and the strong maximum principle that $\psi(0,x;\eta^{*})<\psi(t,x;\eta^{*})<p_{\eta_{1}}(x)\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}.$ (4.38) On the other hand, from the definition of $\eta^{*}$ , since $\psi$ has compact support $B_{R}$ , there exists $x_{0}\in B_{R}$ such that $\psi(0,x_{0};\eta^{*})=p_{\eta_{1}}(x_{0})$ , which reaches a contradiction. $\square$ Now, we can choose $\eta$ sufficiently small such that $\underaccent{{\cc@style\underline{\mskip 10.0mu}}}{H}_{n}(0,x)\geq\psi(0,x;\eta)$ for all $x\in\mathbb{R}$ . Then, it follows from the comparison principle that for any $R>0$ large enough and $\delta>0$ small enough and $n$ large enough $\liminf_{t\to\infty}H_{n}(t_{n}+t,x)\geq\liminf_{t\to\infty}\underaccent{{\cc@style\underline{\mskip 10.0mu}}}{H}_{n}(t,x)\geq p_{n,R,\delta}(x)\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}.$ (4.39) To complete the proof of Lemma 4.11 , it remains to check that $p_{n,R,\delta}$ is far way from $0$ as $n\to\infty$ , $R\to\infty$ and $\delta\to 0$ .
Since $p_{n,R,\delta}$ is bounded from above by $1$ , one can use standard elliptic estimates to get that, as $n\to+\infty$ , $R\to+\infty$ and $\delta\to 0$ , the function $p_{n,R,\delta}(x)$ converges locally uniformly to a stationary solution $p_{\infty}(x)$ of the equation $d_{h}\partial_{xx}p_{\infty}+bp_{\infty}(1-g-p_{\infty})=0.$ Moreover, since the map $t\to\psi(t,x;\eta_{0})$ is nondecreasing, we assert from Claim 4.13 that $p_{n,R,\delta}(0)\geq\psi(0,0;\eta_{0})\geq\eta_{0}\phi_{R}(0)$ . Note that $\phi_{R}(x)\to 1$ locally uniformly as $R\to+\infty$ , and hence $p_{\infty}(0)\geq\eta_{0}$ . As a consequence, we reached a contradiction to ( 4.35 ) and completed the proof of Lemma 4.11 . $\square$ Now we are ready to prove Proposition 4.10 . Proof of Proposition 4.10 . We proceed by contradiction and assume that there exists a sequences $\{t_{n}\}_{n>0}$ with $t_{n}\to\infty$ and $\{x_{n}\}_{n>0}\subset\mathbb{R}$ such that $\liminf_{t\to+\infty}H(t_{n},x_{n})\leq\frac{1}{n}.$ The initial data $H_{0}(x)\equiv 1$ implies that there exist two sequences $\{s_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}$ with $0<s_{n}<t_{n}$ such that for each $n>0$ , $H(t,x_{n})\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{2}\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ t\in[s_{n},t_{n}],$ $H(s_{n},x_{n})=\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{2}.$ We assume as before, possibly along a subsequence, the functions $(F_{n},C_{n},H_{n})(t,x):=(F,C,H)(t_{n}+t,x_{n}+x)$ converges locally uniformly to $(F_{\infty},C_{\infty},H_{\infty})$ , which is an entire solution of $\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}F_{\infty}=\partial_{xx}F_{\infty}+aF_{\infty}(1-C_{\infty}-F_{\infty}),\\
&\partial_{t}C_{\infty}=d_{c}\partial_{xx}C_{\infty}+C_{\infty}(1-C_{\infty}-F_{\infty})+s(F_{\infty}+C_{\infty})H_{\infty},\\
&\partial_{t}H_{\infty}=d_{h}\partial_{xx}H_{\infty}+bH_{\infty}(1-gF_{\infty}-gC_{\infty}-H_{\infty}).\end{aligned}\right.$ (4.40) From the choices of sequences $\{t_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}$ and $\{x_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}$ , one has $H_{\infty}(0,0)=0$ , and hence $H_{\infty}\equiv 0$ . In particular, $|s_{n}-t_{n}|\to\infty$ as $n\to\infty$ , otherwise it contradicts the fact that $\lim_{n\to\infty}H_{n}(s_{n}-t_{n},0)=\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{2}>0.$ Now let us consider the limit functions as follows: $\widetilde{F}(t,x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}F_{n}(t,x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}F(s_{n}+t,x_{n}+x),$ $\widetilde{C}(t,x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}C_{n}(t,x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}C(s_{n}+t,x_{n}+x),$ $\widetilde{H}(t,x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}H_{n}(t,x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}H(s_{n}+t,x_{n}+x),$ which are well defined thanks to standard parabolic estimates.
Then we look on $(\widetilde{F},\widetilde{C},\widetilde{H})$ as a solution of ( 1.1 ) with initial data $(\widetilde{F}_{0},\widetilde{C}_{0},\widetilde{H}_{0}):=\lim_{n\to\infty}(F_{n}(s_{n},x_{n}+x),C_{n}(s_{n},x_{n}+x),H_{n}(s_{n},x_{n}+x)).$ Since $\widetilde{H}_{0}(0)=\varepsilon_{1}/2>0$ , by applying Lemma 4.11 , one has $\limsup_{t\to\infty}\widetilde{H}(t,0)\geq\varepsilon_{1}.$ (4.41) On the other hand, for all $t\in[0,t_{n}-s_{n})$ , it holds $H_{n}(t,0)\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{2}.$ For each fixed $t\geq 0$ , since $t\in[0,t_{n}-s_{n})$ , we get by the locally uniform convergence that $\widetilde{H}(t,0)\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{2}\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ t\geq 0,$ which contradicts the result ( 4.41 ).
Therefore, the proof of Proposition 4.10 is complete. $\square$ 4.3.2 Construction of the strict Lyapunov function Before we investigate the profiles of the $C$ -component and $H$ -component in the finial zone, we first consider the dynamics of the underlying ODE system ( 1.9 ): $\left\{\begin{aligned} &C_{t}=C(1-C)+sCH,\\

if $(C,H)$ denotes the solution of corresponding ODE system ( 1.9 ) with
