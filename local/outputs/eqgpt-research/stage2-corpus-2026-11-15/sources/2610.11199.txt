Email address: immanuel.benporat@austin.utexas.edu Abstract. We derive the pressureless Euler-Maxwell system as a semiclassical limit from the Maxwell-Schrödinger equation. The method relies on studying a quantum modulated energy accounting for the self-consistent magnetic effects. Crucially, a correction is incorporated in the quantum modulated energy as to enable to close the Grönwall estimate provided a smallness assumption on the initial data is imposed. This is the first result on the semiclassical limit of the Maxwell-Schrödinger equation avoiding any regularization or conditional uniform in $\hbar$ bounds on the wave function. 1. Introduction 1.1. Background. One of the most commonly studied models in non-relativistic quantum mechanics is the Hartree equation $\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lc}\begin{cases}i\hbar\partial_{t}\psi_{\hbar}=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}\Delta\psi_{\hbar}+V\ast\left|\psi_{\hbar}\right|^{2}\psi_{\hbar}\\
\psi_{\hbar}(0,\cdot)=\psi_{\hbar}^{\mathrm{in}},&\end{cases}\end{array}$ describing the evolution of a wave function $\psi_{\hbar}$ via a self-consistent quantum Hamiltonian given by means of an interaction $V:\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}$ and scaled via Planck’s constant $0<\hbar\ll 1$ . The problem of identifying the classical dynamics resulting from Hartree’s equation as Planck’s constant $\hbar$ becomes negligible is a prototypical example of a semiclassical limit . The semiclassical limit is motivated by the fact that the scaling parameter $\hbar$ measures how quantum the system is, and as it becomes small the system is approximately classical. We refer to [ 27 ] for a particularly accessible exposition of the physical background. It turns out that the Vlasov equation is the kinetic PDE that is expected to be derived from ( 1.1 ), namely $\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lc}\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f+\xi\cdot\nabla_{x}f-\nabla V\ast\rho_{f}\cdot\nabla_{\xi}f=0,\ \rho_{f}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(t,x,\xi)\ {\rm d}\xi\\
f(0,\cdot)=f^{\mathrm{in}}.\end{cases}\end{array}$ We do not aim to give an exhaustive account of the literature concerning the rigorous justification of the semiclassical limit leading from ( 1.1 ) to ( 1.1 ) but refer to the foundational works by Lions-Paul [ 18 ] and Markowich-Mauser [ 20 ] . Other more modern works on the semiclassical limit include [ 7 , 6 , 13 , 12 , 14 , 26 ] . We refer to Chapter 3 in [ 19 ] for a detailed review of quantum optimal transport and its applications in semiclassical and quantum many-body limits. A more realistic (and hence more involved) description of quantum matter includes a quantum Hamiltonian with a self-consistent electromagnetic part, which leads to the Maxwell-Schrödinger equation given by $\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lc}\begin{cases}&i\hbar\partial_{t}\psi_{\hbar}=\frac{1}{2}(i\hbar\nabla+A_{\hbar})^{2}\psi_{\hbar}+\varphi_{\hbar}\psi_{\hbar}\\
&-\Delta\varphi_{\hbar}=\rho_{\hbar}\\
&(\partial_{tt}-\Delta)A_{\hbar}=\mathbb{P}J_{\hbar}\\
&\mathrm{div}A_{\hbar}=0\end{cases}\end{array}$ supplemented with initial data (MS in ) $\displaystyle(\psi_{\hbar}(0,\cdot),A_{\hbar}(0,\cdot),\partial_{t}A_{\hbar}(0,\cdot))=(\psi^{\mathrm{in}}_{\hbar},A_{0,\hbar}^{\mathrm{in}},A^{\mathrm{in}}_{1,\hbar}),\ \left\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}_{\hbar}\right\|_{2}=1,\ \mathrm{div}(A_{0,\hbar}^{\mathrm{in}})=\mathrm{div}(A^{\mathrm{in}}_{1,\hbar})=0.$ Here we designate by $\rho_{\hbar},J_{\hbar}$ the quantum density and quantum current, respectively, defined by (1.4) $\displaystyle J_{\hbar}\coloneqq-\Re(\overline{\psi_{\hbar}}(i\hbar\nabla+A_{\hbar})\psi_{\hbar}),\ \rho_{\hbar}\coloneqq\left|\psi_{\hbar}\right|^{2}$ and we designate by $\mathbb{P}$ the Helmholtz-Leray projector defined by (1.5) $\displaystyle\mathbb{P}J\coloneqq(I-\nabla\Delta^{-1}\mathrm{div})J.$ Note that $A_{\hbar}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}$ is to be interpreted as a magnetic vector potential. The expected semiclassical limit in this case is the non-relativistic Vlasov-Maxwell equation (VM) $\displaystyle\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{lc}\partial_{t}f+\xi\cdot\nabla_{x}f+(E+\xi\times B)\cdot\nabla_{\xi}f=0\\
\partial_{t}E-\mathrm{curl}B=-J,\ J_{f}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\xi f(t,x,\xi)\ {\rm d}\xi\\
\partial_{t}B+\mathrm{curl}E=0\\
\mathrm{div}E=\rho_{f},\mathrm{div}B=0,\ \rho_{f}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(t,x,\xi)\ {\rm d}\xi.\end{array}\end{cases}$ The rigorous derivation leading from ( 1.1 ) to ( VM ) in the limit as $\hbar\rightarrow 0$ is well recognized as an outstanding open problem. However, there have been several intermediate and related results which we now review. The semiclassical limit for a regularized version of ( 1.1 ) and ( VM ) (a regularization modeling extended charges) has been proved in [ 16 ] . The regularization has the clear advantage that it compensates for the loss of derivative which is usually a major source of difficulty in ( VM ), and results from the fact that $E,B$ are evolve hyperbolically (unlike the electrostatic case demonstrated in ( 1.1 )). In [ 15 ] the regularized Maxwell-Schrödinger equation is obtained as a mean-field limit from the Pauli-Fierz Hamiltonian and in [ 17 ] the author goes further to derive the regularized Vlasov-Maxwell equation as a combined mean-field semiclassical limit from quantum many-body dynamics. It is also possible to study the semiclassical limit for monokinetic initial data in the following manner. Introducing the monokinetic ansatz $f(t,x,\xi)=\rho(t,x)\delta(\xi-u(t,x))$ a direct calculation reveals that $f$ is a solution of ( VM ) iff $(\rho,u,B,E)$ is a solution of the following Euler-Maxwell system : (EM) $\displaystyle\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{lc}\partial_{t}\rho+\mathrm{div}(\rho u)=0\\
\partial_{t}u+uD_{x}u=E+u\times B\\
\partial_{t}E-\mathrm{curl}B=-\rho u\\
\partial_{t}B+\mathrm{curl}E=0\\
\mathrm{div}E=\rho,\ \mathrm{div}(B)=0\end{array}\end{cases}$ supplemented with initial data (EM in ) $\displaystyle(\rho(0,\cdot),u(0,\cdot),B(0,\cdot),E(0,\cdot))=(\rho^{\mathrm{in}},u^{\mathrm{in}},B^{\mathrm{in}},E^{\mathrm{in}}),\ \rho^{\mathrm{in}}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\ \mathrm{div}E^{\mathrm{in}}=\rho^{\mathrm{in}},\ \mathrm{div}B^{\mathrm{in}}=0.$ In this sense, ( EM ) may be viewed as a special case of ( VM ) with initial data $\rho^{\mathrm{in}}\delta(\xi-u^{\mathrm{in}})$ . The derivation of the Euler-Maxwell equation with pressure as a semiclassical limit has been studied in [ 11 ] via the modulated energy method, which will be central for the present work as well. Nevertheless, the result in [ 11 ] is conditional since it assumes a bound uniform in $\hbar$ on $\psi_{\hbar}$ , i.e. a bound of the form (1.6) $\displaystyle\underset{0<\hbar\leq 1}{\sup}\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\left\|\psi_{\hbar}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}<\infty.$ Such bounds are not known at present, and in fact even in the simplified electrostatic settings of the Hartree equation ( 1.1 ) an estimate of the form ( 1.6 ) has been proved in $3D$ only in the case where $V$ is the screened Coulomb interaction and the initial data verifies smallness assumptions [ 14 ] . The related problem of propagation of quantum velocity moments of arbitrary order uniformly in $\hbar$ for ( 1.1 ) is explicitly classified as an open problem in [ 9 ] . However, the pressureless Euler-Poisson equation has already been derived (non-conditionally) as a semiclassical limit in the important work by Golse-Paul [ 8 ] . A more recent work which is particularly relevant to the problem at stake is [ 24 ] , in which the author derives the relativistic Euler-Maxwell equation from the massive Klein–Gordon–Maxwell system (which can be viewed as a relativistic version of ( 1.1 )). Other related works about the semiclassical limit of quantum dynamics coupled to Maxwell type equations include [ 22 ] , where the authors implement WKB methods to derive the Euler-Poisswell equation from the Pauli–Poisswell equation, and [ 21 ] , in which the combined non-relativistic semiclassical limit of the Dirac-Maxwell equation is proved. To finish this discussion, we also mention that it is interesting to study the semiclassical limit when the magnetic field is external rather than coupled – this line of research was initiated by Arnold-Steinrück in [ 2 ] , who employed a Wigner transform approach, and was also undertaken more recently by the author in [ 4 , 5 ] , by exploiting machinery from quantum optimal transport and quantum modulated energies. 1.2. Main results The main purpose of this work is to prove the semiclassical limit leading from ( 1.1 ) to ( EM ) for small initial data. This demonstrates a first step towards the resolution of the formidable problem mentioned earlier regarding the derivation of ( VM ) semiclassically from ( 1.1 ) – our main result settles this problem for small monokinetic initial data. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first result regarding the semiclassical limit of the non-relativistic Maxwell-Schrödinger equation which does not necessitate any regularization or conditional assumptions of the type ( 1.6 ). Our underlying methodology rests upon identifying a corrected quantum modulated energy , which we now define. Given a solution $(\psi_{\hbar},A_{\hbar},\varphi_{\hbar})$ to ( 1.1 ) and a solution $(\rho,u,B,E)$ to ( EM ) set $E_{\hbar}\coloneqq-\partial_{t}A_{\hbar}-\nabla\varphi_{\hbar}$ , $B_{\hbar}\coloneqq\mathrm{curl}A_{\hbar}$ and define the quantum modulated energy by (1.7) $\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\hbar}(t)\coloneqq\underset{\coloneqq\mathcal{U}_{\hbar}(t)}{\underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|(i\hbar\nabla+A_{\hbar}+u)\psi_{\hbar}\right|^{2}\ {\rm d}x}}+\underset{\coloneqq\mathcal{B}_{\hbar}(t)}{\underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|B_{\hbar}-B\right|^{2}\ {\rm d}x}}+\underset{\coloneqq\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(t)}{\underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|E_{\hbar}-E\right|^{2}\ {\rm d}x}}$ and the corrected quantum modulated energy by (1.8) $\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{\hbar}(t)\coloneqq\mathcal{H}_{\hbar}(t)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u\cdot((E_{\hbar}-E)\times(B_{\hbar}-B))\ {\rm d}x.$ Given a solution $\psi_{\hbar}$ to ( 1.1 ) we set $R_{\hbar}(t)\coloneqq|\psi_{\hbar}(t,\cdot)\rangle|\langle\psi_{\hbar}(t,\cdot)|$ . By direct calculation $R_{\hbar}$ is governed by the following equation: $\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lc}\begin{cases}&i\hbar\partial_{t}R_{\hbar}=\left[\frac{1}{2}(i\hbar\nabla+A_{\hbar})^{2}+\varphi_{\hbar},R_{\hbar}\right]\\
&-\Delta\varphi_{\hbar}=\rho_{\hbar}\\
&(\partial_{tt}-\Delta)A_{\hbar}=\mathbb{P}J_{\hbar},\\
&\mathrm{div}A_{\hbar}=0.\end{cases}\end{array}$ Here $\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]$ designates the commutator defined by $\left[T,S\right]\coloneqq TS-ST$ . It will be useful to invoke the density operator formulation in ( 1.2 ) in some of the forthcoming calculations. Remark 1.1 . Note that the quantum modulated energy, defined in ( 1.7 ), may be equivalently written as $\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\hbar}(t)$ $\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}\left((i\hbar\nabla+A_{\hbar}+u)^{2}R_{\hbar}\right)$ $\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|B_{\hbar}-B\right|^{2}\ {\rm d}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|E_{\hbar}-E\right|^{2}\ {\rm d}x.$ In addition, it will be useful to use the duality definition of the quantum current $J_{\hbar}$ , i.e. $J_{\hbar}$ is the unique vector-valued Radon measure such that for all $a\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathbb{R}^{3})$ it holds that $\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}J_{\hbar}\cdot a\ {\rm d}x=-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}\left((i\hbar\nabla+A_{\hbar})\vee aR_{\hbar}\right).$ In the above formula and hereafter we denote by $\vee$ the anti-commutator defined by $T\vee S\coloneqq TS+ST.$ Our main theorem is stated below: Theorem 1.2 . Let $(\rho,u,B,E)$ be the solution to ( EM ) on some time interval $[0,T]$ such that $\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}<1$ (as guaranteed by Theorem ( 2.2 )). • $(\psi^{\mathrm{in}}_{\hbar},A_{0,\hbar}^{\mathrm{in}},A^{\mathrm{in}}_{1,\hbar})\in H^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times H^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})$ satisfy ( MSin ) and let $(\psi_{\hbar},A_{\hbar})$ be the solution of ( 1.1 ) on $[0,T]$ (as guaranteed by Theorem 1.3 ). • $\mathcal{G}_{\hbar}(t)$ is given by ( 1.8 ) and $e_{\hbar}\coloneqq E_{\hbar}-E,b_{\hbar}\coloneqq B_{\hbar}-B$ . Then, it holds that $\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\mathcal{G}_{\hbar}(t)$ $\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{1\leq j,k\leq 3}\mathrm{tr}\left((i\hbar\partial_{x_{j}}+A^{j}_{\hbar}+u^{j})\vee\left((i\hbar\partial_{x_{k}}+A^{k}_{\hbar}+u^{k})\vee\partial_{x_{k}}u^{j}\right)R_{\hbar}\right)$ (1.10) $\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}D_{x}u:\left(e_{\hbar}^{\otimes 2}-\frac{1}{2}\left|e_{\hbar}\right|^{2}I+b_{\hbar}^{\otimes 2}-\frac{1}{2}\left|b_{\hbar}\right|^{2}I\right)\ {\rm d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\partial_{t}u\cdot(e_{\hbar}\times b_{\hbar})\ {\rm d}x.$ Consequently, it holds that (1.11) $\displaystyle\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\mathcal{H}_{\hbar}(t)\underset{\hbar\rightarrow 0}{\rightarrow}0\ \mbox{provided}\ \mathcal{H}_{\hbar}(0)\underset{\hbar\rightarrow 0}{\rightarrow}0.$ Moreover, one has (1.12) $\displaystyle\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\left\|(E_{\hbar}-E)(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}\underset{\hbar\rightarrow 0}{\rightarrow}0,\ \underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\left\|(B_{\hbar}-B)(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}\underset{\hbar\rightarrow 0}{\rightarrow}0$ and (1.13) $\displaystyle\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\left\|(\rho_{\hbar}-\rho)(t,\cdot)\right\|_{H^{-1}}\underset{\hbar\rightarrow 0}{\rightarrow}0,\ \underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\left\|(J_{\hbar}-\rho u)(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathrm{BL}}\underset{\hbar\rightarrow 0}{\rightarrow}0.$ The local well-posedness of ( EM ) will be addressed in § 2.1 . As for the system ( 1.1 ), we need global well-posedness in order to make sure that the time interval on which the solution $\psi_{\hbar}$ is defined does not degenerate as $\hbar\rightarrow 0$ . Below we state the global in time well-posedness of ( 1.1 ), as proved in [ 23 ] . Theorem 1.3 . ( [ 23 ] , Theorem 1.1 and Theorem 1.2) Suppose that $(\psi^{\mathrm{in}}_{\hbar},A^{\mathrm{in}}_{0,\hbar},A^{\mathrm{in}}_{1,\hbar})\in H^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times H^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})$ satisfies ( MSin ). Then there exists a unique solution $(\psi_{\hbar},A_{\hbar})\in C(\mathbb{R};H^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\times C(\mathbb{R};H^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))$ to the Cauchy problem ( 1.1 )-( MSin ). Moreover, $\partial_{t}A_{\hbar}\in C(\mathbb{R};H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))$ . For additional literature concerning the global well-posedness for the equation ( 1.1 ) (or variants thereof) we refer the reader to [ 29 , 3 , 1 ] . 1.3. Strategy and key ideas. Prior to establishing the semiclassical limit leading from a dispersive to a monokinetic PDE, one has to first gain a proper understanding of the weak-strong stability exhibited by the underlying monokinetic PDE. To be concrete, given solutions $(\rho_{1},u_{1},E_{1},B_{1})$ and $(\rho_{2},u_{2},E_{2},B_{2})$ to ( EM ) define the modulated energy by (1.14) $\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(t)$ $\displaystyle\coloneqq\underset{\coloneqq\mathcal{U}(t)}{\underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{1}\left|u_{1}-u_{2}\right|^{2}\ {\rm d}x}}+\underset{\coloneqq\mathcal{E}(t)}{\underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|E_{1}-E_{2}\right|^{2}\ {\rm d}x}}+\underset{\coloneqq\mathcal{B}(t)}{\underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|B_{1}-B_{2}\right|^{2}\ {\rm d}x}}$ and the corrected modulated energy by (1.15) $\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(t)\coloneqq\mathcal{H}(t)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u_{2}\cdot((E_{1}-E_{2})\times(B_{1}-B_{2}))\ {\rm d}x.$ Note that $\mathcal{H}(t)$ and $\mathcal{G}(t)$ are the classical analogues of ( 1.7 ) and ( 1.8 ), respectively. A weak-strong stability principle would typically be an evolution estimate of the type $\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}(\mbox{modulated energy})\leq C(\mbox{modulated energy})\ \mbox{with}\ C=C(\left\|\rho_{2}\right\|_{\infty},\left\|u_{2}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}_{t,x}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}).$ Crucially, the constant $C$ involves only the regularity of one of the solutions. A weak-strong stability inequality is well established for the pressureless Euler-Poisson equation – see appendix A. in [ 25 ] . An attempt to mimic the proof of this weak-strong stability inequality would include the calculation of the time derivative of the modulated energy ( 1.14 ). This calculation is performed in Theorem 2.4 and results in the following formula $\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\mathcal{H}(t)=$ $\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{1}(u_{1}-u_{2})^{\otimes 2}:D_{x}u_{2}\ {\rm d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})u_{2}\cdot(E_{1}-E_{2})\ {\rm d}x$ (1.16) $\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{1}(u_{1}-u_{2})\cdot(u_{2}\times(B_{1}-B_{2}))\ {\rm d}x.$ It is transparent that the first term in the right-hand side of ( 1.16 ) can be bounded by means of $\mathcal{U}(t)$ up to a constant depending only on the regularity of $u_{2}$ – see § 2.2 for details. However, the last term in ( 1.16 ) would require an $L^{\infty}$ bound on $\rho_{1}$ , and therefore such a bound would not enable to obtain a weak-strong stability principle. We stress that we wish to avoid at all costs any dependence on the $L^{\infty}$ norm of $\rho_{1}$ : in the semiclassical limit $\rho_{1}$ will play the role of $\rho_{\hbar}$ , and we already remarked that it is currently not possible to rule out that $\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\left\|\rho_{\hbar}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}$ blows up as $\hbar\rightarrow 0$ . The idea of the corrected modulated energy ( 1.15 ) is to circumvent this issue. Indeed, once the correction is incorporated within the modulated energy it is possible to deduce a weak-strong inequality for small initial data by observing that $\mathcal{G}$ is bounded from below and above in terms of $\mathcal{H}$ as long as $u_{2}$ remains small (see Lemma 2.5 ). More precisely it holds that $\displaystyle(1-\Lambda)\mathcal{H}(t)\leq\mathcal{G}(t)\leq(1+\Lambda)\mathcal{H}(t)\ \mbox{as long as}\ \left\|u_{2}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\Lambda\ \mbox{for some}\ \Lambda<1.$ The plan outlined above enables to prove a weak-strong stability principle for ( EM ) under smallness assumptions. Conceptually, this is similar in spirit to the adapted modulated energy implemented in [ 24 ] , and a major part of the novelty of the present work is that this idea can be imported to the settings of the Maxwell-Schrödinger equation. This component is also the reason the final result is unconditional, in variance with [ 11 ] . Having bypassed this issue, the next task is to quantize the weak-strong stability argument, which is where the quantum modulated energies ( 1.7 )-( 1.8 ) come into play. The paper is organized as follows. In § 2 we prove the local well-posedness of ( EM ). Note that since we are considering the pressureless Euler-Maxwell equation local well-posedness is natural (in variance with [ 11 ] , which includes a pressure term leading to a global theory). Afterwards, we prove the weak-strong stability principle by studying the evolution of the corrected modulated energy $\mathcal{G}(t)$ . In § 3 we present a proof of the semiclassical limit leading from ( 1.1 ) to ( EM ) as formulated in Theorem 1.2 . 2. Local well-posedness and weak-strong stability for ( EM ) 2.1. Local in time existence of classical solutions to ( EM ) We start by demonstrating local in time existence of classical solutions to ( EM ). The proof differs substantially from the local well-posedness theory of Euler-Poisson due to the fact that $E$ and $B$ are governed dynamically according to a hyperbolic equation. The local existence is proved only for small initial data, which is more restrictive than the local existence for the Euler-Poisson equation.
