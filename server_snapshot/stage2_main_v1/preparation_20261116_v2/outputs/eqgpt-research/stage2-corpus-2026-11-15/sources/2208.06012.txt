Global dynamics of the diffusive Hindmarsh-Rose equations with memristors as a new proposed model for neuron dynamics are investigated in this paper. We prove the existence and regularity of a global attractor for the solution semiflow through uniform analytic estimates showing the higher-order dissipative property and the asymptotically compact characteristics of the solutions by the approach of Kolmogorov-Riesz theorem. The quantitative bounds of the regions containing this global attractor respectively in the state space and in the regular space are explicitly expressed by the model parameters. Introduction Starting from the well-known Hodgkin-Huxley equations [ 19 ] (1952), which provided a highly nonlinear four-dimensional model for general neuron dynamics, and the two-dimensional FitzHugh-Nagumo equations [ 14 ] (1961-1962) as a simplified model which explains periodic firing with refractory but not able to generate chaotic neuron burstings, scientists have proposed various types of mathematical neuron models based on the biological characteristics of neuron functions and the biophysical laws. Two key issues in any modeling of neuron dynamics and neuronal networks are the firing-bursting patterns of single neurons and the collective behaviors of neural networks, especially synchronization and chaotic dynamics. All these issues are closely linked to significant applications in many areas such as brain diseases, image and signal processing, encryption of communications, and mostly artificial neural networks and artificial intelligence. The Hindmarsh-Rose equations [ 18 ] (1984) is originally a three-dimensional ODE model for neuron firing-bursting phenomena and has been studied through bifurcation analysis and numerical simulations by many researchers, cf. [ 6 , 10 , 12 , 18 , 21 , 22 ] and the references therein. This model exhibits rich and interesting spatial-temporal bursting patterns [ 5 , 21 , 33 , 37 ] . In particular, the three-dimensional complex bifurcations lead to numerically observed and sophisticated chaotic bursting behaviors. Very recently the author’s group studied global dynamics generated by the spatially diffusive Hindmarsh-Rose equations [ 23 , 24 , 25 ] , random dynamics of the stochastic Hindmarsh-Rose equations [ 26 ] , and synchronization of complex Hindmarsh-Rose neural networks and FitzHugh-Nagumo neural networks [ 27 , 28 ] . In this work, we propose and study global dynamics of the diffusive Hindmarsh-Rose equations with memristors, which is a new mathematical model for neuron dynamics in terms of a hybrid system of PDE and ODE featuring an additional component equation for memristors and its nonlinear coupling to the membrane potential equation for a neuron cell. The concept memristor (meaning a memory resistor) was coined by Leon Chua [ 8 ] (1971), as an electrical device with two terminals, which denotes the relationship between time-varying electromagnetic flux and electric charges. General memristive system was initially tackled in [ 9 ] (1976) and has attracted broad scientific interests since the seminal paper [ 36 ] (2008) published in Nature. The memristors are recognized and used in advanced neuron models to describe the electromagnetic induction effect caused by ions movement across the neuron cell membrane, which has been observed through fluctuations of extracellular calcium and potassium ions’ concentrations in experiments [ 29 , 39 ] . Moreover, the memristor synapsis in a model carries and transmits dynamically memorized information, which serves as a different type of synapses in neuron networks beside the electrical synapses and chemical synapses well known in neuroscience [ 12 , 38 , 42 ] . The research results on memristive Hindmarsh-Rose neuron models and FitzHugh-Nagumo neuron models have been rapidly expanding in the recent decade, cf. [ 1 ] - [ 5 ] , [ 11 , 13 , 16 ] , [ 29 ] - [ 31 ] , [ 34 , 38 , 39 ] , [ 42 ] - [ 45 ] and many references therein. Memristive neuron networks and artificial neural networks currently become an active topic as it shows rich collective dynamical behaviors and chaotic bursting patterns [ 16 , 38 ] by varying the coupling strengths and other parameters, such as the reported coexisting chimeras and local attractors [ 3 , 16 , 31 , 35 ] and clusters [ 3 , 41 ] , and synchronization enhanced by memristive couplings [ 13 , 15 , 20 , 30 , 35 , 39 , 40 , 43 ] . In this paper, we consider the following new model of the diffusive Hindmarsh-Rose equations with memristor for a single neuron: (1.1) $\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}$ $\displaystyle=\eta\Delta u+au^{2}-bu^{3}+v-w+J_{e}-k_{1}\varphi(\rho)u,\,$ (1.2) $\displaystyle\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}$ $\displaystyle=\alpha-\beta u^{2}-v,$ (1.3) $\displaystyle\frac{\partial w}{\partial t}$ $\displaystyle=q(u-u_{e})-rw,$ (1.4) $\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}$ $\displaystyle=u-k_{2}\rho,$ for $t>0,\;x\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}$ ( $n\leq 3$ ), where $\Omega$ is a bounded domain up to three dimension with locally Lipschitz continuous boundary (put in a general mathematical scope). The nonlinear term in a quadratic form (1.5) $\varphi(\rho)=c+\gamma\rho+\delta\rho^{2},\quad c,\gamma\in\mathbb{R},\;\;\delta>0,$ presents the memristive coupling in the equation of membrane potential ( 1.1 ), where the memristive variable $\rho(t,x)$ stands for the memductance of the memristor and $\varphi(\rho)$ represents the electromagnetic induction flux with its coupling strength $k_{1}$ and self-coupling strength $k_{2}$ respectively. All the results proved in this paper are also valid for another type of memristor [ 41 ] , $\varphi(\rho)=\tanh(\rho)$ , simply by adjusting the estimates in proof. In this system ( 1.1 )-( 1.4 ), the variable $u(t,x)$ refers to the membrane electric potential of a neuron cell, the variable $v(t,x)$ represents the transport rate of the ions of sodium and potassium through the fast channels and can be called the spiking variable, while the variables $w(t,x)$ represents the transport rate across the neuron membrane through slow channels of calcium and other ions correlated to the inter-spike quiescence and can be called the bursting variable. All the involved parameters $a,b,\eta,\alpha,\beta,q,r,\delta,k_{1},k_{2}$ and the external input $J_{e}$ can be any positive constants, while the reference value of the membrane potential $u_{e}\in\mathbb{R}$ and the first two parameters in ( 1.5 ) $c,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}$ can be any real number constants. For instance, a set of typical parameter values can be [ 31 , 34 , 37 , 38 ] $\displaystyle J_{e}=3.2,\;\;r=0.002,\;\;q=0.008,\;\;u_{e}=-1.6,$ $\displaystyle a=3.0,\;\;b=1,\;\;\alpha=1.0,\;\;\beta=5.0,$ $\displaystyle\gamma=0.4,\;\;\delta=0.8,\;\;k_{1}=0.9,\;\;k_{2}=6.5.$ We impose the homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions for the $u$ -component, (1.6) $\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\,(t,x)=0,\quad t>0,\;x\in\partial\Omega,$ and the initial conditions of the components are denoted by (1.7) $u_{0}(x)=u(0,x),\;v_{0}(x)=v(0,x),\;w_{0}(x)=w(0,x),\;\rho_{0}(x)=\rho(0,x),\quad x\in\Omega.$ In the listed and many other references, the methodology of investigations into the memristive Hindmarsh-Rose neuron models mainly consists of bifurcation and stability analysis supported with numerical simulations to imitate neuron bursting-firing patterns. Several commonly used methods in this area are bifurcation diagrams and Lyapunov exponents [ 1 , 3 , 13 , 31 , 35 , 39 ] , generalized Hamiltonian functions and Lyapunov functions [ 1 , 38 , 39 , 44 ] , center manifold theory [ 1 , 30 , 41 , 44 ] , dissipativity analysis [ 41 ] , algebraic invariant manifold for analytic solutions [ 1 ] , etc. Notably the proposed memristive neuron model of diffusive Hindmarsh-Rose equations in this paper reflects the structural features of a neuron cell that has the short-branch dendrites receiving incoming signals and the long-branch axon (naturally viewed as a one-dimensional space) propagating outreaching signals, which justifies the diffusive partial differential equation of the membrane potential in ( 1.1 ). We shall present in Section 2 the formulation of the system ( 1.1 )-( 1.4 ) and the preliminaries. In Section 3 we shall conduct uniform estimates to show the absorbing property of this solution semiflow. In Section 4 and Section 5 we shall prove the higher-order dissipativity and the asymptotic compactness of the solution semiflow by means of the Kolmogorov-Riesz theorem. Finally in Section 6, the main result on the existence and regularity of a global attractor, which characterizes the collection of all permanent regimes of the modeled neuron dynamics, will be proved and the quantitative bounds of the regions containing this global attractor respectively in the state space and in the regular space are explicitly expressed by the model parameters. 2. The existence and uniqueness of a weak solution local in time can be proved by the Galerkin approximation method for the PDE together with the basic existence theorem for ODE, based on the estimates similar to what we shall present in Section 3 and by the Lions-Magenes type of weak compactness argument [ 7 , 32 ] . The statement about strong solution follows from the parabolic regularity [ 32 ] of the evolutionary equations in ( 2.1 ).
∎ The goal of this work is to prove the existence of a unique global attractor for the dynamical system generated by this problem ( 2.1 ). The global attractor qualitatively characterizes the longtime and global dynamics in terms of asymptotically permanent patterns of all the solution trajectories of the system. We refer to [ 7 , 32 ] for the theory details of infinite dimensional dynamical systems or called semiflow (if time $t\geq 0$ ). Here just list a few concepts for clarity. Definition 2.3 . Let $\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}$ be a semiflow on a Banach space $\mathscr{X}$ . A bounded set $B^{*}$ of $\mathscr{X}$ is called an absorbing set for this semiflow, if for any given bounded subset $B\subset\mathscr{X}$ there is a finite time $T_{B}\geq 0$ such that $S(t)B\subset B^{*}$ for all $t>T_{B}$ . Definition 2.4 . A semiflow $\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}$ on a Banach space $\mathscr{X}$ is called asymptotically compact, if for any bounded sequence $\{z_{n}\}$ in $\mathscr{X}$ and any monotone increasing sequences $0<t_{n}\to\infty$ , there exist subsequences $\{z_{n_{k}}\}$ of $\{z_{n}\}$ and $\{t_{n_{k}}\}$ of $\{t_{n}\}$ such that $\lim_{k\to\infty}S(t_{n_{k}})z_{n_{k}}$ exists in $\mathscr{X}$ . Definition 2.5 (Global Attractor) . A set $\mathscr{A}$ in a Banach space $\mathscr{X}$ is called a global attractor for a semiflow $\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}$ on $\mathscr{X}$ , if the following two properties are satisfied: (i) $\mathscr{A}$ is a nonempty, compact, and invariant set in the space $\mathscr{X}$ , $S(t)\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{A},\quad t\geq 0.$ (ii) $\mathscr{A}$ attracts any given bounded set $B\subset\mathscr{X}$ in the sense $\text{dist}_{\mathscr{X}}(S(t)B,\mathscr{A})=\sup_{x\,\in\,B}\inf_{y\,\in\,\mathscr{A}}\|S(t)x-y\|_{\mathscr{X}}\to 0,\;\;\text{as}\;\;t\to\infty.$ Proposition 2.6 . [ 7 , 32 ] Let $\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}$ be a semiflow on a Banach space $\mathscr{X}$ . If the following two conditions are satisfied : (i) there exists a bounded absorbing set $B^{*}\subset\mathscr{X}$ for $\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}$ , and (ii) the semiflow $\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}$ is asymptotically compact on $\mathscr{X}$ , then there exists a unique global attractor $\mathscr{A}$ in $\mathscr{X}$ for the semiflow $\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}$ and (2.6) $\mathscr{A}=\bigcap_{\tau\,\geq\,0}\;\overline{\bigcup_{t\,\geq\,\tau}\,(S(t)B^{*})}.$ The Young’s inequality in a general form will be used: For any nonnegative numbers $x$ and $y$ , if $\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1$ , one has (2.7) $x\,y\leq\varepsilon x^{p}+C(\varepsilon,p)\,y^{q},\qquad C(\varepsilon,p)=\varepsilon^{-q/p},$ where constant $\varepsilon>0$ can be arbitrarily small. 3. Uniform Estimates and Absorbing Dynamics The new feature in this four-dimensional memristive Hindmarsh-Rose neuron model ( 1.1 ) - ( 1.4 ) is the product coupling of the nonlinear memductance term $k_{1}\varphi(\rho)u$ in the membrane potential equation ( 1.1 ). In this section we first prove the global existence of all the weak solutions in time of the initial value problem ( 2.1 ). Through careful and sophisticated maneuver of uniform inequality estimates, it will be shown that there exists an absorbing set in the state space $E$ for the solution semiflow. This dissipative dynamics result is valid without any conditions on all the 14 biological parameters in the model equations as naturally described. Theorem 3.1 . ∎ The global existence and uniqueness of the weak solutions as well as their continuous dependence on the initial data enable us to define the solution semiflow [ 32 ] of the diffusive Hindmarsh-Rose equations with memristor ( 1.1 )-( 1.4 ) on the state space $E$ as follows: $S(t):g_{0}\longmapsto g(t;g_{0})=(u(t,\cdot),v(t,\cdot),w(t,\cdot),\rho(t,\cdot)),\quad g_{0}\in E,\;t\geq 0,$ where $g(t;g_{0})$ is the weak solution with $g(0)=g_{0}$ . We call this semiflow $\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}$ the memristive Hindmarsh-Rose semiflow associated with the system ( 2.1 ). The next result exhibits the globally dissipative dynamics of this solution semiflow in the state space $E$ . Theorem 3.2 . Therefore, the $u$ -projection of the global attractor $\mathscr{A}$ is a bounded subset inside the ball $B_{C}=\{u\in C(\overline{\Omega}):\|u\|^{2}_{C(\overline{\Omega})}\leq R\}$ in the space $C(\overline{\Omega})$ and in $L^{\infty}(\Omega)$ as well.
∎ Lemma 6.3 . The $(v,w,\rho)$ -projection of the global attractor $\mathscr{A}$ is a bounded set in the space $L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})$ . Proof. The integral version of the differential equations ( 5.2 ), ( 5.10 ) and ( 5.13 ) shows that, for any $g_{o}=(u_{0},v_{0},w_{0},\rho_{0})\in\mathscr{A}$ , one has (6.5) $\begin{split}&\lim_{t\to\infty}\left|v(t,x)-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)}(\alpha-\beta u^{2})\,ds\right|\leq\lim_{t\to\infty}e^{-t}|v_{0}(x)|=0,\\
&\lim_{t\to\infty}\left|w(t,x)-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-r(t-s)}q(u-u_{e})\,ds\right|\leq\lim_{t\to\infty}e^{-rt}|w_{0}(x)|=0,\\
&\lim_{t\to\infty}\left|\rho(t,x)-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-k_{2}(t-s)}u(s,x)\,ds\right|\leq\lim_{t\to\infty}e^{-k_{2}t}|\rho_{0}(x)|=0.\end{split}$ By Lemma 6.2 , we see that $\max\left\{\left|\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{-(t-s)}(\alpha-\beta u^{2})ds\right|,\left|\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{-r(t-s)}q(u-u_{e})ds\right|,\left|\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{-k_{2}(t-s)}u(s,x)ds\right|\right\}\leq G,$ where (6.6) $G=\max\left\{|\alpha-\beta R|,\,\,\frac{q}{r}\left(\sqrt{R}+|u_{e}|\right),\,\frac{1}{k_{2}}\sqrt{R}\right\}.$ The compactness and invariance ( 6.2 ) of the global attractor $\mathscr{A}$ then implies that $\lim_{t\to\infty}\operatorname{dist}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(\text{Proj}_{(v,w,\rho)}S(t)\mathscr{A},B_{R^{3}}(G)\right)=\operatorname{dist}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(\text{Proj}_{(v,w,\rho)}\mathscr{A},B_{R^{3}}(G)\right)=0.$ Here $B_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(G)$ is the 3D bounded ball of radius $G$ . It means that $\text{Proj}_{(v,w,\rho)}\mathscr{A}$ is a bounded set in the space $L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})$ and the Lemma is proved.
∎ Theorem 6.4 . For spatial domain dimension $\dim\,(\Omega)=1$ , the global attractor $\mathscr{A}$ in ( 6.1 ) of the memristive Hindmarsh-Rose semiflow $\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}$ is a bounded set in the regular space $\Gamma=H^{2}(\Omega)\times L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})$ . Proof. Consider all the solution trajectories $\{S(t)g_{0}:g_{0}\in\mathscr{A}\}$ , which are complete trajectories in terms of $t\in(-\infty,\infty)$ and all inside the global attractor $\mathscr{A}$ . In view of Lemma 6.3 , it suffices to prove that the $u$ -projection of the global attractor $\mathscr{A}$ is in the space $H^{2}(\Omega)$ . The proof goes through three steps. Step 1. For the first component $u(t,x)$ of any solution trajectory in $\mathscr{A}$ , take the $L^{2}$ inner-product $\langle\eqref{ueq},u_{t}\rangle$ , where $u_{t}=\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}$ , to obtain (6.7) $\begin{split}&\|u_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{\eta}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\|\nabla u\|^{2}=\int_{\Omega}(au^{2}-bu^{3}+v-w+J_{e}-k_{1}(c+\gamma\rho+\delta\rho^{2})u_{t})\,dx\\
\leq&\,\int_{\Omega}\left(aR+bR^{3/2}+|v|+|w|+J_{e}+k_{1}|c|+k_{1}\gamma^{2}+k_{1}(1+\delta)\rho^{2}\right)|u_{t}|\,dx\\[2.0pt]
\leq&\,\left(aR+bR^{3/2}+J_{e}+k_{1}|c|+k_{1}\gamma^{2}\right)^{2}|\Omega|\\[5.0pt]
&+\|v\|^{2}+\|w\|^{2}+k_{1}^{2}(1+\delta)^{2}\|\rho^{2}\|^{2}+\frac{3}{4}\|u_{t}\|^{2}\\[5.0pt]
\leq&\,\left(aR+bR^{3/2}+J_{e}+k_{1}|c|+k_{1}\gamma^{2}\right)^{2}|\Omega|+K+k_{1}^{2}(1+\delta)^{2}\|\rho\|^{4}_{L^{4}}+\frac{3}{4}\|u_{t}\|^{2}\\[4.0pt]
\leq&\,\left(aR+bR^{3/2}+J_{e}+k_{1}|c|+k_{1}\gamma^{2}\right)^{2}|\Omega|+K+k_{1}^{2}(1+\delta)^{2}G^{4}\,|\Omega|+\frac{3}{4}\|u_{t}\|^{2}.\end{split}$ The Young’s inequality ( 2.7 ) is used in the second inequality of ( 6.7 ). Take the $L^{2}$ inner-product $\langle\eqref{veq},v_{t}\rangle$ for the second component $v(t,x)$ of any trajectory in $\mathscr{A}$ . We have (6.8) $\begin{split}&\|v_{t}\|^{2}=\int_{\Omega}(\alpha-\beta u^{2}-v)v_{t}\,dx\\
\leq&\,\int_{\Omega}(\alpha+\beta R+|v|)|v_{t}|\,dx=(\alpha+\beta R)^{2}\,|\Omega|+K+\frac{1}{2}\|v_{t}\|^{2}\end{split}$ where the Cauchy inequality and ( 3.16 ) are used. Similarly take the $L^{2}$ inner-product $\langle\eqref{weq},w_{t}\rangle$ for the third component $w(t,x)$ of any trajectory in $\mathscr{A}$ . We see (6.9) $\begin{split}&\|w_{t}\|^{2}=\int_{\Omega}(qu-qu_{e}-rw)w_{t}\,dx.\\
\leq&\,\int_{\Omega}(q\sqrt{R}+q|u_{e}|+r|w|)|w_{t}|\,dx=(q\sqrt{R}+q|u_{e}|)^{2}\,|\Omega|+r^{2}K+\frac{1}{2}\|w_{t}\|^{2}\end{split}$ Next take the $L^{2}$ inner-product $\langle\eqref{peq},\rho_{t}\rangle$ for the fourth component $\rho(t,x)$ of any trajectory in $\mathscr{A}$ to get (6.10) $\|\rho_{t}\|^{2}=\int_{\Omega}(u-k_{2}\rho)\rho_{t}\,dx\leq R\,|\Omega|+k_{2}^{2}K+\frac{1}{2}\|\rho_{t}\|^{2}.$ Now Sum up the above four estimate inequalities. We come up with (6.11) $\begin{split}&\frac{1}{4}\left(\|u_{t}\|^{2}+\|v_{t}\|^{2}+\|w_{t}\|^{2}+\|\rho_{t}\|^{2}\right)+\frac{\eta}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\|\nabla u\|^{2}\\[3.0pt]
\leq&\frac{1}{4}\|u_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\|v_{t}\|^{2}+\|w_{t}\|^{2}+\|\rho_{t}\|^{2}\right)+\frac{\eta}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\|\nabla u\|^{2}\\[3.0pt]
\leq&\,\left(aR+bR^{3/2}+J_{e}+k_{1}|c|+k_{1}\gamma^{2}\right)^{2}|\Omega|+K+k_{1}^{2}(1+\delta)^{2}G^{4}\,|\Omega|\\[8.0pt]
+&\,(\alpha+\beta R)^{2}\,|\Omega|+K+(q\sqrt{R}+q|u_{e}|)^{2}\,|\Omega|+r^{2}K+R\,|\Omega|+k_{2}^{2}K,\\[6.0pt]
=&\,\left(\left[aR+bR^{3/2}+J_{e}+k_{1}|c|+k_{1}\gamma^{2}\right]^{2}+k_{1}^{2}(1+\delta)^{2}G^{4}\right)|\Omega|\\[2.0pt]
+&\,\left(2+r^{2}+k_{2}^{2}\right)K+\left[(\alpha+\beta R)^{2}+(q\sqrt{R}+q|u_{e}|)^{2}+R\right]|\Omega|,\quad t>0.\end{split}$ Integrating the inequality ( 6.11 ) over the time interval $[0,1]$ , we obtain (6.12) $\int_{0}^{1}\left(\|u_{t}(s)\|^{2}+\|v_{t}(s)\|^{2}+\|w_{t}(s)\|^{2}+\|\rho_{t}(s)\|^{2}\right)ds\leq\Phi,$ where the constant (6.13) $.\begin{split}\Phi&=\frac{2\eta}{C_{1}\min\{\eta,\lambda\}}\left(\,\max\{C_{1},1\}K+\lambda M|\Omega|\,\right)\\
&+4\left(\left[aR+bR^{3/2}+J_{e}+k_{1}|c|+k_{1}\gamma^{2}\right]^{2}+k_{1}^{2}(1+\delta)^{2}G^{4}\right)|\Omega|\\
&+4\left(2+r^{2}+k_{2}^{2}\right)K+4\left[(\alpha+\beta R)^{2}+(q\sqrt{R}+q|u_{e}|)^{2}+R\right]|\Omega|.\end{split}$ and in the inequality ( 6.12 ) we have used ( 4.6 ) and ( 6.2 ) to bound the gradient term $(\eta/2)\|\nabla u_{0}\|^{2}$ for all the trajectories in the global attractor. Step 2. For the diffusive Hindmarsh-Rose equations with memristors confined on the set of the global attractor $\mathscr{A}$ , we differentiate the equation ( 1.1 ) with respect to time $t$ and get (6.14) $u_{tt}=\eta\Delta u_{t}+2au\,u_{t}-3\,bu^{2}u_{t}+v_{t}-w_{t}-k_{1}\varphi(\rho)u_{t}-k_{1}(\gamma\rho_{t}+2\delta\rho\rho_{t})u.$ Take the inner product $\langle\eqref{Bp},t^{2}u_{t}\rangle$ . Based on the results shown in Lemma 6.2 and Lemma 6.3 , it yields (6.15) $\begin{split}&\langle u_{tt},t^{2}u_{t}\rangle-\langle\eta\Delta u_{t},t^{2}u_{t}\rangle=-t\|u_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|tu_{t}\|^{2}+t^{2}\eta\|\nabla u_{t}\|^{2}\\
=&\,\int_{\Omega}t^{2}(2au\,u_{t}^{2}-3bu^{2}u_{t}^{2}+v_{t}u_{t}-w_{t}u_{t}-k_{1}\varphi(\rho)u^{2}_{t}-k_{1}(\gamma\rho_{t}+2\delta\rho\rho_{t})uu_{t})\,dx\\
\leq&\,t^{2}\int_{\Omega}\left[2a\sqrt{R}\,u_{t}^{2}+v_{t}^{2}+w_{t}^{2}+2u_{t}^{2}+k_{1}|\varphi(G)|u^{2}_{t}+k_{1}\sqrt{R}\,(|\gamma|+\delta G)\,(\rho_{t}^{2}+u_{t}^{2})\right]dx\\
=&\,t^{2}\left[2a\sqrt{R}+2+k_{1}(|\varphi(G)|+\sqrt{R}\,(|\gamma|+\delta G))\right]\|u_{t}\|^{2}+t^{2}\left(\|v_{t}\|^{2}+\|w_{t}\|^{2}\right)\\[4.0pt]
&+t^{2}k_{1}\sqrt{R}\,(|\gamma|+\delta G)\,\|\rho_{t}\|^{2},\quad t>0.\end{split}$ Here $\varphi(G)=c+\gamma G+\delta G^{2}$ and the first two terms in the first equality of ( 6.15 ) is derived by $\begin{split}&\quad-t\|u_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|tu_{t}\|^{2}=-t\|u_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\langle tu_{t},tu_{t}\rangle\\[3.0pt]
&=-t\|u_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(tu_{t}),tu_{t}\right\rangle+\left\langle tu_{t},\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(tu_{t})\right\rangle\right)\\
&=-t\|u_{t}\|^{2}+\left\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(tu_{t}),tu_{t}\right\rangle=-t\|u_{t}\|^{2}+\langle u_{t},tu_{t}\rangle+\langle tu_{tt},tu_{t}\rangle.\\[3.0pt]
&=-t\|u_{t}\|^{2}+t\|u_{t}\|^{2}+\langle u_{tt},t^{2}u_{t}\rangle=\langle u_{tt},t^{2}u_{t}\rangle.\end{split}$ Now we integrate the differential inequality ( 6.15 ) on $[0,t]$ to obtain $\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\|tu_{t}\|^{2}&\leq\int_{0}^{t}s\|u_{t}(s)\|^{2}ds+\int_{0}^{t}s^{2}\left(\|v_{t}\|^{2}\ +\|w_{t}\|^{2}+k_{1}\sqrt{R}\,(|\gamma|+\delta G)\,\|\rho_{t}\|^{2}\right)ds\\
&+\int_{0}^{t}s^{2}\left(2a\sqrt{R}+2+k_{1}(|\varphi(G)|+\sqrt{R}(|\gamma|+\delta G))\right)\|u_{t}(s)\|^{2}\,ds,\;\;t>0.\end{split}$ In the above inequality we can take $t=1$ and get (6.16) $\begin{split}\|u_{t}(1)\|^{2}&\leq 2\int_{0}^{1}\|u_{t}(s)\|^{2}\,ds+2\int_{0}^{1}\left(\|v_{t}\|^{2}+\|w_{t}\|^{2}+k_{1}\sqrt{R}\,(|\gamma|+\delta G)\,\|\rho_{t}\|^{2}\right)ds\\
&+2\int_{0}^{1}\left(2a\sqrt{R}+2+k_{1}(|\varphi(G)|+\sqrt{R}\,(|\gamma|+\delta G))\right)\|u_{t}(s)\|^{2}\,ds\leq D.\end{split}$ By the inequality in ( 6.12 ), here we have the constant (6.17) $D=2\left(3+2a\sqrt{R}+k_{1}|\varphi(G)|+2k_{1}\sqrt{R}\,(|\gamma|+\delta G)\right)\Phi,$ where the constant $\Phi$ is given in ( 6.13 ) of Step 1. Due to the dynamic invariance of the global attractor $\mathscr{A}$ , $S(t+1)\mathscr{A}=S(t)\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{A},\quad\text{for any}\;\,t\in[0,\infty),$ actually ( 6.16 ) demonstrates that for all the trajectories $\{S(t)g_{0}\}_{t\geq 0}$ in the global attractor $\mathscr{A}$ , the $u$ -component satisfies (6.18) $\|u_{t}(t)\|^{2}\leq D,\quad\text{for any}\;\,t\in[0,\infty).$ Step 3. From the original $u$ -equation ( 1.1 ), put together what we have proved in above steps, it holds that (6.19) $\begin{split}&\eta\|\Delta u(t)\|\leq\|u_{t}(t)\|+a\|u^{2}(t)\|+b\|u^{3}(t)\|+\|v(t)\|+\|w(t)\|+J_{e}|\Omega|^{1/2}\\[4.0pt]
&+k_{1}\|(c+\gamma\rho(t)+\delta\rho^{2}(t))u(t)\|\\
\leq&\,\sqrt{D}+\left(aR+bR^{3/2}+2G+J_{e}+k_{1}(|c|+|\gamma|G+\delta G^{2})\sqrt{R}\,\right)|\Omega|^{1/2},\;\;t>0.\end{split}$ and the constants $R$ in ( 6.4 ), $G$ in ( 6.6 ), and $D$ in ( 6.17 ) are all independent of any initial state in the space $E$ . Since the Laplacian operator $\Delta$ with the homogeneous Neumann boundary condition ( 1.6 ) is self-adjoint and negative definite modulo constant functions, the Sobolev space norm of any function $h(x)$ in $H^{2}(\Omega)$ is equivalent to $\|h\|+\eta\|\Delta h\|$ . Therefore, the inequality ( 6.19 ) together with Theorem 3.2 and the fact $S(t)\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{A}$ shows that the $u$ -component of the global attractor $\mathscr{A}$ is a bounded set in $H^{2}(\Omega)$ . A quantitative bound of the equivalent $H^{2}$ -norm is given by (6.20) $\sup_{g\,\in\,\mathscr{A}}\|u\|_{H^{2}}\leq\sqrt{K}+\sqrt{D}+\left(aR+bR^{3/2}+2G+J_{e}+k_{1}(|c|+|\gamma|G+\delta G^{2})\sqrt{R}\right)|\Omega|^{1/2}.$ Combined ( 6.20 ) with Lemma 6.2 and Lemma 6.3 , we have proved that the global attractor $\mathscr{A}$ is a bounded set in the regular space $\Gamma=H^{2}(\Omega)\times L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})$ .
∎ Conclusions . In this paper, the diffusive Hindmarsh-Rose equations with memristors are proposed as a new mathematical model of neuron dynamics, which is a hybrid system of coupled partial differential equation of the membrane potential for a neuron cell and three ordinary differential equations of the fast and slow ion channels plus a memristive variable featuring the dynamical memory due to the electromagnetic flux effect. The rationality of such a model is at least biologically explicit in view of the long axons of neuron cells in brain and nerve systems. Global dynamics for the solution semiflow of this memristive Hindmarsh-Rose system is studied under no conditions of any naturally involved biological and mathematical parameters. The main result is the existence of a unique global attractor for this dynamical system or called semiflow generated by the weak solutions in the basic state space $E=L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{4})$ . Due to the quadratic nonlinear memductance and its nonlinear coupling with the membrane potential variable in the main partial differential equation, the challenging proofs of dissipativity and asymptotic compactness are carried out through many steps of sophisticated a priori uniform estimates and the Kolmogorov-Riesz compactness approach. Moreover, the spatial regularity of this global attractor in the space $\Gamma=H^{2}(\Omega)\times L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})$ is also proved for one-dimensional domain. The quantitative bounds of the region containing this global attractor in the state space $E$ and the region in the regular space $\Gamma$ are explicitly provided, which can be used to facilitate further researches on stable or unstable equilibria patterns, coexisting chimeras, bifurcation and firing patterns, or chaotic local attractors.
