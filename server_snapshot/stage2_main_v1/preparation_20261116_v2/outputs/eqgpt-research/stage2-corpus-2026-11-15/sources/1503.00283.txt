In this article we consider the inviscid two-dimensional shallow water equations in a rectangle. The flow occurs near a stationary solution in the so called supercritical regime and we establish short term existence of smooth solutions for the corresponding initial and boundary value problem. Key words and phrases: Shallow water equations, inviscid flow, initial and boundary value problems 1. Introduction Motivated by the study of the inviscid primitive equations, we consider in this article the inviscid two-dimensional shallow water equations in a rectangle in the so-called supercritical regime. It has been shown that a certain vertical expansion of the inviscid primitive equations leads to a system of coupled nonlinear equations similar to the inviscid shallow water equations; see [ RTT08b ] and [ HT14a ] . Hence beside their intrinsic interest, the nonlinear shallow water equations can be seen as one mode of the vertical expansion of the primitive equations. The issue of the boundary conditions to be associated with the primitive or shallow water equations has been emphasized as a major problem and limitation for the so-called Local Area Models for which weather predictions are sought and simulations are performed within a domain for which the boundary has no physical significance, so that there are no physical laws prescribing the boundary conditions (see [ WPT97 ] and e.g. [ RTT08a , RTT08b ] , [ CSTT12 , SLTT ] ).
The choice of the boundary conditions relies then on mathematical considerations
(derivation of a well-posed mathematical problem), and on general computational considerations and physical intuition. The boundary conditions suitable for the one-dimensional shallow water equations were derived in an intuitive context in the book of Whitham [ Whi99 ] and in [ NHF08 ] ; see [ PT13 ] for a rigorous study. For general results on boundary value problems for quasilinear hyperbolic system in space dimension one see [ LY85 ] ; for initial and boundary value problems for hyperbolic equations in smooth domain see the thorough book [ BS07 ] . The present article follows the study of the one-dimensional inviscid shallow water equations in [ PT13 , HPT11 ] and the study of the linearized shallow water equations in [ HT14a ] . In the study of the linearized inviscid shallow water equations in [ HT14a ] we have shown that five cases can occur depending on the respective values of the velocity and the height (not counting the non-generic cases and the symmetries). The nonlinear case that we consider in this article relates to what was called the supercritical case in [ HT14a ] ; see [ HT14b ] for the study of a subcritical case. In this article, we consider the inviscid fully nonlinear 2D shallow water equations (SWE) (1.1) $\begin{cases}u_{t}+uu_{x}+vu_{y}+g\phi_{x}-fv=0,\\
v_{t}+uv_{x}+vv_{y}+g\phi_{y}+fu=0,\\
\phi_{t}+u\phi_{x}+v\phi_{y}+\phi(u_{x}+v_{y})=0;\end{cases}$ here $U=(u,v,\phi)^{t},(x,y)\in\Omega:=(0,L_{1})\times(0,L_{2}),t\in(0,T)$ , $u$ and $v$ are the two horizontal components of the velocity, $\phi$ is the height of the water, and $g$ is the gravitational acceleration, $f$ is the Coriolis parameter.
The first and second equations ( 1.1 ) are derived from the equations of conservation of horizontal momentum, and the third one expresses the conservation of mass.
We consider equations ( 1.1 ) for certain values of $u,v,\phi$ as described below, corresponding to a ”supercritical” flow and we associate with ( 1.1 ), initial conditions for $u,v,\phi$ and boundary conditions at $x=0$ and $y=0$ , $u,v,\phi$ vanishing on that part of the boundary. This article is organized as follows. After this introductory section, we derive in Section 2 suitable density theorems, density of certain smooth functions in certain function spaces of Sobolev type. Section 3 is devoted to the modified (symmetrized) SWE operator for the time-independent and the time-dependent cases. It prepares Section 4 in which we deal with the linear SWE, linearized around a non-constant time-dependent flow unlike in [ HT14a ] where the background flow is time-independent. In Section 4 we prove the well-posedness of the linearized SWE at the price of a loss of derivatives (see Theorem 4.1 ), and then the well-posedness of the linearized SWE in a short time without a loss of derivatives (see Theorem 4.2 ). Section 5 considers the fully nonlinear SWE, for which the local well-posedness result is obtained. In the Appendices A and B , we collect some useful theorems about semigroup and evolution systems, and several classical estimates about functions in Sobolev spaces. 2. The density theorems In this section, we establish general density theorems for certain Sobolev spaces; the results supplement and complement those of Section 3 in [ HT14a ] which we recall when needed. These theorems have independent interest, and also will be needed for proving later on that $-A$ generates a quasi-contraction semigroup on certain Sobolev spaces, where $A$ is the 2D modified SWE operator associated with suitable boundary conditions. T\partial^{\alpha}(\tilde{\theta}_{\epsilon}|_{\Omega})\rightarrow T\partial^{\alpha}\theta,\quad\text{in }L^{2}(\Omega),\text{ as }\epsilon\rightarrow 0,\quad\forall\,|{\alpha}|=m.\end{cases}$ Finally, $\tilde{\theta}_{\epsilon}\big|_{\Omega}$ vanishes in a neighborhood of $\boldsymbol{\Gamma}$ since the support of $\tilde{\theta}_{\epsilon}\big|_{\Omega}$ is away from $\boldsymbol{\Gamma}$ by the choice of $\rho$ . We thus completed the proof of Theorem 2.2 .

Boundary conditions We aim to determine the boundary conditions which are suitable for the system (3.5) $\mathcal{A}^{0}(\widehat{U})U=\mathcal{E}_{1}^{0}(\widehat{U})U_{x}+\mathcal{E}_{2}^{0}(\widehat{U})U_{y}=F,$ where $F\in\mathcal{H}^{m}_{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}(\Omega)^{3}$ . With assumption ( 3.4 ), we see that $\mathcal{E}_{1}^{0}$ and $\mathcal{E}_{2}^{0}$ are both positive definite. Thus, it is natural to treat either the $x$ - or $y$ -direction as the time-like direction. Let us choose the $y$ -direction, which means that we first need to specify the boundary conditions at $y=0$ (time-like initial conditions); choosing the $x$ -direction would lead to the same result. Multiplying both sides of ( 3.5 ) by $(\mathcal{E}_{2}^{0})^{-1}$ gives (3.6) $U_{y}+\mathcal{E}_{2}^{0}(\widehat{U})^{-1}\mathcal{E}_{1}^{0}(\widehat{U})U_{x}=\mathcal{E}_{2}^{0}(\widehat{U})^{-1}F.$ We set $\kappa_{0}(\widehat{U})=\sqrt{g(\hat{u}^{2}+\hat{v}^{2}-g\hat{\phi})/\hat{\phi}}$ , and we explicitly compute the eigenvalues of $(\mathcal{E}_{2}^{0})^{-1}\mathcal{E}_{1}^{0}$ : (3.7) $\lambda_{1}=\frac{\hat{u}\hat{v}+\hat{\phi}\kappa_{0}}{\hat{v}^{2}-g\hat{\phi}},\hskip 6.0pt\lambda_{2}=\frac{\hat{u}\hat{v}-\hat{\phi}\kappa_{0}}{\hat{v}^{2}-g\hat{\phi}},\hskip 6.0pt\lambda_{3}=\frac{\hat{u}}{\hat{v}}.$ Note that all the eigenvalues $\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}$ of $(\mathcal{E}_{2}^{0})^{-1}\mathcal{E}_{1}^{0}$ are positive under assumption ( 3.4 ). Therefore, from the general hyperbolic theory (see Chapter 4 in [ BS07 ] ), it is necessary and sufficient to specify the boundary conditions for $U$ at $x=0$ in order to solve ( 3.6 ) in $U$ . In conclusion, in order to solve ( 3.5 ) in $U$ , we need to specify the boundary conditions for $U$ at $x=0$ and $y=0$ . The surjectivity of $\omega+A$ We set $\omega_{0}=C_{1}(M)$ , where $C_{1}(M)$ is the constant appearing in ( 3.17 ), and we prove the following lemma. Lemma 3.2 . Let $\omega$ be a real number which is greater than $\omega_{0}$ . Then if $F$ belongs to $\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}^{m}$ with $m\geq 3$ , the equation (3.18) $\mathcal{E}_{1}^{0}(\widehat{U})U_{x}+\mathcal{E}_{2}^{0}(\widehat{U})U_{y}+\omega U=F,$ associated with the following boundary conditions (3.19) $U=0,\text{ on }{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}=\Gamma_{1}\cup\Gamma_{3}=\{y=0\}\cup\{x=0\},$ admits a unique solution $U$ in $\mathcal{D}(A)$ . Proof. Since $\mathcal{E}_{2}^{0}$ is nonsingular, multiplying by $(\mathcal{E}_{2}^{0})^{-1}$ on both sides of ( 3.18 ) gives (3.20) $U_{y}+\mathcal{E}_{2}^{0}(\widehat{U})^{-1}\mathcal{E}_{1}^{0}(\widehat{U})U_{x}+\mathcal{E}_{2}^{0}(\widehat{U})^{-1}\omega U=\mathcal{E}_{2}^{0}(\widehat{U})^{-1}F.$ Let us treat again the $y$ -direction as the time-like direction; then ( 3.20 ) becomes a 1-dimensional hyperbolic system.
We observe that the boundary ( $x$ -direction only) is a regular open subset in $\mathbb{R}$ , and that the boundary conditions satisfy the uniform Lopatinskii condition (see [ BS07 , Chapter 9] or [ CP82 , Chapter 7] ). Hence, the general results in [ BS07 , Chapter 9] (see also [ CP82 , Chapter 7] ) guarantee the existence and uniqueness of a solution $U$ for ( 3.20 ) and ( 3.19 ).
Using ( 3.20 ) and the boundary condition ( 3.19 ) at $y=0$ , we can conclude by induction that $\partial^{k}_{y}U|_{y=0}=0$ for all $0\leq k\leq m$ . Similar results also holds for the $x$ -direction. Therefore, the solution $U$ also satisfies the compatibility boundary conditions: (3.21) $\begin{cases}\partial^{k}_{x}U=0,\text{ on }\Gamma_{1}=\{x=0\},\,\forall\,0\leq k\leq m,\\
\partial^{k}_{y}U=0,\text{ on }\Gamma_{3}=\{y=0\},\,\forall\,0\leq k\leq m,\end{cases}$ since $F$ belongs to $\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}^{m}$ (i.e. it satisfies ( 3.21 ) with $m$ replaced by $m-1$ ).
It remains to show that the solution $U$ actually belongs to $\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}^{m}$ if $F$ belongs to $\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}^{m}$ . For all $0\leq|\alpha|\leq m$ , we deduce from ( 3.18 ) that $\partial^{\alpha}U$ satisfies the following equations (3.22) $\mathcal{E}_{1}^{0}(\partial^{\alpha}U)_{x}+\mathcal{E}_{2}^{0}(\partial^{\alpha}U)_{y}+\omega\partial^{\alpha}U=\partial^{\alpha}F-[\partial^{\alpha},\mathcal{E}_{1}^{0}]U_{x}-[\partial^{\alpha},\mathcal{E}_{2}^{0}]U_{y}.$ Taking the $L^{2}(\Omega)$ scalar product of each side of ( 3.22 ) with $\partial^{\alpha}U$ and integrating by parts, we arrive at (3.23) $\begin{split}\omega\big\lVert\partial^{\alpha}U\big\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+&\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\int_{0}^{L_{2}}\langle\mathcal{E}_{1}^{0}\partial^{\alpha}U,\partial^{\alpha}U\rangle\bigg|_{x=0}^{x=L_{1}}dy+\int_{0}^{L_{1}}\langle\mathcal{E}_{2}^{0}\partial^{\alpha}U,\partial^{\alpha}U\rangle\bigg|_{y=0}^{y=L_{2}}dx\bigg)\\
&=\langle\partial^{\alpha}F,\partial^{\alpha}U\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle\big(\mathcal{E}_{1,x}^{0}+\mathcal{E}_{2,y}^{0}\big)\partial^{\alpha}U,\partial^{\alpha}U\rangle\\
&\hskip 20.0pt-\langle[\partial^{\alpha},\mathcal{E}_{1}^{0}]U_{x},\partial^{\alpha}U\rangle-\langle[\partial^{\alpha},\mathcal{E}_{2}^{0}]U_{y},\partial^{\alpha}U\rangle.\end{split}$ The compatibility boundary conditions ( 3.21 ) and the fact that $\mathcal{E}_{1}^{0}$ and $\mathcal{E}_{1}^{0}$ are both positive definite imply that the boundary terms in the left-hand side of ( 3.23 ) are nonnegative, and thus the left-hand side of ( 3.23 ) is larger than $\omega\big\lVert\partial^{\alpha}U\big\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}$ . For the right-hand side of ( 3.23 ), we use the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to estimate the first term and the same arguments as for ( 3.14 )-( 3.17 ) to estimate the last three terms; then summing ( 3.23 ) for all $|\alpha|\leq m$ yields: (3.24) $\begin{split}\omega\big\lVert U\big\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}^{m}}^{2}&\leq\big\lVert F\big\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}^{m}}\big\lVert U\big\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}^{m}}+C_{1}(M)\big\lVert U\big\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}^{m}}^{2}\\
&\leq\big\lVert F\big\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}^{m}}\big\lVert U\big\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}^{m}}+\omega_{0}\big\lVert U\big\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}^{m}}^{2},\\
\end{split}$ with $\omega_{0}$ being a constant depending only on $M$ . This implies that $U$ belongs to $\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}^{m}$ by the assumption $\omega>\omega_{0}$ . Finally, $AU$ also belongs to $\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}^{m}$ since $\partial^{\alpha}(AU)|_{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}=0,\,\forall|\alpha|\leq m-1$ and $AU=\mathcal{E}_{1}^{0}(\widehat{U})U_{x}+\mathcal{E}_{2}^{0}(\widehat{U})U_{y}=F-\omega U.$ Therefore $U$ belongs to $\mathcal{D}(A)$ , and the proof is complete.

For a function $w$ defined on $\Omega$ , $(\rho_{\epsilon}*\tilde{w})|_{\Omega}$ stands for the restriction to $\Omega$ of $\rho_{\epsilon}*\tilde{w}$ , where $\tilde{w}$ is the extension of $w$ by $0$ outside $\Omega$ , and similar notations are also used for the functions defined in $\Omega_{T}$ , or the vector functions (with the notation applied to each component of the vector functions).

Hence for $\epsilon$ small enough, we can assume that $\begin{split}&\big\lVert U_{0}^{\epsilon}\big\rVert_{H^{m}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq 2\big\lVert U_{0}\big\rVert_{H^{m}(\Omega)}^{2},\hskip 6.0pt\big\lVert F^{\epsilon}\big\rVert_{L^{\infty}(H^{m})}^{2}\leq 2\big\lVert F\big\rVert_{L^{2}(H^{m})}^{2};\\
&\big\lVert\widehat{U}^{\epsilon}\big\rVert_{L^{\infty}(H^{m})}\leq 2M,\hskip 6.0pt\big\lVert\widehat{U}_{t}^{\epsilon}\big\rVert_{L^{\infty}(H^{m-1})}\leq 2M.\end{split}$ In addition, with the choice of $\rho$ , we have that the support of $U_{0}^{\epsilon}$ is away from ${\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}=\{x=0\}\cup\{y=0\}$ , and so is the support of $F^{\epsilon}(t)$ for all $t\in[0,T]$ . Therefore, $U_{0}^{\epsilon},F^{\epsilon}(t)$ also satisfy ( 3.9 ) for all $t\in[0,T]$ .
Then using Theorem 4.1 and Remarks 4.1 - 4.2 , there exists a smooth solution $U^{\epsilon}$ for system ( 4.5 ) with $U_{0},F,\widehat{U}$ replaced by $U_{0}^{\epsilon},F^{\epsilon},\widehat{U}^{\epsilon}$ , and $U^{\epsilon}$ also satisfies the compatibility boundary conditions ( 3.9 ). For $T>0$ small enough only depending on the bound of the $L^{\infty}(H^{m})$ -norm of $\widehat{U}$ and the $L^{\infty}(H^{m-1})$ -norm of $\widehat{U}_{t}$ , then the a priori estimates ( 4.22 ) gives that (4.27) $\begin{split}\big\lVert U^{\epsilon}\big\rVert_{L^{\infty}(H^{m})}^{2}&\leq 2C_{0}(2M,T)\big(\big\lVert U_{0}^{\epsilon}\big\rVert_{H^{m}(\Omega)}^{2}+T\big\lVert F^{\epsilon}\big\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(H^{m})}\big)\\
&\leq 4C_{0}(2M,T)\big(\big\lVert U_{0}\big\rVert_{H^{m}(\Omega)}^{2}+T\big\lVert F\big\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(H^{m})}\big).\end{split}$ where $2M$ is the bound of the $L^{\infty}(H^{m})$ -norm of $\widehat{U}^{\epsilon}$ and the $L^{\infty}(H^{m-1})$ -norm of $\widehat{U}_{t}^{\epsilon}$ . The inequality ( 4.27 ) gives a uniform bound on the sequence $\{U^{\epsilon}\}$ , which implies that there exists a subsequence of $\{U^{\epsilon}\}$ converging weak-star in $L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{m}(\Omega))$ . The next point is to prove that the sequence $\{U^{\epsilon}\}$ is Cauchy in $L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))$ . For that purpose, we write (4.28) $W^{\epsilon}=U^{\epsilon}-U^{\epsilon^{\prime}},$ and subtracting the corresponding equations of form ( 4.5 ) satisfied by $U^{\epsilon}$ and $U^{\epsilon^{\prime}}$ , we obtain (4.29) $\begin{cases}W^{\epsilon}_{t}+\mathcal{E}_{1}(\widehat{U}^{\epsilon})W^{\epsilon}_{x}+\mathcal{E}_{2}(\widehat{U}^{\epsilon})W^{\epsilon}_{y}+\ell(W^{\epsilon})=\widehat{F},\\
W^{\epsilon}(0)=U_{0}^{\epsilon}-U_{0}^{\epsilon^{\prime}},\\
W^{\epsilon}|_{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}=0,\end{cases}$ where $\widehat{F}=F^{\epsilon}-F^{\epsilon^{\prime}}+\big(\mathcal{E}_{1}(\widehat{U}^{\epsilon^{\prime}})-\mathcal{E}_{1}(\widehat{U}^{\epsilon})\big)U_{x}^{\epsilon^{\prime}}+\big(\mathcal{E}_{2}(\widehat{U}^{\epsilon^{\prime}})-\mathcal{E}_{2}(\widehat{U}^{\epsilon})\big)U_{y}^{\epsilon^{\prime}}.$ Noticing that ( 4.29 ) has the same form as ( 4.5 ), therefore proceeding exactly as for ( 4.17 ), we obtain (4.30) $\big\lVert W^{\epsilon}\big\rVert_{L^{\infty}(L^{2})}^{2}\leq C_{0}(2M,T)\big(\big\lVert U_{0}^{\epsilon}-U_{0}^{\epsilon^{\prime}}\big\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+T\big\lVert\widehat{F}\big\rVert_{L^{\infty}(L^{2})}^{2}\big).$ Using the explicit expressions for $\mathcal{E}_{1}$ and $\mathcal{E}_{2}$ , direct computation shows that (4.31) $\big\lVert\mathcal{E}_{1}(\widehat{U}^{\epsilon^{\prime}})-\mathcal{E}_{1}(\widehat{U}^{\epsilon})\big\rVert_{L^{\infty}(L^{2})}^{2},\big\lVert\mathcal{E}_{2}(\widehat{U}^{\epsilon^{\prime}})-\mathcal{E}_{2}(\widehat{U}^{\epsilon})\big\rVert_{L^{\infty}(L^{2})}^{2}\leq 3\big\lVert\widehat{U}^{\epsilon}-\widehat{U}^{\epsilon^{\prime}}\big\rVert_{L^{\infty}(L^{2})}^{2}.$ Therefore, combining the estimates in ( 4.30 ) and ( 4.31 ), we obtain (4.32) $\begin{split}\big\lVert U^{\epsilon}-U^{\epsilon^{\prime}}\big\rVert_{L^{\infty}(L^{2})}^{2}&\leq C_{0}(2M,T)\big(\big\lVert U_{0}^{\epsilon}-U_{0}^{\epsilon^{\prime}}\big\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+T\big\lVert F^{\epsilon}-F^{\epsilon^{\prime}}\big\rVert_{L^{\infty}(L^{2})}^{2}\\
&\hskip 16.0pt+3T\big\lVert\widehat{U}^{\epsilon}-\widehat{U}^{\epsilon^{\prime}}\big\rVert_{L^{\infty}(L^{2})}^{2}(\big\lVert U_{x}^{\epsilon^{\prime}}\big\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{T})}^{2}+\big\lVert U_{x}^{\epsilon^{\prime}}\big\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{T})}^{2})\big),\end{split}$ which, with the use of the Sobolev embedding $H^{2}(\Omega)\subset L^{\infty}(\Omega)$ and noting that $m\geq 3$ , is furthermore bounded by (4.33) $C_{0}(2M,T)\big(\big\lVert U_{0}^{\epsilon}-U_{0}^{\epsilon^{\prime}}\big\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+T\big\lVert F^{\epsilon}-F^{\epsilon^{\prime}}\big\rVert_{L^{\infty}(L^{2})}^{2}+6T\big\lVert U^{\epsilon^{\prime}}\big\rVert_{L^{\infty}(H^{m})}^{2}\big\lVert\widehat{U}^{\epsilon}-\widehat{U}^{\epsilon^{\prime}}\big\rVert_{L^{\infty}(L^{2})}^{2}\big).$ Since $\{U_{0}^{\epsilon}\}$ is a Cauchy sequence in $L^{2}(\Omega)$ , and $\{F^{\epsilon}\},\{\widehat{U}^{\epsilon}\}$ are Cauchy sequences in $L^{\infty}(L^{2})$ , and $\{U^{\epsilon}\}$ is uniformly bounded in the $L^{\infty}(H^{m})$ -norm by ( 4.27 ), we obtain from ( 4.32 )-( 4.33 ) that $\{U^{\epsilon}\}$ is also a Cauchy sequence in $L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))$ . Hence by $L^{2}-H^{m}$ interpolation, the sequence $\{U^{\epsilon}\}$ converges strongly in $L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{m-1}(\Omega))$ to a function $U$ which belongs to $L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{m}(\Omega))$ .
Using Proposition 2.3 , we obtain that $U$ satisfies the compatibility boundary conditions ( 3.9 ) since $U^{\epsilon}$ satisfies ( 3.9 ). The a priori estimates ( 4.25 ) give a uniform bound on the sequence $\{U^{\epsilon}_{t}\}$ , i.e. (4.34) $\begin{split}\big\lVert U^{\epsilon}_{t}\big\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(H^{m-1})}&\leq 2\big\lVert F^{\epsilon}\big\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(H^{m-1})}+2C_{1}(2M)\big\lVert U^{\epsilon}\big\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(H^{m})}\\
&\leq 4\big\lVert F\big\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(H^{m-1})}+2C_{1}(2M)\big\lVert U^{\epsilon}\big\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(H^{m})},\end{split}$ since the sequence $\{U^{\epsilon}\}$ are uniformly bounded in the $L^{\infty}(H^{m})$ -norm. Proceeding exactly as for ( 4.26 ), we obtain that (4.35) $\big\lVert U^{\epsilon}_{t}-U^{\epsilon^{\prime}}_{t}\big\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(L^{2})}\leq 2\big\lVert\widehat{F}\big\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(L^{2})}+2C_{1}(2M)\big\lVert U^{\epsilon}-U^{\epsilon^{\prime}}\big\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(H^{1})},$ which implies that $\{U^{\epsilon}_{t}\}$ is also a Cauchy sequence in $L^{\infty}(L^{2})$ by using the above estimates for $\widehat{F}$ and noting that $\{U^{\epsilon}\}$ is Cauchy in $L^{\infty}(H^{m-1})$ with $m\geq 3$ . Therefore, by $L^{2}-H^{m-1}$ interpolation, we obtain that $\{U^{\epsilon}_{t}\}$ converges strongly in $L^{\infty}(H^{m-2})$ to a function $V$ which belongs to $L^{\infty}(H^{m-1})$ . Now passing to the limit, we obtain that $U$ solves ( 4.5 ), and $U_{t}=V$ at least in the sense of distributions. Finally, proceeding exactly as in Subsection 4.1 , we see that the solution $U$ satisfies the estimates ( 4.22 ) and ( 4.25 ); the uniqueness directly follows from the estimate ( 4.22 ). We thus completed the proof.

The fully nonlinear shallow water system In this section, we aim to investigate the well-posedness for Eqs. ( 1.1 ) associated with suitable initial conditions and homogeneous boundary conditions, and we will make a remark about the case of non-homogeneous boundary conditions. Keeping the notations introduced in Section 3 and 4 , the fully nonlinear shallow water system reads in compact form (5.1) $U_{t}+\mathcal{E}_{1}(U)U_{x}+\mathcal{E}_{2}(U)U_{y}+\ell(U)=0.$ 5.1. Stationary solution We want to study system ( 5.1 ) near a stationary solution, and we start by constructing such a stationary solution $(u,v,\phi)=(u_{s},v_{s},\phi_{s})$ . These functions are independent of time and satisfy (5.2) $\mathcal{E}_{1}(U)U_{x}+\mathcal{E}_{2}(U)U_{y}+\ell(U)=0.$ The existence of the general stationary solution $U_{s}$ to ( 5.2 ), which satisfies the supercritical condition, is a 1-dimensional hyperbolic problem if we multiply by $\mathcal{E}_{2}^{-1}$ on both sides of ( 5.2 ) and treat the $y$ -direction as the time-like direction as we already did in Lemma 3.2 . The general results in [ BS07 , Chapter 11] guarantee the existence of the stationary solution $U_{s}$ if we specify suitable initial ( $y$ -direction) and boundary ( $x$ -direction) conditions. Actually, Subsection 2.1 in [ HPT11 ] provides an $y$ -independent stationary solution to ( 5.2 ) satisfying the supercritical condition. But in what follows, we think of $U_{s}$ in a general form (i.e. $U_{s}$ depends both $x$ and $y$ ). We thus choose our stationary solution $U_{s}=(u_{s},v_{s},\phi_{s})^{t}$ of ( 5.2 ) satisfying a strong form of the supercritical condition, i.e. (5.3) $\begin{cases}2c_{0}\leq u_{s},v_{s},\phi_{s}\leq\frac{1}{2}c_{1},\\
u_{s}^{2}-g\phi_{s}\geq 2c_{2}^{2},\,v_{s}^{2}-g\phi_{s}\geq 2c_{2}^{2},\\
\end{cases}$ where $c_{0},c_{1},c_{2}$ are given, positive constants which will play the same role as those in ( 4.6 ). We set $U=U_{s}+\widetilde{U}$ .
Note that if we choose $\delta$ sufficiently small so that if $|\widetilde{U}|\leq\delta$ (i.e. $|\tilde{u}|,|\tilde{v}|,|\tilde{\phi}|<\delta$ ), then $U$ satisfies relations similar to ( 5.3 ), that is: (5.4) $\begin{cases}c_{0}\leq\tilde{u}+u_{s},\tilde{v}+v_{s},\tilde{\phi}+\phi_{s}\leq c_{1},\\
(\tilde{u}+u_{s})^{2}-g(\tilde{\phi}+\phi_{s})\geq c_{2}^{2},\,(\tilde{v}+v_{s})^{2}-g(\tilde{\phi}+\phi_{s})\geq c_{2}^{2}.\\
\end{cases}$ The relations ( 5.4 ) will guarantee that we remain in the supercritical case. We then substitute $U=U_{s}+\widetilde{U}$ into ( 5.1 ); we obtain a new system for $\widetilde{U}$ , and dropping the tildes, our new system reads: (5.5) $U_{t}+\mathcal{E}_{1}(U+U_{s})U_{x}+\mathcal{E}_{2}(U+U_{s})U_{y}+\ell(U)=F^{U},$ where (5.6) $\begin{split}F^{U}&=-\mathcal{E}_{1}(U+U_{s})U_{s,x}-\mathcal{E}_{2}(U+U_{s})U_{s,y}-\ell(U_{s})\\
&=\begin{pmatrix}uu_{s,x}+vu_{s,y}\\
uv_{s,x}+vv_{s,y}\\
u\phi_{s,x}+v\phi_{s,y}+\phi(u_{s,x}+v_{s,y})\end{pmatrix}.\end{split}$ In order to have the last equality, we use the fact that $U_{s}$ is a stationary solution satisfying ( 5.2 ).
We supplement ( 5.5 ) with the following initial and homogeneous boundary conditions: (5.7) $\text{I.C. }U(0)=U_{0},\hskip 6.0pt\text{B.C. }U|_{x=0,y=0}=0.$ Observe that we can rewrite ( 5.5 ) as (5.8) $U_{x}=\mathcal{E}_{1}(U+U_{s})^{-1}\big(F^{U}-U_{t}-\mathcal{E}_{2}(U+U_{s})U_{y}-\ell(U)\big);$ at $x=0$ , we immediately see that $U_{x}|_{x=0}=0$ . Applying $\partial_{x}^{k}$ with $1\leq k\leq m-1$ to ( 5.8 ), we can conclude by induction that $\partial_{x}^{k}U|_{x=0}=0$ , for all $k=0,\cdots,m$ .
Similarly, we also obtain that $\partial_{y}^{k}U|_{y=0}=0$ for all $k=0,\cdots,m$ at $y=0$ . Therefore, if $U$ satisfies ( 5.5 ) and ( 5.7 ), then $U$ also satisfies the compatibility boundary conditions ( 3.9 ), i.e. (5.9) $\begin{cases}\partial^{k}_{x}U=0,\text{ on }\Gamma_{1}=\{x=0\},\,\forall\,0\leq k\leq m,\\
\partial^{k}_{y}U=0,\text{ on }\Gamma_{3}=\{y=0\},\,\forall\,0\leq k\leq m.\end{cases}$ 5.2. Nonlinear shallow water system In order to be able to solve the nonlinear system ( 5.5 )-( 5.7 ), we require the initial and boundary conditions to be compatible. We thus assume that $U_{0}$ satisfies the compatibility boundary conditions ( 5.9 ) (i.e. ( 3.9 )). We are now on the stage to prove the following result. Theorem 5.1 . Let there be given the stationary solution $U_{s}\in H^{m+1}(\Omega)$ with $m\geq 3$ , and two positive constants $M_{0},M$ such that $M_{0},M>0,\hskip 6.0pt\sqrt{M_{0}}\in(0,\frac{\delta}{\nu_{m}}],\hskip 6.0ptM=\sqrt{M_{0}}+\big\lVert U_{s}\big\rVert_{H^{m}(\Omega)},$ where $\nu_{m}$ denotes the norm of the Sobolev embedding $H^{m}(\Omega)\subset L^{\infty}(\Omega)$ . We are also given the initial condition $U_{0}\in H^{m}(\Omega)$ which satisfies ( 5.9 ) and (5.10) $\big\lVert U_{0}\big\rVert^{2}_{H^{m}(\Omega)}\leq\text{min}\bigg(M_{0},\,\frac{M_{0}}{2C(U_{s})+4C_{1}(M)C_{0}(M,1)},\,\frac{M_{0}}{4C_{0}(M,1)}\bigg),$ where $C_{0}(M,1)=e^{r_{1}(M)}r_{1}(M)r_{2}$ (resp. $C_{1}(M)$ ) is the constant appearing in ( 4.17 ) (resp. ( 4.25 )), and $C(U_{s})$ only depends on the bound of the $H^{m+1}(\Omega)$ -norm of $U_{s}$ (see ( 5.18 ) below). Then there exists $T>0$ only depending on
the initial data $U_{0}$ and the stationary solution $U_{s}$ such that the system ( 5.5 )-( 5.7 ) admits a unique solution $U$ satisfying $U\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{m}(\Omega)),\hskip 6.0ptU_{t}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{m-1}(\Omega)).$ Proof. As a preliminary, we choose $T$ such that $T\leq 1$ . Considering the compatibility boundary conditions ( 5.9 ), the resolution of the nonlinear system ( 5.5 )-( 5.7 ) will be done using the following iterative scheme (5.11) $\begin{cases}U_{t}^{k+1}+\mathcal{E}_{1}(U^{k}+U_{s})U^{k+1}_{x}+\mathcal{E}_{2}(U^{k}+U_{s})U^{k+1}_{y}+\ell(U^{k+1})=F^{U^{k}},\\
U^{k+1}(0)=U_{0},\\
U^{k+1}|_{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}=0.\\
\end{cases}$ We initiate our iteration scheme by setting $U^{0}=U_{0}$ , and then construct the approximate solutions $U^{k}$ by induction. If we assume that (5.12) $\big\lVert U^{k}\big\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{m}(\Omega))}\leq M_{0},\hskip 6.0pt\big\lVert U_{t}^{k}\big\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{m-1}(\Omega))}\leq M_{0},\hskip 6.0ptU^{k}\text{ satisfies \eqref{eq7.e2}},$ then we have that $F^{U^{k}}$ also satisfies ( 5.9 ). Using the Sobolev embedding $H^{m}(\Omega)\subset L^{\infty}(\Omega)$ , the $L^{\infty}$ -norm of $U^{k}$ is controlled by $\delta$ , which shows that $U^{k}$ satisfies the supercritical condition ( 5.4 ), i.e. $\begin{cases}c_{0}\leq u^{k}+u_{s},v^{k}+v_{s},\phi^{k}+\phi_{s}\leq c_{1},\\
(u^{k}+u_{s})^{2}-g(\phi^{k}+\phi_{s})\geq c_{2}^{2},\,(v^{k}+v_{s})^{2}-g(\phi^{k}+\phi_{s})\geq c_{2}^{2};\end{cases}$ furthermore, we have $\big\lVert U^{k}+U_{s}\big\rVert_{L^{\infty}(H^{m})}\leq\sqrt{M_{0}}+\big\lVert U_{s}\big\rVert_{H^{m}(\Omega)}\leq M,\hskip 6.0pt\big\lVert(U^{k}+U_{s})_{t}\big\rVert_{L^{\infty}(H^{m-1})}\leq\sqrt{M_{0}}\leq M.$ Therefore, for $T>0$ small enough only depending on $M$ ,
applying Theorem 4.2 to ( 5.11 ) with $\widehat{U}=U^{k}+U_{s},F=F^{U^{k}}$ , gives a solution $U^{k+1}$ which satisfies ( 5.9 ), and that (5.13) $\begin{split}\big\lVert U^{k+1}\big\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(H^{m})}&\leq 2C_{0}(M,T)\big(\big\lVert U_{0}\big\rVert_{H^{m}(\Omega)}^{2}+T\big\lVert F^{U^{k}}\big\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(H^{m})}\big)\\
&\leq(\text{using Lemma \ref{lemb.1} $i)$ with $s=m$ and $d=2$ for $F^{U^{k}}$})\\
&\leq 2C_{0}(M,1)\big(\big\lVert U_{0}\big\rVert_{H^{m}(\Omega)}^{2}+TC\big\lVert U_{s}\big\rVert^{2}_{H^{m+1}(\Omega)}\big\lVert U^{k}\big\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(H^{m})}\big),\end{split}$ and (5.14) $\begin{split}\big\lVert U_{t}^{k+1}\big\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(H^{m-1})}\leq 2\big\lVert F^{U^{k}}\big\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(H^{m-1})}+C_{1}(M)\big\lVert U^{k+1}\big\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(H^{m})},\end{split}$ where $C_{0}(M,T)$ (resp. $C_{1}(M)$ ) is the constant appearing in ( 4.17 ) (resp. ( 4.25 )),
and $M$ is the bound of the $L^{\infty}(H^{m})$ -norm of $\widehat{U}$ ( $=U^{k}+U_{s}$ ) and the $L^{\infty}(H^{m-1})$ -norm of $\widehat{U}_{t}$ ( $=U_{t}^{k}$ ). It follows from the explicit form ( 5.6 ) of $F^{U}$ that (5.15) $(F^{U})_{t}=F^{U_{t}}.$ In particular, $(F^{U^{k}})_{t}=F^{U_{t}^{k}}$ . Using also that $U^{k}|_{t=0}=U_{0}$ , we obtain with the mean value theorem that (5.16) $|F^{U^{k}}(t)|\leq|F^{U^{k}}(t)-F^{U_{0}}|+|F^{U_{0}}|\leq t|F^{U_{t}^{k}}(t^{\prime})|+|F^{U_{0}}|,$ for all $t\in[0,T]$ and for some $t^{\prime}\in(0,t)$ , which implies that (5.17) $\begin{split}\big\lVert F^{U^{k}}\big\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(H^{m-1})}&\leq 2T^{2}\big\lVert F^{U_{t}^{k}}\big\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(H^{m-1})}+2\big\lVert F^{U_{0}}\big\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(H^{m-1})}\\
&\leq(\text{using Lemma \ref{lemb.1} $i)$ with $s=m-1$ and $d=2$})\\
&\leq 2T^{2}C\big\lVert U_{s}\big\rVert^{2}_{H^{m}(\Omega)}\big\lVert U_{t}^{k}\big\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(H^{m-1})}+2C\big\lVert U_{s}\big\rVert^{2}_{H^{m}(\Omega)}\big\lVert U_{0}\big\rVert^{2}_{H^{m-1}(\Omega)}.\end{split}$ Gathering the estimates ( 5.13 )-( 5.17 ), and using the assumption ( 5.12 ), we finally arrive at (5.18) $\begin{cases}\big\lVert U^{k+1}\big\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(H^{m})}\leq 2C_{0}(M,1)\big\lVert U_{0}\big\rVert_{H^{m}(\Omega)}^{2}+TC_{0}(M,1)M_{0}C(U_{s}),\\
\big\lVert U_{t}^{k+1}\big\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(H^{m-1})}\leq\big(C(U_{s})+2C_{1}(M)C_{0}(M,1)\big)\big\lVert U_{0}\big\rVert_{H^{m}(\Omega)}^{2}\\
\hskip 100.0pt+TM_{0}C(U_{s})\big(T+2C_{1}(M)C_{0}(M,1)\big),\end{cases}$ where $C(U_{s})$ only depends on the bound of the $H^{m+1}(\Omega)$ -norm of $U_{s}$ .
Note that the first two terms in the right-hand side of ( 5.18 ) are less than $M_{0}/2$ by the assumption ( 5.10 ), and both the second terms in the right hand side of ( 5.18 ) approach $0$ when $T\rightarrow 0$ ; we thus can choose $T$ small enough again such that (5.19) $\big\lVert U^{k+1}\big\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(H^{m})}\leq M_{0},\hskip 6.0pt\big\lVert U_{t}^{k+1}\big\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(H^{m-1})}\leq M_{0},\hskip 6.0ptU^{k+1}\text{ satisfies \eqref{eq7.e2}}.$ Now $U^{k+1}$ also satisfies ( 5.12 ); hence we can continue our construction.
Let us emphasize that the choice of $T$ only depends on $M_{0}$ , $M$ , $U_{s}$ and is independent of $k$ , therefore our iteration scheme can be conducted for all $k$ , and we can construct the sequence $\{U^{k}\}$ as long as the starting point $U^{0}$ satisfies ( 5.12 ), which holds true by the assumption ( 5.10 ). We now have an uniformly bounded sequence $\{U^{k}\}$ at hand, and the next point is to show that the sequence $\{U^{k}\}$ is Cauchy in $L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))$ , which is almost achieved in the proof of Theorem 4.2 .
Let us write $W^{k+1}=U^{k+1}-U^{k}$ ; with $W^{\epsilon},U^{\epsilon},U^{\epsilon^{\prime}},\widehat{U}^{\epsilon},\widehat{U}^{\epsilon^{\prime}},F^{\epsilon},F^{\epsilon^{\prime}}$ replaced by $W^{k+1},U^{k+1},U^{k},U^{k}+U_{s},U^{k-1}+U_{s},F^{U^{k}},F^{U^{k-1}}$ in ( 4.29 ), proceeding exactly as for ( 4.32 )-( 4.33 ) and noticing that $W^{k+1}(0)=0$ , we obtain (5.20) $\begin{split}\big\lVert U^{k+1}-U^{k}\big\rVert_{L^{\infty}(L^{2})}^{2}&\leq C_{0}(M,T)T\big(\big\lVert F^{U^{k}}-F^{U^{k-1}}\big\rVert_{L^{\infty}(L^{2})}^{2}\\
&\hskip 20.0pt+6\big\lVert U^{k}\big\rVert_{L^{\infty}(H^{m})}^{2}\big\lVert U^{k}-U^{k-1}\big\rVert_{L^{\infty}(L^{2})}^{2}\big).\end{split}$ Using the explicit expression ( 5.6 ) for $F^{U}$ and the Sobolev embedding $H^{m}(\Omega)\subset L^{\infty}(\Omega)$ , we estimate (5.21) $\begin{split}\big\lVert F^{U^{k}}-F^{U^{k-1}}\big\rVert_{L^{\infty}(L^{2})}^{2}&\leq C(\big\lVert U_{s}\big\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)})\big\lVert U^{k}-U^{k-1}\big\rVert_{L^{\infty}(L^{2})}^{2}\\
&\leq C(\big\lVert U_{s}\big\rVert_{H^{m}(\Omega)})\big\lVert U^{k}-U^{k-1}\big\rVert_{L^{\infty}(L^{2})}^{2}.\end{split}$ Combining ( 5.20 ) and ( 5.21 ) and using the uniform boundedness of the $L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{m}(\Omega))$ -norm of $U^{k}$ , we obtain (5.22) $\big\lVert U^{k+1}-U^{k}\big\rVert_{L^{\infty}(L^{2})}^{2}\leq C_{0}(M,T)T\big(C(\big\lVert U_{s}\big\rVert_{H^{m}(\Omega)})+6M_{0}\big)\big\lVert U^{k}-U^{k-1}\big\rVert_{L^{\infty}(L^{2})}^{2}.$ Upon reducing $T$ again, we can assume that (5.23) $C_{0}(M,T)T\big(C(\big\lVert U_{s}\big\rVert_{H^{m}(\Omega)})+6M_{0}\big)\leq\frac{1}{4};$ then the inequality ( 5.22 ) implies that (5.24) $\big\lVert U^{k+1}-U^{k}\big\rVert_{L^{\infty}(L^{2})}\leq\frac{1}{2}\big\lVert U^{k}-U^{k-1}\big\rVert_{L^{\infty}(L^{2})}\leq\cdots\leq(\frac{1}{2})^{k}\big\lVert U^{1}-U^{0}\big\rVert_{L^{\infty}(L^{2})}.$ Therefore { $U^{k}$ } is Cauchy in $L^{\infty}(L^{2})$ ; let $U$ be the limit of this sequence.
Note also that { $U^{k}$ } is uniformly bounded in $L^{\infty}(H^{m})$ , so by $L^{2}-H^{m}$ interpolation,
the sequence { $U^{k}$ } converges strongly in $L^{\infty}(H^{m-1})$ to $U\in L^{\infty}(H^{m})$ .
Similarly as for ( 4.35 ), we can obtain that $\{U_{t}^{k}\}$ converges strongly in $L^{\infty}(H^{m-2})$ to a function $V\in L^{\infty}(H^{m-1})$ . Now passing to the limit in ( 5.11 ), we obtain that $U$ solves ( 5.5 ) and $U_{t}=V$ , and that $U$ belongs to $L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{m}(\Omega))$ and $U_{t}$ belongs to $L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{m-1}(\Omega))$ . The uniqueness directly follows from ( 5.22 ). This completes the proof.

After completing this article, we found that we can also use a finite difference method to prove the existence and uniqueness of the fully nonlinear SWE (i.e. Theorem 5.1 ) by observing that we have the energy estimates ( 3.24 ) for the corresponding boundary value problem (although slightly different), which is the one we only need for the finite difference method. We omit the details here. The finite difference method has the advantages that we do not need the density theorems in Section 2 and the evolution semigroup technique. However, the evolution semigroup technique has its own advantage that it tells us how we lost two space derivatives for the well-posedness of the linear SWE (see Theorem 4.1 ), and that explains why we only have local well-posedness for the fully nonlinear SWE in some sense. Appendix A Preliminary results about semigroups and evolution families This appendix collects some basic facts on the semigroups and evolution families and the characterization of their generators. The main references are the classical books by K. Yosida [ Yos80 ] , and by E. Hille and R.S. Phillips [ HP74 ] , and by A. Pazy [ Paz83 ] and the book by K.-J. Engel and R. Nagel [ EN00 ] . Definition A.1 . A family $(S(t))_{t\geq 0}$ of bounded linear operators on a Banach space $X$ is called a strongly continuous (one-parameter) semigroup (or $\mathcal{C}_{0}$ -semigroup) if it satisfies i) $S(0)=I,S(t+s)=S(t)S(s)$ for all $t,s\geq 0$ ; ii) $\xi_{x}:t\mapsto\xi_{x}(t):=S(t)x$ is continuous from $\mathbb{R}_{+}$ into $X$ for every $x\in X$ . Proposition A.1 . For every strongly continuous semigroup $(S(t))_{t\geq 0}$ , there exist constants $\omega\in\mathbb{R}$ and $M\geq 1$ such that (A.1) $\big\lVert S(t)\big\rVert\leq Me^{\omega t}$ for all $t\geq 0$ . Definition A.2 . A strongly continuous semigroup is called quasi-contraction if we can take $M=1$ in ( A.1 ), and called bounded if $\omega=0$ , and called contraction if $\omega=0$ and $M=1$ is possible. Definition A.3 . The generator $A:\mathcal{D}(A)\subset X\mapsto X$ of a strongly continuous semigroup $(S(t))_{t\geq 0}$ on a Banach space $X$ is the operator $Ax:=\dot{\xi}_{x}(0)=\lim_{h\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{h}(S(h)x-x)$ defined for every $x$ in its domain $\mathcal{D}(A):=\{x\in X\,:\,t\mapsto\xi_{x}(t)\text{ is right differentiable in }t\text{ at }t=0\}.$ Note that if $\xi_{x}(t)$ is right differentiable in $t$ at $t=0$ , it is also differentiable at $t$ , for any $t\geq 0$ . Theorem A.1 (Hille-Yosida theorem) . Let $(A,\mathcal{D}(A))$ be a positive operator on a Hilbert space $H$ such that $\omega+A$ is surjective for some $\omega>0$ . Then $-A$ generates a contraction semigroup. We recall that on a Hilbert space, the linear operator $A$ is called positive if $\langle Ax,x\rangle\geq 0$ for all $x\in\mathcal{D}(A)$ . Theorem A.2 (Uniqueness Theorem) . Let $(A,\mathcal{D}(A))$ be a closed, densely defined operator on a Banach space $X$ , and let $Y$ be a subspace of $X$ which is continuously embedded in $X$ (in symbols: $Y\hookrightarrow X$ ). The part of $A$ in $Y$ is the operator $A_{|}$ defined by $A_{|}y:=Ay$ with domain $\mathcal{D}(A_{|})=\{y\in\mathcal{D}(A)\cap Y:Ay\in Y\}$ .
Suppose that $(A,\mathcal{D}(A))$ generates a strongly continuous semigroup $(S(t))_{t\geq 0}$ on $X$ , and $(A_{|},\mathcal{D}(A_{|}))$ also generates a strongly continuous semigroup $(R(t))_{t\geq 0}$ on $Y$ . Then (A.2) $S(t)y=R(t)y$ holds for all $y\in Y$ and $t\geq 0$ . Furthermore, the subspace $Y$ is $A$ -admissible, i.e. $Y$ is an invariant subspace of $S(t),\,t\geq 0$ , and the restriction of $S(t)$ to $Y$ , which is $R(t)$ , is a strongly continuous semigroup on $Y$ . Proof. The identity ( A.2 ) immediately follows from the uniqueness of the following Cauchy problem $\begin{cases}\dot{u}(t)=Au(t),\,\forall\,t\geq 0,\\
u(0)=y\in Y;\end{cases}$ and that $Y$ is $A$ -admissible follows from ( A.2 ) and the assumption. ∎ Theorem A.3 (Bounded Perturbation Theorem I) . Let $(A,\mathcal{D}(A))$ be the infinitesimal generator of a strongly continuous semigroup $(S(t))_{t\geq 0}$ on a Banach space $X$ satisfying $\big\lVert S(t)\big\rVert\leq M_{0}e^{\omega t},\,\forall\,t\geq 0,$ where $\omega\in\mathbb{R},M_{0}\geq 1$ . If $B\in\mathcal{L}(X)$ , then $C:=A+B$ , with $\mathcal{D}(C):=\mathcal{D}(A)$ generates a strongly continuous semigroup $(R(t))_{t\geq 0}$ satisfying $\big\lVert R(t)\big\rVert\leq M_{0}e^{(\omega+M_{0}\big\lVert B\big\rVert)t},\,\forall\,t\geq 0.$ The following results are taken from [ Paz83 , Chapter 5] . Definition A.4 . A two parameter family of bounded linear operators $W(t,s),0\leq s\leq t\leq T$ , on a Banach space $X$ is called an evolution system if the two following conditions are satisfied: i) $W(s,s)=I,W(t,r)W(r,s)=W(t,s)$ for $0\leq s\leq r\leq t\leq T$ ; ii) $(t,s)\mapsto W(t,s)$ is strongly continuous for $0\leq s\leq t\leq T$ . Let $I$ be the interval $[0,T]$ , and $(A(t),\mathcal{D}(A(t))_{t\in I}$ be the family of infinitesimal generators of strongly continuous semigroups $S_{t}(s),s\geq 0,$ on a Banach space $X$ , and let $Y$ be a Banach space which is densely and continuously embedded into $X$ . The following stability definition appeared in [ Kat70 , Kat73 ] : Definition A.5 (Kato-stability and Kato-condition) . We say that the family $\{A(t)\}_{t\in I}$ is Kato-stable , if there exist constants $M\geq 1$ and $\omega\in\mathbb{R}$ such that $\big\lVert\Pi_{j=1}^{n}e^{s_{j}A(t_{j})}\big\rVert\leq Me^{\omega\sum_{j=1}^{n}s_{j}},$ holds for every time-ordered sequence $(t_{1},\cdots,t_{n})$ in $I$ and $s_{j}\geq 0$ ;
and we say that the family $\{A(t)\}_{t\in I}$ satisfies the Kato-condition on $I$ if the following conditions are satisfied: i) $\{A(t)\}_{t\in I}$ is Kato-stable in $X$ . ii) $Y$ is $A(t)$ -admissible for all $t\in I$ , and the family $\{A(t)|_{Y}\}$ of the part of $A(t)$ in $Y$ is Kato-stable in $Y$ . iii) $Y\subset\mathcal{D}(A(t))$ holds for all $t\in I$ , and for all $t\in I$ , $A(t)$ is a bounded operator from $Y$ into $X$ and the mapping $t\mapsto A(t)$ is continuous in the $\mathcal{L}(Y,X)$ norm $\big\lVert\cdot\big\rVert_{Y\rightarrow X}$ . Remark A.1 . If for all $t\in I$ , $A(t)$ is the infinitesimal generator of a quasi-contraction (see Definition A.2 ) semigroup $R_{t}(s)$ satisfying $\big\lVert R_{t}(s)\big\rVert\leq e^{\omega s}$ , then the family $\{A(t)\}_{t\in I}$ is clearly Kato-stable with constants $M=1$ and $\omega$ . Theorem A.4 (Bounded Perturbation Theorem II) . Let $\{A(t)\}_{t\in I}$ be a Kato-stable family of infinitesimal generators with constants $M$ and $\omega$ . Let $\{B(t)\}_{t\in I}$ be bounded linear operators on $X$ . If $\big\lVert B(t)\big\rVert\leq K$ for all $t\in I$ , then $\{A(t)+B(t)\}_{t\in I}$ is a Kato-stable family of infinitesimal generators with constants $M$ and $\omega+KM$ . Theorem A.5 . If the family $\{A(t)\}_{t\in I}$ satisfies the Kato-condition (see Definition A.5 ) then there exists a unique evolution system $W(t,s),0\leq s\leq t\leq T$ , in $X$ satisfying ( $E_{1}$ ) $\big\lVert W(t,s)\big\rVert\leq Me^{\omega(t-s)},\,\forall\,0\leq s\leq t\leq T,$ ( $E_{2}$ ) $\partial^{+}_{t}W(t,s)v\big|_{t=s}=A(s)v,\,\forall\,v\in Y,0\leq s\leq T,$ ( $E_{3}$ ) $\partial_{s}W(t,s)v=-W(t,s)A(s)v,\,\forall\,v\in Y,0\leq s\leq t\leq T$ Here the derivaties in ( $E_{2}$ ) and ( $E_{3}$ ) are in the strong sense in $X$ and in ( $E_{2}$ ) the derivative is from the right. Theorem A.6 . Let the family $\{A(t)\}_{t\in I}$ satisfies the Kato-condition (see Definition A.5 ) and let $W(t,s),0\leq s\leq t\leq T$ be the evolution system given in Theorem A.5 . Suppose that $W(t,s)$ further satisfies both $(E_{4})$ and $(E_{5})$ , where ( $E_{4}$ ) $W(t,s)Y\subset Y,\,\forall\,0\leq s\leq t\leq T,$ ( $E_{5}$ ) $(t,s)\mapsto W(t,s)v\text{ is continuous in }Y\text{ for all }v\in Y.$ Let $f\in\mathcal{C}([s,T];Y)$ , then for every $v\in Y,u(t)=W(t,s)v+\int_{s}^{t}W(t,r)f(r)dr$ is the unique $Y$ -valued solution of the following initial value problem: $\begin{cases}\dfrac{du(t)}{dt}=A(t)u(t)+f(t),\,\forall\,0\leq s\leq t\leq T,\\
u(s)=v;\end{cases}$ and $u$ belongs to $\mathcal{C}([s,T];Y)\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}([s,T];X)$ . Remark A.2 . The definition of $Y$ -valued solution only requires that $u$ belongs to $\mathcal{C}([s,T];Y)\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}((s,T];X)$ (see Definition 4.1 in [ Paz83 ] ), but from the proof of Theorem 5.2 in [ Paz83 ] , we actually have $u\in\mathcal{C}([s,T];Y)\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}([s,T];X)$ .
