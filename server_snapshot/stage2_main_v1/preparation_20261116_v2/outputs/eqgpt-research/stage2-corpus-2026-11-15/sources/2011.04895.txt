We study in this article the blow-up of the solution of the generalized Tricomi equation in the presence of two mixed nonlinearities, namely we consider $\displaystyle(Tr)\hskip 28.45274ptu_{tt}-t^{2m}\Delta u=|u_{t}|^{p}+|u|^{q},\quad\mbox{in}\ {\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times[0,\infty),$ with small initial data, where $m\geq 0$ . For the problem $(Tr)$ with $m=0$ , which corresponds to the uniform wave speed of propagation, it is known that the presence of mixed nonlinearities generates a new blow-up region in comparison with the case of a one nonlinearity ( $|u_{t}|^{p}$ or $|u|^{q}$ ). We show in the present work that the competition between the two nonlinearities still yields a new blow region for the Tricomi equation $(Tr)$ with $m\geq 0$ , and we derive an estimate of the lifespan in terms of the Tricomi parameter $m$ . As an application of the method developed for the study of the equation $(Tr)$ we obtain with a different approach the same blow-up result as in [ 18 ] when we consider only one time-derivative nonlinearity, namely we keep only $|u_{t}|^{p}$ in the right-hand side of $(Tr)$ . Key words and phrases: blow-up, generalized Tricomi equation, lifespan, critical curve, nonlinear wave equations, time-derivative nonlinearity. 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification 35L15, 35L71, 35B44 1. Introduction We consider the following semilinear damped wave equation characterized by a speed of propagation of polynomial type in time, namely the well-known Generalized Tricomi wave equation with mixed nonlinearities reads as follows: (1.1) $\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle u_{tt}-t^{2m}\Delta u=|u_{t}|^{p}+|u|^{q},\quad\mbox{in}\ {\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times[0,\infty),\\
u(x,0)={\varepsilon}f(x),\ u_{t}(x,0)={\varepsilon}g(x),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N},\end{array}\right.$ where $m\geq 0$ , $\ N\geq 1$ denotes the space dimension, ${\varepsilon}>0$ is a sufficiently small parameter describing the smallness of the initial data,
and $f,g$ are positive functions chosen in the energy space with compact support.
Moreover, the functions $f$ and $g$ are supposed to be positive and compactly supported on $B_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}(0,R),R>0$ . Throughout this article, we assume that $m\geq 0$ , $p,q>1$ and $q\leq\frac{2N}{N-2}$ if $N\geq 3$ . The study of the Cauchy problem ( 1.1 ) has been taking different considerations depending on the choice of the nonlinear terms. Being not at all exhaustive, we will recall in the following some existing works in the literature in this direction. Let us first rewrite the system ( 1.1 ) in a more general context. More precisely, we consider the following family of semilinear wave equations: (1.2) $\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle u_{tt}-t^{2m}\Delta u=a|u_{t}|^{p}+b|u|^{q},\quad\mbox{in}\ {\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times[0,\infty),\\
u(x,0)={\varepsilon}f(x),\ u_{t}(x,0)={\varepsilon}g(x),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N},\end{array}\right.$ where $a$ and $b$ are two nonnegative constants. Let $(a,b)=(0,1)$ and $m=0$ in ( 1.2 ). It is worth mentioning that the obtained system was subject to several works and the related blow-up phenomenon is more or less well understood and is linked to the Strauss conjecture. More precisely, this case involves the critical power, $q_{S}$ , which is the positive root of the quadratic equation $(N-1)q^{2}-(N+1)q-2=0$ ,
and is given by (1.3) $q_{S}=q_{S}(N):=\frac{N+1+\sqrt{N^{2}+10N-7}}{2(N-1)}.$ Indeed, if $q\leq q_{S}$ then no global solution for ( 1.2 ) does exist (under some assumptions on the initial data), and for $q>q_{S}$ a global solution exists for small initial data; see e.g. [ 17 , 22 , 26 , 27 ] . Now, the case $m=0$ and $(a,b)=(1,0)$ is connected with the Glassey conjecture saying that the critical power $p_{G}$ should be given by (1.4) $p_{G}=p_{G}(N):=1+\frac{2}{N-1}.$ The critical value $p_{G}$ generates two regions for the power $p$ ; one stating the global existence (for $p>p_{G}$ ) and another one for the nonexistence (for $p\leq p_{G}$ ) of a global small data solution; see e.g. [ 12 , 14 , 16 , 21 , 24 , 28 ] . The case $m=0$ and $a,b\neq 0$ (we may assume that $(a,b)=(1,1)$ ) is characterized by two mixed nonlinearities. In this case, and having $p\leq p_{G}$ or $q\leq q_{S}$ , the blow-up of the solution of ( 1.2 ) is easy. However, compared to a one single nonlinearity, the presence of mixed nonlinearities produces a supplementary blow-up region. In fact, for $p>p_{G}$ and $q>q_{S}$ , the new blow-up frontier is characterized by the following relationship between $p$ and $q$ : (1.5) $\lambda(p,q,N):=(q-1)\big[(N-1)p-2\big]<4;$ see e.g. [ 1 , 7 , 13 , 25 ] for more details. Now, let us consider $m\geq 0$ and $(a,b)=(0,1)$ in ( 1.2 ). This case corresponds to the semilinear generalized Tricomi equation with power nonlinearity which has been widely studied in several works. In [ 9 , 19 ] , the authors proved the blow-up results for both the subcritical and critical cases. However, the question of global existence for the system ( 1.2 ) with small data in the supercritical case has been proved only for $m=1$ and for dimensions $N=1,2$ ; see e.g. [ 8 , 9 , 10 , 11 ] . Hence, the critical exponent for ( 1.2 ), that we denote by $q_{C}(N,m)$ ,
should be given by the greatest root of the following quadratic equation (1.6) $\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{tr}(q):=((m+1)N-1)q^{2}-((m+1)N+1-2m)q-2(m+1)=0.$ Naturally, for $m=0$ we find again the well-known Strauss exponent $q_{S}(N)$ , namely $q_{C}(N,0)=q_{S}(N)$ , where $q_{S}(N)$ is given by ( 1.3 ). For $m\geq 0$ and $(a,b)=(1,0)$ , the system ( 1.2 ) is given by (1.7) $\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle u_{tt}-t^{2m}\Delta u=|u_{t}|^{p},\quad\mbox{in}\ {\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times[0,\infty),\\
u(x,0)={\varepsilon}f(x),\ u_{t}(x,0)={\varepsilon}g(x),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}.\end{array}\right.$ Concerning the blow-up results and lifespan estimate of the solution of ( 1.7 ), it was proven in [ 20 ] that for $p\leq p_{G}(N(m+1))$ we have the blow-up. To prove this result, the authors used, among other tools, the integral representation formula in one space dimension. However, this result was recently improved in [ 18 ] . The main tool in the latter work is the construction of adequate test functions using the properties of Bessel functions. Hence, the new obtained region involves an almost sure candidate for the critical exponent, that is (1.8) $p\leq p_{tr}(N,m):=1+\frac{2}{(m+1)(N-1)-m}.$ The authors in [ 18 ] conjecture that $p_{tr}(N,m)$ is in fact the critical exponent for the blow-up of the solution of ( 1.7 ). Of course this has to be confirmed by a global existence result for ( 1.7 ). We are interested in this article in studying the blow-up result of the solution of ( 1.1 ) for $m\geq 0$ . First, let us recall that the case $m=0$ in ( 1.1 ) corresponds in fact to the classical wave equation with mixed nonlinearities which, compared to the case with one nonlinearity $|u_{t}|^{p}$ or $|u|^{q}$ , for $p>p_{G}(N)$ or $q>q_{S}(N)$ , generates a new blow-up region, given by ( 1.5 ), due to the interaction between the two mixed nonlinearities. In this work we will examine the influence of the parameter $m$ on the blow-up result and the lifespan estimate for the Cauchy problem ( 1.1 ) for $m\geq 0$ . Naturally, the emphasis will be here for the exponents $p>p_{tr}(N,m)$ or $q>q_{C}(N,m)$ . Our target is to give the curve delimiting the new blow-up region for the Tricomi equation given by ( 1.1 ), and to look into its change compared to ( 1.5 ). The Tricomi equation constitutes somehow a time-dependent wave speed of propagation. As shown below (see ( 1.10 )), the effect of this Tricomi term is more or less similar to a scale-invariant damping. For instance, let $v(x,\tau)=u(x,t)$ where (1.9) $\tau=\xi(t):=\frac{t^{m+1}}{m+1}.$ Hence, the solution $v(x,\tau)$ verifies the following equation: (1.10) $\displaystyle v_{\tau\tau}-\Delta v+\frac{\mu_{m}}{\tau}\,\partial_{\tau}v=C_{m,p}\tau^{\mu_{m}(p-2)}|\partial_{\tau}v|^{p}+\mathcal{K}_{m}\tau^{-2\mu_{m}}|v|^{q},\quad\mbox{in}\ {\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times[0,\infty),$ where $\mu_{m}:=\frac{m}{m+1}$ , $\mathcal{K}_{m}=(m+1)^{-2\mu_{m}}$ and $C_{m,p}=(m+1)^{\mu_{m}(p-2)}$ . Note that the equation ( 1.10 ) is somehow related to the Euler-Darboux-Poisson equation. Moreover, thanks to this transformation, which implies a kind of similarity with the scale-invariant damping case, we can inherit the methods used in our previous works [ 4 , 5 , 6 ] to build the proofs of our main results which are related, as a first target, to the blow-up of the solution of ( 1.1 ) and, as a secondary aim, to the blow-up of ( 1.7 ). The article is organized as follows. The beginning of Section 2 is devoted to the setting of the weak formulation of ( 1.1 ) in the energy space. Then, we state the main theorems of our work, and we end this section by some remarks. To develop the proofs of our results, we prove some technical lemmas in Section 3 . Finally, Sections 4 and 5 are committed to the proofs of Theorems 2.2 and 2.3 . 2. Main Results In this section, we start by giving a sense to the energy solution of ( 1.1 ) which reads as follows: Definition 2.1 . Let $f,g\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})$ . We say that $u$ is an energy solution of
( 1.1 ) on $[0,T)$ if $\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u\in\mathcal{C}([0,T),H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}))\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,T),L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})),\\
u\in L^{q}_{loc}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N})\ \text{and}\ u_{t}\in L^{p}_{loc}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}),\end{array}\right.$ satisfies, for all $\Phi\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times[0,T))$ and all $t\in[0,T)$ , the following equation: (2.1) $\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}u_{t}(x,t)\Phi(x,t)dx-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}u_{t}(x,0)\Phi(x,0)dx\\
\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}u_{t}(x,s)\Phi_{t}(x,s)dx\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}s^{2m}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\nabla u(x,s)\cdot\nabla\Phi(x,s)dx\,ds\\
\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\left\{|u_{t}(x,s)|^{p}+|u(x,s)|^{q}\right\}\Phi(x,s)dx\,ds.\end{array}$ It is clear that the weak formulation corresponding to ( 1.7 ) is also given by an analogous identity to ( 2.1 ) with omitting the nonlinear term $|u|^{q}$ . Now, we state the main results of this article which are related to the blow-up region and the lifespan estimate of the solutions of ( 1.1 ) and ( 1.7 ), respectively. Theorem 2.2 . Let $p,q>1$ and $m\geq 0$ such that (2.2) $\Lambda(p,q,N,m)<4,$ where $\Lambda(p,q,N,m)$ is defined by (2.3) $\Lambda(p,q,N,m):=(q-1)\big[(m+1)(N-1)p-m(p-2)-2\big],$ and $p>p_{tr}(N,m)$ and $q>q_{C}(N,m)$ . Suppose that $f,g\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})$ are non-negative functions which are compactly supported on $B_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}(0,R)$ ,
and do not vanish everywhere.
Let $u$ be an energy solution of ( 1.1 ) on $[0,T_{\varepsilon})$ (in the sense of ( 2.1 )) such that $\mbox{\rm supp}(u)\ \subset\{(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times[0,\infty):|x|\leq\xi(t)+R\}$ .
Then, there exists a constant ${\varepsilon}_{0}={\varepsilon}_{0}(f,g,N,R,p,q,m)>0$ such that $T_{\varepsilon}$ verifies $T_{\varepsilon}\leq C\,{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{2p(q-1)}{4-\Lambda(p,q,N,m)}},$ where $C$ is a positive constant independent of ${\varepsilon}$ and $0<{\varepsilon}\leq{\varepsilon}_{0}$ . Theorem 2.3 . Let $p>1$ and $m\geq 0$ . Assume that the initial data $f$ and $g$ verify the same hypotheses as in Theorem 2.2 . Then, for $u$ an energy solution of ( 1.7 ) on $[0,T_{\varepsilon})$ which satisfies $\mbox{\rm supp}(u)\ \subset\{(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times[0,\infty):|x|\leq\xi(t)+R\}$ , there exists a constant ${\varepsilon}_{0}={\varepsilon}_{0}(f,g,N,R,p,m)>0$ such that $T_{\varepsilon}$ verifies $T_{\varepsilon}\leq\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}C\,{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{2(p-1)}{2-((m+1)(N-1)-m)(p-1)}}&\ \text{for}\ 1<p<p_{tr}(N,m),\\
\exp\left(C{\varepsilon}^{-(p-1)}\right)&\ \text{for}\ p=p_{tr}(N,m)=1+\frac{2}{(m+1)(N-1)-m},\end{array}\right.$ where $C$ is a positive constant independent of ${\varepsilon}$ and $0<{\varepsilon}\leq{\varepsilon}_{0}$ . Remark 2.1 . Note that for $m=0$ in ( 1.1 ), we have $\Lambda(p,q,N,0)=\lambda(p,q,N)$ , which are respectively given by ( 2.3 ) and ( 1.5 ), and this gives raise to the classical wave equation with combined nonlinearities. Remark 2.2 . Let $p_{0}:=p_{tr}(N,m)+\beta_{1}=1+\frac{2}{(m+1)(N-1)-m}+\beta_{1},$ and $\displaystyle q_{0}:=1+\frac{4}{(m+1)(N-1)p_{0}-m(p_{0}-2)-2}-\beta_{2}>1+\frac{4}{(m+1)N-1}-\beta_{3},$ where $\beta_{i}$ is a positive constant taken small enough for all $i=1,2,3$ . It is easy to see that $\mathcal{Q}_{tr}\left(1+\frac{4}{(m+1)N-1}\right)>0$ . Then, for $\beta_{1}$ and $\beta_{2}$ small enough, we have $\mathcal{Q}_{tr}(q_{0})>0$ . Consequently, we have $q_{0}>q_{C}(N,m)$ for $\beta_{1}$ and $\beta_{2}$ small enough. Hence, the pair $(p_{0},q_{0})$ satisfies ( 2.2 ), $p_{0}>p_{tr}(N,m)$ and $q_{0}>q_{C}(N,m)$ . Hence, the hypothesis on $p$ and $q$ in Theorem 2.2 makes sense. Remark 2.3 . We note that, for $p>p_{G}(N)$ and $q>q_{S}(N)$ , the equality in ( 1.5 ) yields the global existence of the solution of ( 1.2 ) (with $m=0$ and $(a,b)=(1,1)$ ) without the intermediate step of “almost global solution”; see [ 13 ] . This is in fact related to the presence of mixed nonlinearities. Naturally, it is interesting to see whether this phenomenon still occurs for the case $m>0$ , namely when $\Lambda(p,q,N,m)=4$ and for $p>p_{tr}(N,m)$ and $q>q_{C}(N,m)$ . To complete the whole picture, the global existence in-time of the solution of ( 1.1 ) will be studied in a subsequent work. 3. Proof of Theorem 2.2 This section is aimed to detail the proof of the first main blow-up result which is related to the solution of the Cauchy problem ( 1.1 ). To this end, we will make use of the lemmas proven in Section 3 together with a Kato’s lemma type. Following the hypotheses in Theorem 2.2 , we recall that $\mbox{\rm supp}(u)\ \subset\{(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times[0,\infty):|x|\leq\xi(t)+R\}$ .
For $t\in[0,T)$ , we set (4.1) $F(t):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}u(x,t)dx.$ Recall here the weak formulation ( 2.1 ) in which we set a test function $\Phi$ such that $\Phi\equiv 1$ in $\{(x,s)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times[0,t]:|x|\leq\xi(s)+R\}$ 2 2 2 This choice is possible thanks to the fact that the energy solution $u$ verifies $\mbox{\rm supp}(u)\ \subset\{(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times[0,\infty):|x|\leq\xi(t)+R\}$ . , and we obtain (4.2) $\begin{array}[]{r}\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}u_{t}(x,t)dx-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}u_{t}(x,0)dx=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\left\{|u_{t}(x,s)|^{p}+|u(x,s)|^{q}\right\}dx\,ds.\end{array}$ Using the definition of $F(t)$ , the equation ( 4.2 ) yields (4.3) $F^{\prime}(t)=F^{\prime}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\left\{|u_{t}(x,s)|^{p}+|u(x,s)|^{q}\right\}dx\,ds.$ Differentiating the above equation in time, we infer that (4.4) $F^{\prime\prime}(t)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\left\{|u_{t}(x,t)|^{p}+|u(x,t)|^{q}\right\}dx.$ Integrating ( 4.4 ) twice in time over $(0,t)$ and using the positivity of $F(0)$ and $F^{\prime}(0)$ , we deduce that (4.5) $\displaystyle F(t)\geq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\left\{|u_{t}(x,\tau)|^{p}+|u(x,\tau)|^{q}\right\}dx\,d\tau\,ds.$ Thanks to the Hölder’s inequality and the estimates ( 3.10 ) and ( 3.24 ), we have (4.6) $\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|u_{t}(x,t)|^{p}dx&\geq&\displaystyle G_{2}^{p}(t)\left(\int_{|x|\leq\xi(t)+R}\Big(\psi(x,t)\Big)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}dx\right)^{-(p-1)}\\
&\geq&C\rho^{-p}(t)e^{-p\xi(t)}{\varepsilon}^{p}(\xi(t))^{-\frac{(N-1)(p-2)}{2}},\quad\forall\ t\geq T_{1}.\end{array}$ From the expression of $\xi(t)$ , given by ( 1.9 ), ( 3.3 ) and ( 3.22 ), we deduce that (4.7) $\displaystyle\rho(t)e^{\xi(t)}\leq Ct^{\frac{-m}{2}},\ \forall\ t\geq T_{0}/2.$ Combining ( 4.6 ) and ( 4.7 ), we get (4.8) $\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|u_{t}(x,t)|^{p}dx\geq C{\varepsilon}^{p}t^{-\frac{(m+1)(N-1)(p-2)-mp}{2}},\ \forall\ t\geq T_{1}.\\
$ Plugging the above inequality into ( 4.5 ), we obtain (4.9) $\displaystyle F(t)$ $\displaystyle\geq C{\varepsilon}^{p}t^{2-\frac{(m+1)(N-1)(p-2)-mp}{2}},\ \forall\ t\geq T_{1}.$ Otherwise, we have (4.10) $\displaystyle\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}u(x,t)dx\Big)^{q}\leq\int_{|x|\leq\xi(t)+R}|u(x,t)|^{q}dx\Big(\int_{|x|\leq\xi(t)+R}dx\Big)^{q-1},$ which implies that (4.11) $\displaystyle F^{q}(t)\leq C(1+t)^{N(q-1)(m+1)}\int_{|x|\leq\xi(t)+R}|u(x,t)|^{q}dx.$ Gathering ( 4.4 ) and ( 4.11 ), we deduce that (4.12) $F^{\prime\prime}(t)\geq C\frac{F^{q}(t)}{(1+t)^{N(q-1)(m+1)}},\ \forall\ t>0.$ From ( 4.3 ) we have $F^{\prime}(t)>0$ . Then, multiplying ( 4.12 ) by $F^{\prime}(t)$ and integrating the obtained inequality, we get (4.13) $\Big(F^{\prime}(t)\Big)^{2}\geq C\frac{F^{q+1}(t)}{(1+t)^{N(q-1)(m+1)}}+\left((F^{\prime}(0))^{2}-CF^{q+1}(0)\right),\ \forall\ t>0.$ Since we consider here small initial data, we can easily see that the last term in the right-hand side of ( 4.13 ) is positive, and more precisely this holds for ${\varepsilon}$ small enough.
Therefore ( 4.13 ) gives (4.14) $\frac{F^{\prime}(t)}{F^{1+\delta}(t)}\geq C\frac{F^{\frac{q-1}{2}-\delta}(t)}{(1+t)^{\frac{N(q-1)(m+1)}{2}}},\ \forall\ t>0,$ for $\delta>0$ small enough. Integrating the inequality ( 4.14 ) on $[t_{1},t_{2}]$ , for all $t_{2}>t_{1}\geq T_{1}$ , and using ( 4.9 ), we obtain (4.15) $\frac{1}{\delta}\Big(\frac{1}{F^{\delta}(t_{1})}-\frac{1}{F^{\delta}(t_{2})}\Big)\geq C({\varepsilon}^{p})^{\frac{q-1}{2}-\delta}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{(1+s)^{(2-\frac{(m+1)(N-1)(p-2)-mp}{2})(\frac{q-1}{2}-\delta)}}{(1+s)^{\frac{N(q-1)(m+1)}{2}}}ds,\ \forall\ t_{2}>t_{1}\geq T_{1}.$ Neglecting the second term in the left-hand side of ( 4.15 ) and using the definition of $\Lambda(p,q,N,m)$ (given by ( 2.3 )) yield (4.16) $\frac{1}{F^{\delta}(t_{1})}\geq C\delta{\varepsilon}^{\frac{p(q-1)}{2}-p\delta}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(1+s)^{-\frac{\Lambda(p,q,N,m)}{4}-\delta\left(2-\frac{(m+1)(N-1)(p-2)-mp}{2}\right)}ds.$ Thanks to the hypothesis ( 2.2 ), we can choose $\delta=\delta_{0}$ small enough such that $\gamma:=-\frac{\Lambda(p,q,N,m)}{4}-\delta_{0}\left(2-\frac{(m+1)(N-1)(p-2)-mp}{2}\right)>-1$ . Then, the estimate ( 4.16 ) implies that (4.17) $\frac{1}{F^{\delta_{0}}(t_{1})}\geq C{\varepsilon}^{\frac{p(q-1)}{2}-p\delta_{0}}\,\left((1+t_{2})^{\gamma+1}-(1+t_{1})^{\gamma+1}\right),\ \forall\ t_{2}>t_{1}\geq T_{1}.$ From ( 4.9 ), we infer that (4.18) ${\varepsilon}^{\frac{p(q-1)}{2}}\,\left((1+t_{2})^{\gamma+1}-(1+t_{1})^{\gamma+1}\right)\leq C_{3}(1+t_{1})^{-\delta_{0}\left(2-\frac{(m+1)(N-1)(p-2)-mp}{2}\right)},\ \forall\ t_{2}>t_{1}\geq T_{1}.$ Consequently, we have (4.19) $\displaystyle{\varepsilon}^{\frac{p(q-1)}{2}}\,(1+t_{2})^{\gamma+1}$ $\displaystyle\leq$ $\displaystyle C_{3}(1+t_{1})^{-\delta_{0}\left(2-\frac{(m+1)(N-1)(p-2)-mp}{2}\right)}$ $\displaystyle+{\varepsilon}^{\frac{p(q-1)}{2}}\,(1+t_{1})^{\gamma+1},\ \forall\ t_{2}>t_{1}\geq T_{1}.$ Since $-\frac{\Lambda(p,q,N,m)}{4}+1>0$ , then for all ${\varepsilon}>0$ , we choose $\tilde{T}_{2}$ such that (4.20) $\tilde{T}_{2}=C_{3}^{-\frac{4}{4-\Lambda(p,q,N,m)}}{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{2p(q-1)}{4-\Lambda(p,q,N,m)}}.$ Finally, we set $t_{1}=\max(T_{1},\tilde{T}_{2})$ and we plug ( 4.20 ) in ( 4.19 ) to obtain that (4.21) $t_{2}\leq 2^{\frac{1}{\gamma+1}}(1+t_{1})\leq C{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{2p(q-1)}{4-\Lambda(p,q,N,m)}}.$ The proof of Theorem 2.2 is now complete. $\Box$ 5.
