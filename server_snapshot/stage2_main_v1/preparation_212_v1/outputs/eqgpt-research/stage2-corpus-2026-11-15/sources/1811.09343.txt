This paper is concerned with the Neumann initial-boundary value problem for the two-species chemotaxis system with consumption of chemoattractant $\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0ptu_{t}=\Delta u-\chi_{1}\nabla\cdot(u\nabla w),&{}x\in\Omega,\ t>0,\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0ptv_{t}=\Delta v-\chi_{2}\nabla\cdot(v\nabla w),&{}x\in\Omega,\ t>0,\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0ptw_{t}=\Delta w-(\alpha u+\beta v)w,&{}x\in\Omega,\ t>0\end{array}\right.$ in a smooth bounded domain $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}$ ( $n\geq 2$ ), where the parameters $\chi_{1}$ , $\chi_{2}$ , $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are positive. It is proved that if $\max\{\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\}\|w(x,0)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<\sqrt{\frac{2}{n}}\pi$ the problem possesses a unique global classical solution that is uniformly bounded. Moreover, we prove that $u(x,t)\to\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}u(x,0),\quad v(x,t)\to\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}v(x,0)\quad\mbox{and}\quad w(x,t)\to 0\quad\mbox{as}\ t\to\infty$ uniformly with respect $x\in\Omega$ . Key words and phrases: chemotaxis system, global solution, boundedness, stabilization. 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification 35A09, 35B40, 35K55, 92C17. 1. Introduction In this paper, we deal with the Neumann initial-boundary value problem for the chemotaxis system $\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0ptu_{t}=\Delta u-\chi_{1}\nabla\cdot(u\nabla w),&{}x\in\Omega,\ t>0,\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0ptv_{t}=\Delta v-\chi_{2}\nabla\cdot(v\nabla w),&{}x\in\Omega,\ t>0,\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0ptw_{t}=\Delta w-(\alpha u+\beta v)w,&{}x\in\Omega,\ t>0,\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}=\frac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}=0,&{}x\in\partial\Omega,t>0,\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0ptu(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad v(x,0)=v_{0}(x),\quad w(x,0)=w_{0}(x),&{}x\in\Omega\end{array}\right.$ in the bounded domain $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}$ $(n\geq 2)$ with smooth boundary, where $\chi_{1}$ , $\chi_{2}$ , $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are positive constants, the unknown functions $u=u(x,t)$ and $v=v(x,t)$ stand for polulation densities and $w=w(x,t)$ denotes the concentration of chemoattractant. The symbol $\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}$ represents differentiation with respect to the outward normal $\nu$ on $\partial\Omega$ . The initial data $u_{0}$ , $v_{0}$ and $w_{0}$ are given positive functions satisfying (1.7) $u_{0}\in C^{0}(\bar{\Omega}),\quad v_{0}\in C^{0}(\bar{\Omega})\quad\mbox{and}\quad w_{0}\in W^{1,q}(\Omega)$ with some $q>n$ . The model ( 1 ) is used in mathematical biology to describe the movement of two populations in respond to the concentration gradient of one common chemical signal. It is a generalization of the famous Keller-Segel system [ 8 ] $\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0ptu_{t}=\Delta u-\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla w),\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0ptw_{t}=\Delta w-uw,\end{array}\right.$ which has been studied from a mathematical viewpoint in the last years. It is proved that if $n\leq 2$ or $\chi\|w(x,0)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<\frac{1}{6(n+1)}$ in higher dimensions $n\geq 3$ , the problem ( 1 ) possesses a unique global classical solution which is bounded and satisfies (1.11) $u(x,t)\to\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}u_{0}\quad\mbox{and}\quad w(x,t)\to 0\quad\mbox{as}\ t\to\infty$ uniformly with respect to $x\in\Omega$ [ 14 , 15 , 26 ] . Moreover, the problem admits at least one global weak solution which is eventually smooth and enjoys the convergence properties ( 1.11 ) in bounded convex domain $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}$ [ 15 ] . Recently, some blow-up properties of ( 1 ), including blow-up criteria for the local classical solution, lower global blow-up estimate on $\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}$ and local non-degeneracy property for the blow-up points, have been obtained in [ 6 ] . Furthermore, global solutions and the stabilization for the corresponding variants of ( 1 ), such as chemotaxis-consumption systems with tensor-valued sensitivities [ 9 , 11 , 22 , 25 ] and singular sensitivities [ 24 ] , system ( 1 ) with logistic source [ 10 , 1 ] or coupled chemotaxis-fluid system [ 12 , 21 , 20 , 23 ] have also been investigated. For more results on the model variations of ( 1 ), we refer to the recent survey [ 2 ] and the references therein. As to the problem ( 1 ) with Lotka-Volterra competitive kinetics $\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0ptu_{t}=\Delta u-\chi_{1}\nabla\cdot(u\nabla w)+\mu_{1}u(1-u-a_{1}v),&{}x\in\Omega,\ t>0,\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0ptv_{t}=\Delta v-\chi_{2}\nabla\cdot(v\nabla w)+\mu_{2}v(1-a_{2}u-v),&{}x\in\Omega,\ t>0,\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0ptw_{t}=\Delta w-(\alpha u+\beta v)w,&{}x\in\Omega,\ t>0,\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}=\frac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}=0,&{}x\in\partial\Omega,t>0,\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0ptu(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad v(x,0)=v_{0}(x),\quad w(x,0)=w_{0}(x),&{}x\in\Omega,\end{array}\right.$ it is shown that solutions exist globally and remain bounded if either $n=2$ [ 5 ] or $\chi_{i}\|w_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{n+1}},\quad i=1,2$ in the case $n\geq 3$ [ 16 ] , and if $a_{1}$ , $a_{2}\in(0,1)$ these solutions satisfy $(u(\cdot,t),v(\cdot,t),w(\cdot,t))\to\left(\frac{1-a_{1}}{1-a_{1}a_{2}},\frac{1-a_{2}}{1-a_{1}a_{2}},0\right)\quad\mbox{in}\ L^{\infty}(\Omega)$ and if $a_{1}\geq 1>a_{2}$ , $(u(\cdot,t),v(\cdot,t),w(\cdot,t))\to(0,1,0)\quad\mbox{in}\ L^{\infty}(\Omega)$ as $t\to\infty$ [ 5 , 16 ] . On the other hand, the global existence and stabilization of (weak) solutions to the two-species chemotaxis-fluid system with Lotka-Volterra competitive kinetics have also been established, see e.g. [ 5 , 3 , 4 , 7 ] . However, to the best of our knowledge, the problem ( 1 ) seems not be investigated yet in the literature. In the present paper, we prove global existence, boundedness and stabilization of classical solutions to the problem ( 1 ). Our main results are stated as follows. Theorem 1.1 . Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}$ $(n\geq 2)$ be bounded domain with smooth boundary and let the parameters $\chi_{1},\chi_{2},\alpha,\beta>0$ . If $\chi_{i}\|w_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<\sqrt{\frac{2}{n}}\pi,\quad i=1,2,$ then for any initial data $u_{0}$ , $v_{0}$ and $w_{0}$ satisfying ( 1.7 ) , the problem ( 1 ) possesses a unique global classical solution which is bounded in $\Omega\times(0,\infty)$ . The second result concerns stabilization of the solution provided by Theorem 1.1 . Theorem 1.2 . Under the assumptions of Theorem 1.1 , the global classical solution of ( 1 ) satisfies $\displaystyle\|u(\cdot,t)-\bar{u}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\to 0,$ $\displaystyle\|v(\cdot,t)-\bar{v}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\to 0,$ $\displaystyle\|w(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\to 0$ as $t\to\infty$ , where $\bar{u}_{0}:=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}u_{0}$ and $\bar{v}_{0}:=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}v_{0}$ . The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we list some preliminaries. Section 3 is devoted to the proof of Theorem 1.1 . Finally, we prove Theorem 1.2 in Section 4. 2. Preliminaries As a preparation to the proof, we first state one result concerning local existence of classical solution to the problem ( 1 ). Lemma 2.1 . Let $u_{0}$ , $v_{0}$ and $w_{0}$ satisfy ( 1.7 ) , and let $\chi_{1},\chi_{2},\alpha,\beta>0$ . Then there exist $T_{\max}\leq\infty$ and a uniquely determined triple $(u,v,w)$ of nonnegative functions $\displaystyle u\in C^{0}(\bar{\Omega}\times[0,T_{\max}))\cap C^{2,1}(\bar{\Omega}\times(0,T_{\max})),$ $\displaystyle v\in C^{0}(\bar{\Omega}\times[0,T_{\max}))\cap C^{2,1}(\bar{\Omega}\times(0,T_{\max})),$ $\displaystyle w\in C^{0}(\bar{\Omega}\times[0,T_{\max}))\cap C^{2,1}(\bar{\Omega}\times(0,T_{\max}))\cap L_{loc}^{\infty}([0,T_{\max});W^{1,q}(\Omega)),$ which solves ( 1 ) in the classical sense. If $T_{\max}<\infty$ , then $\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|v(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|w(t)\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega)}\to\infty\quad\mbox{as}\ t\nearrow T_{\max}.$ Furthermore, the solution $(u,v,w)$ satisfies (2.1) $\|u(t)\|_{L^{1}{(\Omega)}}=\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}{(\Omega)}}\quad\mbox{for all}\ t\in(0,T_{max})$ and (2.2) $\|v(t)\|_{L^{1}{(\Omega)}}=\|v_{0}\|_{L^{1}{(\Omega)}}\quad\mbox{for all}\ t\in(0,T_{max})$ as well as (2.3) $0\leq w\leq\|w_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\quad\mbox{in}\ \bar{\Omega}\times[0,T_{max}).$ Proof. The local existence and regularity of the solution is based on standard contraction mapping arguments and parabolic regularity theory, which can be found in [ 19 , Lemma 1.1] (see also [ 14 , Lemma 2.1] ). Integrating the first and the second equations in ( 1 ), we immediately obtain $\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}u(x,t)dx=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}v(x,t)dx=0$ which yield ( 2.1 ) and ( 2.2 ). The statement ( 2.3 ) follows from an application of the maximum principle.
∎ The following lemma is a generalization of [ 25 , Lemma 3.2] , which asserts that a bound for $\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}$ and $\|v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}$ with $p>\frac{n}{2}$ for all $t\in(0,T_{\max})$ can guarantee the global existence and boundedness of classical solutions to ( 1 ). Lemma 2.2 . Suppose that the initial data $u_{0}$ , $v_{0}$ and $w_{0}$ satisfy ( 1.7 ) and the parameters $\chi_{1},\chi_{2},\alpha,\beta>0$ . Let $p\geq 1$ . If the first and second components of solution satisfy (2.4) $\sup_{t\in(0,T_{\max})}\left(\|u(t)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|v(t)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right)<\infty,$ for some $p>\frac{n}{2}$ , then the solution of ( 1 ) is global in time. Moreover, the solution fulfills $\sup_{t\in(0,\infty)}\left(\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|v(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|w(t)\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega)}\right)<\infty.$ Proof. Since our assumption $q>n$ and for each fixed $p>\frac{n}{2}$ there holds $\displaystyle\frac{np}{(n-p)_{+}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\infty,&{}\mbox{if}\ p\geq n,\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\frac{np}{n-p}>n,&{}\mbox{if}\ \frac{n}{2}<p<n,\end{array}\right.$ it is possible to find $1<p_{0}<q$ fulfilling (2.6) $n<p_{0}<\frac{np}{(n-p)_{+}},$ which enables us to choose $k>1$ such that (2.7) $n<kp_{0}<\frac{np}{(n-p)_{+}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad kp_{0}\leq q.$ We shall argue by contradiction. Assume that $T_{\max}<\infty$ . Applying the variation-of-constants formula $w(\cdot,t)=e^{t\Delta}w_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}(\alpha u(\cdot,s)+\beta v(\cdot,s))w(\cdot,s)ds,$ we get $\|\nabla w(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{kp_{0}}(\Omega)}\leq\left\|\nabla e^{t\Delta}w_{0}\right\|_{L^{kp_{0}}(\Omega)}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\nabla e^{(t-s)\Delta}(\alpha u(\cdot,s)+\beta v(\cdot,s))w(\cdot,s)\right\|_{L^{kp_{0}}(\Omega)}ds$ for all $t\in(0,T_{\max})$ . By standard smoothing estimates for the Neumann heat semigroup [ 18 , Lemma 1.3] , ( 2.3 ) and $kp_{0}\leq q$ due to ( 2.7 ), we obtain positive constants $C_{1}$ and $C_{2}$ such that $\displaystyle\|\nabla w(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{kp_{0}}(\Omega)}$ $\displaystyle\leq$ $\displaystyle C_{1}\left\|w_{0}\right\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega)}+C_{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left(1+(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{kp_{0}})}\right)e^{-\lambda_{1}(t-s)}$ $\displaystyle\times\left\|(\alpha u(\cdot,s)+\beta v(\cdot,s))w(\cdot,s)\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}ds$ $\displaystyle\leq$ $\displaystyle C_{1}\left\|w_{0}\right\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega)}+C_{2}\|w_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\int_{0}^{t}\left(1+(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{kp_{0}})}\right)e^{-\lambda_{1}(t-s)}$ $\displaystyle\times\left(\|u(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|v(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right)ds$ for all $t\in(0,T_{\max})$ . Here and below, $\lambda_{1}>0$ denotes the first nonzero eigenvalue of $-\Delta$ in $\Omega$ under homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions. Since $\frac{1}{2}+\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{kp_{0}})<1$ by the right-hand side of the first inequality in ( 2.7 ) and ( 2.4 ), we can take constants $C_{3}>0$ and $C_{4}>0$ fulfilling (2.8) $\displaystyle\|\nabla w(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{kp_{0}}(\Omega)}$ $\displaystyle\leq$ $\displaystyle C_{1}\left\|w_{0}\right\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega)}+C_{3}\left(\sup_{t\in(0,T_{\max})}\left(\|u(t)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|v(t)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right)\right)$ $\displaystyle\leq$ $\displaystyle C_{4}\quad\mbox{for all}\ t\in(0,T_{max}).$ Next by the variation-of-constants formula $u(\cdot,t)=e^{t\Delta}u_{0}-\chi_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\nabla e^{(t-s)\Delta}u(\cdot,s)\nabla w(\cdot,s)ds,$ we have $\displaystyle\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}$ $\displaystyle\leq$ $\displaystyle\left\|e^{t\Delta}u_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\chi_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\big\|\nabla e^{(t-s)\Delta}u(\cdot,s)\nabla w(\cdot,s)\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}ds$ for all $t\in(0,T_{\max})$ . In view of the maximum principle and smoothing estimates for the Neumann heat semigroup [ 18 , Lemma 1.3] , we obtain $C_{5}>0$ satisfying (2.9) $\displaystyle\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+C_{5}\int_{0}^{t}\left(1+(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{n}{2p_{0}}}\right)e^{-\lambda_{1}(t-s)}\big\|u(\cdot,s)\nabla w(\cdot,s)\big\|_{L^{p_{0}}(\Omega)}ds$ for all $t\in(0,T_{\max})$ . Here by the Hölder inequality, interpolation inequality, ( 2.1 ) and ( 2.8 ), we can find find $C_{6}>0$ such that $\displaystyle\big\|u(\cdot,s)\nabla w(\cdot,s)\big\|_{L^{p_{0}}(\Omega)}$ $\displaystyle\leq$ $\displaystyle\big\|u(\cdot,s)\big\|_{L^{k^{\prime}p_{0}}(\Omega)}\big\|\nabla w(\cdot,s)\big\|_{L^{kp_{0}}(\Omega)}$ $\displaystyle\leq$ $\displaystyle\big\|u(\cdot,s)\big\|^{r}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\big\|u(\cdot,s)\big\|^{1-r}_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\big\|\nabla w(\cdot,s)\big\|_{L^{kp_{0}}(\Omega)}$ $\displaystyle\leq$ $\displaystyle C_{6}\big\|u(\cdot,s)\big\|^{r}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\quad\mbox{for all}\ s\in(0,T_{\max}),$ where $k^{\prime}$ is the dual exponent of $k$ and $r=1-\frac{1}{k^{\prime}p_{0}}\in(0,1)$ . Inserting this into ( 2.9 ), it follows that $\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+C_{7}\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\|u(\cdot,t)\|^{r}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\quad\mbox{for all}\ T\in(0,T_{\max})$ with $C_{7}=C_{5}C_{6}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1+\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{n}{2p_{0}}}\right)e^{-\lambda_{1}\sigma}d\sigma$ is finite thanks to the left-hand side of ( 2.6 ). Therefore, there exists a constant $C_{8}>0$ such that $\displaystyle\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C_{8}\quad\mbox{for all}\ t\in(0,T_{\max}).$ Arguing similarly as above, we see that $\displaystyle\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C_{9}\quad\mbox{for all}\ t\in(0,T_{\max}).$ with some $C_{9}>0$ . This is a contradiction to Lemma 2.1 . Hence we complete the proof.
∎ For the proof of the main result we also need the following technical lemma which provides the desired weight function. Lemma 2.3 . Let $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$ and $p>1$ . ∎ 3. Global existence. Proof of Theorem 1.1 With the preliminaries at hand, we are now prepared to prove global existence and boundedness in ( 1 ).
